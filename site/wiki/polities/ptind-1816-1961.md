# Portuguese India (Estado da India Portuguesa) — PTIND-1816-1961

## Overview

Portuguese India (Estado da India Portuguesa) was Portugal's colonial territory on the Indian subcontinent, comprising three enclaves: Goa (the largest, ~3,702 km²), Daman (~72 km²), and Diu (~38 km²). Total area ~3,812 km². Portuguese presence dates to 1510 (Goa) and 1535 (Diu), but WHEP span begins 1816 (Congress of Vienna normalisation of colonial possessions). The territory was annexed by India in December 1961 (Operation Vijay).

## Territorial Extent

- **Goa**: West-coast enclave between Maharashtra and Karnataka, ~3,702 km²
- **Daman**: Small enclave on the Gujarat coast, ~72 km²
- **Diu**: Island off the Kathiawar Peninsula (Gujarat), ~38 km²
- **Total**: ~3,812 km²

The three enclaves were non-contiguous. Borders were formally surveyed and unchanged between 1816 and 1961 (no annexations or cessions within the WHEP span). This stability justifies using modern GADM administrative boundaries as a period proxy.

## Polygon Status

**ESTIMATE** — composed union of modern GADM 4.1 units:
- Goa state (Indian administrative state, inherits 1961 annexation lines)
- Daman and Diu Union Territory

**Why ESTIMATE and not assigned:**
No historical GIS vector for Portuguese India was found in any priority source:
1. CShapes 2.0 — no separate Portuguese India / Goa entry
2. Cliopatria v0.1.3 — no Portuguese India / Goa series
3. Paine et al. 2024 — Africa-focused, does not cover South Asian colonies

**Justification for GADM proxy:** The 1961 Indian annexation preserved the colonial boundary lines almost exactly when creating the Goa state and Daman & Diu UT. Enclave borders were established by formal survey and not altered between 1816 and 1961. Territorial drift between the proxy polygon and the historical territory is therefore negligible (~0%).

**polygon_vintage_drift note:** Polygon vintage is 2024 (GADM 4.1), data years 1816–1961. The
underlying source record is [GADM 4.1 §known-limitations](../sources/gadm-4.1.md#known-limitations),
whose first limitation is exactly this one: GADM contains no historical entities, so it can say
where Goa's borders are now but not whether they moved — the stability claim below rests on the
annexation history, not on GADM. (The dataset version is 4.1, compiled 2022; `2024` here is the
access year.) Flag is raised by the system, but the drift is a documentation artifact — not a real extent question — because boundary stability is confirmed.

## Double claim with India, 1949-1961 (issue 84, measured 2026-08-13)

**3,719 km² of this polygon — 98.4% of Portuguese India — is also inside
[ind-1949-2025](ind-1949-2025.md)**, and for 1949 to December 1961 that is a real double claim:
both rows are live, and the ground was Portuguese, not Indian.

```
IND-1949-2025 ∩ PTIND-1816-1961    3,719 km²   98.4% of this row, 0.118% of India
```

The **six earlier British India periods no longer contain it**. Under issue 84 they were rebuilt as
their own base geometry minus this polygon and minus French India, so `IND-1800-1886` through
`IND-1947-1949` now hold 0-14 km² of Goa — the remainder being a write-time simplification rim, not
the recipe. Only `IND-1949-2025` still holds the enclave in full, because that row spans 1949-2025
and therefore straddles the annexation: subtracting for the whole span would trade this 1949-1961
double claim for a 1961-2025 hole in India.

**The split at 1961 was considered and declined**, with the reasoning and the measurements recorded
on [ind-1949-2025](ind-1949-2025.md) — chiefly that it would rename the modern-India polity code,
which is referenced outside this repository, for a 0.12% correction, and that Sikkim (6,988 km²,
wrong for 27 years) sits in the same polygon untouched. So the overlap above is **accepted and
documented rather than fixed**: any spatial use of India before 1962 double-counts these
3,719 km².

The subtrahend used by the British India builders is *this* polygon, not a re-derivation, so the
pair cannot drift apart.
