# China (to 1895)

## Summary

The current CSV tracks this row as China from **1800** to **1895** --
a **96-year row** covering the Qing Dynasty from the database floor
to the Treaty of Shimonoseki [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
COW code 710 [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

This row spans the late Qing Dynasty, including the First Opium War
(1839-1842), the Treaty of Nanjing (1842) ceding Hong Kong to
Britain, the Second Opium War (1856-1860), the Taiping Rebellion
(1850-1864), and the Sino-Japanese War (1894-1895). The end-of-row
event is the **Treaty of Shimonoseki (17 April 1895)**, in which
China ceded Taiwan and the Pescadores to Japan and recognized the
independence of Korea -- a real territorial event satisfying the
WHEP polity-definition rule.

The row also encompasses major territorial losses to Russia: the
Treaty of Aigun (1858) and Treaty of Peking (1860), by which China
ceded the Amur and Ussuri regions (~1 million km²). These are not
split points in the CSV despite being substantial territorial
changes. Under a strict reading of the WHEP rule, these are
candidate mid-row splits.

Predecessor per CSV: NA (pre-database floor). Successor per CSV:
[chn-1895-1912](chn-1895-1912.md).
