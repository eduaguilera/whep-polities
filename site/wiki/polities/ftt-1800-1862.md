# Imamate of Futa Toro

## Summary

The Imamate of Futa Toro was a Fula Islamic state along the middle Senegal River in northern Senegal, founded in 1776 through a Torodbe clerical revolution that overthrew the Denianke dynasty. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1862, when French conquest under Governor Faidherbe effectively ended its independence. The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). After its fall, the territory was incorporated into French West Africa and is eventually succeeded by modern Senegal.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from Paine et al. (2024). The imamate occupied the middle Senegal River valley in northern Senegal (the region known as Futa Toro). No CShapes data is available for this pre-colonial polity (no COW code assigned). The territory stretched along both banks of the middle Senegal River, roughly between Matam and Dagana.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** none recorded in WHEP; the Denianke dynasty predates the 1800 database floor; the Torodbe revolution of 1776 established the imamate.
- **Successor:** [sen-1960-2025](sen-1960-2025.md) — modern Senegal (via French colonial incorporation from ~1862)
