# Oise (département)

## Summary

Oise is a French département in the Hauts-de-France region, on the northern fringe of the Île-de-France basin, straddling the historic provinces of Île-de-France and Picardy. It is one of the roughly 83 original départements created by the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary administrative reform that replaced the old provinces with a uniform grid of units, and it is not one of the four recognized departures from that default history that this pipeline tracks for French subnational units (not carved from Alsace-Lorraine, not split off like the Territoire de Belfort, not part of the 1975 Corse split, and not a post-1946 overseas département), so it has kept its present administrative form continuously since creation. This entry is `type: subnational` rather than `national` because Oise is an ordinary administrative subdivision of the sovereign state of France, not a state in its own right and not a historical territory with an identity distinct from the modern département -- the same reasoning already applied to the sibling entries for Aisne, Calvados, Cher, Côte-d'Or and Doubs created in this same pipeline run. It exists as a row in this table only because a statistical source reports production/agricultural data for Oise specifically, at a finer grain than any national FRA row carries; before this entry, that data had no département-level home and would have had to be routed to whichever national FRA row spans the relevant year, a roughly ninety-fivefold territorial overstatement relative to Oise's actual extent, since metropolitan France has around 95-96 départements.

**Why this entry exists.** **Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision, a data source reports a production/agricultural series labelled specifically for Oise, beginning in 1862, entirely inside this entry's 1800-2025 span (the routing decision's own dataset row count was not independently re-verified in this authoring pass -- flagged in sourced_claims). Previously, in the absence of any Oise-level polity, this data had no natural home except one of the national France rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, or FRA-1919-2025 depending on year), which would have meant attributing département-scale series to the whole of metropolitan France -- roughly a ninety-fivefold territorial overstatement in département-count terms. What confirms Oise is a distinct, real administrative entity rather than a source-invented label: it is one of the original ~83 départements created by the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform, it has carried its own INSEE administrative code (60) and préfecture (Beauvais) ever since, and GADM 4.1's global admin-1 layer digitises it as a discrete first-order administrative feature of France, even though the copy of that dataset present in this repository (an 81-country subset) happens to exclude France.

**Territory description:** Oise is a département in the Hauts-de-France region, bordering the départements of Somme, Aisne, Seine-et-Marne, Val-d'Oise, Eure and Seine-Maritime, and lying just north of metropolitan Paris. Its administrative seat (préfecture) is Beauvais; other significant towns include Compiègne, Creil and Senlis. On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE département code 60, drained by the Oise river (which gives the département its name) and its tributaries the Aisne, Automne and Thérain, and it includes the Forêt de Compiègne and the Château de Chantilly. Its modern area is approximately **5,860 km²** (a figure quoted from published French administrative statistics for the present-day département, not measured from any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached).

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not Oise's 1790 creation date, per the container floor**

The routing decision proposed start_year 1790, citing the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform that created Oise along with the roughly 83 original départements. That administrative date is correct, but it is not usable here: the FRA container chain in the polity table begins at FRA-1800-1871 (cshapes-europe, first vintage 1816), and there is no France row covering 1790-1800 for this entry to container against. The containment gate rejects any edge that falls outside either party's span, so a 1790 start with no container for the first decade would fail immediately -- the container-floor rule enforced since commit 11e8ca1. Rather than leave a gap or invent a France row this pipeline was not asked to create, this entry starts at 1800, matching the earliest available container, and the 1790-1800 gap is left open below, exactly as done for the sibling entries FRA-02/14/18/21/25-1800-2025 (Aisne, Calvados, Cher, Côte-d'Or, Doubs). No data is lost by this choice since Oise's underlying series start well after 1800.

### d-subunit-code-insee-60

**Subunit code '60' follows INSEE numbering, matching the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern and sibling entries**

French départements are conventionally identified by their INSEE two-digit code rather than a postal or historical abbreviation. Oise's INSEE code is 60. This keeps the code in the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> family (57 of 66 subnational rows) and matches the convention already established for the sibling département entries created in this same pipeline run (02, 14, 18, 21, 25, and others). Oise has no name of its own distinct from the modern département and is not one of the four listed departures from the default département history (not Territoire de Belfort, not an Alsace-Lorraine département, not the Corsican split, not a post-1946 overseas département), so bespoke-code treatment (like ALK or HYD) would be unwarranted.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap-uncontained

**1790-1800 creation decade has no France container row to attach to**

Oise existed as a département from March 1790, a full decade before this entry's start_year of 1800. The FRA container chain in the polity table begins at FRA-1800-1871, so there is currently no polity row representing France 1790-1800 for Oise (or any other French département entered in this pipeline run) to container against. If a pre-1800 France row is ever added to the table, this entry's start_year should be reconsidered and moved back to 1790, consistent with the département's actual creation date and the routing decision's original span_basis reasoning.

### oq-no-polygon-source-registered

**No registered, locally-present source can supply an Oise boundary today**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning found that gadm-4.1-adm1, the only registered source with département-level granularity for France, is present locally only as an 81-country subset that excludes France entirely (0 rows for FRA, confirmed for feature FRA.60_1 in particular). cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe, the sources used for the national FRA rows, carry only whole-country geometry, not sub-national units. No union/difference of currently registered, locally-present features can construct Oise's boundary. A real boundary certainly exists (GADM 4.1's full global adm1 layer, or IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS/GEOFLA products for France specifically) but is not registered in scripts/sources.yaml today, so polygon_source is 'none' and polygon_status is 'unassigned' rather than a proxy. This should be revisited once gadm-4.1-adm1 is re-sourced as its full global layer, or an IGN product is registered.

### oq-wwi-proximity-not-addressed

**Oise's proximity to the 1914-1918 front is not reflected in the territorial/data record**

Oise sat close to, and in its northern arrondissements (Compiègne, Noyon) was directly affected by, the Western Front during the First World War (the 1918 Spring Offensive and Battle of the Matz reached into the département, and the Armistice was signed in a railway carriage in the Forêt de Compiègne). This entry's container basis treats 1871-1919 as continuous, unaffected metropolitan French territory, which is true in the strict sovereignty sense (France never ceded Oise), but any agricultural or production series reporting Oise data for 1914-1918 may reflect wartime disruption, occupation in the north, or non-reporting rather than ordinary peacetime figures. Whether the underlying series this entry receives actually cover those years, and whether a data-quality flag is warranted, has not been checked in this authoring pass.
