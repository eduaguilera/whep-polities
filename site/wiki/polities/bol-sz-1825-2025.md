# Santa Cruz (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

Santa Cruz is a department (admin-1 unit) of Bolivia in the eastern lowlands, one of the six original departments organized at the republic's founding in 1825/1826 alongside Chuquisaca, La Paz, Cochabamba, Potosi, and Oruro. Its capital, Santa Cruz de la Sierra, sits in a tropical lowland plain that historically lagged the Andean highlands in population and connectivity but became, over the 20th century, Bolivia's most economically dynamic agricultural and commercial region (soy, cattle, sugar, and later hydrocarbons). This entry is `type: subnational` because Santa Cruz has never been sovereign — Bolivia held sovereignty throughout the entire 1825-2025 span — and the row exists purely to carry department-level statistical reporting territory, the same convention already established for BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and BOL-POT-1825-2025 and applied uniformly across this batch of Bolivian departments. Santa Cruz is not among the departments the country convention documents as later separations (Tarija, Beni in 1842, Pando in 1938), so the default 1825/1826 founding-era start applies rather than a later creation date, even though the specific data extract motivating this routing decision was first observed starting only in 1900.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Bolivia's Santa Cruz department as a first-level administrative reporting unit. The routing decision (unit_id BOL-SANTACRUZ) states that no existing BOL candidate represented Santa Cruz specifically: only national-level BOL rows existed prior to this batch, spanning the whole country, and folding department-level statistics into a national row would misattribute territory that a source reports at sub-country granularity. The underlying data (source and exact row count not given in the routing decision itself, an acknowledged gap — see open questions) is first observed starting in 1900, which was previously implicitly absorbed into whichever national BOL row was active in that year, obscuring that it describes only one department rather than the whole country. What confirms Santa Cruz is a distinct, long-standing administrative entity rather than a matcher artefact is that it has been one of Bolivia's six constitutive departments since the republic's founding in 1825/1826, administered separately from La Paz, Potosi, and the other departments ever since, and is not listed among the documented later separations (Tarija, Beni, Pando) that would imply a different start date. This is the same class of continuous-administrative-distinctness evidence that justified the Beni and Potosi entries created in the same batch, applied here to fill in Santa Cruz as one of the remaining original departments still missing its own polity row.

## Territorial extent

The routing decision's polygon_route is `new_source_needed`: GADM 4.1 adm1 is the correct eventual registered source for department-level Bolivian boundaries (the same target already used for BOL-BEN and BOL-POT), but the GeoPackage cached locally at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg is an 81-country subset that excludes Bolivia entirely, so there is no feature to attach today even though the source category is known. **Territory description:** Santa Cruz department occupies Bolivia's eastern lowlands, a vast plain transitioning from tropical dry forest and savanna in the west toward Amazonian and Chaco ecosystems in the north and south, bordering Brazil and Paraguay. Its capital and largest city is Santa Cruz de la Sierra. On a modern map, present-day Santa Cruz department covers approximately 370,600 km2, Bolivia's largest department by area, roughly a third of the country's total territory. No km2 figure is derived from any attached geometry here since none is assigned; the 370,600 km2 figure is the modern department's commonly cited administrative area, offered only to help a reader locate the territory on a current map, not as a measurement of this entry's (currently absent) polygon. Whether this present-day extent is a valid proxy for the department's 1825-1900 boundary is unresolved and flagged as an open question, since Santa Cruz's lowland frontier was subject to gradual settlement and administrative definition over the 19th and 20th centuries in a way the more settled Andean departments (Potosi, Chuquisaca) were not.

## Predecessors and successors

Santa Cruz has no predecessor row: Bolivia's only prior subnational entries are BOL-BEN, BOL-POT, and the other sibling department pages created in this same batch, and Santa Cruz's 1825 origin coincides with Bolivia's own founding rather than continuing from an earlier differently-coded unit. It has no successor: Santa Cruz remains a department of Bolivia today, so end_year: 2025 marks the open/current bound rather than a dissolution, split, or merger. Unlike Beni (which lost territory to the 1938 creation of Pando), no internal territorial event is recorded for Santa Cruz in the routing decision or in the sources consulted here — its department boundary is treated as continuous across all four Bolivian national eras, though this continuity has not been independently verified against 19th- or 20th-century Bolivian administrative sources, and Santa Cruz's own frontier expansion into its Chaco and Amazonian lowlands over the 20th century is a plausible but unconfirmed source of boundary change (see open questions).

## Decisions

### d-code-sz-not-santacruz

**Used BOL-SZ-1825-2025 rather than BOL-SANTACRUZ or a single-letter ISO 3166-2 code**

Bolivia's ISO 3166-2 code for Santa Cruz is BO-S (a single letter), but the dominant subnational pattern already established for this country (BOL-BEN, BOL-POT, and the sibling Chuquisaca/La Paz/Oruro/Pando pages created in the same batch) is a two-to-three-letter mnemonic abbreviation, not the raw ISO 3166-2 letter and not the routing decision's own verbose unit_id string 'BOL-SANTACRUZ'. 'SZ' was chosen over 'SC' because SC risks visual confusion with Bolivia's own department abbreviations used elsewhere in casual references (and with unrelated SC-prefixed codes in other countries' rows in this table), whereas SZ is unambiguous and still legible as Santa Cruz; this is a judgment call rather than a source-derived abbreviation, so it is recorded here in case a future page needs to reconcile it against a differently-abbreviated reference.

### d-start-1825-default-rule

**Applied the country's default 1825 founding start rather than the data's own 1900 start**

The routing decision's routing_reasoning explicitly instructs applying the country convention's default start rule rather than the year (1900) implied by the underlying data extract, because Santa Cruz is one of Bolivia's original constituent departments established at independence in 1825/1826 and is not among the three departments the convention documents as later separations (Tarija, Beni in 1842, Pando in 1938). This mirrors the same choice already made for Chuquisaca, La Paz, Oruro, and Potosi in the same batch: department-level administrative continuity from founding is treated as the governing fact for the span, and the data's own first-observed year (1900) is not read as evidence of a later territorial creation, only as evidence of when statistical reporting for the unit happens to begin. The gap between administrative founding (1825) and first observed data (1900) is noted as an open question below rather than resolved here.

### d-polygon-source-none-not-route-name

**Set polygon_source to none rather than the polygon_route category or a fabricated slug**

The routing decision's polygon_route is 'new_source_needed' and its polygon_source/polygon_feature_id fields were left blank, with the actual candidate (a full-country GADM 4.1 download for Bolivia, since the locally cached gadm-4.1-adm1 GeoPackage is an 81-country subset excluding Bolivia entirely) described only in polygon_detail as a candidate_new_source, not a registered, fetched source. Per the harness's binding rule that new_source_needed and registered_source_unfetched are route categories, not values validate_declared_sources will accept, polygon_source is written as the literal 'none' with polygon_status 'unassigned', and the GADM full-country-download candidate is preserved as an open question instead of being smuggled into the source field.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-bolivia-not-cached-santacruz

**GADM 4.1 adm1 for Bolivia (including Santa Cruz) is not present in the locally cached GeoPackage**

The routing decision confirms that data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg is a subset covering only 81 countries and does not include Bolivia, so no feature id can be attached for Santa Cruz today; this is the same gap already logged against BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and BOL-POT-1825-2025 and is not specific to this entry. The routing decision's polygon_detail names a full-country GADM 4.1 download for Bolivia as the candidate remedy (licence_known: true, free for non-commercial/academic use per gadm.org, redistribution restricted), which distinguishes this case slightly from Beni/Potosi's unverified-licence framing. Once that country package is fetched, the correct NAME_1='Santa Cruz' feature (expected GID_1 pattern BOL.8_1 or similar by analogy with Beni's BOL.2_1, but unconfirmed) needs to be looked up directly rather than guessed, and polygon_status can then move from unassigned toward assigned or proxy depending on vintage.

### oq-santacruz-vintage-risk-modern-boundary

**A present-day GADM Santa Cruz polygon would be a two-century-old proxy for the 1825 department**

The routing decision's polygon_detail flags vintage_risk explicitly: GADM reflects current (post-2010s) administrative boundaries, while Santa Cruz's borders as a department founded in 1825 have shifted over two centuries, including the later creation of neighboring departments and provinces carved from adjacent territory (and, more consequentially, Santa Cruz's own eastward and northward expansion during 20th-century colonization of its Chaco and Amazonian lowlands, which is not confirmed here against a specific source). Whether the present-day department boundary meaningfully over- or under-states the 1825-1900 extent, and by how much, has not been checked against any period map; this should be resolved before treating a future GADM fetch as anything better than a `proxy` for the pre-20th-century span, and possibly warrants splitting this row if a documented boundary change is found.

### oq-santacruz-data-provenance-not-reviewed

The routing decision's proposed object shows only a container/span proposal and does not state which source (e.g. juan-subnational, mitchell, or another already-registered source), how many rows, or what specific years within 1900-2025 actually report 'Santa Cruz' as a distinct reporting unit distinct from all-Bolivia national totals. Unlike the Beni entry, which cites Bolivia's own department history as continuous administrative evidence, this entry relies on the same class of general evidence (continuous departmental existence since 1825) without a specific dataset in hand; the underlying data-matching output should be checked to confirm the 1900 start seen in the data is a reporting artefact and not evidence of a real administrative gap between 1825 founding and 1900 first observation.
