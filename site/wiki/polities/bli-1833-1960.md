## Why this polity exists

FAO 1952 reports rows for 1937–1951 under the label "British West Indies Leeward Islands" (also appearing as "Br West Indies Leeward Islands"). These rows measure agricultural production (notably cotton lint, cottonseed, and related crops) for the British Leeward Islands Colony as a distinct administrative reporting unit — not for the broader British West Indies aggregate (BWI-1833-1962, ~34,766 km²). Routing these rows to BWI-1833-1962 would overstate territory approximately 20× and violate the sub-territory rule. No WHEP polity previously existed for this sub-aggregate.

The British Leeward Islands Colony (1833–1960) was a formal Crown Colony grouping administered under a single Governor-General based in Antigua, comprising: Antigua (with Barbuda and Redonda), St. Kitts-Nevis-Anguilla, Montserrat, British Virgin Islands, and Dominica. It was constituted as a federation in 1871 (the Leeward Islands Federation) and dissolved in 1956–1960 as constituent territories became separate Crown Colonies or joined the West Indies Federation.

The colony comprised five island groups with a combined land area of approximately 1,716 km²:

| **Total** | | **~1,716** | |

**Dominica transfer note:** Dominica was part of the Leeward Islands Colony from 1833 until 1940, when it was transferred to the Windward Islands Colony. From 1940 onward, the Leeward Islands Colony comprised ATG + KNA + MSR + VGB only (~965 km²). FAO 1952 data covering 1937–1951 therefore spans both configurations. The single BLI polity row uses the pre-1940 full extent (~1,716 km²) as the polygon basis; the 1940–1951 portion of matched FAO data carries a polygon_vintage_drift of approximately −44% (Dominica's ~751 km² removed). This approximation is acceptable given the absence of a more granular source; it is documented here so users are aware.

Start year 1833: consistent with BWI-1833-1962 (post-slavery-abolition colonial administrative period). End year 1960: dissolution of the Leeward Islands federation; the last constituent territory (British Virgin Islands) became a separate Crown Colony by 1960.

## Polygon decision

Polygon status is **unassigned**. No single historical GIS source (CShapes 2.0, Cliopatria, GADM) covers the British Leeward Islands Colony as an administrative aggregate. The correct method is a composed union of GADM 4.1 adm0 polygons for ATG, KNA, MSR, DMA, VGB (~1,716 km² combined).

- CShapes 2.0 does not cover non-sovereign British colonial groupings at this level.
- Cliopatria/Seshat has no Leeward Islands Colony entry.
- GADM 4.1 adm0 polygons exist for all five constituents and provide the best available proxy.

All constituents are islands with stable physical extents; the composed union is geographically invariant across 1833–1960 (modulo the Dominica transfer in 1940, documented above). The polygon must be built and added to `data/geodata/constructed/constructed.geojson` before `polygon_status` can be set to `assigned`.

**Polygon confidence:** medium — the composed union faithfully represents the territory, but the Dominica transfer creates a ~44% area discontinuity at 1940 that is not captured in the single polygon.

## Relationship to BWI-1833-1962

BWI-1833-1962 covers the full British West Indies colonial aggregate (~34,766 km², including Jamaica, Trinidad & Tobago, Barbados, Bahamas, Windward Islands, and Leeward Islands). BLI-1833-1960 is a distinct sub-aggregate covering only the Leeward Islands grouping (~1,716 km², ~5% of BWI). Data labeled "British West Indies Leeward Islands" must route to BLI-1833-1960, not BWI-1833-1962, to avoid a ~20× territorial overstatement.

## Open questions

### oq-dominica-split

For strict accuracy, a split at 1940 could be modeled as two polity rows: BLI-1833-1940 (ATG+KNA+MSR+DMA+VGB, ~1,716 km²) and BLI-1940-1960 (ATG+KNA+MSR+VGB, ~965 km²). This is the correct historical representation. However, the FAO 1952 data period (1937–1951) spans the transfer, making a split more complex for data routing. A single polity with documented drift is acceptable for now; revisit if more granular production data for this period is added.

### oq-anguilla-kna-disambiguation

Anguilla was administered as part of St. Kitts-Nevis-Anguilla throughout this period but was not formally separated until 1967. KNA-1800-2025 correctly covers all three islands; no separate Anguilla polity is needed for the 1833–1960 period.
