# Nord (département)

## Summary

Nord is a French département in the far north of the country, on the Belgian border, home to Lille, Dunkirk, Roubaix, and Tourcoing — one of the most industrialized and densely populated départements in France, historically built on coal mining, textiles, and metallurgy. It is one of the roughly 83 original départements created by the March 1790 Revolutionary reform that replaced the old provinces (here, principally French Flanders, Hainaut, and Cambrésis) with a uniform grid of administrative units, and it is not among the recognized departures from that default founding history: it was not carved out of Alsace-Lorraine in 1871, not split off from a neighboring département like the Territoire de Belfort, not part of the 1975 division of Corse, and not a post-1946 overseas département. This entry is `type: subnational` rather than `national` because Nord is an ordinary administrative subdivision of the French state, not a sovereign polity or a historical territory with a separate identity of its own (contrast the bespoke-coded regional entries like Manchuria or Hyderabad) — it exists in this table purely because statistical sources report data at département granularity, finer than the national FRA rows can carry without misattributing département-level series to the whole of metropolitan France.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision for unit FRA-FRE11 (admin unit "Nord", country France), sourced series labelled specifically for Nord span 1862-2023; the decision does not give an exact row count, and no underlying data file was opened directly during this authoring pass to confirm one (flagged as an open question). Previously matched to: nothing — before this entry, Nord-level data had no polity row of its own and would have had to be routed to whichever national FRA row spans the relevant year (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, or FRA-1919-2025), which would misattribute a single département's figures to the whole of metropolitan France, a territory roughly ninety times larger (France has on the order of 96 metropolitan départements). What confirms this entity was distinct: Nord is one of the original ~83 départements created by the March 1790 Revolutionary administrative reform (formed from the pre-Revolutionary provinces of French Flanders, Hainaut, and Cambrésis), carries its own INSEE code (59) and préfecture (Lille), is digitised as a discrete administrative unit by GADM globally, and is not one of the country convention's listed departures from the default 1790 founding pattern (not Alsace-Lorraine-derived, not the Belfort split, not the Corse division, not an overseas département) — it is an ordinary, continuously-existing subnational unit that a statistical source chose to report on individually, which under the policy in issue 400 (a reporting unit qualifies for a row when statistics were collected on it) is sufficient grounds for this entry.

**Territory description.** Nord occupies the northeasternmost corner of metropolitan France, bordering Belgium, with the North Sea coast around Dunkirk to its northwest; it corresponds to the historical core of French Flanders, Hainaut, and Cambrésis. On a modern map it is the département containing Lille (the regional capital), Dunkirk, Roubaix, Tourcoing, Valenciennes, and Cambrai — the northern tip of the Hauts-de-France region. Its area is approximately 5,742 km2, making it, despite that modest size, one of France's most populous départements owing to its industrial and urban density. No polygon has been attached to measure this figure against; the 5,742 km2 value is drawn from general geographic reference, not from any geometry in this repository.

## Predecessors and successors

Nord has no predecessor or successor polity row in this table: it is not a re-span of an existing entry and nothing replaces it going forward. Administratively it was created in March 1790 as one of the original ~83 Revolutionary départements, formed largely from the pre-1790 provinces of French Flanders, Hainaut, and Cambrésis; it has kept its present boundaries (with only the town-level adjustments typical of any long-lived European administrative unit) ever since, and remains a current département of the Hauts-de-France region today. ## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the proposed 1790**

The routing decision's span_basis correctly identifies Nord as one of the ~83 original 1790 départements with no listed departure, which under the country convention gives a default start_year of 1790. But the FRA container chain in the polity table only reaches back to FRA-1800-1871 — there is no FRA row covering 1790-1800, and the containment gate rejects a container edge whose interval falls outside the container's own span. Extending this entry back to 1790 would leave a ten-year gap with no valid container. Every sibling département page created so far (Calvados/FRA-14, and FRA-18, FRA-21, FRA-25, FRA-27, FRA-28, FRA-36, FRA-37, FRA-39, FRA-41, FRA-50, FRA-58, FRA-61, FRA-70, FRA-71, all created in this same batch) uses start_year 1800 for the identical reason, so this entry follows that established precedent rather than the country-convention default, which is a fact about this repository's current FRA chain rather than about Nord's own administrative history. The data being routed here (1862-2023) is unaffected either way, since it falls entirely within 1800-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-fra-1790-1800-gap

**No FRA container exists for 1790-1800, so Nord's true founding decade is uncovered**

Nord was created in March 1790 along with the other original départements, but this repository's FRA chain starts at FRA-1800-1871 with nothing before it. If a pre-1800 FRA polity is ever added (e.g. FRA-1790-1800 for the Revolutionary/Directory-era state), this entry's start_year should probably move back to 1790 to match the country convention's default and its own administrative founding, rather than staying pinned to 1800 for want of a container. Until then this entry understates Nord's span by a decade at the front end, purely as an artifact of container availability, not of Nord's own history.

### oq-nord-polygon-source

**No département-level polygon source is locally available for Nord**

GADM 4.1 admin-1 is registered as a source in this repository but the locally-present copy is an 81-country subset that excludes France, so no polygon can be assigned yet (polygon_source: none, polygon_status: unassigned). Whether the fix is downloading the full global GADM 4.1 ADM1 layer, or switching to IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS départements dataset (which would have better vintage fidelity for French boundaries specifically), has not been decided. Nord's borders have been essentially stable since 1790 modulo the 1871-1919 loss/return of nothing (Nord was never part of Alsace-Lorraine), so vintage risk for either candidate source should be low once one is fetched.
