# Wyoming (state of the United States)

## Summary

This entry captures the U.S. state of Wyoming from its admission to the Union in 1890 through the present. type is subnational because Wyoming has never held sovereignty of its own: it is, and always has been since 1868, a constituent unit first of a federally administered territory and then of a state within the United States, and this row exists specifically to carry statistics reported at the state level rather than to represent an independent country. The routing decision that created this entry found no existing polity representing Wyoming specifically -- the only USA-prefixed rows in the table (USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025) are national totals for the whole country -- while the underlying data (GADM 4.1 admin-1 polygon USA.51_1, matched 1:1 by name) is unambiguous. Wyoming's start_year follows this country's default convention of using the year of formal admission to statehood (1890) rather than the data extract's earlier start (1882, when Wyoming existed only as an organized territory); this default was chosen because Wyoming is one of the 37 "ordinary" post-original states with no territorial peculiarity (such as Alaska's or Hawaii's separation by ocean, or Texas's brief independent-republic period) that would justify a departure. Wyoming remains a state today, so end_year is set to 2025, the database's open horizon.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: state-level statistics for "Wyoming" within the United States, drawn from the historical data extract feeding this pipeline's routing pass (the same extract that produced the 1882-1889 pre-statehood rows discussed in the open question below); the routing decision names the unit "USA-WYOMING" with country "United States of America." Row counts and exact year coverage of the source series were not restated in the routing decision itself, only that the span runs from the extract's 1882 start through the present, of which 1882-1889 predates statehood and is back-cast onto this entry rather than modeled separately. Previously, this data had no home at all in the polity table: the only USA-prefixed candidates (USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025) are whole-country national totals, and matching Wyoming-specific statistics to them would silently merge state-level figures into the national aggregate, which is wrong because Wyoming is a distinct sub-national reporting unit with its own economy, population, and administrative government (its own governor, legislature, and, before 1890, its own territorial administration) that the national total does not distinguish. What confirms Wyoming is a distinct, nameable, boundaried entity rather than an arbitrary aggregation: it is one of the fifty constitutionally defined states of the United States, admitted by a specific act of Congress on a specific date (1890-07-10), with a boundary fixed in federal law and represented unambiguously in the modern GADM administrative hierarchy as admin-1 unit USA.51_1 -- the same class of evidence already relied on for the sibling entry ALK-1867-1959.

## Territorial extent

Polygon: assigned from `gadm-4.1-adm1`, feature `USA.51_1`, an exact 1:1 name match for the present-day U.S. state of Wyoming. This is the same source and vintage already used for the sibling state-level entry ALK-1867-1959 (Territory of Alaska), so it is the natural consistent choice for a second US subnational row rather than introducing a new source. Area, measured from the attached geometry, is approximately 253,600 km2 -- this is a measurement of the polygon itself, not an independently sourced historical figure, though it is consistent with the U.S. Census Bureau's published land-and-water area for the state (253,335 km2). Vintage risk is treated as immaterial: Wyoming's rectangular exterior boundary (defined by lines of latitude and longitude, running roughly 41°N-45°N and 104°03'W-111°03'W) has been fixed since admission in 1890 and has not been altered by any subsequent territorial cession, unlike several neighboring states. On a modern map, the territory is the rectangular state occupying the northern Rocky Mountains and high plains of the north-central United States, bordered by Montana to the north, South Dakota and Nebraska to the east, Colorado to the south, and Utah and Idaho to the west; it contains Yellowstone National Park in its northwest corner. No proxy substitution or boundary-change adjustment was needed, since the state's exterior line predates and postdates the entire 1890-2025 span this entry covers.

## Predecessors and successors

Wyoming has no predecessor or successor polity in this table: it is a single continuous state from admission to the Union on 1890-07-10 through the present (end_year 2025 by the database's open-ended convention for still-existing polities). The pre-statehood Wyoming Territory (organized 1868, following earlier inclusion in the Dakota, Idaho, and other western territories) is not modeled as a separate predecessor row here -- see open question oq-pre-statehood-territory-not-separately-modeled -- so data recorded against Wyoming from 1882 (the extract's start) through 1889 is back-cast onto this same polity code rather than routed to a distinct territorial entry, per the routing decision's explicit instruction and the country's default admission-year convention.

## Sourced claims

- Wyoming was admitted as the 44th state of the United States on July 10, 1890, per the U.S. Congressional admission act -- this is the basis for the start_year, not the 1882 start of the underlying data extract.
- GADM 4.1's admin-1 feature USA.51_1 corresponds to the modern state of Wyoming, with an area of approximately 253,600 km2 (97,914 sq mi), consistent with the U.S. Census Bureau's published state land-and-water area figure of 253,335 km2.
- Wyoming's exterior boundary is a set of straight lines defined by latitude/longitude in its 1868 organic act as a territory and has not been altered by any subsequent cession or annexation, unlike neighboring Idaho, Montana, and the Dakotas, which underwent territorial boundary changes in the 1860s-1880s before their own admissions.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> majority pattern, not a bespoke name**

Wyoming is one of 37 states admitted after the original thirteen, with a boundary that has never changed since admission and no separate name or administrative identity distinct from 'the state of Wyoming' itself (unlike Alaska, which spent 1867-1959 as an organized/unorganized territory under a different governing structure before statehood, justifying ALK-1867-1959 as bespoke). There is no comparable pre-statehood administrative entity here that needs its own row: the routing decision explicitly back-casts the 1882-1889 Wyoming Territory data onto this same polity rather than spinning up a separate territorial polity, so a single code covering 1890-2025 is sufficient and the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern (57 of 66 subnational rows) applies. Code chosen: USA-WY-1890-2025, using the standard two-letter USPS abbreviation as the subunit, consistent with how other US states would be expected to be coded if added.

### d-container-splits-at-1959

**Container is two edges, not one, because the national row itself splits at 1959**

The polities table shows the USA national chain broken into USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025, with the break driven by Alaska and Hawaii statehood changing the country's total territory in 1959 -- not by anything internal to Wyoming. Since Wyoming's span (1890-2025) crosses that break, a single container edge citing only one of the two national rows would leave either 1890-1959 or 1959-2025 uncontained and would also fail validation because the edge would fall outside the cited national row's own span. Two edges, one per national era, tile the full 1890-2025 range with no gap, matching the pattern already used for ALK-1867-1959 (which cites only USA-1959-2025 because its own span starts after the split).

## Open questions

### oq-pre-statehood-territory-not-separately-modeled

**The 1882-1889 Wyoming Territory data is back-cast onto the state polity, not modeled as its own entity**

The routing decision that produced this entry explicitly notes that rows dated 1882-1889 (the Wyoming Territory period, before the 1890-07-10 admission to the Union) are assigned to USA-WY-1890-2025 even though the polity as a state did not yet exist in those years. Wyoming Territory (1868-1890) had its own governor, legislature, and boundary history (it briefly included land later ceded to Idaho and Montana during the broader Dakota/Idaho/Montana territorial reorganizations of the 1860s-1880s) that is not captured by this entry's 1890 start_year or by its present-day GADM boundary. If a future pass finds enough Wyoming-Territory-specific data or administrative-history sources to justify it, a bespoke pre-statehood polity analogous to ALK-1867-1959 could be split out, with this entry's start_year and the back-cast policy revisited then.

### oq-boundary-vintage-of-southwest-corner

**GADM 4.1's Wyoming boundary is a present-day (2020s) survey, not an 1890 one**

polygon_status is 'assigned' rather than 'proxy' because Wyoming's rectangular exterior state line has been fixed by federal statute since admission and no territorial change has occurred since 1890 (unlike, e.g., Oklahoma or the Dakotas, which absorbed or shed territory after admission). That said, the GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature reflects modern surveying and boundary-dispute resolutions (e.g., minor Wyoming-Montana and Wyoming-Utah boundary line clarifications resolved by Supreme Court survey rulings in the early 20th century) rather than the exact 1890 line as originally drawn. This is treated as immaterial here because the differences are on the order of survey-error corrections, not territorial change, but it has not been independently verified against a historical GLO (General Land Office) plat.
