# Sevilla (province of Spain)

## Summary

Sevilla is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, carved out of the larger historic Reino de Sevilla whose remaining territory was split into what became Huelva and part of Cadiz province. This entry is type: subnational because Sevilla is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Sevilla separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES618, for over a century and a half. Unlike Asturias, Cantabria or Madrid, Sevilla's 1833 province boundary does not coincide with a single-province autonomous community: since 1981 it has been one of eight provinces making up the autonomous community of Andalusia, so its statistical identity rests entirely on the province/NUTS3 tier rather than any community-level coincidence. The routing decision that produced this page (unit_id ESP-ES618) follows the same default country convention already applied to sibling Spanish NUTS3 units: no departure rule (such as the Canary Islands' different founding date) applies to Sevilla, so it takes the standard 1833 start and an open 2025 end for a still-current unit.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES618) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES618 ("Sevilla"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES112 through ES241), following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, since Sevilla is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing distinct -- routing_reasoning states no existing polity matches Sevilla by name, and containment by ESP-1800-2025 is not identity, so the only prior fallback would have been the national aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for the other Spanish provinces already in this database. What confirms distinctness: Sevilla's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES618) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 36x the area of Sevilla province (14,036 km2).

Its capital and largest city is the city of Seville, Spain's fourth-largest city and historically its principal Atlantic-trade port via the Guadalquivir (seat of the Casa de Contratacion during the Spanish colonial era). ## Predecessors and successors

Sevilla has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was part of the larger historic Reino de Sevilla, an Antiguo Regimen kingdom whose extent exceeded the 1833 province (parts became Huelva and Cadiz provinces), so this database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct predecessor row rather than misrepresenting a boundary that does not match. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Madrid).

## Decisions

### d-code-format-as-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation SE rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025 (Asturias), ESP-CA-1833-2025 (Cantabria), ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES618) or the INE province numeric code (41). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-SE-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so that the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. SE was chosen because it is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-SE) and the everyday abbreviation for the province, reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row; it is distinct from the vehicle-plate-derived abbreviation SV sometimes seen in older Spanish administrative tables, which was rejected to avoid confusion with San Salvador's ISO3 SLV or with any future El Salvador department code.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Kingdom of Seville (Reino de Sevilla) has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the broader territory including modern Sevilla, Huelva and Cadiz provinces was organized as the historic Reino de Sevilla, an Antiguo Regimen jurisdiction dating to the Reconquista partition of Al-Andalus, with a different administrative form and a larger extent than the 1833 province (Huelva and most of Cadiz were carved out as separate provinces in 1833). This database does not model that earlier kingdom as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent or over-broad row, mirroring the treatment recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025's pre-1833 Principado de Asturias. Whether the historic Reino de Sevilla should eventually be modeled as its own bespoke-code polity (given it has a genuine historical name and identity spanning multiple modern provinces) is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.

### oq-nuts-boundary-drift

**Whether the modern NUTS3/INE cpro=41 boundary exactly matches the 1833 provincial boundary for the full span**

The routing decision treats ES618 (NUTS3 Sevilla) as equivalent to INE province code 41 and assumes the 1833 provincial boundary has been stable through 2025, following the country-wide convention already applied to Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna and Madrid. Unlike Asturias or Madrid, however, Sevilla's boundary interacts with a province (Huelva, split off from the old Reino de Sevilla around the same 1833 reform) rather than with an autonomous-community coincidence, so there is more room for a minor 19th-century adjustment between Sevilla and its neighbours (Huelva, Cadiz, Malaga, Cordoba, Badajoz) to have gone unrecorded in this pipeline. This should be checked once the mapspain-ign feature is actually fetched, comparing it against any documented 19th-century partido-level boundary changes.
