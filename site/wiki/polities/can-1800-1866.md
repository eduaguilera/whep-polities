# Canada (pre-Confederation)

## Summary

Canada (pre-Confederation) covers British North America from the start of the WHEP time window (1800) until 1866, just before Confederation (1 July 1867) united the Province of Canada, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick into the Dominion of Canada. During this period the territory was administered as several separate British colonies, including Upper Canada, Lower Canada, and the Hudson's Bay Company territories. The database uses 1866 as the end year because the CShapes polygon changes at Confederation.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `cshapes-2.0` gwcode 20, year 1886 — used as a **proxy**
(`polygon_status: proxy`) because CShapes 2.0 has no pre-1886 Canada
feature. The modern-Canada outline (~9.55M km²) over-represents
pre-Confederation Canada significantly: in 1861 the populated/administered
territories (Province of Canada — Ontario + Quebec — plus New Brunswick,
Nova Scotia, Newfoundland, and PEI) totalled roughly 1.6M km². Rupert's
Land (the vast Hudson's Bay Company territory covering most of modern
Prairie Canada and the Arctic watershed) wasn't transferred to the Dominion
until 1870. Agricultural production in the pre-1961 input data for
"Canada" 1850-1866 refers almost entirely to the Province of Canada +
Maritimes. Using the post-1886 CShapes polygon is a deliberate trade-off
for cross-period visual consistency across the CAN chain; period-accurate
boundaries would need a dedicated British-North-America source (Newberry
Atlas, or manual construction from GADM provinces).

The Canada--United States boundary is "the longest land boundary between
any two countries in the world" at 3,987 miles (6,416 km) in the south,
plus the Alaska boundary of 1,538 miles (2,477 km).

## Open questions

### oq-multiple-colonies

Pre-Confederation British North America consisted of several distinct colonies (Upper/Lower Canada, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, etc.) with their own governance structures. Whether the WHEP database should track these as a single entity or multiple rows is an open question.
