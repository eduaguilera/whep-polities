# Mossi Kingdoms

## Summary

The Mossi Kingdoms (or Mossi States) were a cluster of related kingdoms on the central plateau of modern Burkina Faso, founded c. 15th century. The three principal states were Ouagadougou (ruled by the Mogho Naba), Yatenga, and Tenkodogo. Despite centuries of pressure from the Mali Empire, Songhai, and the Sokoto Caliphate, the Mossi kingdoms maintained independence through to the late 19th century. The database tracks them as a single WHEP row from 1800 until 1897, when France conquered the kingdoms (Ouagadougou fell in 1896, Yatenga in 1897). Biger provides context for Burkina Faso (formerly Upper Volta): "In 1919 a decree was passed establishing the colony of Upper Volta by detaching a number of regions from Haut Senegal and Niger." The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). The Mossi plateau lies roughly between 11°N and 14°N latitude, covering the central portion of modern Burkina Faso. The composite polygon encompasses the territories of Ouagadougou, Yatenga, Tenkodogo, and Fada N'Gourma.

## Open questions

### oq-aggregation-appropriateness

The Mossi kingdoms were historically distinct sovereign entities. The Mogho Naba of Ouagadougou did not rule Yatenga or Tenkodogo. Treating them as one WHEP row raises the question of whether they had a shared external boundary that makes sense as a single polygon, or whether the aggregation obscures important territorial distinctions.

### oq-conquest-sequence

France conquered Ouagadougou in September 1896 and Yatenga in 1897. The WHEP end year 1897 covers the final conquest, but the two kingdoms ended separately. Whether this warrants separate rows is an open question.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger covers Burkina Faso only at the colonial-era (Upper Volta) level. A specialist source (e.g., Izard 1970 *Introduction à l'histoire des royaumes Mossi*; Skinner 1964 *The Mossi of the Upper Volta*) is needed.
