# Cantabria (province of Spain)

## Summary

Cantabria is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province -- the same reform that produced the two existing sibling entries ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid). Until a 1984 renaming law it was officially named Santander, after its capital city; since 1981 it has also constituted its own single-province autonomous community, Comunidad Autónoma de Cantabria, one of only a handful of Spain's autonomous communities that coincide exactly with a single province. This entry is type: subnational because Cantabria is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated the province separately, at NUTS3 code ES130, since long before NUTS existed under its administrative predecessor. The routing decision for unit ES130 found no existing polity row for this territory and proposed creating ESP-CA-1833-2025 following the country's default province-start convention (1833), noting that Cantabria is not one of the enumerated departures from that default (unlike the Canary Islands split) and that the data's own start (1865) should not override the administrative start.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES130 ("Cantabria"), country Spain, data starting 1865, under the same country convention already applied to sibling Spanish provincial units, following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, since Cantabria is not among the enumerated start-year departures like the Canary Islands) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing decision found no existing polity row covering this exact territory -- unlike ESP-CO and ESP-MD, whose database rows predated their wiki pages, Cantabria (ES130) had neither a row nor a page, so absent this entry the data for ES130 would have had no province-level destination and would have needed to fall back to the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, roughly 95x larger (505,000 km2 vs Cantabria's ~5,321 km2) and incapable of representing province-level series without collapsing them into the national total. What confirms distinctness: Cantabria's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES130) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity, and its further distinction since 1981 as its own single-province Comunidad Autónoma, is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entries ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.

Territory description: Cantabria occupies a narrow strip of Spain's northern Atlantic (Cantabrian Sea) coast, between Asturias to the west, Castile and León to the south, and the Basque Country (Biscay) to the east. Its capital and largest city is Santander, a major port; the province also contains the Picos de Europa mountain range along its southern border and the historic Liébana valley. On a modern map it is bounded by the Bay of Biscay to the north and the Cantabrian Mountains inland. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 5,321 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low by the same reasoning already applied to ESP-CO and ESP-MD, though this has not been individually verified for Cantabria's specific boundary (see open question).

## Predecessors and successors

Cantabria has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory now covered by the province was organized as part of the historic region/Kingdom administrative divisions of Old Castile and the Cantabrian Sea coast, which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain, and since 1981 constitutes its own single-province autonomous community (Comunidad Autónoma de Cantabria), as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish provincial units of treating 2025 as the open end for still-current entries. Note the province's own name changed from Santander to Cantabria in 1984 (see open question below); this is a renaming of the same continuous administrative unit, not a predecessor/successor split, and no separate row is created for the pre-1984 "Santander" era.

Cantabria is a standard modern Spanish province with no name of its own distinct from its administrative status (unlike ALK/HYD/RYU, which are historical territories with proper names predating or independent of their current administrative framing). Start and end years in the code (1833, 2025) equal the frontmatter start_year/end_year exactly, satisfying the code/frontmatter equality gate.

## Open questions

### oq-cantabria-santander-name

**Province was named Santander until 1984, not Cantabria**

From its creation in 1833 until a 1984 law (Ley 3/1984), this province was officially named Santander (after its capital), not Cantabria. Statistical and administrative sources for the bulk of this entry's 1865-1970s data span (e.g. Mitchell, INE historical series) will label the unit 'Santander' rather than 'Cantabria', and any alias table feeding this polity must account for both names mapping to the same territory and the same polity_code. This has not been checked against the harness's country_conventions.json alias list, and is worth flagging before ingestion assumes 'Cantabria' is the only label in play for pre-1984 rows.

### oq-cantabria-polygon-vintage

**mapspain-ign not yet fetched; provincial boundary stability across 1833-2025 unverified for this specific province**

As with ESP-CO and ESP-MD, the registered polygon source mapspain-ign (IGN provinces.gpkg, id_column=cpro, expected cpro=39 for Cantabria) is present_locally=False and cannot be attached yet. The general claim that Spanish provincial boundaries have been stable since 1833 is asserted by convention for all ESP provinces, but Cantabria's specific boundary has not been individually checked against 19th-century partido or municipal reorganizations (e.g. minor municipal boundary adjustments between Cantabria and neighbouring Basque provinces/Castile-León in the Liébana and Campoo areas) that could matter for a full 1833-2025 span. This should be verified once mapspain-ign is fetched, rather than assumed identical to the ESP-CO/ESP-MD precedent by default.
