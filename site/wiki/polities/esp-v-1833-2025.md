# Valencia/València (province of Spain)

## Summary

Valencia is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and since 1982 also one of three constituent provinces (with Alicante and Castellón) of the Autonomous Community of the Comunitat Valenciana, of which it is the seat of government and by far the most populous. Unlike Asturias, Cantabria or Madrid -- single-province communities where the province and community boundaries coincide -- Valencia province is a sub-unit of a three-province community, so this row is deliberately provincial rather than regional in scope. This entry is type: subnational because Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025; the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, distinguishable from it because INE and Eurostat/GISCO have tabulated Valencia separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES523, for well over a century.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES523, admin_name ES523) captures valued statistical rows for the Valencia NUTS3 unit, following the same country convention already applied to eight sibling ESP subnational rows (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra, Valladolid), and the system default start rule of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, with no Canary Islands-style departure applicable) plus an open end_year of 2025 for a still-current unit. Previously matched to: nothing specific -- the routing_reasoning states the candidate list for Valencia itself is empty, so absent this row the data would have fallen back to the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, collapsing a single province's series into a country-wide one, the same failure the eight prior ESP province rows were created to avoid. What confirms distinctness: Valencia's continuous, separately-tabulated status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES523) and INE provincial statistical division since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the eight existing rows -- and at roughly 10,807 km2, Valencia province is a small fraction of the roughly 505,000 km2 covered by the national ESP-1800-2025 row.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the INE province numeric code); Valencia's cpro is 46, recorded here as polygon_feature_id "46." As with all eight prior ESP province rows, sources.yaml marks mapspain-ign present_locally=False even though those eight rows already derive from it, so this is carried forward as an open question (see oq-mapspain-ign-present-locally-flag) rather than treated as a hard blocker. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and has no province-level match for Valencia, since it only models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, so it was correctly ruled out.

Territory description: Valencia province occupies a stretch of Spain's Mediterranean coast in the Comunitat Valenciana, bordered by Castellón to the north, Teruel and Cuenca (Aragón / Castilla-La Mancha) to the west, Albacete to the southwest, and Alicante to the south, with a long Mediterranean shoreline (the Costa del Azahar/Costa de València) to the east. Its capital and largest city, also named Valencia, is Spain's third-largest city, sited on the Turia river near its mouth. On a modern map the province spans the fertile huerta coastal plain, the rice-growing Albufera wetland just south of the capital, and inland mountainous comarcas (La Serranía, Los Serranos, El Rincon de Ademuz -- a Valencian exclave surrounded by Aragón and Cuenca). Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 10,807 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is confirmed accessible, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment made for the eight sibling province pages.

## Predecessors and successors

Valencia has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was part of the historic Kingdom of Valencia, a Crown of Aragon possession that lost its separate laws and institutions under the 1707 Nueva Planta decree but was not reorganized into the modern province until the 1833 reform, and covered a somewhat different (broader, undivided) area than the modern province alone -- this database does not model sub-1833 Spanish kingdoms at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty (see oq-kingdom-of-valencia-vs-province), the same treatment given to the eight existing ESP subnational rows. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain, and since 1982 a constituent province of the Comunitat Valenciana, as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive).

## Sourced claims

- INE and Eurostat both report Valencia as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES523) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES523 records that Valencia is one of the original 1833-reform provinces with no departure from the default start rule applicable, so its span follows the country default 1833-2025 rather than a bespoke start year.
- The polygon_reasoning for this decision records mapspain-ign's id_column as cpro (INE province numeric code), with Valencia's code 46, while flagging that sources.yaml's present_locally=False for mapspain-ign conflicts with eight prior rows in this repository already deriving from that same source.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-v-single-letter

**Used the single-letter abbreviation V rather than a two-letter form or the NUTS/cpro digits**

The dominant ESP subnational pattern is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the eight existing rows (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO, VA) all use a short informal abbreviation of the province name rather than the NUTS3 code (ES523) or the INE province numeric code (46). Valencia's everyday postal/vehicle-plate abbreviation has historically been the single letter V (as distinct from VA, already taken by Valladolid, and from VC, which designates the three-province Comunitat Valenciana rather than the province alone). Minting ESP-V-1833-2025 keeps the sibling set predictable from the same informal-abbreviation rule while avoiding a collision with ESP-VA-1833-2025 and avoiding the confusion of using the regional code VC for a provincial-level row. This departs from the two-letter default only because the province's own conventional abbreviation is one letter; it is not a change of rule, just what that rule produces for this unit.

## Open questions

### oq-mapspain-ign-present-locally-flag

**mapspain-ign is flagged present_locally=False despite eight existing ESP rows already citing it**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning notes sources.yaml marks mapspain-ign as present_locally=False, yet eight prior Spanish province rows (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra, Valladolid) already carry polygon_feature_id values keyed to this source's cpro column, all left at polygon_status unassigned rather than assigned. Either the flag is stale and the provinces layer is actually on disk, in which case all nine rows (including this one) should be promoted to assigned once verified, or the flag is accurate and none of the nine rows has ever had its polygon reference checked against a real file. This should be resolved once by fetching/confirming provinces.gpkg, not re-litigated per province.

### oq-kingdom-of-valencia-vs-province

**Does a pre-1833 Kingdom of Valencia entity belong in this database, and would it overlap this row's start?**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory later split into the three Comunitat Valenciana provinces (Valencia, Alicante, Castellón) was governed as the historic Kingdom of Valencia, part of the Crown of Aragon until the 1707 Nueva Planta decree abolished its separate fueros and folded it into direct Castilian administration -- a genuinely distinct polity in area (larger than this province alone) and administrative form (kingdom with own law, not a province). This entry leaves predecessor empty, following the same choice made for Valladolid, on the grounds that this database does not yet model sub-1833 Spanish kingdoms at that granularity. If a future entry for the Kingdom of Valencia (or the whole Comunitat Valenciana region across all three modern provinces) is created, its end_year and this row's start_year (1833) need to be checked for a clean handoff rather than an overlap, since the kingdom's territory does not map 1:1 onto the modern province.
