# United States (1867-1959)

## Summary

This row tracks the United States from the **Alaska Purchase (1867)**
to **Hawaii and Alaska statehood (1959)** — a **93-year row**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). With Alaska
(+1.7M km2), the continental US plus Alaska reaches ~9.4M km2.
Territory is stable throughout the row. Hawaii was annexed as a
territory in 1898. The Spanish-American War (1898) brought
Philippines, Puerto Rico, and Guam under US control.

The end-of-row event is the **admission of Alaska and Hawaii as the
49th and 50th states in 1959**, which formalized their status within
the union. While not a territorial *acquisition* (both were already
US territories), statehood is the CSV's chosen split point.

Predecessor: [usa-1848-1867](usa-1848-1867.md). Successor:
[usa-1959-2025](usa-1959-2025.md).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` gwcode 2, the step
  **1959-01-03 / 1959-08-20** (the 49-state United States: the
  contiguous states plus Alaska, without Hawaii), pinned by
  `polygon_feature_date: 1959-01-03`. Until 2026-09-24 this row carried
  Cliopatria's `United States of America` 1880-1889 step; see
  [decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state](#decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state)
  for why it was replaced and why this step was chosen.
- **What the polygon includes.** The contiguous United States (borders
  fixed since the 1853 Gadsden Purchase) **plus Alaska**, which this
  table counts as American from the 1867 purchase — the row's own
  start-of-row event, and the reason
  [ALK-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md) declares this row as its container.
  It **excludes Hawaii**, which this table carries as its own row
  [HAWI-1898-1959](hawi-1898-1959.md) until statehood, the same way the
  1898 overseas acquisitions (Puerto Rico, Guam, the Philippines) are
  separate rows.
- **Area.** **9,446,212 km²** (EPSG:6933). This is exactly CShapes'
  pre-statehood contiguous US (gwcode 2, 1886-1959, 7,939,971 km²)
  united with its Territory of Alaska (gwcode 3, 1886-1959,
  1,506,241 km²): the union of those two features measures the same
  9,446,212 km², so the 1959 step is not a different border, only
  CShapes' rendering of the two pre-statehood units as one. Like every
  CShapes US polygon, it includes the US share of the Great Lakes.
- **Stated areas measure a narrower unit.** IIA (7,839,432 km²) and
  fao1952 (7,827,830 km²) state the *continental* land area, without
  Alaska and without lake water
  [source_stated_area_basis](../../data/final/source_stated_area_basis.csv).
  That is the reporting unit of this row's layer-B series (see the
  Mitchell contradiction below: Alaska's agricultural contribution is
  immaterial), not a different border.

### mitchell's series is the continental United States, excluding Hawaii

Recorded 2026-08-18 from `suspect_wiki_pages`, which asked whether the series
includes Hawaii (annexed 1898) and Alaska (purchased 1867, this row's start year).

**Hawaii is excluded, and sugar proves it.** Hawaii's only material contribution
to any item Mitchell reports would be sugar. Mitchell's `sugar cane` for the USA:

| | 1890-1897 | 1899-1906 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| production (t) | 287,000 | 314,000 | **1.09** |
| area (ha) | 22,000 | 35,000 | 1.59 |

Against that, `fao1952` reports Hawaii's own sugar at about **896,500 t** — nearly
**three times the entire mainland cane figure**. Folding Hawaii in from 1898 would
have multiplied this series several-fold. It rises 9%, which is mainland
expansion, so Hawaii is not in it.

**Alaska is undetectable and immaterial.** Every item Mitchell reports here is
temperate mainland agriculture — apples, barley, buckwheat, maize, oats, rye,
wheat, potatoes, cotton, tobacco, and the livestock series. Alaska contributed
effectively none of any of them, so whether the reporting convention nominally
included it cannot be tested from these figures and does not affect them.

**One thing that cannot be checked here:** Mitchell carries **no Hawaii label at
all** (0 rows), so the exclusion is established from the absent step rather than
from a separate Hawaii series. Only `fao1952` carries `Hawaii` (102 rows), which is
where the 896,500 t figure comes from.

The cotton expansion over the same window — 8,587,500 ha (1890-1897) to 11,231,000
(1899-1906) — is worth noting so it is not mistaken for a territorial step: cotton
was never grown in Hawaii or Alaska, and the rise is the well-documented spread of
cotton into Texas and Oklahoma.

### decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state

**`polygon_source` moved `cliopatria` (`United States of America`, 1880) ->
`cshapes-2.0` (gwcode 2, step 1959-01-03 / 1959-08-20) on 2026-09-24 (issue 655).**

Issue 655 found that this row and its successor published a **6.2% area step at
1959** (8,906,417 -> 9,461,945 km²) where no border moved, and read it as two
territorial conventions meeting at one seam. Measuring the Cliopatria polygon
against CShapes showed the step was mostly something else: **the Cliopatria 1880
step leaves out most of Arizona and New Mexico**. Differenced against CShapes'
contiguous US (1886-1959), the largest missing piece is **492,363 km²** centred
near 108.4°W 33.4°N, and against the state polygons this table already places
inside this row:

| member (declares this row as container) | share of its polygon outside the Cliopatria container |
|---|---|
| USA-AZ-1863-2025 | 71.0% |
| USA-NM-1912-2025 | 65.5% |
| USA-TX-1845-2025 | 11.4% |

Both were US territories for the whole row (New Mexico Territory 1850, Arizona
Territory 1863; states 1912), so this was a polygon defect, not a convention: the
Cliopatria series itself only returns them in its 1890-1897 step (9,402,121 km²).
The remainder of the gap is the Great Lakes (about 179,000 km² of US lake water,
which CShapes includes and Cliopatria does not) and coastline generalisation.

**Why this CShapes step.** The table's own structure already answers issue 655's
question 1 — which convention governs territory that is not yet a state:

- **Alaska counts from the 1867 purchase.** This row *starts* at the purchase, and
  [ALK-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md) declares this row as its container for
  1867-1959.
- **Hawaii is a separate row until statehood.** [HAWI-1898-1959](hawi-1898-1959.md)
  is a national row bound to CShapes gwcode 4 and names
  [USA-1959-2025](usa-1959-2025.md) as its successor — the same treatment as the
  other overseas acquisitions of 1898.

So the polygon this row needs is the contiguous US plus Alaska, without Hawaii.
CShapes carries exactly that as its 49-state step, whose area (9,446,212 km²) equals
the union of its pre-statehood gwcode 2 and its Territory of Alaska (gwcode 3), so
the choice needs no constructed polygon. Both sides of the 1959 seam are now one
source under one convention, and the step becomes **9,446,212 -> 9,462,898 km²
(+16,686 km², +0.18%)** — exactly HAWI-1898-1959's area, i.e. Hawaii joining the
national row. That is a real change of this table's national territory, which
answers issue 655's question 2: the 1959 split is kept, and it is carried by
Hawaii (August 1959), not by Alaska.

**What this does not change.** The step is dated inside 1959, a vintage later than
most of the row; the contiguous border did not move between 1867 and 1959 (Gadsden
1853, Chamizal only in 1963), and CShapes draws Alaska's panhandle on the post-1903
tribunal line for its whole 1886-1959 span, so the pre-1903 decades carry that line
too. Stated areas from IIA and fao1952 (about 7.83M km²) describe the continental
reporting unit, not this polygon; the ratio rises from 1.14 to 1.21, still under
the 1.5 review threshold of `write_stated_area_basis.py`. In
`border_stability.csv` the row's `source_gap_pct` moves from 12.17 to 18.96: that
stage's reference is gwcode 2 alone, which leaves Alaska out until statehood, and
18.96% is Alaska (1,506,241 / 7,939,971 km²) and nothing else — where the old 12.17
was Alaska minus Arizona, New Mexico and the Great Lakes netting out.

**What it surfaces upstream.** With both rows at the 1867 seam no longer from one
source, `validate_source_change_steps.py` now sees USA-1848-1867 -> USA-1867-1959
(2.72x) and it is baselined there as an ARTEFACT: Cliopatria's 1849 polygon omits
most of the trans-Mississippi West (about 4.37M km²), so only about 1.5M km² of that
step is the Alaska Purchase. The same understatement was already published before
this change (2.57x, Cliopatria to Cliopatria); fixing it needs a constructed polygon
for [USA-1848-1867](usa-1848-1867.md) and is left open.

**Resolved 2026-09-24.** USA-1848-1867 now carries CShapes gwcode 2's 1886-1959
step (7,939,971 km²) directly — no constructed polygon was needed, because that
step is the contiguous US without Alaska — so the 1867 seam is 7,939,971 ->
9,446,212 km² (1.19x), exactly CShapes' Territory of Alaska (1,506,241 km²), and
the ARTEFACT baseline entry is removed. See
[usa-1848-1867 §decision-polygon-cshapes-contiguous](usa-1848-1867.md#decision-polygon-cshapes-contiguous).

## Open questions

### oq-1959-split-justification

**Answered 2026-09-24 (issue 655):** the split is carried by Hawaii, which this
table holds as a separate row (HAWI-1898-1959) and which joins the national row in
1959; Alaska is inside this row from 1867. See
[decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state](#decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state). The
original question is kept below.

The 1959 split is based on Alaska and Hawaii achieving statehood,
but both were already US territories (Alaska from 1867, Hawaii
from 1898). No new territory was acquired. Under the strict WHEP
polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition),
statehood is a political/administrative change, not a territorial
one. The CSV may be using statehood as a proxy for the last
meaningful change in US territorial configuration. Not a high-
priority issue but worth flagging for consistency with the rule.

### oq-1898-overseas-territories

The Spanish-American War (1898) brought Philippines, Puerto Rico,
Guam, and (indirectly) Hawaii under US control. Philippines was
granted independence in 1946. Puerto Rico and Guam remain US
territories. These are tracked as separate WHEP rows, but the
acquisition of overseas territories in 1898 is arguably a larger
territorial event than the 1959 statehood. The CSV does not split
at 1898 — consistent with the principle that overseas territories
are separate rows, but the logic differs from the 1959 split.

### oq-polygon-93-year-span

**Verified 2026-09-24 (issue 655):** the Cliopatria polygon was checked and found to
omit most of Arizona and New Mexico; the row now carries CShapes' 49-state step. See
[decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state](#decision-polygon-cshapes-49-state). The
original question is kept below.

A single Cliopatria 1877–1879 polygon applied across 93 years is
a very long temporal span. The polygon should be broadly accurate
for the continental + Alaska footprint, but it cannot represent
Hawaii (annexed 1898) or overseas territories. Verification
against Cliopatria geometry not yet done.
