# Canada (post-Rupert's Land 1870-1886)

## Summary

Split from the retired CAN-1866-1886 at the 1870 transfer of Rupert's Land and the
North-Western Territory from the Hudson's Bay Company to the Dominion. The row's predecessor
[can-1866-1870](can-1866-1870.md) covers the four original provinces and carries no polygon;
this row is the first CAN row with one. It receives **53 layer-B rows**, routed by the alias
`canada` 1870–1885 and by the `trade-sources` reporter label `Canada` for the same years.

`end_year` is exclusive: this row covers 1870–1885 and 1886 belongs to
[can-1886-1949](can-1886-1949.md), which is why the alias stops at 1885 and a separate
single-year alias sends 1886 forward.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status: proxy.** CShapes 2.0 `gwcode = 20`, `gwsyear = 1886` — the earliest vintage
CShapes offers for Canada — stood in for the whole 1870–1885 span. Measured directly from
`data/geodata/cshapes-2.0/CShapes-2.0.shp` in ESRI:54034: **9,553,644 km²**, matching the
9,553,560 km² in the frontmatter to 0.001%.

Two independent cross-checks say that polygon is the right *shape*:

- CShapes' next vintage (`gwsyear = 1948`) measures **9,952,067 km²**, and GADM 4.1's
  thirteen adm1 provinces and territories sum to **9,955,229 km²** — 0.03% apart. Biger
  gives 9,976,140 km² for Canada in 1994 ([biger-1995
  §canada](../sources/biger-1995.md#canada), p.139).
- The 1886→1948 step is **+398,423 km²**, against GADM's Newfoundland and Labrador at
  **407,309 km²**. So the 1886 vintage is modern Canada minus Newfoundland — exactly what
  it should be, since Newfoundland acceded on 31 March 1949 (see
  [can-1886-1949](can-1886-1949.md)).

## The "~10% overstatement" applies to one year of the fifteen, not to the span

The alias registry's note on `canada` 1870–1885 reads: *"CShapes 2.0 gwcode=20 gwsyear=1886
proxy ~9.55M km²; ~10% overstatement (BC 1871/PEI 1873 not yet joined) acceptable."* The
magnitude is right and the scoping is wrong, in the direction that makes the proxy **better**
than the note claims. Measured from GADM 4.1 adm1 in ESRI:54034:

| Span | Provinces in the 1886 polygon but not yet in Canada | Overstatement |
|---|---|---|
| 1870 – 20 Jul 1871 | British Columbia (948,848 km²) + Prince Edward Island (5,731 km²) | **9.99%** |
| 1871 – 1 Jul 1873 | Prince Edward Island (5,731 km²) | **0.06%** |
| 1873 – 1885 | none | **0%** from this cause |

British Columbia entered Confederation on 20 July 1871 and Prince Edward Island on 1 July
1873. So the ~10% figure describes 1870 alone; from 1873 the error is zero, because for
thirteen of the row's sixteen calendar years (1873–1885) no province is missing from the
polygon at all. The province-accession error is not what limits this row.

**What does limit it, and points the other way.** Canada did not hold the Arctic Archipelago
until Britain transferred the British Arctic Territories on 1 September 1880. The 1886
polygon includes them, so for 1870–1880 the proxy overstates the territory by the
archipelago's area instead — a *different* error from the one on record, affecting a
*different* eleven years. It is not quantified here: the archipelago is not a GADM adm1 unit
(its islands are split across Nunavut and the Northwest Territories, whose 1886 boundaries
also differ from today's), so no honest figure can be read off the sources in this
repository. See oq-can-1870-1886-arctic.

Net effect on the data: the alias's "acceptable" verdict survives, but for a reason the note
did not give. The years where the province-accession error is largest (1870–1871) are also
the earliest and thinnest in the trade series, and from 1873 that error is zero; the standing
uncertainty is the pre-1880 Arctic inclusion, which is uncorrected and unmeasured.

## Sourced claims

- Canada became a Dominion in 1867 — *"Independence was achieved through a lengthy process in
  1867"* ([biger-1995 §canada](../sources/biger-1995.md#canada), p.139). That is the boundary
  of [can-1866-1870](can-1866-1870.md), not of this row.
- The Canada–United States boundary, which bounds this polity to the south for its whole
  span, was fixed well before 1870 and Biger records it as stable: *"Since 1905 the
  boundaries between Canada and the United States have been well maintained"* (p.144), with
  the Alaska segment defined by the 1825 Anglo-Russian convention (p.143). No Canada–US
  boundary change falls inside 1870–1885, so the southern limit of the 1886 polygon is
  correct for the whole span.
- Routing: `applied_aliases.csv` carries `canada` → CAN-1870-1886 for 1870–1885 and the
  `trade-sources` reporter label `Canada` (FAOSTAT area 33) for the same years.

## Open questions

### oq-can-1870-1886-arctic

Quantify the pre-1880 overstatement from the British Arctic Territories transfer
(1 September 1880), which the 1886 CShapes polygon includes for years Canada did not hold
them. Needs a source that separates the Arctic Archipelago from mainland Nunavut/NWT;
neither CShapes nor GADM adm1 does. Until then the 1870–1880 portion of this row should be
treated as an upper bound on territory, not an estimate of it.

### oq-can-1870-1886-manitoba-1881

Manitoba's boundaries were enlarged in 1881 and again in 1912, and Ontario's and Quebec's
northern limits moved after 1870 (the reason
[can-1866-1870](can-1866-1870.md) refuses a modern-province polygon entirely). These are
*internal* re-divisions of territory the Dominion already held after 1870, so they do not
change this row's external extent — but they do mean no province-union recipe can be used to
build a vintage-correct polygon for this row.
