# Antigua and Barbuda

## Summary

WHEP row covering Antigua, Barbuda and Redonda across the whole 1800–2025 span, added to
carry FAOSTAT/FABIO reporting code ATG. The row receives **134 layer-B rows** — the largest
data exposure of any polity page still flagged as a CSV-derived stub under issue 19, which
is why it was documented first.

The territory this row denotes never changed. What changed repeatedly is the *administrative
container* it was reported inside, and that is what a data match has to get right here: for
most of the row's span "Antigua" is a sub-unit of a British colonial aggregate, and the same
source can report it at either level in the same year.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source:** GADM 4.1 adm0 (`GID_0 = ATG`), used as a proxy for the full span.
- **Measured extent:** **435.8 km²** (GADM 4.1 adm0, ESRI:54034). Decomposed from GADM's
  own adm1 layer: Antigua's six parishes total **283.6 km²**, Barbuda **151.3 km²**, and the
  uninhabited islet of Redonda **0.9 km²**. [biger-1995
  §antigua-and-barbuda](../sources/biger-1995.md#antigua-and-barbuda) records the entry as a
  Caribbean island state with **no land boundaries** (p.36).
- **Why the proxy is defensible across 1800–2025, unlike most proxies in this database.**
  The failure mode a modern-borders proxy usually has — a boundary that moved inside the row's
  span — cannot occur for an island group with no land neighbours and no cession or
  annexation on record. Barbuda and Redonda were administered with Antigua throughout, and
  Barbuda's 1976–present constitutional disputes with Antigua are internal, not territorial.
  So the proxy's weakness here is *not* geometry.
- **The real weakness is that the polity is smaller than its reporting unit for most of the
  span.** See the next section: a source-level match, not a polygon-level one, is what can
  go wrong.

## Administrative containers, 1833–1981

Antigua was never separately administered before 1981; it was a component of a succession of
British groupings, each of which is a *different* territory from this row:

| Period | Container | WHEP polity | Area |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1833–1956/60 | British Leeward Islands Colony (federated 1871) | [BLI-1833-1960](bli-1833-1960.md) | ~1,716 km² to 1940, ~965 km² after |
| 1958–1962 | West Indies Federation | — | — |
| 1967–1981 | Associated State of Antigua (self-governing, UK-defended) | ATG-1800-2025 | ~435.8 km² |
| 1981– | independent Antigua and Barbuda | ATG-1800-2025 | ~435.8 km² |

`decolonization_events.csv` records independence from Britain on **1981-11-01**; COW v2024
lists Antigua and Barbuda as COW 58 from 1981 onward. The row nonetheless starts at 1800,
following this database's convention of one long row per modern territory where no polygon
source distinguishes the colonial vintages — not a claim that a polity called "Antigua and
Barbuda" existed in 1800.

**Routing consequence:** for the whole 1833–1956 stretch, a source label naming the *group*
(`British West Indies Leeward Islands`, `Leeward Islands`) must route to BLI-1833-1960, ~4×
this row's area, and a label naming the *island* must route here. Routing a group label here
understates territory ~4×; routing an island label to BLI overstates it ~4×.

## Sourced claims

- **The FAO 1952 labels are island-level, and this was measured rather than assumed.**
  `applied_aliases.csv` routes `British West Indies Antigua` (1949–1950),
  `Br West Indies Antigua` (1951) and the sub-entry form `Leeward Islands  Antigua` (1950)
  to this row. The confirming measurement is the source's own land-use total: FAO 1952
  reports **44.0 thousand ha = 440 km²** for `Leeward Islands  Antigua` in 1950, **1.0%**
  from GADM's 435.8 km² — island scale, against the same source's **109.0 thousand ha**
  for the Leeward group as a whole in the same column. The qualifier after the group
  heading is therefore load-bearing and is being read correctly.
- **The same source reports both levels in the same year**, which is why the two rules must
  coexist: 1950 carries `Leeward Islands` (group → BLI) *and* `Leeward Islands  Antigua`
  (island → here). A single label-level rule would double-count Antigua's output.
- **Dominica's 1940 transfer out of the Leeward group does not touch this row.** It changes
  BLI's extent (~1,716 → ~965 km², documented at
  [bli-1833-1960](bli-1833-1960.md#territorial-scope)) and therefore the group-level match,
  not Antigua's own.
- Biger 1995 confirms the no-land-boundary status, which is the whole of what that source
  offers for this entry ([biger-1995
  §antigua-and-barbuda](../sources/biger-1995.md#antigua-and-barbuda), p.36).

## Open questions

### oq-atg-polygon-area-null

`polygon_area_km2` is `null` while the prose above carries a measured ~435.8 km² from the
same GADM feature the row already binds. The figure is left out of the frontmatter rather
than typed in, because in this database that field records what a *source* states for the
polity and no source consulted here states an area for Antigua and Barbuda — Biger gives
none, and GADM's is a derived measurement of the shipped geometry. Populating it from the
geometry would make the field self-referential. Flagged, not silently filled.

### oq-atg-pre-1833-reporting

Nothing here establishes how, or whether, Antigua was reported separately before the
1833 Leeward Islands Colony. No layer-B row currently matched to this polity predates
1937, so the question is not yet load-bearing; it becomes so if any pre-1833 series is
ingested against ATG.
