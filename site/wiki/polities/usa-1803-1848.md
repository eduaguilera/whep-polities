# United States (1803-1848)

## Summary

This row tracks the United States from the **Louisiana Purchase
(1803)** to the **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848)** — a
**46-year row** of massive territorial expansion
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). During this
period the US grew from ~1.2M km2 to ~3.5M km2 through the
Louisiana Purchase (+2.1M km2), the acquisition of Florida
(Adams-Onis Treaty, 1819), the annexation of Texas (1845), and the
Oregon Treaty (1846).

The end-of-row event is the **Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848)**
ending the Mexican-American War, which transferred ~1.4M km2
(California, Nevada, Utah, most of Arizona and New Mexico, parts of
Colorado and Wyoming) from Mexico to the United States. This is
another massive territorial change justifying the split under the
WHEP polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition).

Predecessor: [usa-1800-1803](usa-1800-1803.md). Successor:
[usa-1848-1867](usa-1848-1867.md).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `Cliopatria (United States of America
  1842-1845)` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). A single
  Cliopatria time-step from 1842–1845 applied across all 46 years
  of the row. For the early part of the row (1803–1819), the
  polygon overstates the actual territory — it includes Florida
  (acquired 1819) and the full Louisiana Purchase extent that was
  not yet fully surveyed or settled. For the late part (1845–1848),
  it understates the territory — Oregon Treaty (1846) gains and
  Texas annexation (1845) may or may not be reflected in the
  1842–1845 Cliopatria polygon.
- **Area.** ~3.5M km2 by 1845 per CSV notes
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

## Predecessors and successors

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-pre-louisiana

The Cliopatria 1842–1845 polygon is applied across the full row
including 1803–1819, when US territory was significantly smaller
(no Florida, partial Louisiana exploration). The polygon
overstates early-row territory. Not load-bearing if analyses
focus on the 1840s, but relevant for 1803–1819 sub-period work.
