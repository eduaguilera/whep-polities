# New South Wales (to 1900)

## Summary

The Colony of New South Wales from the 1800 database floor to the eve of Australian federation. NSW was the parent British colony in Australia — founded 7 February 1788 by the First Fleet under Arthur Phillip — and during the 19th century it progressively shed territory as four separate colonies were carved out of it (Van Diemen's Land 1825, South Australia 1836, Victoria 1851, Queensland 1859). The WHEP row ends on 31 December 1900 on the eve of federation (1 January 1901), when the remaining NSW territory became a state of the Commonwealth of Australia.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from CShapes 2.0. CShapes models NSW as a single dependency-level polity over this period; area shrinks in four discrete steps as Tasmania, South Australia, Victoria, and Queensland are separated out. The CSV uses `iso3=NSW` as a WHEP-internal subnational-style code (not an ISO-3166 value). Post-1900 territorial extent corresponds to the modern state of New South Wales.

## Contradictions

None identified. South Australia's 1836 separation is not captured by the date list here because South Australia was carved primarily from *unorganised* territory nominally under NSW governance rather than from a contiguous populated NSW settlement area — but NSW's legal jurisdiction did cover the future SA prior to 1836.

### the 1859 Queensland separation is not detectable in this series

Recorded 2026-08-18. **A queued note read the absence of a step at 1859 as
evidence that Queensland's wheat was negligible. That inference is not
supported, and the reason is worth recording.**

`sa_colonial` wheat for New South Wales (Bushels), around the separation:

| 1854 | 1855 | 1856 | 1857 | 1858 | **1859** | 1860 | 1861 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 786,278 | 133,446 | 1,756,964 | 1,289,044 | 1,565,532 | **1,605,353** | 1,581,598 | 1,606,034 |

There is indeed **no step at 1859** — 1,565,532 → 1,605,353 → 1,581,598 is flat.
But the series' own year-to-year movement is enormous: 786,278 to 133,446 in one
year (0.17x), then to 1,756,964 the next (13x), and 226,027 as late as 1866. In a
series whose noise spans an order of magnitude, a level shift of 10-20% cannot be
seen at all.

So the correct statement is that **this series cannot detect the separation**,
not that Queensland's wheat was negligible. The observation is sound; the
inference from it is not. Colonial NSW wheat was drought-dominated, and any
territorial test on it needs either a smoother series or a much larger expected
step.

### d-end-year-1901-not-1900

**The colony existed through all of 1900, so its exclusive `end_year` is 1901**

The Commonwealth of Australia was proclaimed on 1 January 1901, so this colony existed for every day of 1900 and ceased on the last of it. With `end_year` exclusive that is 1901, and the row previously said 1900 -- covering only to the end of 1899 and leaving 1900 with no colony at all.

The error was visible inside this table before it was visible in the data: the aggregate row [AUS-1800-1901](aus-1800-1901.md) already ends in 1901 and the successor states all begin in 1901, so the six colonies were the only rows dating federation to 1900. What surfaced it was the panel -- six Australian units report 1900 and those rows resolved to nothing, because the colony alias stopped at 1899 and the state alias began at 1901. Corrected 2026-09-17.

## Open questions

### oq-nsw-start-date

CSV `start_year=1800` is a database floor, not a historical event. NSW existed continuously from 1788. WHEP convention: leave start at 1800; record the 1788 foundation in `Sourced claims`.

### oq-nsw-tasmania-date

The 1825 Tasmania separation removed territory before the 1800 database floor had a territorial change event. No WHEP row split is needed because NSW-1800-1901 is already a single row absorbing these colony separations.
