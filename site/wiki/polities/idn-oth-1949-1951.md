>
> This row is a complement, so it is only as good as what it subtracts. It was blocked behind
> [idn-blb-1949-1951](idn-blb-1949-1951.md): with **Sumbawa wrongly inside** Bali/Lombok, this
> polygon would have removed Sumbawa from the other islands as well — **misplacing ~15,000 km²

>
> **West Papua was subtracted on 2026-08-07, and the declared area corrected with it.** The first
> version of this polygon subtracted only Java and Bali, matching the page's then-declared
> 1,757,495 km² to 0.6% — but that figure was itself wrong, so the agreement was two errors
> cancelling. The Netherlands held West Papua until 1962, which is the entire reason
> [nng-1949-1963](nng-1949-1963.md) exists as a row; leaving Papua inside this complement put
> **405,513 km² — 98.8% of NNG — inside two live polities at once**, which is the overlap
> [eduaguilera/whep#514](https://github.com/eduaguilera/whep/issues/514) reported.
>
> FAO's own 1952 yearbook settles it, because it lists the two as **separate reporting units**:
>
> ```
> Indonesia (ASIA)     1947    1,904,350 km²
> New Guinea (ASIA)    1951      412,780 km²
> ```
>
> NNG-1949-1963 declares 410,361 — a 0.6% match to FAO's New Guinea. The 1,904,350 figure is a
> **pre-independence 1947 total for the whole Dutch East Indies**, and that is how the anachronism
> entered: the old declared value was that total less Java and Bali, carrying Papua along with it.
>
> **Papua is subtracted as GADM provinces, not as NNG's own CShapes feature**, and the reason is
> measured rather than assumed. Cutting both rows from one source ought to keep them from drifting,
> so `_cshapes2_feature(851, 1955)` was tried first. It does not survive publication:
>
> ```
> minus CShapes 851             1,341,857 km²   ∩ published NNG   341 km²
> minus GADM Papua provinces    1,335,103 km²   ∩ published NNG     0 km²
> ```
>
> GADM's Papua coastline does not follow CShapes', so subtracting 851 leaves a hairline coastal
> **rim** of GADM Papua behind — 1,031 fragments, 6,140 km², largest 268 km². Every fragment is
> outside 851 by construction, so the *unsimplified* intersection is genuinely zero; then
> `build_database` simplifies the two rows **independently** at 0.01° (~1.1 km), the rim's edges
> wobble back across the boundary, and 341 km² of double claim reappears in the published
> GeoPackage. Cutting GADM with GADM leaves no rim to wobble.
>
> It also fits FAO better. Against FAO 1952's New Guinea of 412,780 km²:
>
> ```
> GADM Papua + Papua Barat   412,305 km²   0.1%
> CShapes 851                410,361 km²   0.6%
> ```
>
> **The residual is now a gap, not an overlap, and that is the intended direction.** GADM Papua is
> ~1,900 km² larger than CShapes 851, so that much coastal fringe is subtracted here while
> [nng-1949-1963](nng-1949-1963.md) — still CShapes-bound — does not cover it, leaving it in no
> polity. A 1,900 km² gap is strictly preferable to a 405,513 km² double count: unassigned territory
> is visible, whereas doubly-claimed territory silently inflates every area-weighted aggregate over
> it. `audit_family_shadowing`'s `PINNED_DISJOINT` asserts the zero on every run, so this cannot
> regress quietly the way the prose baseline did.
>
> This polygon — tens of thousands of islands — is the one that crossed OGR's per-object GeoJSON
> size limit and made `scripts/sources/constructed/build.py` write a GeoPackage instead. The
> comment at its `OUT` constant records that.
## Summary

This WHEP polity covers **all of Indonesia except Java/Madura and Bali/Lombok**, as the third reporting unit used by the **FAO 1952 dataset** for the period 1949–1951. The FAO source disaggregated Indonesia into three distinct island-group sub-units: "Indonesia Java and Madura" (`IDN-JVM-1949-1951`, ~132,000 km²), "Indonesia Bali and Lombok" (`IDN-BLB-1949-1951`, ~10,505 km²), and "Indonesia Other islands" (this polity, ~1,349,065 km²). This three-part structure reflects a sub-national agricultural census framework used in the immediate post-independence period.

"Other islands" comprises **Sumatra, Kalimantan (Indonesian Borneo), Sulawesi, the Maluku archipelago, West and East Nusa Tenggara (excluding Lombok)** — but **not** the Papua region, which was
Netherlands New Guinea until 1962 and is carried by [nng-1949-1963](nng-1949-1963.md). This is
the vast majority of Indonesia's land area, but a smaller share of its agricultural output (the FAO data shows lower rice paddy and cassava yields than Java, reflecting the less-intensively farmed outer islands).

Routing this data to `IDN-1949-1963` (full Indonesia, ~1,900,000 km²) would overstate the represented territory by a factor of ~1.41; more critically it would **double-count** with the separately-matched Java/Madura and Bali/Lombok entries, which is the primary reason a dedicated polity is required.

The area is computed as FAO 1952's Indonesia total for 1947 (1,904,350 km², which is a whole-Dutch-East-Indies figure) minus FAO's separately-listed New Guinea (412,780 km², implemented as GADM's Papua + Papua Barat at 412,305 km², a 0.1% match) minus Java+Madura (~132,000 km²) minus Bali+Lombok (~10,505 km²).

**Polygon provenance**: GADM 4.1 sub-national Indonesia level-1 features for all provinces **not** covered by IDN-JVM-1949-1951 and IDN-BLB-1949-1951: specifically, the Sumatra provinces (Aceh, North Sumatra, West Sumatra, Riau, Riau Islands, Jambi, South Sumatra, Bengkulu, Lampung, Bangka-Belitung), the five Kalimantan provinces, the six Sulawesi provinces (North, Central, South, Southeast Sulawesi, Gorontalo, West Sulawesi), Maluku, North Maluku, and East Nusa Tenggara. **Papua and West Papua are excluded** — they were
Netherlands New Guinea for this entire span. The province of West Nusa Tenggara (IDN.20_1) is **not** included here because the BLB polity already uses the full NTB province polygon as a proxy for Bali+Lombok (an acknowledged approximation documented on that wiki page); Sumbawa's area is thus counted toward the BLB polygon but belongs conceptually to "Other islands". This is recorded as an open question (see below).

All island physical extents are stable geographic features; modern GADM province boundaries are a valid proxy for 1949–1951 boundaries.

**Polygon status: proxy** — modern GADM boundaries used for geographically stable island extents; no period-specific historical GIS available for Indonesian sub-national provinces.

**Polygon confidence: high** — island/province boundaries are fixed physical geography; the main approximation is the NTB/Sumbawa exclusion (see open question).

## Predecessors and successors

This is a **sub-territory reporting unit** rather than a politically distinct sovereign entity. The outer islands remained part of Indonesia continuously. The parent polity chain is:

- **Parent (1949–1963):** [idn-1949-1963](idn-1949-1963.md) — all of Indonesia

This entry exists solely to prevent double-counting and the routing error that would arise from matching FAO sub-national "Other islands" data to the whole-Indonesia polity alongside the already-matched Java+Madura and Bali+Lombok entries.

## Decisions

- **Coverage gap**: Created to fix the third panel of the FAO 1952 Indonesia sub-national reporting structure. Issues `indonesia-java-madura-missing-polity` and `indonesia-bali-lombok-coverage-gap` were resolved by IDN-JVM and IDN-BLB; this polity resolves `indonesia-other-islands-missing-polity`.
- **No routing to IDN-1949-1963**: Even though the 8% area overstatement would be minor on its own, routing is prohibited because it would double-count with the already-matched JVM and BLB sub-national entries in the same dataset.
- **NTB/Sumbawa handling**: The BLB polygon uses the full West NTB province (IDN.20_1), which includes Sumbawa (~15,400 km²) as well as Lombok (~4,725 km²). Sumbawa belongs geographically to "Other islands" but is not separately extractable from the GADM province without sub-provincial processing. The OTH area estimate of ~1,349,065 km² therefore slightly understates the true "Other islands" territory by ~15,400 km² (Sumbawa). This is a ~0.9% discrepancy and does not materially affect data routing.

### oq-sumbawa-double-attribution

Sumbawa island (~15,400 km²) is part of West Nusa Tenggara province (IDN.20_1), which BLB uses as its polygon proxy. Sumbawa therefore falls in the BLB polygon but belongs geographically and agriculturally to "Other islands". The area discrepancy is ~0.9% and unlikely to affect data values materially, but if sub-provincial polygon extraction is performed for BLB (to limit it to Lombok only), OTH should be updated to add the Sumbawa portion of NTB.

### oq-fao-reporting-years

The exact year range for which "Indonesia Other islands" appears as a distinct FAO label should be confirmed against the raw FAO 1952 source file. The 15 currently-unmatched rows span 1949–1951; if the label also appears in earlier colonial-period FAO data (pre-1949), a predecessor entry may be warranted.
