# French Polynesia

## Summary

Draft WHEP row for French Polynesia, added to cover FAOSTAT/FABIO reporting code PYF. The row uses a current GADM 4.1 adm0 polygon as a proxy for the full WHEP period.

## Territorial Extent

- **Polygon source:** GADM 4.1 adm0 (`GID_0 = PYF`).
- **Proxy note:** The current polygon is used for the whole 1800-2025 row pending historical boundary review.

## Verification notes

- **The modern GADM proxy is defensible for the early 20th century**
  (2026-07-24). French Polynesia's constituent archipelagos (Society,
  Marquesas, Tuamotu-Gambier, Austral) were consolidated under a single
  colonial administration — the *Établissements français de l'Océanie* — by
  **1903**, and the territory has not changed since. So the present-day
  polygon stands in acceptably for the 1909-1938 reporting territory. It does
  **not** stand in for the 19th century: French acquisition of the island
  groups was progressive (Marquesas 1842, Tahiti protectorate 1842 and
  annexation 1880, Leeward Islands 1888, Australs 1900), so no assertion
  spanning those decades can be `verified_equal` against this row.

- **"Makatea" carries the territory's entire phosphate output.** Makatea is a
  single ~24 km² island, but it was the *sole* phosphate-mining site in French
  Oceania (worked 1908-1966 by the Compagnie Française des Phosphates de
  l'Océanie), so no other sub-unit shares that reporting and routing the label
  here is correct rather than a sub-territory fold-up.

- **Open: a possible unit-basis discrepancy in the FAO phosphate series.**
  FAO 1952 reports a median ~92,000 tonnes for Makatea 1949-1951, while the US
  Bureau of Mines (*Minerals Yearbook* 1954) gives ~247,000 and ~252,000 long
  tons of exports for 1950 and 1951 — a ~2.5-3× gap. Most likely FAO is on a
  P₂O₅-nutrient-equivalent basis rather than gross ore weight. **Not yet
  established:** check other `fao1952` phosphate-rock entries before treating
  this as a settled convention.

- **Still to do:** this page is a CSV-derived stub (flagged
  `page_inadequate`) and the 1800 start year predates any French presence in
  the islands; the row should be reviewed for splitting or re-dating.
