# Lozi Kingdom (Barotseland)

## Summary

The Lozi Kingdom (also Barotse Kingdom or Barotseland) was a pre-colonial state in the upper Zambezi floodplain in western Zambia, ruled by the Litunga paramount chief. Founded in the 17th century as the Luyana Kingdom, it was temporarily conquered by the Kololo (1838–1864) before the Lozi reasserted control under Sipopa. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1890, when King Lewanika signed the Lochner Concession with Cecil Rhodes's British South Africa Company (BSAC), granting mineral rights and accepting a British protectorate. Biger notes that "the central part of Africa remained free of European occupation until the 1880s." The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). The Lozi Kingdom centred on the Barotse Floodplain (upper Zambezi), covering approximately 350,000 km² at its maximum, including modern Western Province of Zambia. The kingdom exercised suzerainty over tributary peoples across a vast area including parts of modern Namibia (Caprivi), Botswana, and Zimbabwe.

## Open questions

### oq-lochner-concession

The end year 1890 is the date of the Lochner Concession. However, the concession was later disputed (Lewanika claimed he did not understand its full import), and effective BSAC administration was not established until the mid-1890s. Whether 1890 or c. 1897 is the better WHEP end year needs sourcing.

### oq-kololo-interlude

The Kololo conquered the Lozi c. 1838 and were overthrown 1864. This internal dynastic rupture is not reflected in WHEP (the row runs continuously from 1800). If the Kololo occupation constituted a territorial change (it did not — same territory, different ruling dynasty), the single continuous row is appropriate under WHEP rules. If not, an annotation would help clarify.

### oq-chain-gap

Same chain gap as BMB and KZM: the BSAC (1890–1924) and Northern Rhodesia (1924–1964) periods are not represented as separate WHEP rows.
