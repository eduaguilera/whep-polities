# Segovia (province of Spain)

## Summary

Segovia is one of Spain's fifty provinces, created in the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform that replaced the country's older kingdoms and intendencias with a uniform administrative grid. It sits in the north of the modern Autonomous Community of Castile and León, on the northern slope of the Sistema Central mountains, with its capital at the city of Segovia. This entry is `type: subnational` because Segovia was never a sovereign state — it is, and has always been, a second-tier administrative division of Spain — but it is tracked as its own polity row because economic and demographic statistics (mitchell-style historical series and modern sources spanning 1865-2021) are published at the province level, distinct from both the national Spain total and from neighboring provinces such as Ávila, Valladolid, or Madrid. The span 1833-2025 covers the entire life of the province as a stable administrative unit: it begins at the 1833 creation date (the default start rule for Spanish provinces in this dataset, since Segovia is not one of the Canary Islands departure cases) and runs open to 2025, since the province is still current and unchanged in form.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data keyed to unit id ESP-ES416 (NUTS-3 code, Segovia province, Spain), spanning 1865-2021, source-tagged in the routing decision without an explicit row count but described as fitting the country's per-province ingestion pattern already applied to six sibling provinces. Before this entry, statistics for Segovia had no dedicated subnational polity to route to: the only ESP-family rows available were the national ESP-1800-2025 row (roughly 50x too large in area and population to represent a single province) and the six unrelated sibling provinces (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra), none of which cover Segovia's territory. Routing Segovia-specific data to the national row would have silently inflated or diluted province-level series against the wrong denominator. The entity's distinctness is confirmed by the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform itself, which created all fifty Spanish provinces — including Segovia — as simultaneous, formally distinct administrative and statistical units still in use by INE today under cpro code 40; Spanish provincial statistics have been published separately by province, including Segovia, since the reform, which is why a routing_reasoning of "sibling units for this country have consistently been create_new except when they matched an existing named polity" applies directly here.

This is a widely cited figure for the modern province's extent and is stated here as background locating the territory, not as a measurement of the (currently absent) attached geometry — no `area_km2_measured` figure is being asserted for this row since no polygon is attached.

## Predecessors and successors

Segovia has no predecessor polity in this table: it was created directly by the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform, which carved Spain's fifty modern provinces largely out of the old Ancien Régime kingdoms, corregimientos, and intendencias without any of those prior units being separately tracked here as ESP subnational rows. There is likewise no successor: Segovia remains a current province of Spain, administratively unchanged in kind (part of the Autonomous Community of Castile and León since 1983) through the open end_year of 2025.

## Sourced claims

- Segovia province was established, along with the other 49 Spanish provinces, by the royal decree of 30 November 1833 promoted by Secretary of State Javier de Burgos, replacing the prior patchwork of kingdoms and intendencias with a uniform provincial grid that has persisted with only minor boundary adjustments to the present day.
- Segovia's INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadística) province code is 40 (cpro=40), the identifier the routing decision uses to select feature id '40' from the mapspain-ign source; INE assigns this code consistently across its province-level statistical products.
- Since 1983, Segovia has been one of nine provinces of the Autonomous Community of Castile and León, per the 1983 Statute of Autonomy of Castile and León, without any change to the province's own external boundary.

### oq-boundary-vintage

**Provincial boundary vintage between 1833 and today is asserted, not checked against known municipal transfers**

polygon_reasoning claims province boundaries from 1833 to today are essentially unchanged for Segovia, which is the standard assumption for the 1833 Javier de Burgos provinces generally, but Spanish provincial boundaries have had minor municipal-level adjustments over nearly two centuries (boundary disputes, municipal mergers crossing provincial lines). No specific check was done for Segovia's boundary against, e.g., 19th-century provincial maps or the Diccionario geográfico-estadístico-histórico. Given the whole span is carried by one polygon reused across 192 years, even a small transferred municipality would matter more than usual to the area figure once the geometry is actually fetched.
