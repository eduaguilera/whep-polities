# Nuevo León

## Summary

Nuevo León is a constituent state of Mexico in the country's northeast, bordering Texas (USA) and Tamaulipas, Coahuila, San Luis Potosí, and Zacatecas, with its capital at Monterrey. This entry captures it as a `subnational` unit within the Mexican federation, spanning 1824 (when it was named among the original states admitted under the 1824 Constitution establishing the Federated Republic of Mexico) through to the present (2025, open end). The `type` is `subnational` rather than `national` because Nuevo León never held sovereign statehood of its own: it has always been a federated unit inside Mexico, first inside the pre-1848 Mexican republic and then inside the Mexico that emerged from the territorial losses of the Mexican-American War. No prior polity row in this database represented Nuevo León specifically — only the two national MEX rows (MEX-1800-1848 and MEX-1848-2025) existed as candidates before this entry was created, meaning any state-level statistical series for Nuevo León would previously have had nowhere finer-grained to be routed than the whole of Mexico.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Nuevo León as a distinct subnational reporting unit within Mexico, addressing the gap that only two national-level MEX rows (MEX-1800-1848, spanning the Federated Republic of Mexico to 1848, and MEX-1848-2025, spanning post-Mexican-American-War Mexico to the present) existed as candidates for any Nuevo León-specific data before this page was created. Any state-level statistic for Nuevo León — population, production, administrative reporting — would previously have had no finer routing target than the whole of Mexico, which is territorially and administratively far broader than the state itself (Mexico's national territory is roughly 1.96 million km2 against Nuevo León's ~64,220 km2, a ratio of about 30:1). That mismatch is itself the case for creating this row: routing Nuevo León-specific data to a national total silently dilutes a state-level figure into a country-level one. Historical confirmation that Nuevo León is a distinct, persistent political entity rather than a shifting administrative label comes from its status as one of the states explicitly named in the 1824 Constitution of the United Mexican States, and its continuous listing as a constituent state through every subsequent Mexican federal constitution and state-admission table since. No polygon data or valued statistical rows are cited as the trigger for this specific entry in the routing decision; the routing_reasoning instead frames this as a structural gap-filling row for the Mexican federal system, created ahead of, rather than in response to, a specific dataset needing to be re-routed away from the national MEX total.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct source family is `gadm-4.1-adm1` (already registered in scripts/sources.yaml with id_column GID_1), and GADM does publish a Nuevo León feature (GID_1 'MEX.19_1'), but the copy of gadm41_adm1.gpkg present in this repository's data/geodata is a curated subset covering only 81 countries, and Mexico is not among them — a direct on-disk lookup for MEX returns zero features. This is the `registered_source_unfetched` case: the route exists and the slug is known, but execution requires fetching the full global GADM adm1 layer (or a Mexico-specific export) rather than constructing a substitute from other registered sources, since neither cshapes-2.0 nor cliopatria carry state-level Mexican subdivisions to union or difference against.

**Territory description:** Nuevo León occupies Mexico's northeastern corner, a wedge-shaped state running from the semi-arid Monterrey metropolitan basin at its southern/central core up to a narrow northern extension that reaches the Texas border along the Rio Grande. Its modern extent is approximately 64,220 km2 (a figure drawn from standard modern references for the current state boundary, not measured from any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached). A reader can locate it on a modern map as the state containing Monterrey, immediately south of Texas and sharing its eastern edge with Tamaulipas, its western and southern edges with Coahuila, San Luis Potosí, and a short stretch with Zacatecas. The state's exact boundary shifted over the 19th century as neighboring territories were reorganized (including the 1848 loss of Mexico's far northern territories and later boundary adjustments with Coahuila and Tamaulipas), so the modern ~64,220 km2 figure should not be read as necessarily accurate for the 1824-1848 portion of this entry's span.

## Predecessors and successors

This entry has no predecessor or successor polity row: Nuevo León is a continuously existing federal state from its 1824 constitutional founding through to 2025, with no change of statehood, absorption, or split recorded in this database. It sits instead beneath two successive national containers, MEX-1800-1848 (the pre-Mexican-American War Mexican state) and MEX-1848-2025 (Mexico after the 1848 territorial reorganization), reflecting that the containing national polity's own row changed while Nuevo León's own status as a constituent state did not.

## Sourced claims

- The 1824 Constitution of the United Mexican States (Constitución Federal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos, 4 October 1824) named Nuevo León among the original states of the federation, distinct from federal territories that only later converted to statehood.
- GADM v4.1's administrative level-1 layer for Mexico assigns Nuevo León the feature identifier GID_1 'MEX.19_1', representing the state's present-day boundary as used for modern administrative and statistical reporting.

## Decisions

### d-code-abbreviation

**Used two-letter abbreviation NL rather than full state name in the code**

The dominant pattern for the 57 <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> codes uses short subunit abbreviations (e.g. ESP-AS, ESP-CA, DZA-CVD), so MEX-NL-1824-2025 follows precedent rather than spelling out 'NUEVOLEON'. Since MEX has zero existing subnational codes, there is no in-country precedent to match, so I followed the cross-country dominant convention instead of inventing a new style. NL is the standard postal/ISO 3166-2 abbreviation for Nuevo León (MX-NLE uses NL as the common short form), keeping the code compact and readable.

### d-start-year-1824

**Start year set to 1824 despite modern boundaries only stabilizing later**

The routing decision fixes start_year at 1824 because Nuevo León was named among the original states in the 1824 Constitution, and the system floor for this dataset also sits at 1824. I did not override this to a later 'boundary stabilization' date (e.g. 1848 territorial reorganization) because no specific reorganization date was given as more defensible than the constitutional founding date, and doing so would contradict the explicit routing_reasoning input. The polygon vintage mismatch this creates (modern GADM shape applied across a span where the state's actual boundary evolved) is instead flagged via polygon_status and the open question below, rather than by truncating the span.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-mexico-not-fetched

**GADM adm1 polygon for Nuevo León is not present in the local GeoPackage subset**

The registered source gadm-4.1-adm1 (scripts/sources.yaml, id_column GID_1) is the correct polygon family for a modern Mexican state, and GADM does publish a Nuevo León feature (GID_1 'MEX.19_1'), but the on-disk data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Mexico (0 features found for MEX). Fetching the full global GADM adm1 layer, or a Mexico-specific slice, would resolve this and let polygon_status move from unassigned to assigned with polygon_feature_id set to MEX.19_1. Until then this entry carries no geometry, which also means no polygon_area_km2 is available to sanity-check against the roughly 64,220 km2 a reader would expect for the modern state of Nuevo León.

### oq-boundary-vintage-1824-1848

**Whether a single modern polygon can validly represent the 1824-1848 span at all**

Even once GADM adm1 is fetched, the feature it provides reflects present-day Nuevo León, whose borders were reshaped by 19th-century state reorganizations (including adjustments after the loss of Texas and the 1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo redrew the wider northern Mexican frontier this state bordered). Applying a single present-day polygon uniformly back to 1824 risks materially overstating or misplacing the state's actual early-19th-century extent, particularly its northern and eastern limits. This entry does not attempt to split the span into a pre-1848 and post-1848 polygon period, unlike the container edges which do split at 1848; whether the polygon should be similarly split, or whether a period-specific historical map source exists for early Nuevo León, is unresolved.

### oq-original-1824-status-confirmation

**Whether Nuevo León's designation as one of the original 1824 states needs independent confirmation**

The routing_reasoning itself flags uncertainty here: it states this should be verified against a Mexican state-admission table rather than accepted at face value. Nuevo León appears in most standard accounts of the 1824 Constitution's nineteen founding states, but this page does not cite a specific primary or scholarly source fixing 1824 (versus, for example, a slightly later formal admission date if Nuevo León was briefly combined with Coahuila under some administrative arrangements in this period, as happened later in the 1850s-1860s Nuevo León y Coahuila union). This should be checked against a dedicated Mexican federal entity admission timeline before the start_year is treated as fully settled.
