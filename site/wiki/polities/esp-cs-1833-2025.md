# Castellón/Castelló (province of Spain)

## Summary

Castellón (Valencian: Castelló) is one of the 50 provinces created in the Javier de Burgos territorial reorganization of 30 November 1833, carved out of the old Kingdom of Valencia territory together with the provinces of Valencia and Alicante. It occupies the northern third of the modern Valencian Community, on Spain's Mediterranean coast between Valencia province to the south and Tarragona/Teruel to the north and west. Its provincial capital is the city of Castellón de la Plana. This entry is `type: subnational` because it is a province within the sovereign Spanish state, not an independent polity — sovereignty over this territory belonged to Spain throughout 1833-2025, and the entry exists purely to carry statistics reported at the province level, distinct from both the national ESP total and neighbouring provinces such as Valencia (ESP-V) or Tarragona (ESP-T). The 1833 reform boundaries for Castellón have not been subject to any subsequent major reorganization (no province splits, mergers, or cross-border transfers are recorded for it), so the province is treated as a single, territorially stable unit across the full 1833-2025 span.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures province-level statistical rows for Castellón (NUTS/administrative unit ES522) that were not matched to any existing polity: the national row ESP-1800-2025 represents the whole of Spain and cannot stand in for a single province's figures, and neighbouring province entries such as ESP-V (Valencia) or ESP-CO (Córdoba, an unrelated southern province despite the similar three-letter feel) do not cover this territory. Prior to this pipeline run, Castellón-level data had no dedicated polity row at all — it was either left unmatched or folded incorrectly into the national total, which would double-count or misattribute any series that also reports Spain-wide figures. Castellón's status as a distinct administrative and statistical unit is confirmed by its inclusion as one of the 50 provinces instituted in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, an administrative division still in force today and used by INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadística) and IGN as the standard unit for provincial-level Spanish statistics and cartography. This entry follows the same create_new pattern already applied to sibling Spanish provinces in this batch (e.g. ESP-17, ESP-A, ESP-AB), all of which lacked pre-existing candidates for the same structural reason.

**Territory description:** Castellón province occupies the northern part of the Valencian Community on Spain's Mediterranean (Costa del Azahar) coast, bordered by Tarragona and Teruel to the north/northwest, Valencia province to the south, and the Mediterranean Sea to the east. Its modern area is approximately 6,632 km², comprising the coastal plain around the capital Castellón de la Plana and the inland mountainous comarcas (Alt Maestrat, Els Ports, Alto Palancia) extending toward the Iberian System. This is the modern INE-published provincial area, not a measurement of any attached geometry, since no polygon is yet attached to this entry. The province's boundaries as fixed in 1833 are understood to have persisted essentially unchanged through 2025.
