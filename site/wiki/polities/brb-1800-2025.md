# Barbados

## Summary

Single WHEP row for Barbados spanning the whole 1800-2025 window, added to cover FAOSTAT/FABIO reporting code BRB. **309 layer-B rows** route here (`pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/territory_basis.csv`), overwhelmingly sugarcane and its derivatives. At **435 km²** it is among the smallest polities in the database. The row is undivided because Barbados is a single island, continuously British from 1627 to 1966, with no land boundary and no territorial change in the span — an unusually clean case, and the reason a 226-year row with a modern polygon is defensible here.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** GADM 4.1 adm0 feature `GID_0 = BRB`, carried as a **proxy** (`polygon_status: proxy`). Measured in an equal-area CRS (ESRI:54034) from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`: **435 km²**. The conventional land area is 430-432 km²; the ~1% excess is coastline generalisation.

**Barbados is the strongest possible case for a constant-extent row, for a reason no other Caribbean polity in this database shares.** It is a **single island** — not an island group — so there is not even the wholesale-island-addition mechanism by which Grenada's or Fiji's extent could in principle have changed. It has no land boundary with any polity. And it was under uninterrupted British rule from 1627 until independence in 1966, so unlike most of the Lesser Antilles it never changed hands between European powers during the Napoleonic reshuffles that open this row's window. No source on disk asserts any territorial change, and there is no physical mechanism by which one could have occurred.

**Note that Biger has no Barbados entry.** The `### barbados` heading does not exist in `wiki/sources/biger-1995.md`, so unlike Grenada or Fiji this page cannot cite even a "no land boundaries" line. That absence is recorded rather than worked around: the constant-extent claim here rests on the geographic argument above plus COW's dating, not on a boundary-encyclopaedia entry.

**Territorial changes inside 1800-2025: none. The row should not be split.** The events a reader might expect as row boundaries, and why each is not one:

- **1834-1838** — abolition of slavery and the apprenticeship period. Transforms the labour system behind this row's sugar data completely, but is not a territorial event. Worth flagging as a *series* discontinuity for anyone using the production data across it.
- **1958-1962** — membership of the West Indies Federation, whose capital was in Trinidad and in which Barbados was a leading member. As with Grenada this is a *reporting-scope* risk rather than a territorial one; see [oq-brb-west-indies-federation](#oq-brb-west-indies-federation).
- **1966-11-30** — independence. A sovereignty transfer over an unchanged territory, which does not open a new row under the WHEP polity definition ([log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition)). Dated to the day by COW's `statelist2024.csv` and, independently, by `decolonization_events.csv`.
- **2021-11-30** — Barbados becomes a republic. Constitutional; the territory is unchanged and the same polity continues to report.

**Why CShapes is not used, and why it could never help.** Both of CShapes' documented thresholds exclude Barbados independently. Dependencies are restricted "to those units with a population greater than 250,000 during the sample period" ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)) — Barbados' population has never reached that — so colonial Barbados is outside CShapes' dependency list, and smaller dependencies are silently excluded. And border changes are coded only above 100 × 100 km ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)), which is 23 times the island's total area. So no CShapes observation can bear on this row in either direction, and "CShapes records no change here" would be a vacuous statement rather than corroboration — a caveat the verification pipeline should apply to any assertion about this polity.

## Predecessors and successors

## Sourced claims

COW therefore covers only 59 of this row's 226 years, and the colonial 1800-1966 period is absent by design: entities that do not meet COW's recognition and population criteria never appear ([cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion), and limitation 1 of [cow-state-system-v2024 §known-limitations](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#known-limitations)). The frontmatter `cow: 53` is a code assignment for the modern state, not a membership claim spanning the row.
- `decolonization_events.csv` records Barbados independence from Britain on 1966-11-30 — the same day COW gives, from an independently compiled file.
- CShapes excludes dependencies below 250,000 population ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)) and territorial transfers below 100 × 100 km ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)). Barbados fails both by wide margins, which is why this row uses GADM and why CShapes silence about it is uninformative.
- Biger 1995 has **no Barbados entry** — verified by the absence of a `### barbados` heading in `wiki/sources/biger-1995.md`. Stated so that a future pass does not spend effort looking for one, and so that the absence of a Biger citation on this page is not mistaken for an omission.

## Open questions

### oq-brb-west-indies-federation

Were Barbados' 1958-1962 returns reported separately, or folded into a West Indies Federation aggregate? Barbados was a leading Federation member, which makes the fold-up risk higher for it than for the smaller members. This is the only identified threat to this row's 309 data rows, and it is a label question rather than a territorial one. Checkable by looking for a Federation-scoped label in `data/final/source_label_lexicon.csv` and for a break in the Barbadian sugar series across 1958 and 1962.
