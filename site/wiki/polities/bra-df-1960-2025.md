# Distrito Federal (Brasília)

## Summary

The Distrito Federal (Brasília) is Brazil's federal district, created in 1960 when the national capital was relocated inland from Rio de Janeiro to the purpose-built city of Brasília. It is `type: subnational` because it is a first-order administrative division of the Brazilian federation (equivalent in status to a state, but without full statehood), never a sovereign entity in its own right -- national sovereignty rests continuously with Brazil (BRA-1909-2025) throughout this entry's span. The territory was excised from the state of Goiás specifically to house the new seat of federal government, following the constitutional mandate (dating back to 1891) to relocate the capital away from the coast; construction under President Juscelino Kubitschek culminated in the 21 April 1960 inauguration. This is a distinct place from the pre-1960 'Distrito Federal', which was not a separate territory carved from a state but rather the federal-capital status held by the city of Rio de Janeiro itself from 1889 until the 1960 transfer -- the two share a label but not a territory, which is why the routing decision this page implements treats them as two separate polities rather than one continuous entry.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Brazilian data (country: Brazil, iso3 BRA) reported under the admin-unit label 'Distrito Federal' for the years following the 1960 capital relocation to Brasília -- the routing decision does not state the exact row count or source dataset, since that detail lives in the upstream admin_unit extract rather than in the verdict passed to this page, and is flagged as an open question. Before this entry existed, any 'Distrito Federal' rows risked being matched either to the national BRA-1909-2025 row (losing the sub-national reporting granularity entirely) or, worse, conflated with the pre-1960 Distrito Federal (Rio de Janeiro), which is a different city and a different territory sharing only the administrative label. Both would be wrong: the routing reasoning found no name match or boundary-feature match tying this post-1960 data to any existing candidate polity, and the country convention's start-rule departures list does not cover this unit because its history is not a simple state carve-out but a label reused for two unrelated territories. What confirms this entity is distinct is straightforward administrative history rather than a specialist trading-polity source like Federico-Tena: the Brazilian constitution's own capital-relocation mandate and the 21 April 1960 transfer of federal-district status from Rio de Janeiro to a newly built, newly bounded territory around Brasília, carved out of Goiás -- a change in both government seat and physical territory occurring in the same year, which no existing polity row represents.

**What the panel's pre-1960 years are (2026-09-24).** The subnational panel's BRA-DISTRITOFEDERAL unit reports 1900-2023, and its land-use classes sum to **5,921 km2 in every one of those years** -- the extent of this polity (5,761 km2 in the attached polygon), not of Rio de Janeiro's old federal district (roughly 1,200 km2, BRA-DFRJ-1900-1959). Its 1900-1959 values are therefore a reconstruction of the Brasilia district's ground backwards in time, and they route here with `disposition: back_cast` rather than to the Rio polity, which they had been sent to by name alone. This entry's own span still begins in 1960, when the territory was created.

**Territory description.** The modern Distrito Federal is a roughly circular/polygonal administrative unit in central Brazil's Central-West region, entirely surrounding the city of Brasília, and bordered on all sides by the state of Goiás (with a short border touching Minas Gerais to the southeast in some administrative reckonings, though in practice it is a Goiás enclave). It sits on the Planalto Central at roughly 15.5-16.1°S, 47.3-48.3°W. Its area has been essentially stable since 1960 at approximately **5,760 km2** (roughly the size of Trinidad and Tobago, or about a fifth the size of the state of Sergipe) -- this figure is drawn from published IBGE territorial-area statistics for the unit, not measured from any geometry attached to this entry, since no geometry is attached. The boundary has not undergone the dramatic changes seen in some subnational units (no significant annexation or partition since 1960), which is why, once geobr-ibge is fetched, a single modern-vintage polygon should suffice for the whole 1960-2025 span without the proxy-rejection concerns that apply to units like post-Trianon Hungary. *(Superseded 2026-09-24: a polygon is now attached — `geobr-ibge` feature `DF` (feature year 2020), 5,761 km² as shipped; see the Polygon status line under Territorial extent.)*

## Predecessors and successors

No successor is recorded either, since the Distrito Federal (Brasília) continues to exist today without any administrative change, so the entry runs to the table's open end year 2025 (decision d-end-year-2025).

## Sourced claims

- Brazil's national capital relocated from Rio de Janeiro to the newly constructed city of Brasília on 21 April 1960, and the Distrito Federal was recreated at that time as the federal district surrounding the new capital, carved from the state of Goiás.
- IBGE (Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística) treats the Distrito Federal as a state-equivalent administrative unit and digitises its boundary at admin-1 level in the geobr package (id_column abbrev_state = 'DF'), the source this entry's polygon route points to but has not yet fetched locally.

## Decisions

### d-code-iso3-start-end

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern**

BRA has no existing subnational rows, so this entry sets the country's precedent. I chose BRA-DF-1960-2023 (renamed BRA-DF-1960-2025 on 2026-09-24; see below) rather than a bespoke code like the ALK/HYD/RYU examples, because 'Distrito Federal' is a standing administrative division type (a federal district), not a one-off historical territory with a proper name of its own the way Alaska Territory or Ryukyu are. DF is also IBGE's own two-letter abbreviation for the unit (abbrev_state column in geobr-ibge), so using it as the subunit code keeps this row consistent with how a future geobr-ibge fetch would key the feature.

### d-end-year-2025

**Re-spanned from 1960-2023 to 1960-2025 (2026-09-24)**

The entry was first written with end_year 2023, following the routing verdict, and this page itself flagged that as probably the extract's last data year rather than an administrative change. It is: the panel's BRA-DISTRITOFEDERAL series ends in 2023 because the extract does, and the Distrito Federal has not been abolished or restructured. Every other Brazilian state in the table runs to the open convention year 2025, as does the container BRA-1909-2025, so the code was changed from BRA-DF-1960-2023 to BRA-DF-1960-2025 and the containment edge extended with it. The aliases now route the unit's 1960-2023 data years here as observed, and 1900-1959 as `back_cast` (see above).
