# Bolzano/South Tyrol (province of Italy)

## Summary

Bolzano/South Tyrol (Italian: provincia di Bolzano; German: Südtirol) is the northernmost province of Italy, corresponding to the historic Austrian crownland territory around the city of Bozen/Bolzano. Before 1919 it was part of the County of Tyrol within Austria-Hungary; the Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye (1919) transferred it, together with Trentino, to Italy as part of the post-WWI settlement, over the Brenner Pass line drawn to give Italy a defensible Alpine frontier. The territory was administered as part of the region Venezia Tridentina/Trentino-Alto Adige from the Italian annexation, formalized as the modern province of Bolzano in 1927, and given increasing autonomy in 1948 and especially 1972, when the province (rather than the region) became the primary locus of self-government for the German-speaking majority. This is a `type: subnational` entry, not a national one: Italy held sovereignty throughout 1919-2025, and this row exists purely to carry a reporting territory that is administratively distinct from national Italian totals in the statistical sources that motivate it, following the same convention already applied to other Trentino-Alto Adige/South Tyrol departure-group territories under the country's routing convention (start year 1919, not the general Italian container's own earlier start).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures unit ITA-ITH1 (admin code ITH1, corresponding to the province of Bolzano) referenced in the routing decision for Italy's country-convention pipeline. The data previously had no distinct polity to route to: only the broad national ITA row existed, and a province is not its country, so `match_existing` against Italy itself was ruled out as a category error (a provincial reporting unit cannot be represented by a national polygon/administration without losing the distinction the source itself is making). The country convention singles out Trentino-Alto Adige/South Tyrol territories as a departure group with start_year 1919 (the year of annexation from Austria-Hungary), overriding the general default of following the earliest available container span, because the pre-1919 territory was administratively Austrian (Kreis Bozen), not Italian, and treating it as Italian before 1919 would misstate sovereignty. What confirms this entity is a genuinely distinct reporting/administrative unit, rather than a restatement of national Italy: it is a real, still-existing modern Italian province (Autonomous Province of Bolzano/South Tyrol) with its own elected government, its own statistical office (ASTAT), and — since the 1972 autonomy statute — devolved competencies that make it common practice for statistical series (population, agriculture, production) to be reported at province level for this specific territory rather than folded into regional or national Italian figures.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon feature identified in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct registered source is `gadm-4.1-adm1` (GADM admin-1 boundaries would include the modern Province of Bolzano/South Tyrol as a distinct feature), but the GADM 4.1 admin-1 file currently present in this repository's local data is a curated subset covering only 81 countries, and Italy is not among them (0 ITA features on disk) even though GADM's global release does digitize Italian provinces. This is therefore a `registered_source_unfetched` case: the slug is recorded as the source of record, but `polygon_feature_id` is left null pending either replacing the local GADM extract with full/global coverage or fetching the Italy admin-1 layer directly, after which the Bolzano feature (GADM code GID_1 for Bolzano) can be identified and attached. **Territory:** the modern Province of Bolzano/South Tyrol, Italy's northernmost province, bordering Austria and Switzerland, comprising the upper Adige/Etsch valley and its side valleys (Val Gardena, Val Pusteria, Val Venosta) around the provincial capital Bolzano/Bozen. Its present-day area is approximately 7,400 km2. Applying this present-day boundary across the full 1919-2025 span is a stated approximation: the modern provincial boundary was only formalized in 1927 (as a separate province split from the earlier joint Venezia Tridentina administration) and the territory's competencies changed substantially with the 1948 and 1972 autonomy statutes, though the geographic boundary itself has been stable since the 1919 annexation border was drawn.

## Sourced claims

- The Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye (1919) transferred South Tyrol from Austria to Italy, fixing the Brenner Pass as the new border, per standard post-WWI treaty accounts.
- The province of Bolzano was formally constituted as a separate provincial administrative unit in 1927, having previously been administered jointly with Trento under Venezia Tridentina; its modern autonomous-province status dates to the 1972 Second Statute of Autonomy for Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1919-override

**Start year set to 1919 per country-convention departure group, not earliest container**

The generic Italian country convention would set start_year to the earliest available container span for ITA, but Trentino-Alto Adige/South Tyrol territories are an explicit departure group in that convention, starting instead at 1919 (the year of annexation from Austria-Hungary). This entry follows that override rather than the default rule, matching the routing decision's own reasoning and matching how a sibling entry for Trento (ITA-ITH3) would be expected to be handled if/when created.

### d-code-pattern-subunit

**Followed dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern rather than a bespoke code**

Bolzano/South Tyrol is a real, still-existing administrative subdivision (a province) rather than a historical territory whose identity has since been dissolved or absorbed without a direct present-day successor (unlike e.g. Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State, which motivate the bespoke-code exception). The dominant 57-of-66 pattern (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end) was used, with subunit ITH1 taken from the routing decision's admin_name, giving ITA-ITH1-1919-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-italy-coverage

**Local GADM 4.1 admin-1 extract excludes Italy entirely**

The repository's local gadm-4.1-adm1 dataset is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Italy at all (0 ITA features), even though GADM's global release does digitize Italian provinces including Bolzano. Until that local extract is replaced with full/global GADM admin-1 coverage (or the Italy layer is fetched separately), no `polygon_feature_id` can be assigned here and `polygon_status` must remain `unassigned`. This same gap likely blocks every other Italian subnational entry (e.g. a future ITA-ITH3/Trento row), so it may be worth fixing once at the source level rather than per-entry.

### oq-bolzano-trento-joint-region

**Whether Bolzano should be modeled jointly with Trento as a Trentino-Alto Adige region row instead**

The routing decision itself flags this as unresolved: whether ITA-ITH1 (Bolzano) should be created standing alone, or jointly with ITA-ITH3 (Trento) as a single Trentino-Alto Adige region entry, especially for the 1919-1947 period before the two provinces had meaningfully separate self-government (autonomy powers before 1972 were largely exercised at the regional, not provincial, level). This entry takes the province-level view since that is the level at which modern statistical series (e.g. ASTAT) report, but if the underlying source data for the 1919-1947 window in fact only distinguishes at the region level, a merged region row may be more accurate for that sub-span.

### oq-boundary-vintage-1927-split

**Applying a present-day province boundary back to 1919 predates the 1927 provincial split**

The province of Bolzano as a distinct administrative unit was only created in 1927; from 1919-1927 the annexed territory was administered as the undivided Venezia Tridentina region alongside Trentino. Whether the geographic extent of what became the Bolzano province was already distinguishable as a reporting unit in 1919-1927 data, or whether that period's data should instead route to a joint regional entry, is unresolved and depends on what the underlying data sources report for that window.
