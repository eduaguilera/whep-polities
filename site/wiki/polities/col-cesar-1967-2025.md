# Cesar (department of Colombia)

## Summary

Cesar is a department of the Republic of Colombia, located in the northeastern part of the country between the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the Serrania de Perija, with its capital at Valledupar. It was created in 1967 when the national government split it off from the then-larger Magdalena department, part of a broader mid-20th-century pattern of subdividing Colombia's oversized 19th-century departments into smaller administrative units as population and economic activity grew. This entry is typed `subnational` rather than `national` because Colombia (COL-1922-2025) has held continuous sovereignty over this territory throughout; Cesar is a first-order administrative division within that sovereign state, never an independent or quasi-independent polity in its own right. The entry's start_year of 1967 marks the department's formal creation; its end_year of 2025 reflects that Cesar remains an active, current department of Colombia with no successor entity, consistent with the "still active" convention used elsewhere in this database for ongoing subnational units. Statistically, this entry exists to receive two distinct kinds of source rows: post-1967 data reported directly for the Cesar department as it has existed since its creation, and a large body of earlier (1915-1966) data that a source has back-cast or interpolated onto Cesar's present-day territory before the department formally existed -- the latter reflects a reconstruction convention in the source data rather than any claim that "Cesar" was administratively meaningful before 1967.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures source-data rows currently routed to the Colombia national polity (COL-1922-2025) that are actually reported for the Cesar department specifically: a directly-attributable span from the department's 1967 creation through 2023, plus a much larger pre-1967 span (1915-1966) that is 98.4% scaled/interpolated_scaled -- a back-cast reconstruction of a Cesar-shaped series onto the years before the department existed. These rows were previously matched (implicitly, by lack of any Cesar-specific polity) to the national COL row, which conflates a sub-national administrative reporting unit with the whole country's total and cannot be right once a department-specific series exists in the source. What confirms Cesar is a distinct reporting/administrative entity is its own 1967 legislative creation as a first-order department split off from Magdalena under Colombia's unitary departmental system -- an administrative fact, not a trading-polity designation from a source like Federico-Tena (which does not track Colombian departments as separate trading units in this period). The routing decision's own caveats -- that the 1967 creation date is from general knowledge rather than a verified primary source, and that pre-1967 data might instead belong to an as-yet-uncreated Magdalena polity -- are carried forward here as open questions rather than resolved silently.

Why this entry exists: source data associates roughly two decades of rows (1967-2023 for the department's own directly attributable span, per the routing decision) plus a much larger body of 1915-1966 rows (98.4% flagged scaled or interpolated_scaled) with the label "Cesar" or its Colombian department code. These rows were previously unrouted or implicitly absorbed into the national COL-1922-2025 total, which is wrong for two reasons: first, Cesar's creation in 1967 makes it a distinct administrative and statistical reporting unit from that year forward, not interchangeable with all-Colombia totals; second, the pre-1967 rows are themselves evidence of a source deliberately reconstructing a Cesar-shaped series before the department existed (the back_cast pattern), which only makes sense if there is a Cesar-specific polity row to receive it. No historical trading-polity source such as Federico-Tena treats sub-national Colombian departments as separate trading entities in this period, since departments are ordinary internal administrative divisions rather than distinct customs or trading territories; the confirmation of distinctness here instead comes from the department's own 1967 legislative creation as a first-order administrative unit of the unitary Colombian state, and from the source data's own practice of tabulating a Cesar-specific series both before and after that date.

Territory description: Cesar department occupies the northeastern interior of Colombia, bordered by La Guajira and Magdalena to the north and west, Norte de Santander and Santander to the south, Bolivar to the southwest, and Venezuela to the east. Its terrain spans the Cesar River valley between the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the Serrania del Perija, with the departmental capital at Valledupar. Its present-day area is approximately 22,900 km2 (a widely cited figure for the modern department, not a measurement from any polygon attached to this entry, since none is yet assigned). This is a moderate-sized Colombian department, roughly comparable in area to a small European country such as Slovenia, situated well inland from the Caribbean coast that Magdalena department (its parent before 1967) retains.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-iso3-subunit-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

Chose COL-CESAR-1967-2025 over a bare COL-1967-2025 form. Cesar is a first-order administrative department, exactly the kind of unit the 57-row dominant pattern (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919) already covers, and it is not a historical territory with a proper name of its own the way ALK or HYD are -- it is one of Colombia's ordinary departments, distinguished from its siblings only by which one it is. Since Colombia sets its own precedent (0 existing subnational codes for COL), this entry establishes SUBUNIT as a short department abbreviation (CESAR), which is legible and matches the abbreviation style used for other countries' subunits.

### d-no-predecessor-for-pre1967-territory

**No predecessor polity is linked for the pre-1967 back-cast years**

The routing decision back-casts 1915-1966 scaled/interpolated data onto this polity even though Cesar did not exist as a department until 1967 -- that territory was part of Magdalena department before the split. No COL-MAGDALENA polity currently exists in the database to serve as a predecessor, and creating one is outside this entry's scope (a separate routing decision, per this pipeline's own note that pre-1967 data might instead belong on a Magdalena polity was not checked). Leaving predecessor empty rather than fabricating a link is preferred to an unverified cross-reference; this is flagged again in open_questions.

## Open questions

### oq-magdalena-predecessor-unresolved

**Should pre-1967 data instead route to a Magdalena department polity?**

The routing decision explicitly flags this as unchecked: whether the 1915-1966 back-cast data (98.4% scaled/interpolated_scaled) should instead be routed to a COL-MAGDALENA polity, since that is the department Cesar was split off from in 1967 and which actually existed on the ground during those years. No COL-MAGDALENA polity currently exists in the table. If one is created later, this entry's predecessor field should be updated to point to it, and the back_cast years may need re-routing entirely rather than being reconstructed onto Cesar's post-1967 territory. Until that is resolved, this entry silently absorbs territory it did not administratively cover for more than half its data span.

### oq-cesar-creation-date-unverified

**1967 creation date for Cesar department is unverified against a primary source**

The routing decision itself notes the 1967 creation date was taken from general historical knowledge of Colombian administrative divisions, not confirmed against an authoritative source document (e.g. the founding law/decree, or a DANE or IGAC administrative history). If the true creation year differs even by one or two years, both start_year here and the corresponding boundary of the back-cast window in the source data routing would need correction, and the code COL-CESAR-1967-2025 itself would need renaming since the years in the code must equal the frontmatter years.

### oq-cesar-boundary-stability-since-1967

**Whether Cesar's departmental boundary has remained unchanged since 1967**

The routing decision asserts vintage_risk is low because Cesar's boundary has supposedly been stable since 1967, making a present-day GADM polygon a good proxy for the full 1967-2025 span. This has not been checked against any documented history of Colombian departmental boundary changes (e.g. municipality transfers between Cesar and neighbouring La Guajira, Magdalena, Norte de Santander, or Bolivar departments over nearly six decades). A single undocumented boundary adjustment would mean the eventual GADM-derived polygon slightly misrepresents earlier years of this span.
