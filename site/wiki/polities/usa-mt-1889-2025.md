# Montana (state of the United States)

## Summary

This entry captures Montana as a constituent state of the United States, from its admission to the Union on November 8, 1889 through the present (end_year 2025, exclusive, as a still-current unit). Montana was organized as a U.S. territory in 1864, carved from portions of the Idaho and (later) Dakota territories, and achieved statehood in 1889 alongside three other states admitted that year (North Dakota, South Dakota, Washington). Type is set to `subnational` because Montana is a constituent unit of the sovereign United States, never itself an independent state — sovereignty throughout this span belongs to the USA national chain, and this row exists purely to carry state-level reporting data that the national polity is too coarse to represent. No existing polity in the database represented Montana by name or shared its iso3 subnational code, so this is a new creation under the default U.S. state admission rule rather than a departure case like Alaska, Hawaii, or the original thirteen colonies.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures state-level source data for Montana (country label likely "United States of America" or state-specific labels, iso3 USA, with years spanning at least 1873-1888 pre-statehood and continuing post-1889) that the agent-harness routing decision identified as having no existing matching polity — no name match and no iso3-sharing subnational candidate existed prior to this creation. Previously, any Montana-labeled rows would have fallen through to the national USA polity (USA-1867-1959 / USA-1959-2025), which aggregates all fifty states and is far too coarse for state-level statistical series (e.g., agricultural production, census figures) that a source reports specifically for Montana. That national-level match was wrong because it discards the sub-national reporting granularity the source actually provides. Montana's distinctness as a reporting and administrative unit is confirmed by its formal admission as a U.S. state in 1889, an act creating a discrete political and statistical entity recognized in federal census and agricultural reporting from that point forward — the same category of evidence used for sibling entries like USA-CALIFORNIA, decided under the same routing convention referenced in the routing_reasoning above.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.27_1`, assigned directly (not a proxy) because Montana is a real, currently-existing U.S. state with a single unambiguous GADM match and no documented external-boundary changes since 1889 admission. **Area:** approximately 380,831 km2 (measured from the attached GADM 4.1 geometry; this is a measurement of the polygon, not an independently sourced historical figure), consistent with widely cited modern reference figures for the state (~380,800 km2 / ~147,000 sq mi), the fourth-largest U.S. state by area. **Territory description:** the modern U.S. state of Montana, bordered by Idaho to the west/southwest, Wyoming to the south, North Dakota and South Dakota to the east, and the Canadian provinces of British Columbia, Alberta, and Saskatchewan to the north — readily locatable on any modern map as the state occupying the northern Rocky Mountains and northern Great Plains immediately south of the Canada-U.S. border between Idaho and North Dakota. No boundary adjustments are known to have occurred since 1889, so the modern GADM polygon is treated as representative of the entire 1889-2025 span rather than a period-specific proxy.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end code pattern**

Montana is a real, currently-existing U.S. state with no historical name of its own distinct from the modern state (unlike Alaska Territory or Hawaii's pre-statehood administration, which earned bespoke codes). It follows the 57-row dominant pattern: USA-MT-1889-2025. No departure from the default admission-year start rule was warranted since Montana is not one of the three documented exceptions (Alaska, Hawaii, original 13 colonies).

### d-container-two-edges

**Split the container into two USA eras to avoid a containment gap**

The polity table shows the U.S. national chain as two consecutive rows: USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025, split at Alaska/Hawaii statehood. A single container edge citing only USA-1959-2025 would leave 1889-1959 uncontained and fail the containment gate, so two edges were added, each scoped to its own container's span, jointly tiling 1889-2025 with no gap.

### d-polygon-assigned

**Assigned GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature directly rather than treating as proxy**

USA.27_1 is a direct, unambiguous 1:1 match to the modern state of Montana with stable historical boundaries since 1889 (no subsequent territorial changes), so polygon_status is set to assigned rather than proxy. The GADM boundary is present-day (c. 2020s) but Montana's external boundary has not moved since admission, so no material vintage risk exists, unlike states carved from larger unorganized territories after admission.

## Open questions

### oq-territory-era-data

**Whether pre-1889 Montana Territory data (1873-1888) should route here or to a separate territory entry**

The routing decision notes the source data appears to include years 1873-1888, which predate Montana's 1889 statehood and instead fall within the Montana Territory era (organized 1864). This page currently back-casts that data onto the 1889-2025 state polity per country convention, but Montana Territory had different (larger, since portions were later split into Wyoming and parts of the Dakotas by 1868) boundaries than the modern state. If Montana Territory data proves substantial or boundary-sensitive, a separate MT-TERR or USA-MT-TERR predecessor entry with its own polygon may be warranted rather than folding it into the modern-boundary state row.

### oq-gadm-vintage

**GADM 4.1 boundary vintage against any 19th/early-20th century Montana boundary adjustments**

Montana's external state boundary has been treated as stable since 1889 admission for this page, but this has not been independently verified against a historical GIS source (e.g., Newberry Library Atlas of Historical County Boundaries) — only asserted from general knowledge that no state-level boundary changes are commonly documented for Montana post-admission. Any minor surveyed boundary corrections (e.g., with Idaho or the Dakotas) in the early statehood period would not be reflected, though such adjustments are expected to be immaterial at the ~380,831 km2 scale of the whole state.
