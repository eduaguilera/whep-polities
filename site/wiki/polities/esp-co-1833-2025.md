# A Coruña (province of Spain)

## Summary

A Coruña is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today also one of the four provinces making up the Galicia autonomous community in the northwestern corner of the Iberian Peninsula. It takes its name from its capital, the port city of A Coruña, and occupies Galicia's northern and western coastal strip, bordered by Lugo province to the east, Pontevedra to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the north and west. This entry is type: subnational because A Coruña is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated A Coruña separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES111, for over a century and a half. The row ESP-CO-1833-2025 already existed in the polities database (created in the same Spanish-provinces convention pass that produced ESP-MD-1833-2025 and its siblings) but had no wiki page documenting it; this page supplies that missing documentation rather than allocating a new code, since the routing decision for unit ES111 explicitly identified ESP-CO-1833-2025 as the existing candidate matching this exact territory by name and span.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES111 ("A Coruña"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES112 Lugo, ES113 Ourense, and onward through ES243), all following a system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing decision found that a polity row with this exact name and span, ESP-CO-1833-2025, already exists in the database's polity table -- created earlier in the same convention pass -- but that row had no accompanying wiki page, meaning the database carried a bare CSV entry (status: draft, polygon_status: unassigned) with no documented reasoning, territory description, or sourcing behind it. Without a page, the row was effectively undocumented: a reader or future gate could not tell why it was created, what data it captures, or what remains unresolved. What confirms distinctness: A Coruña's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES111) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entry ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which is roughly 505,000 km2 against A Coruña's ~7,950 km2, about 64x larger and incapable of representing province-level series without collapsing them into the national total.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish province numeric code), under which A Coruña is conventionally cpro 15 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence has not been checked, so the feature_id cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet, exactly as ESP-MD-1833-2025 does for the same reason. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and A Coruña province does not coincide with the Galicia autonomous community boundary (Galicia contains three other provinces: Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra), so an adm1 feature would be the wrong granularity entirely, unlike Madrid's case where province and community happen to coincide. Territory description: A Coruña province occupies the northwestern tip of the Iberian Peninsula, forming Galicia's Atlantic-facing coast. Its capital and largest city is the port of A Coruña; the province also contains Ferrol (a major naval port) and Santiago de Compostela (Galicia's regional capital, seat of the autonomous community's government, though administratively part of A Coruña province rather than a province of its own). On a modern map it is bounded by Lugo province to the east, Pontevedra province to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the north and west, including Cape Finisterre. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 7,950 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for ESP-MD-1833-2025.

## Predecessors and successors

A Coruña has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the Kingdom of Galicia's historic partido system, which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1978, one of the four provinces making up the Galicia autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish NUTS units of treating 2025 as the open end for still-current entries.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report A Coruña as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES111) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ES111 records the official name 'A Coruña' as confirmed via GISCO's NUTS 2021 nomenclature, and notes that sibling Spanish NUTS3 units (ES112, ES113, and others through ES243) were all routed create_new under the same 1833-start/2025-open-end country convention, which is the evidentiary basis for treating A Coruña as a distinct, continuously-tabulated reporting geography rather than folding it into the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-co-abbreviation

**Reused the two-letter abbreviation CO rather than a numeric province code, matching the already-decided ESP house convention**

The database already carries a row named ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña, province of Spain), created earlier in the same convention pass that produced ESP-MD-1833-2025 and the rest of the Spanish NUTS3/province sibling set (ES111 through ES243). That existing code follows the dominant subnational pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name (CO) rather than the NUTS digits (ES111) or the INE province numeric code (15 for A Coruña). This page targets that pre-existing code exactly rather than minting a new one, because the routing decision for unit ES111 explicitly found that 'the existing candidate ESP-CO-1833-2025 already matches this exact territory by name and span' -- the database row exists but had no wiki page backing it, which this page supplies. Rejecting the routing decision's own suggestion to reconsider this as match_existing against a genuinely new code would have produced a duplicate; instead this is authorship of the missing documentation for the code that already exists.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and A Coruña's cpro=15 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which A Coruña is conventionally cpro 15 -- but the routing decision for ES111 could not confirm this because the source file is marked present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence for redistribution has not been checked. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=15 feature is confirmed to be A Coruña (and confirmed to match the 1833-2025 province boundary rather than some other vintage), this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry, exactly as ESP-MD-1833-2025 does for the same reason. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for A Coruña, distinct from Galicia's other three provinces (Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra), before assigning it here.

### oq-duplicate-routing-signal

**The routing decision itself flagged this as possibly match_existing rather than create_new**

The routing_concerns field for unit ES111 states plainly: 'A candidate polity ESP-CO-1833-2025 with the identical name and span already exists in the vocabulary -- this looks like it should be match_existing rather than create_new, unless the harness intends a distinct code per NUTS unit even when an identical pre-existing polity exists.' This page resolves that tension by treating ESP-CO-1833-2025 as the single target row for A Coruña -- i.e. by writing the wiki page for the already-existing database row rather than allocating a second code -- but the underlying process question (should create_new verdicts check the existing polity table by name before firing, to avoid ever reaching this ambiguity) is unresolved at the harness level, not just for this entry. If a second ESP-CO code is ever independently created by a future harness run, that would be a genuine duplicate needing a merge, not a documentation gap like this one.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became A Coruña province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by A Coruña province was part of the Kingdom of Galicia's older territorial divisions (partidos, and the historic Reino de Galicia jurisdiction), which do not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and are not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. A Coruña's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-MD-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Galician administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
