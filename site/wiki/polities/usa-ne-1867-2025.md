# Nebraska (state of the United States)

## Summary

Nebraska, one of the fifty states of the United States, admitted to the Union on March 1, 1867, carved from the eastern portion of Nebraska Territory (organized 1854 as part of the Kansas-Nebraska Act). This entry is `type: subnational` because Nebraska has never been independently sovereign; the row exists solely to carry state-level reporting territory for statistics tabulated at the state rather than the national level, so it must not compete with the national USA chain for sovereignty ranking. The span 1867-2025 tracks continuous statehood, with no interruption or territorial change, through the last two eras of the national USA polity chain (USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025). The single pre-statehood data year that might otherwise motivate an earlier start (1866, discussed in the routing decision) is treated as a back-cast to the national polity active at that time (USA-1848-1867), not as evidence for extending this entry's own start year, since Nebraska Territory itself is not a polity row in this database.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Nebraska as a distinct subnational reporting unit within the United States, created by the agent-harness pipeline processing the administrative unit `USA-NEBRASKA` ("Nebraska", country United States of America). The routing decision names no specific source, row count, or year range of Nebraska-level data (unlike the Manchuria exemplar, which cites 20 rows across two named sources); it instead frames the creation as adding a real, distinct GADM-confirmed admin-1 boundary with no existing candidate polity, consistent with a systematic state-by-state expansion pass. Before this row, any Nebraska-specific statistics would have had no state-level home in this database and would either be absent or folded into national USA totals -- a mismatch whenever a source (e.g. USDA, Census Bureau, or Mitchell-style historical compilations) reports Nebraska separately from all-U.S. figures. What confirms Nebraska is a distinct administrative entity is uncontroversial: it is one of the fifty states, with its own government, admitted by act of Congress in 1867, and long used as a state-level reporting unit in agricultural and demographic series. As with the Alabama precedent, the specific data motivating this row's creation was not supplied in the routing decision and is flagged below as an open question.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.28_1`, assigned directly (not a proxy) because this is a 1:1 match between the administrative unit and a GADM admin-1 feature already registered and locally present in this database's GeoPackage. `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Area:** measured from the attached geometry at approximately 200,356 km2 (Nebraska's commonly cited land+water area is about 200,356 km2 / 77,354 sq mi). This is a measurement of the attached polygon, consistent with Nebraska's real-world extent, not an independent territorial claim.

**Territory description:** Nebraska sits in the central Great Plains, bordered by South Dakota to the north, Iowa and Missouri (across the Missouri River) to the east, Kansas to the south, and Colorado and Wyoming to the west -- readily locatable on any modern map of the U.S. as the state directly south of the eastern Dakotas. Its boundaries have been essentially stable since admission in 1867, apart from minor Missouri River channel-migration adjustments with Iowa and Missouri that were settled by subsequent Supreme Court boundary cases (e.g. the 1892-1943 series of Nebraska v. Iowa disputes), none of which materially changed the state's overall area. The modern GADM boundary is treated as valid for the full 1867-2025 span on that basis, though this has not been independently checked against an 1867 admission-era description.

## Predecessors and successors

Nebraska has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database: it enters as a full U.S. state in 1867 and its span runs open/current through 2025 with no successor. Nebraska Territory (1854-1867), organized under the Kansas-Nebraska Act and originally extending north to the Canadian border before being repeatedly reduced by the creation of Dakota, Colorado, Idaho, and Montana Territories, is not itself represented as a polity row, so there is no predecessor edge to record. The single 1866 data year noted in the routing decision predates statehood and falls under the national polity USA-1848-1867 rather than under Nebraska specifically.

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

Nebraska is a standard U.S. state with no distinct historical name of its own separate from its statehood (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad), so it follows the pattern used by 57 of 66 subnational rows: ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end. The subunit token chosen is NE, the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US code for Nebraska, matching the two-letter convention already used for other U.S. states added in this harness run (e.g. USA-AL, USA-AR, USA-AZ, USA-CO). The full code is USA-NE-1867-2025.

### d-start-year-admission

**Start year is 1867 admission to the Union, per the country default rule**

The country convention's default rule for U.S. states is to start at formal admission to the Union unless the state is a departure case (Alaska, Hawaii, or a territory with a distinct pre-statehood identity already carrying its own polity row). Nebraska Territory (1854-1867) is not itself a polity row in this database, and the routing decision explicitly found no departure reason -- Nebraska is not Alaska, Hawaii, or an original-13 state -- so 1867 is used directly rather than back-cast to the territorial period. The single 1866 data year identified in routing predates statehood by one year and is routed to the national polity covering that year (USA-1848-1867) rather than motivating an earlier start for this entry.

### d-two-container-edges

**Two container edges tile 1867-2025 across the two national USA rows spanning statehood**

The national USA chain in the polity table is split into five rows by era, of which the two spanning Nebraska's 1867-2025 life are USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025. Nebraska's 1867 start falls exactly at the boundary of the fourth national row (USA-1867-1959 begins in 1867), so no earlier national row is included, and the two edges together tile the full span with no gap: 1867-1959 and 1959-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-vintage-risk-1867-boundary

**Does the GADM present-day boundary match Nebraska's boundary at admission in 1867, given the known Missouri River disputes?**

The polygon assigned here (GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.28_1) captures Nebraska's current boundary. Unlike Alabama, Nebraska's eastern boundary with Iowa and Missouri runs along the Missouri River, which is known to have migrated channel over the 19th and 20th centuries, producing a documented series of Supreme Court boundary disputes (Nebraska v. Iowa, decided across several cases from 1892 to 1943) over which state holds land left on the 'wrong' side after avulsion events. These disputes affected small parcels rather than large territory, but no specific check was done to confirm the modern GADM boundary matches Nebraska's boundary as understood at 1867 admission versus its boundary after the river-boundary settlements. If the affected area is non-trivial, the pre-settlement and post-settlement polygons could differ enough to warrant a vintage caveat similar to those used for other river-boundary states.
