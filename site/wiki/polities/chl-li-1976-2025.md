# O'Higgins (region of Chile)

## Summary

O'Higgins is the sixth region of Chile (Región del Libertador General Bernardo O'Higgins), a fertile agricultural and mining territory in central Chile immediately south of the Santiago Metropolitan Region, centered on the city of Rancagua and the Cachapoal and Colchagua river valleys. It was created in its present form in 1976 when Decreto Ley 575 replaced the country's old provincial structure with thirteen (now sixteen) numbered regions; before that date the area existed as the provinces of Cachapoal, Colchagua and Cardenal Caro within a larger administrative grouping (the old Provincia de O'Higgins and neighboring provinces), but was not a standalone first-order administrative unit in the sense the modern statistical series use. This entry is `type: national`... actually reclassify below.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: source data reported at the Chilean first-order administrative region level for O'Higgins (unit_id CHL-LI), consistent with the sibling regional entries already staged in this batch (Aysén, Antofagasta, Atacama, La Araucanía, Arica y Parinacota) which share the same source family (juan-subnational) and reporting granularity. Previously this territory's data had no dedicated polity: the only CHL rows in the table were whole-country national totals (CHL-1810-1884, CHL-1884-1899, CHL-1899-1902, CHL-1902-2025), which are containers for O'Higgins territory across all periods but are not substitutes for a sub-national reporting unit — routing regional figures to the national row would conflate a sixteenth of the country's territory with the whole, the same category of mismatch already corrected for the other Chilean regions in this batch. What confirms this entity is distinct: O'Higgins region is a first-order administrative unit of the Chilean state, established by Decreto Ley 575 (1974, effective 1976) as Región VI and later formally named Región del Libertador General Bernardo O'Higgins; it has its own regional government, statistical reporting (INE regional series), and ISO 3166-2:CL code (CL-LI), distinguishing it clearly from both the national aggregate and from neighboring regions.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision (unit_id CHL-LI, admin_name "O'Higgins") found source data reported at the Chilean-region level for O'Higgins with no existing polity able to receive it: the only CHL rows in the table before this batch were whole-country national totals, which are containers for this territory, not substitutes for it — matching regional figures to the national row would duplicate the same category of conflict already flagged and resolved for the sibling region entries (Aysén, Antofagasta, Arica y Parinacota, La Araucanía, Atacama). Under this country's convention, Chilean regions default to a 1976 start year (the date DL 575 became operational) unless a unit-specific departure is documented; O'Higgins is not among the three named departure regions (which split off later from larger parents), so the default applies and this is a newly created polity rather than a re-span of an existing one.

**Territory description:** O'Higgins occupies the central valley and coastal range immediately south of Santiago, bordered by the Metropolitan Region to the north, Maule region to the south, the Andes and Argentina to the east, and the Pacific to the west — roughly the area a reader would locate between 33°50'S and 35°S on a modern map of Chile, centered on Rancagua. The region's official area is approximately 16,387 km², making it one of the smaller Chilean regions by area but one of the most important for fruit, wine, and copper mining (including the El Teniente mine). No km² figure is stated here as a measurement of the (currently absent) attached geometry, since none is attached; the 16,387 km² figure is the region's own officially published area, not derived from any polygon in this repository.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years code pattern**

57 of 66 subnational rows in the table use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. O'Higgins is a standard first-level Chilean administrative region with no historical name distinct from its administrative label (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad), so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. The code CHL-LI-1976-2025 uses LI as the subunit abbreviation, matching ISO 3166-2:CL's CL-LI code for the Región del Libertador General Bernardo O'Higgins, consistent with the abbreviation convention already used for sibling entries (CHL-AI, CHL-AN, CHL-AP, CHL-AR, CHL-AT).

### d-type-subnational

**Set type to subnational, not national**

Chile held continuous, undisputed sovereignty over this territory throughout 1976-2025. This entry exists to carry a sub-national reporting territory inside that sovereignty, not to compete with the national CHL-1902-2025 row in the matcher's family/sovereignty ranking, exactly as documented for the sibling Chilean region entries (e.g. CHL-AI-1976-2025). type: subnational is therefore the correct classification despite an earlier draft field mistakenly carrying 'national' before this decision was written.

### d-polygon-route

**Declared polygon_source as the registered slug, not the route name**

The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed, but the polygon_detail names a candidate that is in fact the already-registered gadm-4.1-adm1 source, just not fetched in full for Chile (the local file is an 81-country subset). Per the harness's own correction — confirmed against the identical situation for sibling entries CHL-AI, CHL-AN, CHL-AP, CHL-AR, CHL-AT, which all cite gadm-4.1-adm1 as unfetched rather than new_source_needed — this is properly a registered_source_unfetched case: the slug is registered, and polygon_source is set to gadm-4.1-adm1 with polygon_status unassigned, not to the literal route name new_source_needed, which is rejected by validate_declared_sources.

### d-default-start-year

**Used the default 1976 start year, not a departure**

The routing_reasoning confirms O'Higgins is not among the three named departure regions that split off later from larger parents (e.g. Arica y Parinacota split from Tarapacá in 2007). O'Higgins/Región VI was one of the original thirteen regions created directly by DL 575 in 1976, so the country's default start-year convention applies without modification.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1976-ohiggins-coverage

**What polity should carry pre-1976 O'Higgins data years?**

As with the sibling Aysén entry, if the source series for unit_id CHL-LI extends before 1976, those years describe a territory that existed under a different administrative arrangement — the historical provinces of Cachapoal, Colchagua and Cardenal Caro, part of a larger agrupación before DL 575 — and should not be silently absorbed into this entry, since doing so would misstate its span_basis, which is tied specifically to the 1976 reform. This entry deliberately leaves any pre-1976 years unrouted pending confirmation of what earlier administrative unit and boundary covered this territory; a predecessor polity should be created once that is confirmed.

### oq-gadm-vintage-check

**Does the modern GADM O'Higgins boundary match the region's boundary across 1976-2025?**

O'Higgins region's boundaries have been broadly stable since 1976, but Chile's regions underwent a minor province-level reshuffle in 2018 (the Provincia de Marga Marga move affecting Valparaíso, not O'Higgins directly) and there have been periodic proposals to adjust commune boundaries within the region. Once the full GADM 4.1 release is fetched and a feature is attached for CHL, its vintage should be checked against 1976-era boundaries specifically, rather than assumed identical to the present-day administrative map, before polygon_status is upgraded from unassigned to assigned.
