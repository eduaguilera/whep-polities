# Dordogne (département)

## Summary

Dordogne is one of the original départements of Revolutionary France, created by the decree of 4 March 1790 that redrew the kingdom's ancien-régime provinces into a grid of roughly equal administrative units named mostly for rivers and physical features -- Dordogne takes its name from the river crossing the territory of the former province of Périgord, which it essentially replaced. It has kept its boundaries and its name continuously since, through every regime change France went through in the following two centuries: the First Republic and Empire, the Restoration, the Second Empire, the Third Republic (through the loss and recovery of Alsace-Lorraine, which never touched this inland south-western département), and the Fourth and Fifth Republics to the present. This entry is `type: subnational` because Dordogne was never a sovereign entity in its own right -- sovereignty sits with the France national chain (FRA-1800-1871 / FRA-1871-1919 / FRA-1919-2025) that contains it throughout. The entry exists to give the pipeline a place to route any statistics that are reported at the département level for Dordogne specifically, distinct from national French totals, the same purpose subnational rows serve for Spanish provinces (e.g. ESP-AS, ESP-CA) elsewhere in this table. It is one of a large, so-far mostly unrepresented set of ~83-96 French départements that could each warrant a similar row; this is the first for Dordogne.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry was created from a proactive administrative-unit routing pass (unit_id FRA-FRI11, admin_name FRI11, the GISCO NUTS3 code for Dordogne) rather than from a batch of already-unmatched valued data rows -- no specific dataset has yet been confirmed to report Dordogne separately. The routing decision found that no existing polity in the table represents Dordogne itself; only the national France rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) share its iso3, and those rows contain but are not identical to this département, so matching Dordogne to the national row would misrepresent any département-level statistic as if it covered all of France. What confirms Dordogne is a distinct, stable administrative unit worth its own row is its uninterrupted existence as one of the original 1790 Revolutionary départements: it is not among the four documented exceptions to the default 1790 start rule for French départements (the 1871-1919 loss of Belfort as a separate territory, the Alsace-Lorraine départements' 1871-1919 absence and 1919 return, the 1975 Corsican split into Haute-Corse and Corse-du-Sud, and the four/five overseas départements created much later). Its administrative continuity since 1790 is exactly the kind of stable, long-lived reporting unit this table is meant to carry a row for, independent of whether any specific dataset has been routed to it yet.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision (polygon_route: new_source_needed) established that no source registered in this repository's `scripts/sources.yaml` carries French sub-national boundaries: the two sources used for FRA (cshapes-europe, cshapes-2.0) are national-boundary-only, and the locally cached `gadm-4.1-adm1` subset -- despite covering 81 other countries at admin-1 -- has zero rows for France. Building either requires registering a new source and running it through `construct_from_registered` or an equivalent constructed-source path; neither has been done, so `polygon_area_km2` is left blank here rather than guessed.

**Territory description:** Dordogne is a département in south-western France, corresponding closely to the historical province of Périgord, in the modern Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. Its administrative seat is Périgueux. On a modern map it sits inland, roughly between Bordeaux (Gironde, to its west) and Limoges (Haute-Vienne, to its north-east), bordered by the départements of Charente, Charente-Maritime, Gironde, Lot-et-Garonne, Lot, Corrèze, and Haute-Vienne. Its area is commonly cited at approximately **9,060 km²**, making it the third-largest département in mainland France by area -- this figure is drawn from standard INSEE/general-reference statements about the département's current extent, not measured from any geometry attached to this entry, since no polygon has yet been built for it.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Start year set to 1800, not the proposed 1790**

The routing decision that created this entry proposed start_year 1790, reasoning that Dordogne is one of the ~83 original départements created by the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform. That is correct as administrative history, but the polities database has no France row that begins before 1800 -- the earliest national chain member is FRA-1800-1871. A container edge is only valid if it falls inside both parties' own spans, so a Dordogne row starting in 1790 would leave 1790-1800 uncontained and the containment gate would reject it. Rather than fabricate a container edge for a decade no France row covers, this entry starts at 1800, matching the earliest available container. The 1790-1800 gap is therefore a limitation of the current France chain, not evidence that Dordogne did not exist as a département in that decade -- it plainly did. If a pre-1800 France row is ever added to the table, this entry's start_year should be reconsidered and moved back to 1790.

### d-subunit-code-24

**Subunit code chosen as the INSEE department number, not the NUTS code**

The routing decision's admin_name was FRI11 (the GISCO/NUTS3 code for Dordogne). The dominant pattern in the table for subnational entries is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and for readability and to match how Dordogne is universally identified (on road signs, vehicle plates, postal addresses, INSEE statistics), the subunit token used here is 24, Dordogne's INSEE/postal department number, rather than the NUTS3 code FRI11. NUTS codes for French départements are stable equivalents of the INSEE numbers, so no information is lost, and 24 is far more recognizable to a reader trying to place this polity.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-built

**No polygon exists yet for Dordogne; a source still needs to be registered and built**

The routing decision explicitly flagged polygon_route as new_source_needed: neither of the two sources this repo uses for France (cshapes-europe, cshapes-2.0) carries sub-national boundaries, and the locally cached gadm-4.1-adm1 subset omits France entirely (it covers 81 other countries but has zero FRA rows). Two real candidates were named -- IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS départements layer, or a non-subset global GADM release that does include a France ADM layer -- but neither has been fetched, licence-checked, or registered in scripts/sources.yaml. Until one is, this entry can carry no polygon at all, and polygon_status is unassigned rather than proxy or assigned. Whoever picks this up next should check IGN's licence (Etalab Licence Ouverte, which should be compatible) before building, and should build all ~83 pre-1790-reform-stable départements from the same layer in one pass rather than one at a time.

### oq-boundary-stability-unverified

**Dordogne's boundary stability since 1790 is asserted, not checked against a source**

This page and the routing decision both assume Dordogne's boundary has been essentially unchanged since its creation in 1790, which is why a single present-day polygon proxy would be treated as valid back to 1800 once one exists. That assumption has not actually been checked against a source describing 18th/19th-century French administrative boundary changes -- some départements did have canton- or arrondissement-level adjustments (and occasionally lost or gained communes to neighbours) over two centuries. Before adopting a modern polygon as a strict proxy for 1800, someone with access to a French administrative history source (e.g. INSEE's own historical geography notes, or Motte's atlas of French administrative divisions) should confirm no boundary change large enough to matter occurred for this particular département.

### oq-no-data-rows-yet-routed

Unlike the exemplar MAN-1950-1955 page, which documents specific mitchell and fao1952 rows that motivated its creation, this Dordogne entry is being created ahead of any specific data ingestion: the routing decision that produced it was a proactive administrative-unit pass (unit_id FRA-FRI11) rather than a response to unmatched valued data.
