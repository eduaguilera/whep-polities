# Wisconsin (state of the United States)

## Summary

This entry tracks Wisconsin from its admission to the Union on 1848-05-29, as the 30th state, to the present. Before 1848 the area was Wisconsin Territory (organized 1836, split off from Michigan Territory, itself carved from the old Northwest Territory), which this entry does not cover — that predecessor period is not currently represented as a separate polity in this database, so `predecessor` is left empty rather than pointing at a code that does not exist. `type: subnational` because Wisconsin is a constituent state of a sovereign country (USA), never itself sovereign; its rows must not compete with the national USA-* chain in the matcher. The state's boundaries have been stable since admission (the western border along the St. Croix and Mississippi rivers and the Lake Michigan shore to the east were fixed at statehood, with only minor surveyed adjustments), which is why one polygon and one polity code cover the full 1848-2025 span rather than needing an era split.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: none is cited in the routing decision beyond source coverage running 1866-2025 under the label "Wisconsin" / USA.50_1 in GADM; the decision notes this reflects source coverage, not the state's administrative start (1848). This entry was created directly from the harness's per-US-state routing pass (the same pass that produced ALK-1867-1959 for Alaska), which walks the 50 states and creates one subnational polity per state not already represented. Wisconsin data was previously matched to the national USA-* rows (USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025), which is wrong because those rows are ~9.8 million km2 national totals, roughly 58x the area of the single state the data actually describes. What confirms Wisconsin is a distinct reporting/administrative unit: it has been a constituent state of the Union, with its own government and statistics-gathering apparatus, continuously since 1848; GADM 4.1 admin-1 carries it as its own feature (USA.50_1) with a stable boundary, and U.S. federal statistical series (agricultural census, Bureau of Economic Analysis, etc.) report Wisconsin as a distinct state-level unit throughout this span.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Copied from the registered source `gadm-4.1-adm1`, feature `USA.50_1` (`assigned`, not a proxy). This is a directly-matching, present-day-boundary feature, the same route already used for other U.S. states in this database (e.g. Alaska, ALK-1867-1959, uses `USA.2_1` from the same source). Unlike the Alaska entry's earlier history — where the curated GADM subset once excluded the United States entirely — the fuller `gadm41_adm1.gpkg` extract now present in `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/` does carry all 51 USA admin-1 features (50 states plus DC), confirmed directly against the file.

**Territory description:** Wisconsin is the U.S. state occupying the western shore of Lake Michigan and the southern shore of Lake Superior, bordered by Minnesota and Iowa to the west (across the St. Croix and Mississippi rivers), Illinois to the south, and Michigan to the northeast (both the Upper Peninsula border and the Lake Michigan water boundary). Its capital is Madison; its largest city Milwaukee. Measured from the attached GADM 4.1 geometry (equal-area projection, ESRI:54034), the polygon covers **169,299 km2** — this is a measurement of the attached shape, not an independent historical figure, but it is consistent with Wisconsin's commonly cited land+water area of roughly 169,600-176,000 km2 depending on whether Great Lakes waters are included. Because the state's borders were essentially fixed at 1848 admission and have not materially changed since, using the present-day GADM boundary as the polygon for the full 1848-2025 span is a like-for-like proxy, not an approximation across a changed territory (unlike, e.g., the post-Trianon Hungary or post-1918 Poland cases where a boundary change would make the reuse wrong).

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity codes exist in this database. The pre-1848 Wisconsin Territory (1836-1848) is the natural predecessor administratively but is not currently modeled as a separate row — it may be worth creating if data ever surfaces for that period, at which point `predecessor` here should be updated to point at it. There is no successor: Wisconsin remains a current U.S. state, so `end_year: 2025` (exclusive) simply marks the data horizon, not an administrative end.

## Sourced claims

- Wisconsin was admitted to the Union as the 30th state on 1848-05-29, per the Wisconsin Enabling Act of 1846 and the state's own ratified constitution of 1848 — this is the basis for start_year: 1848 rather than the earlier territorial organization date of 1836.
- GADM 4.1 admin-1 records Wisconsin as feature GID_1 = USA.50_1, distinct from all other admin-1 features in the gadm41_adm1.gpkg extract at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/, with a measured area (ESRI:54034 equal-area projection) of approximately 169,299 km2.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-with-subunit

**Used the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern, not the bespoke ALK/HYD/RYU style**

Wisconsin is an ordinary constituent state with no separate historical identity of its own outside the Union (unlike the Territory of Alaska, Hyderabad, or the Ryukyus, each of which had a distinct pre- or post-annexation political existence that earned a bespoke code). 57 of 66 subnational rows already use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and ALK-1867-1959 is the only existing USA subnational precedent but is itself the bespoke exception documented for a territory with its own name and a distinct pre-statehood status (a purchased territory administered separately from any existing state or Union member for 92 years). Wisconsin has no such separate identity — it went from organized territory directly to state in 1848 — so USA-WI-1848-2025 follows the dominant pattern instead.

### d-single-era-polygon-no-split

**One polygon and one polity code for the full 1848-2025 span, not split at each national-era boundary**

The routing decision flagged that the container chain crosses three national eras (USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025) and asked how multi-era containment should be represented. Rather than splitting the polity into three rows (one per national era, mirroring how the national chain itself is split), I kept Wisconsin as a single polity row with three container edges, one per era, together tiling 1848-2025 with no gap. This matches how MAN-1950-1955 and other subnational entries in this database represent containment: the territory (Wisconsin) is continuous even though its container (the USA as a national entity) is periodized, so the split belongs on the container edge list, not on the polity itself. Splitting the polity itself would create three near-identical Wisconsin rows differing only in which national era they nest under, which is exactly the kind of re-spanning/proliferation the container edge mechanism exists to avoid.

## Open questions

### oq-territorial-predecessor-missing

**Wisconsin Territory (1836-1848) is not modeled as a predecessor polity**

Wisconsin existed as an organized U.S. territory from 1836 (split from Michigan Territory) to 1848 statehood, with a somewhat larger extent than the modern state (the original Wisconsin Territory briefly included parts of present-day Iowa and Minnesota before those were split off in 1838 and 1849). If pre-1848 data ever surfaces under a 'Wisconsin' or 'Wisconsin Territory' label, it should NOT be routed to this entry (which starts at statehood) without checking whether the territorial boundary differed enough to need its own polygon and code, and this entry's `predecessor` field should be updated once that row exists.

### oq-water-area-convention

**Whether the 169,299 km2 GADM measurement follows the same land/water convention as other USA state entries in this database**

Commonly published Wisconsin area figures range from about 169,600 km2 (land + inland water) to 176,000 km2 (including its share of the Great Lakes). The 169,299 km2 figure measured here from GADM 4.1 falls in the land+inland-water range, but I have not cross-checked this against how Alaska's polygon_area_km2 (1,518,800 in ALK-1867-1959) or other USA state entries handle Great Lakes / offshore water inclusion, so there may be a systematic under- or over-count relative to sibling state entries that a later territorial-extent audit should reconcile.

### oq-source-coverage-gap

**No specific data source or row count is cited in the routing decision for this polity**

Unlike the Manchuria exemplar, which documents exact source labels and row counts (mitchell: 13 rows, fao1952: 7 rows) that motivated the entry, the routing decision for Wisconsin gives only a coverage range (1866-2025) without naming the source(s) or row count.
