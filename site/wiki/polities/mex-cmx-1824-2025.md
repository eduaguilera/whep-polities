# Ciudad de México (Distrito Federal)

## Summary

Ciudad de Mexico (until 2016 the Distrito Federal, DF) is the seat of the Mexican federal government, carved out of the State of Mexico by decree of the Constituent Congress on 18 November 1824 to house the national capital, following the U.S. model of a federal district separate from any constituent state. Unlike Mexico's other first-order units, it was never admitted as a state and has no statehood-admission date to key a start_year to; the country convention for MEX explicitly departs from the states' admission-year rule for this reason and instead dates the district's creation itself. It underwent territorial growth in 1898-1902 as outlying municipalities (Xochimilco, Tlalpan, etc.) were folded into the DF in stages, but has existed continuously as a distinct administrative unit since 1824. In January 2016 a constitutional reform renamed it Ciudad de Mexico and granted it a status similar to a state (its own constitution, elected legislature) while keeping its boundaries and its role as national capital unchanged; that reform is an administrative-status change, not a break in the entity's territorial identity, so it does not end this entry. type is subnational because at no point did this unit hold sovereignty separate from Mexico -- it is a federal capital district and later a constituent entity of the Mexican federation, always contained within the national MEX chain.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is being created from a routing decision (unit_id MEX-CMX) rather than from a data ingest with rows already in hand: the decision document identifies Ciudad de Mexico/Distrito Federal as a real, continuously existing administrative territory that is currently unrepresented in the polities table. The only two MEX rows are national totals -- MEX-1800-1848 and MEX-1848-2025 -- and any subnational data keyed to the Federal District or Ciudad de Mexico would previously have had nowhere to route except one of those two national polities, which cover roughly 30x this unit's area and would silently blend a capital-district series into a whole-country total. The federal district's distinctness as an administrative entity is confirmed by its uninterrupted appearance in Mexican federal law from the 1824 constitutional decree onward, and by INEGI/Mexican statistical practice, which has always tabulated the Distrito Federal/Ciudad de Mexico as a distinct one-of-32 first-order entity (31 states + the capital) in national accounts and censuses, separate from the State of Mexico that surrounds it.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** `none`. No adm1-level boundary source currently loaded in the repository's GeoPackage covers Mexican states or the Federal District -- the locally fetched `gadm-4.1-adm1` layer is a curated 81-country subset that does not include MEX, and the country-level sources present (`cshapes-2.0`, and the `cliopatria` MEX-1800-1848 feature) are national polygons, not subdivisions. Attaching a proxy from either would misrepresent an area roughly 30 times too large, so no geometry is attached; the honest value is `none`, not a guessed proxy. Fetching the global (not the 81-country subset) GADM 4.1 adm1 release is the concrete remedy and is recorded as an open question below rather than acted on here, since acquiring a new source is outside the scope of authoring this page.

**Territory description:** Ciudad de Mexico occupies the roughly bowl-shaped former lakebed at the center of the Valley of Mexico, in the south-central Mexican highlands, entirely surrounded by the State of Mexico (Estado de Mexico) except for a short southern border with Morelos. Its modern extent is approximately 1,485 km2 across 16 boroughs (alcaldias), running from the historic center in Cuauhtemoc borough south to the Xochimilco wetlands and the volcanic slopes of Ajusco. This is a small fraction of national territory (Mexico is about 1.96 million km2) but the country's largest single urban economy. The unit's boundaries were not static across the full 1824-2025 span: the original 1824 district was smaller, and its most significant expansion came via decrees between 1898 and 1902 annexing surrounding municipalities (including Xochimilco, Tlalpan, Coyoacan, and Tacubaya) that today make up its southern and western boroughs -- a reader mapping the early-19th-century district onto the modern 1,485 km2 outline would be overstating the earlier territory considerably.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: the Federal District was created in 1824 out of previously undifferentiated national/State-of-Mexico territory rather than by splitting or renaming an existing polity row, so predecessor is left empty. No successor: the entity continues to the present (end_year 2025, open), having been renamed Ciudad de Mexico in 2016 without a change of code-worthy identity -- that rename is treated as continuity within this single entry rather than a predecessor/successor break, consistent with how other renamed-but-continuous units are handled elsewhere in the table.

## Sourced claims

- The Federal District was established by decree of the Constituent Congress of the United Mexican States on 18 November 1824, carving it out of the State of Mexico to house the seat of federal government, modeled on the U.S. District of Columbia.
- Between 1898 and 1902 the Federal District's original boroughs (prefecturas/municipalities) were reorganized and expanded via federal decree to incorporate Xochimilco, Tlalpan, Coyoacan and other formerly separate municipalities, enlarging the district toward its present southern extent.
- The 29 January 2016 constitutional reform (published in the Diario Oficial de la Federacion) renamed the Distrito Federal as Ciudad de Mexico and reorganized it into 16 alcaldias with an elected constitutional convention, effective as the new local constitution took force in 2017-2018, without altering its external boundary or its status as national capital.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-choice

**Code uses <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, the dominant subnational pattern**

Of 66 existing subnational rows, 57 follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. Ciudad de Mexico/the Federal District is a first-order administrative unit like a state, not a historical territory with its own name independent of the country (unlike Alaska's Territory-era ALK or Hyderabad's HYD), so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. MEX-CMX-1824-2025 was chosen, using CMX (the common abbreviation for Ciudad de Mexico, also used as the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code MX-CMX) as the subunit token, since MEX has no existing subnational rows to set a conflicting precedent.

### d-two-container-edges

**Two container edges are needed to tile 1824-2025, not one**

The routing decision proposed a single container_code of MEX-1848-2025, but that polity's own span starts in 1848, 24 years after this entry's 1824 start_year. Using only that edge would leave 1824-1848 uncontained and would fail the containment gate, which rejects an edge falling outside either party's span. The national polity table has a row for exactly that earlier era, MEX-1800-1848, so the container list uses both edges back to back: MEX-1800-1848 for 1824-1848, then MEX-1848-2025 for 1848-2025, tiling the full span with no gap.

## Open questions

### oq-feature-was-state-of-mexico

**This row briefly carried the State of México's outline, and the cause is worth keeping**

It was assigned `MEX.15_1`, which is GADM's `México` — the surrounding STATE, 22,351 km² — while Mexico City is 1,495 km². The name matcher normalised "Ciudad de México (Distrito Federal)" by stripping the parenthetical, which discarded the only distinguishing part, and then its substring fallback let GADM's shorter `México` match inside `Ciudad de México`. Corrected to `MEX.9_1`, GADM's `Distrito Federal` at 1,495 km², which was the only Mexican feature no row had claimed.


### oq-gadm-global-adm1-fetch

**No adm1 polygon source is fetched for Mexico at all**

The repository's GADM 4.1 adm1 layer is an 81-country curated subset and Mexico is not among the countries included, so there is currently no way to attach a Ciudad de Mexico polygon from a registered source without first fetching the global GADM 4.1 adm1 release (or an equivalent Mexican states source, e.g. INEGI's own marco geoestadistico). Until that fetch happens, this entry and any future Mexican state-level entries will have polygon_status none. Whoever performs that fetch should also check licensing terms for redistribution, which the original routing decision flagged as unverified.

### oq-boundary-vintage-1824-1902

**Federal District boundary before the 1898-1902 annexations is not modeled**

The Federal District's territory grew significantly between 1898 and 1902 when Xochimilco, Tlalpan, Coyoacan and other municipalities were annexed. This entry spans 1824-2025 as one row, but any future polygon (once fetched, see oq-gadm-global-adm1-fetch) will most likely reflect the modern ~1,485 km2 boundary, which would overstate the district's actual 19th-century extent by a large but currently unquantified margin. Whether this warrants splitting the entry at 1902, or simply flagging the pre-1902 portion as boundary-vintage-mismatched once a polygon exists, is unresolved.

Unlike the exemplar pattern where an entry is created because specific dated rows in a named source were misrouted, this entry originates purely from the routing-decision document (unit_id MEX-CMX) with no source/row-count evidence cited. It is unclear which dataset(s), if any, currently report a Distrito Federal or Ciudad de Mexico series that would actually route here once this polity exists -- that check should be run against the production/trade and population data now that the row exists, rather than assumed from the routing decision alone.
