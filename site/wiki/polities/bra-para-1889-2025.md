# Pará

## Summary

Pará is a Brazilian state in the eastern Amazon, at the mouth of the Amazon River, with Belém as its capital -- one of Brazil's oldest colonial captaincies (Grão-Pará), historically administered somewhat separately from the rest of Portuguese Brazil until well into the colonial period. This entry covers Pará from 1889, when the Proclamation of the Republic converted the former Imperial province into a state of the federation, to the present. `type: subnational` is used because Pará has been a first-order administrative division of Brazil (province, then state) rather than an independently sovereign polity for this entire span; it is not among the small set of documented BRA departures with a genuinely separate sovereignty or federal-territory history before statehood (Acre, Rondônia, Roraima), so the default republic-founding start year of 1889 applies. Unlike the cleaner-continuity sibling entries such as Bahia or Alagoas, Pará did lose substantial northern territory in 1943 when the Território Federal do Amapá was carved out of it -- a real discontinuity that this entry does not yet split into separate rows because the routing decision provides no year-level breakdown of the underlying data, so the change is instead documented as a territorial caveat and open question rather than a hard predecessor/successor boundary.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision this page implements (unit_id BRA-PARA) found no existing polity in the database representing Pará itself: the only BRA rows are three national totals (BRA-1800-1903, BRA-1903-1909, BRA-1909-2025), none of which is state-scale. Source rows describing Pará specifically -- a state-level Brazilian subnational dataset, following the same pattern as the sibling BRA-BAHIA, BRA-CEARA and BRA-GOIAS verdicts (proposed span 1900-2023 for that sibling batch, exact row count and years for Pará itself not given in this decision object) -- had previously nowhere correct to route except the national BRA-1909-2025 row, a container many times Pará's own territory, which would misattribute state-level agricultural/production data (Pará is a major producer of items like cassava, açaí, pepper and, in the mining sector, bauxite and iron ore) to the whole country. What confirms Pará is a distinct reporting unit rather than folded into national Brazil figures is the same practice documented for the sibling entries: the source reports Pará as a named first-order administrative unit (a Brazilian state), consistent with IBGE's own state-level statistical reporting since the founding of the Republic. This entry is one of a batch of Brazilian state-level polities created under the same default start-year rule, with the routing decision explicitly noting no boundary/GADM feature was found matching Pará by name in gadm-4.1-adm1, so a constructed or alternate polygon source (geobr-ibge) is needed before this entry's polygon can be finalized.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct source is `geobr-ibge` (Brazilian state boundaries, id_column `abbrev_state`), which is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` specifically to carry Brazilian ADM1 units but whose file (`data/geodata/geobr-ibge/states.gpkg`) is not present locally, per the routing decision's own polygon_reasoning. `gadm-4.1-adm1` excludes Brazil entirely from its locally curated subset, and the only BRA source currently present, `cshapes-2.0`, digitises Brazil at whole-country scale only. Once `geobr-ibge` is fetched, this entry should match `abbrev_state='PA'`.

**Territory description:** modern Pará covers approximately 1,245,870 km2, the second-largest Brazilian state by area (after Amazonas) -- roughly the size of South Africa -- occupying much of the eastern Brazilian Amazon along the lower Amazon and Tocantins-Araguaia river basins. It borders Amazonas and Roraima to the northwest, Amapá to the north (across the Amazon's mouth), Maranhão and Tocantins to the east and southeast, and Mato Grosso to the south, with its capital Belém sited at the Amazon's mouth on Guajará Bay. This present-day extent, however, is smaller than the historical province/state prior to 1943: the Território Federal do Amapá was detached from Pará's northern reaches that year, removing roughly 140,000 km2, so a present-day boundary used as a proxy for the full 1889-2025 span understates Pará's actual pre-1943 territory (see open question below). No `polygon_area_km2` is recorded here since no geometry is yet attached; the km2 figure above is a modern reference figure for locating the state, not a measurement of any attached geometry.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row is created by this decision: Pará's status as a first-order Brazilian administrative unit runs continuously back to 1889, when the Proclamation of the Republic converted the Imperial province of Grão-Pará into the state of Pará, with no sovereignty break of the kind that separates BRA-ACRE from its Bolivian-sovereignty and federal-territory predecessors. The pre-1889 Imperial province is left to the national BRA-1800-1903 row, consistent with the default convention applied to sibling states. No successor row is created either, despite the real 1943 territorial split that produced the Território Federal do Amapá (entered separately as BRA-AMAPA-1943-2025): the routing decision gives a single undivided 1889-2025 span for Pará with no year-level data to justify cutting the row at 1943, so that discontinuity is carried instead as a polygon-vintage caveat and open question rather than a hard predecessor/successor boundary. Pará remains a Brazilian state to the present, so the span runs to 2025 as still-current.

## Sourced claims

- The Proclamation of the Republic (15 November 1889) converted the Imperial provinces, including Grão-Pará -- one of Brazil's original colonial captaincies, seated at Belém since the 17th century -- into states of the new federation, the event that fixes this entry's 1889 start under the country convention's default rule.
- In 1943 the Vargas government detached the Território Federal do Amapá from Pará's northern territory between the Amazon's north bank and French Guiana, a documented administrative split (Decree-Law 5,812) that later became the state of Amapá, meaning any polygon for Pará dated after 1943 covers materially less area than the province/state did from 1889-1943.
- IBGE (Brazil's national statistics institute) reports agricultural and demographic data at the state level for Pará continuously since the early Republic, the same reporting practice cited for sibling entries BRA-BAHIA, BRA-ALAGOAS and BRA-AMAZONAS as evidence the unit is a distinct, statistically tracked reporting territory rather than a component folded into national totals.

## Decisions

### d-code-iso3-subunit-start-end-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

Used BRA-PARA-1889-2025 rather than a bespoke code, matching the pattern already established for sibling Brazilian state entries (BRA-BAHIA, BRA-ALAGOAS, BRA-AMAZONAS, BRA-CEARA, BRA-GOIAS, BRA-MARANHAO, BRA-MATOGROSSO, BRA-MINASGERAIS) created under the same batch of routing verdicts. Para is a first-order administrative division (a Brazilian state) with no historical identity as a distinct sovereign polity: it was a captaincy, then an Imperial province before 1889, and has been a Republic state since, with the same continuity pattern as Bahia and Alagoas, not the discontinuous federal-territory-then-state history of Acre or Amapa (both of which were carved partly out of Para's own 19th-century territory). That places it in the 57/66 dominant subnational pattern, not among the three bespoke historical-territory codes. SUBUNIT is the plain state name 'PARA' (unaccented, matching repo convention for names with diacritics) rather than an IBGE abbreviation, consistent with sibling pages.

### d-container-three-edges-tile-full-span

**Three container edges are needed, not one, because the national row itself changes across 1889-2025**

The routing decision's proposed container_code is BRA-1909-2025 alone (its span_basis text even cites 1889 as the start, an internal inconsistency in the decision object, exactly as flagged on the BRA-BAHIA sibling entry), but BRA-1909-2025 only begins in 1909 -- leaving 1889-1909 uncontained if a single edge were used, which the containment gate would reject as falling outside the container's own span. The polity table shows three successive national BRA rows: BRA-1800-1903 (to 1903), BRA-1903-1909, and BRA-1909-2025. This entry's container list therefore has three edges, one per national era, together tiling the full 1889-2025 span with no gap: 1889-1903 under BRA-1800-1903, 1903-1909 under BRA-1903-1909, and 1909-2025 under BRA-1909-2025 (the routing decision's own proposed container, correctly used only for its own portion of the span). This mirrors the identical fix already made on BRA-BAHIA and BRA-ALAGOAS.

### d-no-1943-amapa-split-predecessor

**No predecessor row created despite the 1943 loss of territory to Amapa**

Para lost a substantial northern portion of its territory in 1943 when the federal government carved out the Territorio Federal do Amapa (later the state of BRA-AMAPA, itself entered separately in this batch). This is a real territorial discontinuity, unlike Bahia's clean continuity, but it is handled here as a polygon-vintage caveat rather than a predecessor/successor split: the routing decision gives a single 1889-2025 span for Para with no year-segmented row counts, so there is no data-driven basis in this decision object for cutting the row at 1943. The territorial change is instead recorded as an open question below, since a present-day geobr-ibge boundary (post-1943, and post the smaller 1988 cession to Amapa's final borders) will understate Para's pre-1943 extent by a non-trivial margin.

### oq-1943-amapa-territorial-loss-not-modeled

**The 1943 (and later) cessions to Amapa mean a present-day Para boundary understates the pre-1943 extent**

In 1943 the federal government detached the Territorio Federal do Amapa from Para's northern reaches (the area between the Amazon's north bank and French Guiana), and a further boundary adjustment followed a 1990s dispute with Amapa over the Ilha de Marajo region's eastern limit. A present-day geobr-ibge boundary for Para (abbrev_state='PA') therefore reflects only the post-1943, smaller extent, while data routed to this entry for 1889-1943 nominally describes a larger province/state that included the future Amapa. No per-year row breakdown was given in the routing decision to determine how much of the routed data actually falls before 1943, so this entry cannot yet be split into a predecessor/successor pair at that date. If a future source documents a per-year breakdown, a BRA-PARA-1889-1943 / BRA-PARA-1943-2025 split (mirroring how BRA-AMAPA is entered as its own row from 1943) should be considered instead of the current single proxy span.

The routing decision implemented here gives only the qualitative description that Para 'matches the pattern already recorded for sibling states' (BRA-BAHIA, BRA-CEARA, BRA-GOIAS, etc.) without stating how many rows or which exact years of Para data are being routed to this new polity, beyond the general 1900-2023 range mentioned for the sibling batch.
