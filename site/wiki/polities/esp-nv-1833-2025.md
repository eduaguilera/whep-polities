# Navarra (province of Spain)

## Summary

Navarra (ESP-NV-1833-2025) is one of Spain's 50 historic provinces, corresponding on a modern map to the Comunidad Foral de Navarra in north-central Spain, bordering France to the north, the Basque Country to the west, La Rioja and Aragon to the south and east. Unlike most Spanish provinces, which were fresh administrative creations of the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, Navarra continues the territory of the historic Kingdom of Navarre and retained (and still retains, as a chartered "Comunidad Foral" rather than an ordinary autonomous community) a distinct fiscal and administrative regime confirmed by the 1841 Ley Paccionada. Its capital is Pamplona/Iruna. This entry is type: subnational because Spain held sovereignty over Navarra throughout 1833-2025; the row exists to carry a province-level statistical reporting geography (NUTS3 code ES220) distinguishable from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, matching the treatment of the other seven ESP province rows already in the database (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES220) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES220 ("Navarra"), country Spain, following the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (A Coruna/ES111, Madrid, and onward through ES241): a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Navarra is not one of the enumerated Canary Islands departure units) and an open end_year of 2025 (exclusive) for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states plainly that "no existing polity represents Navarra specifically" among the ESP subnational rows (only A Coruna, Madrid, and the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate existed), so the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national aggregate, collapsing a province-level series into a country-level one, the same failure mode already rejected for the other seven ESP province rows. What confirms distinctness: Navarra matches GADM admin1 feature ESP.9_1 as its own named unit (unlike some provinces, GADM does carry Navarra distinctly because it is coextensive with its own autonomous community), and INE/Eurostat have tabulated Navarra separately as NUTS3 ES220 for over a century -- the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling province rows, reinforced here by Navarra's additional historical distinctness as a former chartered kingdom (see the foral-regime open question).

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE province numeric code; Navarra's conventional cpro is 31), but the polygon_reasoning notes sources.yaml marks mapspain-ign as not present_locally -- the provinces.gpkg source file has not been downloaded, so the route cannot be executed until that data is fetched, exactly as recorded for the other seven ESP province rows despite mapspain-ign already being their declared polygon_source. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP among 18 features (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, and even where a community and province coincide, as they do for Navarra, adm1 carries no province-level attribution), so it was correctly ruled out as an alternative route. Territory description: Navarra occupies north-central Spain, from Pyrenean valleys on the French border in the north down to the semi-arid Ebro basin (Ribera de Navarra) in the south, bordered by the Basque provinces of Gipuzkoa and Araba/Alava to the west, La Rioja to the southwest, and Aragon (Zaragoza and Huesca provinces) to the east and southeast. Its capital and largest city is Pamplona/Iruna. On a modern map it is the same outline as the Comunidad Foral de Navarra, one of Spain's 17 autonomous communities, since (like Asturias, Cantabria, Madrid, Murcia and La Rioja) Navarra was never subdivided among multiple provinces after 1833. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 10,391 km2 -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling ESP province rows.

## Predecessors and successors

Navarra has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the same territory was organized as the Kingdom of Navarre, a chartered (foral) territory that retained distinct fiscal and legal institutions even after joining the Spanish crown, and this database does not model that pre-1833 structure as a separate row -- see the open question on the foral regime for why this is a live gap rather than a settled call, unlike ESP-CO or ESP-MD where no comparable pre-1833 identity exists. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (coextensive since 1982 with the Comunidad Foral de Navarra) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish province rows.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Navarra as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES220) with continuous province-level data collection, consistent with its status as one of the 50 Spanish provinces recognized in the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform.
- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES220 records that Navarra matches GADM admin1 unit ESP.9_1 as its own distinct polygon feature, and that no existing ESP polity row (only A Coruna, Madrid, and the national Spain row existed at the time) already represented it, confirming this is a genuinely unmatched territory rather than a duplicate.
- The 1841 Ley Paccionada is the historical source establishing Navarra's continued distinct fiscal-administrative status within the Spanish state after 1833, in contrast to the uniform provincial model applied to the other 49 provinces created by the same 1833 reform.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-nv-abbreviation

**Used NV rather than the ISO 3166-2 code NA, to avoid a reserved-value collision**

The dominant ESP subnational pattern already established by ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-OR and ESP-PO is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name. Navarra's actual ISO 3166-2 subdivision code is ES-NA and its INE province code is 31, but the two-letter suffix NA collides with the schema's own convention for a missing/not-applicable COW code (the frontmatter cow field elsewhere in this table uses NA as a literal null marker), and could also be misread as North America's continent abbreviation by a future automated matcher. To keep the code unambiguous while staying inside the established letter-abbreviation convention, this page mints ESP-NV-1833-2025 using NV -- the everyday informal abbreviation for Navarra/Navarre in Spanish cartographic and statistical usage -- rather than the ISO subdivision letters. This departs slightly from a strict a-priori read of 'use the ISO 3166-2 suffix' but stays inside the documented house pattern of a short, human-legible, collision-free two-letter code; a future author matching a new Navarra data source should check for NV specifically, not NA.

## Open questions

### oq-foral-regime-not-modeled

**Navarra's distinct foral (chartered) status is not modeled as a predecessor or as a territorial carve-out**

Unlike the 49 other Spanish provinces created wholesale by the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, Navarra was not a fresh administrative creation in 1833: it continued as the successor to the historic Kingdom of Navarre, which had retained its own fueros (charter privileges), separate fiscal administration, and a distinct legal-administrative identity even after the Nueva Planta-era centralizations elsewhere in Spain, formalized further by the 1841 Ley Paccionada that fixed Navarra's special fiscal regime within the Spanish state. This entry treats 1833 as the start year purely to match the country-wide provincial convention (the same default applied to ES111, ES120, and the other siblings), but Navarra's actual administrative continuity arguably runs back further and its governance form (a chartered province with its own Diputacion Foral) differs materially from an ordinary 1833 province. No predecessor row exists for the pre-1833 Kingdom of Navarre/foral territory in this database, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row. Whether Navarra's foral history should eventually motivate either an earlier start_year or a bespoke pre-1833 predecessor entry (the ALK/HYD/RYU-style exception this pipeline explicitly allows for a historical territory with a name of its own) is unresolved and should be checked against Federico-Tena or another pre-1833 Iberian source before deciding either way.

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and Navarra's cpro=31 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish INE province numeric code, under which Navarra is conventionally cpro 31 -- but the polygon_reasoning attached to this routing decision explicitly flags that sources.yaml marks mapspain-ign as not present_locally, i.e. the provinces.gpkg file has not been downloaded or placed on disk, even though it is already the polygon_source used for 7 sibling ESP province rows. That is an inconsistency worth resolving (re-check whether the local file actually exists before assuming the registry snapshot is current) rather than silently treating the route as immediately executable. Until the fetch happens and the cpro=31 feature is confirmed against a modern outline of Navarra, this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry, matching every other Spanish province row in this table.
