# Washington Territory

## Summary

Washington Territory was organised by the Organic Act of 2 March 1853 out of the part of Oregon Territory lying north of the Columbia River and the 46th parallel. When Oregon was admitted as a state on 14 February 1859 the remainder of Oregon Territory east of the new state (today's southern Idaho and a corner of western Wyoming) was attached to Washington Territory, which briefly stretched to the Continental Divide. The Idaho Organic Act of 3 March 1863 took everything east of the Snake River and the 117th meridian, and from that date Washington Territory had the outline the state still has. It was admitted as the 42nd state on 11 November 1889 under the Enabling Act of 22 February 1889, the same act that admitted Montana and the two Dakotas.

This row carries the territory from 1863 to 1889 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-wa-1889-2025](usa-wa-1889-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span starts in 1863, not at the 1853 organisation, because 1863 is when the territory reached the boundary this row carries. The 1853-1863 territory (before 1859 without, and after 1859 with, southern Idaho) is a different territory and receives no data here, so no row is created for it.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-WASHINGTON`, label `WASHINGTON`),
reports 144 rows for 1879-1888 (10 year(s)) — wheat, oats, barley, rye, maize and potatoes (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as Washington Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The Pacific Northwest between the Columbia River and the 49th parallel, from the Pacific coast and Puget Sound east to the Idaho line at 117°02'W: the modern state of Washington.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.48_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 174,876 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1863
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  One real difference exists inside the span. The **San Juan Islands** between Vancouver Island and the mainland were jointly occupied with Britain after the 1859 'Pig War' and were awarded to the United States only by the German Emperor's arbitration of 21 October 1872, so for 1863-1872 the modern polygon includes an archipelago of a few hundred km² whose sovereignty was undecided. That is well under 1% of the territory and outside the data years (1879-1888), so the row keeps `assigned`.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. Washington Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1889 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1879-1888; the row spans 1863-1889, the years this boundary
existed as Washington Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-WAT-1863-1889` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-territorial-series-provenance

**Were the 1879-1888 figures published for the territory at the time, or rebuilt later?**

USDA NASS Quick Stats serves these years under the state name. The frame existed as a unit of government, but whether Quick Stats reproduces those contemporary estimates or a later revision is not established here. Either way the boundary is the one this row carries, which is why the years route as observed rather than back_cast.

### oq-pre-1863-territory

**No row exists for Washington Territory before 1863**

The larger 1853-1863 territory has no row because no data in the panel reports it. If a source with pre-1863 figures is ingested, it needs its own row(s) (1853-1859 and 1859-1863 differ by southern Idaho) rather than an extension of this one.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
