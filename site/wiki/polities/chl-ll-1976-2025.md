# Los Lagos (region of Chile)

## Summary

Los Lagos ("the Lakes") is a first-order administrative region of southern Chile, on the Pacific coast between Araucania to the north and Aysen to the south, centred on Puerto Montt and including Chiloe Island. It was created by the 1974 Decreto Ley 575 regionalization reform and became operational in 1976, replacing the older province-based structure (which had grouped this area within Llanquihue, Chiloe and neighbouring provinces) with the current region/province/comuna hierarchy. Since 2007 the region has been smaller than at creation: Los Rios region (capital Valdivia) was split off from Los Lagos's northern provinces, one of the three documented later-departure reforms alongside Arica y Parinacota (2007) and Nuble (2018). This entry is `type: subnational`, not national, because Chile held unbroken sovereignty over this territory throughout 1976-2025; the entry exists to carry a distinct sub-national *reporting territory*, not a claim of statehood. It starts in 1976 under the default DL 575 rule (the region is not one of the later split-off units itself, it is the unit a later split was carved FROM), and remains open to 2025 because the region persists today, smaller since 2007 but not renamed or dissolved.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision (unit_id CHL-LL) reports that data for the Los Lagos territory exists across a long span, but no existing polity row is this specific sub-national reporting unit: the four CHL-* rows in the polity table (CHL-1810-1884, CHL-1884-1899, CHL-1899-1902, CHL-1902-2025) are national totals for the whole of Chile, which contain Los Lagos but are far larger in area and would conflate region-level series with the rest of the country if left matched there. The method breakdown for CHL-LL rows prior to 1976 -- interpolated_scaled 31.8%, scaled 29.5%, fallback variants roughly 23%+, luh2_calibrated 5.3% -- shows the large majority are allocations/reconstructions rather than direct period observations, consistent with a source projecting the modern region boundary backwards onto national or old-province data before the region existed administratively. That is the back_cast case (data FOR this territory before the territory existed as its own reporting unit), so 1900-1975 routes to this entry as back_cast rather than justifying an earlier polity start, and 1976-2023 is the polity's own observed span. What confirms Los Lagos is a distinct, statistically tracked unit rather than an arbitrary carve-out is DL 575 itself, the Chilean legal instrument establishing it as a first-order administrative region with its own boundary, capital (Puerto Montt) and government from 1976, plus Chile's national statistics institute (INE) and derived regional series reporting at this level from that date onward.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon has actually been pulled for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_unfetched: GADM 4.1 admin-1 is already a registered source in sources.yaml (id_column GID_1) and a present-day admin-1 boundary for Los Lagos would be a reasonable proxy in principle, but the GADM 4.1 layer cached locally in this repository is only an 81-country curated subset that excludes Chile entirely, so no feature_id can be fetched or verified from disk today. Per harness convention this is recorded as `polygon_source: gadm-4.1-adm1` (the registered slug itself) with `polygon_status: unassigned`, not `registered_source_unfetched` and not `new_source_needed` -- the correct source is known, it just has not been fetched for this country yet, and no feature_id is claimed since none has been inspected.

There is an added wrinkle here that the Atacama/Antofagasta siblings do not have: **vintage risk is real for this specific region**, not just theoretical. Los Lagos's present-day (post-2007) extent is smaller than its 1976-2007 extent, since Los Rios region was split off from its northern provinces (Valdivia and Ranco) in 2007. A present-day GADM feature for Los Lagos would therefore understate the region's original 1976-2007 territory -- this is flagged as `polygon_vintage_drift` risk for that earlier sub-period rather than assumed away, unlike Atacama where no such split occurred.

**Territory description:** Los Lagos Region occupies Chile's Lake District / northern Patagonian coast, roughly 41 S to 43.5 S latitude in its present (post-2007) extent, bordered by Los Rios Region to the north, Aysen Region to the south, Argentina to the east, and the Pacific Ocean to the west, including Chiloe Island. Its capital is Puerto Montt. It comprises the provinces of Llanquihue, Chiloe, Osorno and Palena, and covers roughly 48,000 km2 by present-day official Chilean government (INE/SUBDERE) figures for the post-2007 boundary -- a figure not yet independently confirmed from an attached polygon since none has been fetched for this entry. Prior to the 2007 Los Rios split, the region additionally included Valdivia and Ranco provinces and was roughly 65,000 km2, so any polygon eventually assigned for the 1976-2007 span specifically must not simply reuse the present-day (smaller) boundary without flagging that gap.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor is recorded: prior to 1976 there was no distinct Los Lagos administrative entity, only the pre-DL 575 province structure (Llanquihue, Chiloe and neighbours) and the national government, and pre-1976 territory-level data is treated as a back-cast reconstruction rather than evidence of a genuine administrative predecessor. No successor row is recorded for the 2007 Los Rios split either: Los Lagos itself was not dissolved or renamed in 2007, it lost territory to a newly created neighbouring region (CHL-LR, not yet a row in this table), so Los Lagos continues under the same code through 2025 with a smaller de facto extent from 2007 onward, rather than terminating and restarting.

## Sourced claims

- Decreto Ley 575 of 1974 established Chile's current region/province/comuna administrative hierarchy, with the new regions including Los Lagos becoming operational in 1976, replacing the earlier province-based system.
- Law 20.174 (2007) created Los Rios Region by splitting Valdivia and Ranco provinces off from Los Lagos Region, reducing Los Lagos to its present four-province extent (Llanquihue, Chiloe, Osorno, Palena).

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern, not a bespoke name**

Los Lagos is a first-order Chilean administrative region created by statute (DL 575, 1974) rather than a historical territory with a name and identity independent of the modern administrative map, unlike the three bespoke-code precedents (Alaska, Hyderabad, Ryukyu) which name pre-existing polities or occupation regimes. It therefore follows the dominant 57-of-66 pattern, CHL-LL-1976-2025, matching the CHL-AT-1976-2025 and CHL-AI-1976-2025 siblings already in the table and consistent with the country precedent those entries set (CHL had zero subnational rows before this batch).

### d-polygon-source-registered-slug-with-vintage-flag

**polygon_source set to the registered GADM slug with status unassigned; vintage risk flagged explicitly rather than assumed away**

The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_unfetched: GADM 4.1 admin-1 is registered in sources.yaml, but the locally cached extract excludes Chile, so no feature_id can be fetched or verified today. Following the same convention as the CHL-AT sibling, polygon_source is recorded as the slug itself (gadm-4.1-adm1) with polygon_status: unassigned, not registered_source_unfetched (rejected by validate_declared_sources) and not new_source_needed (a suitable source exists in principle). Unlike CHL-AT, no feature_id is even provisionally guessed here, because Los Lagos's 2007 territorial loss to Los Rios means a present-day GID_1 feature is confirmed-wrong for the 1976-2007 sub-period rather than merely unverified -- this is flagged as an open question rather than papered over with an unverified ID.

## Open questions

### oq-2007-split-polygon-approach

**How should the pre-2007 (larger) Los Lagos extent be represented once a polygon is assigned?**

A present-day GADM 4.1 feature for Los Lagos reflects the post-2007 boundary (Llanquihue, Chiloe, Osorno, Palena only), which is roughly 26% smaller than the region's 1976-2007 extent that also included Valdivia and Ranco provinces (later Los Rios). Simply attaching the modern feature across the full 1976-2025 span would understate the region for its first 31 years. Whether to split this entry's polygon assignment at 2007, construct a union feature for the earlier sub-period, or accept the modern boundary as a documented approximation with a stated km2 gap needs to be decided once a CHL-inclusive GADM extract or Los Rios sibling entry exists -- neither is available yet.

### oq-pre1976-data-routing-convention

**Whether pre-1976 Los Lagos-territory data should back_cast to this entry or instead match the national CHL rows, given inconsistent sibling precedent**

The routing_concerns explicitly flag that the back_cast treatment assumes the 1900-1975 Los Lagos-area rows are reconstructions/allocations onto the modern region boundary (based on the 69%+ scaled/interpolated/fallback method mix) rather than direct observations at the level of the old pre-1976 provinces. If any of those rows are in fact observed provincial-level reports under the old system, they should instead argue for an earlier start year rather than back-casting. This mirrors an unresolved inconsistency already logged on the CHL-AT sibling page, where CHL-LI's pre-1976 data was matched to the national CHL rows instead of back_cast -- the same either/or applies here and has not been resolved across the batch of Chilean DL-575 regional entries.

### oq-gadm-feature-id-not-even-guessed

**No GID_1 feature ID has been proposed for Los Lagos, unlike neighbouring sibling entries**

Sibling pages such as CHL-AT-1976-2025 and CHL-AP-2007-2025 record a provisional (unverified) GADM GID_1 feature_id despite the same locally-missing-Chile problem. This entry deliberately leaves polygon_feature_id null rather than guessing, because Los Lagos's 2007 boundary change makes a naive present-day GID_1 lookup actively misleading for the 1976-2007 portion of the span, not just unverified. Once the full GADM 4.1 adm1 layer is fetched, both the post-2007 feature ID and the question of an appropriate pre-2007 (larger) representation need to be resolved together.
