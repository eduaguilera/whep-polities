# Territory of New Guinea (1949-1975)

## Summary

The Trust Territory of New Guinea covered north-eastern New Guinea island, the
Bismarck Archipelago and Bougainville/Buka. This row runs from the 1949
administrative union with the Territory of Papua until Papua New Guinea's
independence in 1975.

The Papua and New Guinea Act 1949 joined the two territories under ONE
administration but did not merge them. New Guinea stayed a United Nations Trust
Territory, administered by Australia under the 1946 trusteeship agreement and
reporting to the UN Trusteeship Council, while Papua stayed an Australian
possession. Their legal status stayed distinct until 1975, and statistical
publications kept tabulating them separately.

This is a **component entry**, and `type: subnational` keeps it from competing
with the combined [png-1949-1975](png-1949-1975.md) in the matcher's family
ranking. Its sibling is [tpap-1949-1975](tpap-1949-1975.md).

**Why this entry exists.** `fao1952` reports `New Guinea` and `Papua` as two
separate units for 1950 and 1951. Both labels were routed to the combined
`PNG-1949-1975`, so the two territories' figures shared **9 (polity, source,
year, item, unit, indicator) keys** on that polity. A consumer that reduces
duplicate keys with `mean()` then published the average of the two territories
as the value for the whole union. In 1951, for example, New Guinea's population
of 1,103 thousand and Papua's 369 thousand came out as 736 thousand. The full
table is on [tpap-1949-1975](tpap-1949-1975.md). With each label routed to its
own territory, all 9 collisions are gone.

`New Guinea` 1937-1949 already routes to [tngu-1920-1949](tngu-1920-1949.md).
That page's verification notes establish that the label means the Mandated (later
Trust) Territory alone and not Papua plus New Guinea: fao1952's population of
1,006,000 matches the 1941 Australian administrative figure. The label keeps the
same meaning after 1949, and the 1951 population of 1,103 thousand continues that
series.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** CShapes 2.0 feature `912`, vintage 1930 (the feature dated
1920-06-28 to 1949-06-30), the same geometry
[tngu-1920-1949](tngu-1920-1949.md) uses (237,536 km2 in ESRI:54034). See
[CShapes 2.0](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md).

`polygon_status: proxy`, because CShapes stops coding New Guinea separately on
1949-06-30 and codes only the combined unit (`910`, 461,689 km2) after that. The
feature is reused past its coded end date. The boundary itself did not change
in 1949-1975: the Papua / New Guinea line is the pre-union line, and it is still
the provincial boundary between the Papuan and New Guinea regions of modern Papua
New Guinea. Features 911 and 912 sum to 461,684 km2 against 461,689 for the
combined `910`, a 0.001% difference, and they meet in zero area. So the two
component rows tile the union without a gap or an overlap.

## Predecessors and successors

`predecessor` and `successor` are left empty on purpose, and the containment
edge to `PNG-1949-1975` carries the relation instead. The union row already
takes [tngu-1920-1949](tngu-1920-1949.md) as predecessor and
[png-1975-2025](png-1975-2025.md) as successor. A second succession path
through 1949-1975 for the same iso3 would make the chain ambiguous.

## Decisions

### d-component-row-not-respan

This is a new row rather than an extension of `TNGU-1920-1949`. That code
embeds its span, and the PNG family splits at the 1949 union everywhere else.

### d-trust-territory-typed-subnational

`subnational` describes the row's role in routing, a component inside the
union, and not its legal status. Legally the Trust Territory was never an
Australian subdivision. The type was chosen because the matcher ranks
`national` above everything else, and a component must never outrank the union
it sits inside.

### d-no-declared-area

`polygon_area_km2` is left null, as on [png-1949-1975](png-1949-1975.md). The
only figure that could be declared is the reused feature's own measurement
(237,536 km2). That would be self-referential (issue 195), and the one yearbook
figure available describes a different territory (see below).

after 1949. UN Trusteeship Council reports on the territory exist and are not
ingested.
