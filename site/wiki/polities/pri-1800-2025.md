# Puerto Rico

## Summary

Single WHEP row for Puerto Rico spanning the whole 1800-2025 window, added to cover FAOSTAT/FABIO reporting code PRI. It is one of the database's heaviest small-territory data receivers — **936 layer-B rows** route here (`pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/territory_basis.csv`), covering agricultural production across both the Spanish colonial period and the post-1898 US administration. The row is undivided because the island's territorial extent never changed; only its sovereign changed, in 1898.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** GADM 4.1 adm0 feature `GID_0 = PRI`, carried as a **proxy** (`polygon_status: proxy`). Measured in an equal-area CRS (ESRI:54034) from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`: **8,972 km²** — the main island plus Vieques, Culebra and Mona. The conventionally cited land area of Puerto Rico is 8,868 km²; the 1.2% excess is coastline/generalisation, not extra territory.

**Why one 226-year row with a modern polygon is defensible here, unlike most such rows.** Puerto Rico is an island group with **no land boundary with any other polity**, so there is no frontier that a treaty could have moved. Every sovereignty event in the span transferred the *whole* island at once:

- Spain held the island continuously from 1493 until the 1898 cession, i.e. through the first 98 years of this row. Where every mainland American colony was lost in the 1810s-1820s, Cuba and Puerto Rico are the two the source records as "Retained ... Until 1898" ([wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §colonial-losses](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#colonial-losses)).
- The Treaty of Paris, signed 10 December 1898, transferred it in its entirety to the United States: "Spain ceded Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines to the United States" ([wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §1898-treaty](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#1898-treaty)), where it has remained. This is a change of sovereign over an unchanged territory — exactly the case the WHEP polity definition treats as *not* opening a new row when the reporting unit's extent is constant ([log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition)).

**No in-span territorial change was found, and there is no candidate for one.** The 1898 transfer is the only sovereignty event; it moved a boundary of *ownership*, not of extent. The island's internal municipal reorganisations are sub-national and below any WHEP threshold. **Should this row be split? No** — but note carefully what the row therefore is: a *reporting-territory* row, constant in extent, spanning two colonial regimes. Analyses that need the Spanish/US break must cut on year, not on polity code.

**Why the polygon is a proxy rather than an assigned period polygon.** No period source on disk covers it. CShapes 2.0 begins in 1886 and, more decisively, excludes dependencies below a 250,000-population threshold only — Puerto Rico clears that — but its dependency list derives from the Territorial Change Dataset plus the original COW dependency list ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)), and no CShapes feature is bound to this row in `data/final/polities_database.csv`. Because the extent is genuinely constant, a modern polygon is not an approximation of a moving target here — it is the same target. That is a stronger position than most `proxy` rows in this database can claim, and it is the reason the assertion-verification pipeline's 2026-07-27 pass on `puerto rico|juan|1900-1960` concluded the routing was sound despite the page being a thin draft (`pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/suspect_wiki_pages.csv`).

## Predecessors and successors

- **No predecessor and no successor.** The row is open at both ends of the WHEP window by construction, not because a chain link is missing: the 1800 start is the database floor and 2025 is its ceiling. There is no Spanish-era or US-era sibling row to link to, deliberately — see *Territorial extent* for why the 1898 transfer does not split the row.

## Key dates

- 1493: Spanish claim established (far outside this row's span; given for context only).
- 1898-04-21 to 1898-08-13: Spanish-American War ([wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §1898-treaty](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#1898-treaty)).
- 1898-12-10: Treaty of Paris — Spain cedes Puerto Rico to the United States ([wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §1898-treaty](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#1898-treaty)). The only sovereignty event inside the span.
- 1917-03-02: Jones-Shafroth Act confers US citizenship. Constitutional, not territorial.
- 1952-07-25: Commonwealth (*Estado Libre Asociado*) constitution. Constitutional, not territorial.

## Sourced claims

- Puerto Rico (with Cuba) was "Retained ... Until 1898" while every mainland Spanish American colony was lost between 1816 and 1824 ([wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §colonial-losses](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#colonial-losses)); the Treaty of Paris of 10 December 1898 then ceded it to the United States ([wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §1898-treaty](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#1898-treaty)).
- **Puerto Rico is absent from COW entirely.** Direct inspection of `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv` finds no tenure row for Puerto Rico under any `ccode` ([cow-state-system-v2024 §schema](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#schema) for the file's structure). This is expected rather than a gap: COW is a state-system list, and dependencies that never meet its recognition criteria "never appear in COW, even if they exist as historical polities" ([cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion), and limitation 1 of [cow-state-system-v2024 §known-limitations](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#known-limitations)). The row's `cow: NA` frontmatter is therefore correct.
- CShapes' dependency definition — "units that are under the control of an independent state, but that are not considered part of its core territory ... typically non-adjacent territories that are ruled as colonies or protectorates" ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)) — describes Puerto Rico's status in both halves of this row, which is why neither a Spanish nor a US *core-territory* polygon is the right binding.
- CShapes assigns disputed territory by de facto control and codes territorial change only above a 100 × 100 km threshold ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)); at 8,972 km² Puerto Rico is an order of magnitude below that threshold, so **even if** an intra-island change had occurred CShapes would not record it. Any future claim of territorial change here must come from a non-CShapes source.
- Geometric measurement (this ingest, not an external source): GADM `PRI` in ESRI:54034 = **8,972 km²**, from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`.
- Data footprint (this ingest): **936 layer-B rows** route to this code per `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/territory_basis.csv`, the largest count of any page that was a sub-1 KB uncited stub before this documentation pass.

## Open questions

### oq-pri-spanish-era-data-basis

The row's 936 layer-B rows straddle 1898. Whether the Spanish-era portion was compiled on the same territorial basis as the US-era portion (both "the island") or on some *Antilles* aggregate is not established by anything on disk, and the constant-extent argument above does not settle it: a source can report a constant territory under an inconstant label. The way to close this is `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/08_source_stated_areas.py` — check whether any yearbook states an area for its "Puerto Rico" label and compare it against 8,868-8,972 km².
