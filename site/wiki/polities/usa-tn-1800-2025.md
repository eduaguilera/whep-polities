# Tennessee (state of the United States)

## Summary

Tennessee is a present-day U.S. state occupying the region between the southern Appalachians and the Mississippi River, admitted to the Union on June 1, 1796 as the 16th state, after existing briefly as the "Southwest Territory" (Territory South of the River Ohio, organized 1790 from land Congress accepted from North Carolina's western cession). This entry is `type: subnational` because Tennessee has never held sovereignty of its own — it is, and always has been, a constituent unit of the United States — and it exists to carry any statistical series (agricultural, demographic, economic) that a data source reports at state-level granularity rather than folding into national U.S. totals. Unlike Alaska (ALK-1867-1959), which earned a bespoke code because it passed through a distinct named administrative status (Territory of Alaska) before statehood, Tennessee's own pre-statehood phase (the Southwest Territory, 1790-1796) is not represented by a data-bearing row anywhere in this table and is treated as history rather than as a polity to encode; Tennessee itself has had one continuous administrative identity since 1796 and so follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end coding convention used for the large majority of subnational rows. The entry's start_year (1800) is set four years later than Tennessee's actual admission year (1796) solely because no USA container era exists in the table before 1800; see the open question and decision notes below for the resulting gap.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is created ahead of any specific ingested data row, as part of the harness's systematic build-out of U.S. state-level subnational polities (the same policy that created Kentucky, Alabama, and the other USA-XX rows already in the table), implementing decision object unit_id USA-TENNESSEE. No prior data extract or row count is attached to this specific creation task — the justification is structural: the harness's routing pass found no existing polity representing Tennessee specifically, meaning any future data source reporting a "Tennessee" label at U.S.-state granularity would currently misroute to a national USA row roughly 73 times Tennessee's territory (the contiguous U.S. is about 8 million km2 against Tennessee's ~109,247 km2). This data was previously implicitly matched to nothing more specific than the national USA chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025), which is wrong for the same reason it was wrong for every other state: a national total cannot stand in for a single state's series without a roughly two-order-of-magnitude area distortion. Tennessee's status as a distinct, separately-admitted, continuously self-governing unit of the federal system is confirmed by the U.S. National Archives' own admission records (Tennessee admitted June 1, 1796, the first state carved from federal territory under the Southwest Territory Act rather than from an existing state's direct cession-to-statehood, as Kentucky and Vermont had been) and requires no additional historian's source to establish, unlike ambiguous historical trading-territory cases elsewhere in this table.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `assigned` from `gadm-4.1-adm1`, feature `USA.43_1`, the standard present-day GADM admin-1 boundary for the State of Tennessee. This is a direct, non-proxy assignment: Tennessee's external borders (Kentucky and Virginia to the north; North Carolina to the east along the Appalachian crest; Georgia, Alabama, and Mississippi to the south; Arkansas and Missouri across the Mississippi River to the west) have been essentially unchanged since admission in 1796, so the modern boundary is treated as valid for the entire 1800-2025 span with no reconstruction needed. Whether any 19th-century river-boundary shift along the Mississippi (a common source of small adjustments for river-bounded states) occurred is flagged as an open question rather than assumed away.

**Territory description:** Tennessee is a long, narrow state stretching roughly 750 km east-west between the Appalachian Mountains and the Mississippi River in the south-central United States, containing Nashville (the state capital), Memphis, Knoxville, and Chattanooga. On a modern map it is the state immediately south of Kentucky and Virginia, bordered by eight other states — more than any U.S. state except Missouri. Reported land area (U.S. Census Bureau) is approximately 109,247 km2 (42,181 sq mi). **This figure, and the figure measured from the attached GADM polygon, describe the same present-day shape; no independent 18th/19th-century territorial figure has been checked against it.**

## Predecessors and successors

Tennessee has no predecessor or successor polity row: its immediate pre-statehood phase, the Southwest Territory (Territory South of the River Ohio, 1790-1796), is not encoded anywhere in this polity table as a data-bearing row, so there is no row to list as a predecessor — the transition from territory to state in 1796 is an internal jurisdictional change with no corresponding pre-1796 entity in the database. Tennessee continues without interruption to the present day as a U.S. state, so the successor list is empty. The entry spans the entire ingestible USA container chain from USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025, four years short of Tennessee's actual 1796 admission for the reason given in the decisions section.

## Sourced claims

- Tennessee was admitted to the Union as the 16th state on June 1, 1796, having been organized as the Southwest Territory (Territory South of the River Ohio) since 1790 from land ceded by North Carolina (U.S. National Archives, statehood records).
- GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.43_1 delineates the present-day boundary of the State of Tennessee, approximately 109,247 km2, bordered by eight other states — Kentucky, Virginia, North Carolina, Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, Arkansas, and Missouri.
- Tennessee's land area has been reported at roughly 109,247 km2 (42,181 sq mi) by the U.S. Census Bureau in modern gazetteer files, consistent with the GADM polygon measurement used here.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1796

**start_year set to 1800, not Tennessee's 1796 admission year**

Tennessee was admitted to the Union on June 1, 1796 (the 16th state, organized from the Southwest Territory), and the harness's default rule for U.S. states is to use the year of formal admission. However, the earliest USA container era present in the polity table is USA-1800-1803 (data/final/polities_database.csv), and the containment gate rejects any edge falling outside either party's own registered span. Rather than fabricate a container edge into a non-existent pre-1800 USA era, this entry starts at 1800, the first year for which a valid container exists — the same truncation already applied to Kentucky (admitted 1792, also started at 1800). The 1796-1800 gap for Tennessee specifically is recorded as an open question below; a proper fix would add an earlier USA container era once the harness's container table is extended backward, but that is out of scope for this single-polity page.

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end majority pattern**

Tennessee is an ordinary present-day U.S. state with a continuous, unbroken administrative existence since 1796 and no distinct historical name of its own as a state (unlike Alaska's 1867-1959 Territory period, which justified the bespoke ALK code) — its pre-statehood Southwest Territory phase is not itself a row in this table and so does not create a naming conflict. It therefore follows the dominant 57-of-66 pattern already used for Kentucky, Alabama, and the other USA-XX rows: USA-TN-1800-2025, using the standard two-letter state postal abbreviation TN.

## Open questions

### oq-1796-1800-gap

**Tennessee's actual statehood (1796) predates this entry's start_year (1800) by 4 years**

Tennessee was formally admitted to the Union on June 1, 1796, but the earliest USA container era available in the polity table (data/final/polities_database.csv) is USA-1800-1803. This entry's start_year was set to 1800 rather than 1796 purely to satisfy the containment-tiling gate, which rejects a container edge falling outside either party's own registered span. This means 1796-1800 Tennessee statehood, and the preceding 1790-1796 Southwest Territory phase, are currently uncontained and unrepresented anywhere in the database — the same gap already open for Kentucky (oq-1792-1800-gap on usa-ky-1800-2025.md). The correct fix is likely a single earlier USA container era (e.g. USA-1790-1800) covering all such early-admission states at once, rather than a one-off patch per state; until that happens, any data keyed to Tennessee 1790-1799 has nowhere valid to route.

### oq-polygon-vintage

**GADM 4.1 boundary reflects present-day Tennessee; no check for 18th/19th-century boundary stability**

polygon_feature_id USA.43_1 (GADM 4.1 admin-1) is a modern boundary applied back to 1800. Tennessee's borders have been essentially stable since admission, but this has not been independently verified against a historical GIS layer or atlas for the earliest decades of statehood. If any 19th-century adjustment exists (e.g. along the Mississippi River boundary with Arkansas/Missouri, where channel migration has shifted river-defined state lines elsewhere in the country), polygon_status should be revisited from 'assigned' to 'proxy'.
