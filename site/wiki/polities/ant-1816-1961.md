# Dutch Caribbean (colonial)

## Summary

This row tracks the Dutch Caribbean island group — Curaçao, Bonaire, Sint Maarten (Dutch half), Sint Eustatius, and Saba — during Dutch colonial administration. **Aruba is deliberately excluded**: `ABW-1800-2025` models it as a parallel row across this whole span, so counting it here too would double-count the island (see the `ant-aruba-excluded` decision), from the formal re-establishment of Dutch sovereignty in 1816 (following the Napoleonic-era British occupation) through to 1960, when the successor statistical unit ANT-1961-2010 begins in WHEP. The Charter of the Kingdom of the Netherlands (in force 15 December 1954) converted the Netherlands Antilles from a colony into an autonomous country within the Kingdom, but no territorial change occurred at 1954 or 1961; the 1961 break follows the existing ANT-1961-2010 aggregate row. Suriname (South American mainland) was reported separately by FAO and is covered by SUR-1886-2025. This row fills the gap for 21 rows of fao1952 livestock/agricultural data labelled 'Netherlands W Indies' for years 1936-1951.

## Territorial Extent

- **Polygon source:** the `reporting-areas` feature `ANT-1961-2010`, which is a composed union of five islands — Curaçao, Bonaire, Sint Eustatius, Saba and Sint Maarten — and does **not** include Aruba. These are small Caribbean islands whose boundaries have not changed since the 1816 restitution. ESTIMATE flag: no historical GIS source (CShapes 2.0, Cliopatria, Paine-2024) covers this territory at any colonial-era date. Modern island boundaries are a faithful proxy for the colonial period given island geography.
- **Polygon method:** composed union of current island ADM0 polygons (ESTIMATE), borrowed from `ANT-1961-2010`.
- **Area:** approximately 800 km² total (Curaçao ~444 km², Bonaire ~288 km², Sint Maarten Dutch half ~34 km², Sint Eustatius ~21 km², Saba ~13 km²). The shipped polygon measures **794.6 km²**, or 0.993x this figure. Aruba's ~180 km² belongs to `ABW-1800-2025` and is excluded here.
- **Confidence: high.** Island geography makes modern polygons a faithful proxy for historical extent.

## Predecessors and Successors

- **Predecessor:** No WHEP predecessor row (territory was Dutch from the 17th century; WHEP temporal boundary at 1800; no separate row exists for the pre-1816 British occupation period).
- **Successor:** [ant-1961-2010](ant-1961-2010.md) — Netherlands Antilles (FAOSTAT/FABIO area code 151), 1961–2010. No territorial change at the 1960/1961 break; this is a statistical continuity boundary driven by the start year of the existing aggregate row.

## Sourced Claims

- The Charter of the Kingdom of the Netherlands came into force on 15 December 1954, converting the Netherlands Antilles from a colony into an autonomous country within the Kingdom (Wikipedia, Netherlands Antilles).
- The islands were restored to the Netherlands under the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814 and Congress of Vienna 1815; Dutch colonial administration resumed in 1816 (Wikipedia, Netherlands Antilles).
- Suriname was administered separately and reported as a distinct FAO unit; covered by SUR-1886-2025.
- Land area by island: Curaçao 444 km², Aruba 180 km², Bonaire 288 km², Sint Maarten (Dutch) 34 km², Sint Eustatius 21 km², Saba 13 km² (Wikipedia individual island articles). All six sum to 980 km²; the five modelled here sum to **800 km²**.

## Contradictions

- **The declared area used to include an island the polygon does not** (resolved 2026-08-24, issue 554).
  This page stated 960 km² while pointing `polygon_feature_id` at `ANT-1961-2010`, whose own page lists
  its components as "Bonaire/Sint Eustatius/Saba, Curacao, and Sint Maarten" — five islands, no Aruba.
  The shipped geometry measures 794.6 km², so the declared figure was 0.828x the polygon and the entire
  20.8% gap was Aruba's 180 km². It passed check A only because 0.828 sits inside the 25% tolerance.
  A second tell was internal: the six components listed on this page sum to **980**, not the 960 the page
  declared, so the page did not agree with itself either. Corrected to 800 km², the five-island sum, which
  the polygon reproduces to 0.993x.

## Decisions

- **Code ANT-1816-1961.** Uses the same ISO3 prefix ANT as the successor to signal statistical continuity. Start year 1816 reflects formal restoration of Dutch sovereignty post-Napoleonic occupation. The row's last covered year is 1960, the final year before the existing ANT-1961-2010 row begins; because `end_year` is EXCLUSIVE, that is written `end_year: 1961`.
- **ant-1960-hole-closed** (2026-08-17, issue 252) — `end_year` 1960 -> 1961, code renamed `ANT-1816-1960` -> `ANT-1816-1961`. The sentence above used to read "End year 1960 is the final year before the existing ANT-1961-2010 row begins", which is an INCLUSIVE reading of an exclusive column: with `end_year: 1960` this row covered 1816-1959 and 1960 fell in no ANT row at all. Evidence that 1960 was meant to be inside it, not outside: this row's own eleven published aliases ("Netherlands W Indies" and variants) all carry the inclusive `year_end: 1960`, and the repo's convention for a consistent pair is `end_year == year_end + 1`. Moving the polity boundary makes the pair consistent and clears ten lines from `scripts/validate_alias_year_baseline.txt`, where those aliases had been recorded as claiming a year their target could not cover. No territory changes: the polygon is the same composed-union proxy for every year of the span.
- **Type: colonial.** Islands were a Dutch colony/overseas territory throughout this period (formally autonomous within the Kingdom from 1954, but territory and reporting structure unchanged).
- **ant-aruba-excluded** (2026-08-24, issue 554) — `polygon_area_km2` 960 -> 800, and Aruba removed from
  the island list in the summary and the territorial extent. Aruba is modelled by `ABW-1800-2025`, which
  spans 1800-2025 and therefore runs in parallel with every year of this row; a row that also claimed
  Aruba would double-count it for 145 years. The polygon this page has always pointed at already excluded
  Aruba, so this changes no geometry — it makes the declared area describe the territory actually shipped.
- **Polygon: composed-union ESTIMATE.** No historical GIS source covers these small Caribbean islands for the colonial period. GADM 4.1 modern island polygons are the appropriate proxy; island geography ensures modern borders are identical to historical borders.

## Open Questions

### oq-ant-1816-start-year

The 1816 start year reflects the restoration of Dutch administration after the Napoleonic period. If WHEP sources contain data for years before 1816, the start year may need extension to 1800 (WHEP temporal boundary).

### oq-ant-pre-1816-polity

No WHEP row currently covers the Dutch Caribbean islands before 1816. If data from the 1800–1815 period is found, a separate row covering the British-occupation period (1807–1816) and/or the earlier Dutch colonial period (1800–1807) would be needed.
