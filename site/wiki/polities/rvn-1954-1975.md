# Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam)

## Summary

The Republic of Vietnam (Việt Nam Cộng hòa), commonly called South Vietnam,
existed from the Geneva Accords (21 July 1954) to the fall of Saigon
(30 April 1975), when it was absorbed by the Democratic Republic of Vietnam
and eventually merged into the unified Socialist Republic of Vietnam (1976).

The state occupied the southern half of the Indochina peninsula south of the
17th Parallel demilitarised zone (DMZ), corresponding approximately to the
former French protectorates of Cochinchina, southern Annam, and the Central
Highlands. Capital: Saigon (Ho Chi Minh City from 1976). COW code 817.

**Western statistical sources of the 1950s and 1960s — including Mitchell's
historical statistics — used "Vietnam" or "Viet Nam" to mean the RVN
exclusively**, as South Vietnam was the internationally recognised state and
the one admitted to UN-affiliated organisations. The DRV is referred to in
these sources as "North Vietnam" or not at all. Mitchell data labeled
"viet nam" for 1955–1974 should therefore be routed to this polity.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** CShapes 2.0, COW code 817, polygon vintage 1960. South Vietnam's
territory was defined by the 17th Parallel DMZ in the north (established by
the Geneva Accords 1954), with coastline on the South China Sea to the east
and south, and borders with Cambodia and Laos to the west. Area approximately
173,000–175,000 km² depending on measurement method.

The boundary did not change formally during 1954–1975, though de facto control
fluctuated substantially due to the Vietnam War. CShapes records the
internationally recognised boundary; no polygon split is required.
Polygon vintage 1960 is representative of the stable recognised extent; the
1954 borders are the same (the DMZ was fixed at Geneva).

**Why this entry exists:** Mitchell's pre-1975 "viet nam" rows (93 rows,
1955–1960 in the immediate need; potentially later years in other Mitchell
volumes) cannot be routed without this polity. The resolved_family before
this fix only included VNM-1975-2025, which begins 15+ years after the data.
Routing pre-1975 "viet nam" data to VNM-1975-2025 would be anachronistic and
spatially inexact: the RVN covered only the southern half of unified Vietnam.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [fid-1887-1954](fid-1887-1954.md) — French Indochina.
  The Geneva Accords of 21 July 1954 dissolved French Indochina and
  provisionally partitioned Vietnam at the 17th Parallel, establishing the
  State of Vietnam (later the Republic of Vietnam) in the south and the
  Democratic Republic of Vietnam in the north.
- **Successor:** [vnm-1975-2025](vnm-1975-2025.md) — unified Vietnam.
  After the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 the RVN ceased to exist; the
  Socialist Republic of Vietnam was proclaimed on 2 July 1976, merging North
  and South.

## Contradictions

- VNM-1975-2025 carries COW code 816 (which COW assigns to the DRV and then
  to unified Vietnam after 1975). This is correct for unified Vietnam but
  means RVN-1954-1975 (COW 817) and VNM-1975-2025 (COW 816) are not a simple
  code continuation. The two codes represent distinct entities in COW;
  CShapes has separate polygons for 816 and 817 pre-1975.

## Decisions

- Created 2026-06-24 to route Mitchell "viet nam" 1955–1974 data.

## Open questions

### oq-rvn-1954-1955-state-of-vietnam

COW records RVN membership from 1954-06-04 but the Republic of Vietnam was
only proclaimed after the October 1955 referendum (previously called the
State of Vietnam under Bảo Đại). WHEP uses 1954 as the start year to align
with the Geneva Accords partition and COW, treating the State of Vietnam and
the Republic of Vietnam as one continuous WHEP entity.

### oq-rvn-dmz-control

The 17th Parallel DMZ is used as the de jure boundary in the polygon. De
facto, large parts of the northern provinces were contested or under DRV
control by the early 1970s. Any area-normalised production statistics for
late RVN years (1970–1975) should be interpreted with this caveat.
