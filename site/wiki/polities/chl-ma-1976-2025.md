# Magallanes (region of Chile)

## Summary

Magallanes is one of Chile's sixteen first-order administrative regions (Region de Magallanes y de la Antartica Chilena), covering the country's southernmost mainland territory, the Chilean side of Tierra del Fuego and the Strait of Magellan, plus Chile's Antarctic claim. This entry captures it as a subnational reporting unit rather than a sovereign polity: `type: subnational` because Chile held sovereignty throughout, but statistics reported at the regional level (population, production, administrative data keyed to "Magallanes") describe a territory that is a small fraction of national Chile and must not be matched to the national CHL row. It was created because an earlier routing attempt matched Magallanes-labelled data to CHL-1902-2025, the national polity, which is wrong for the same structural reason flagged for the O'Higgins region: a subnational unit cannot be assigned the same territory as the whole country it sits inside. The entry starts in 1976, when Chile's current Regiones system (fixed by DL 573/575 of 1974) took effect and gave Magallanes its modern administrative form and boundary; no evidence is in hand of a stable, identically-bounded predecessor territory across the prior three-quarters of a century, so that earlier span is deliberately left unrouted rather than guessed at.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data reported under the "Magallanes" regional label for Chile from 1976 onward — the routing decision that motivated this page identified Magallanes as one of Chile's actual first-order regions, a genuine reporting territory rather than a container or a duplicate of the national total. That data was previously being matched (or was at risk of being matched, per the routing rejection) to CHL-1902-2025, the national Chilean polity — which is wrong because Magallanes is roughly 132,000 km2 against continental Chile's roughly 756,000 km2, and folding regional statistics into the national row would silently double-count or misattribute subnational figures as if they described the whole country. The Region de Magallanes y de la Antartica Chilena is confirmed as a distinct, formally constituted administrative and statistical entity by Chile's own regionalization law, Decreto Ley 573/575 of 1974 (effective 1976), which created the sixteen-region structure Chile's national statistics have been organized around ever since — the same structural basis already used for its sibling region O'Higgins (CHL-LI), whose routing was rejected for the identical double-claim-on-the-national-container problem.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the repository's local GeoPackages for this period. The correct registered source is `gadm-4.1-adm1` — GADM's global admin-1 layer is exactly the kind of source this repo already uses for other national first-order subdivisions (schema and `GID_1` id_column already match usage elsewhere) — but the GADM 4.1 file staged locally in this repo carries only a curated subset of about 81 countries, and Chile is not among them: zero Chilean features exist in the local file to select from. This is a data-availability gap, not a modelling decision: fetching the full global GADM 4.1 admin-1 release (or just the Chile slice) would resolve it directly, so `polygon_source` is recorded as the registered slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` with `polygon_status: unassigned` rather than inventing a placeholder.

**Territory description:** Magallanes y de la Antartica Chilena is Chile's southernmost region, comprising the mainland south of roughly 52°S, the Chilean portion of Tierra del Fuego, the islands and channels along the Strait of Magellan and the Beagle Channel, and (administratively, though not internationally recognized) Chile's claimed Antarctic sector. Its regional capital is Punta Arenas. On a modern map it is the wedge of southern Patagonia and Tierra del Fuego between the Argentine border (much of which runs along the Andes and across Tierra del Fuego) and the Pacific/Southern Ocean coastline. Excluding the Antarctic claim, the mainland-and-islands area is commonly cited at approximately 132,000 km2, making it Chile's largest region by area despite being one of its least populated. No polygon is attached yet, so this figure is a description of the territory from general reference geography, not a measurement of any geometry in this repository.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor polity is listed: the pre-1976 administrative territory referred to as Magallanes (Territorio/Provincia de Magallanes) is not represented by any existing row with a confirmed matching boundary, and that gap is recorded as an open question rather than bridged with a guess. No successor is listed either, since the region remains current at end_year 2025 (open end, matching the national CHL-1902-2025 row's own open end).

## Sourced claims

- Chile's Regiones administrative system, under which Magallanes became a first-order region (Region XII, Region de Magallanes y de la Antartica Chilena), was established by Decreto Ley 573 and 575 of 1974 and became operative from 1976, replacing the earlier provincial system.
- Magallanes y de la Antartica Chilena is the southernmost region of continental Chile, encompassing the Chilean portion of Tierra del Fuego, the Strait of Magellan, the city of Punta Arenas (the regional capital), and Chile's claimed Antarctic territory; its populated mainland and island area (excluding the Antarctic claim) is commonly cited at roughly 132,000 km2.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern**

Chose CHL-MA-1976-2025 following the 57-of-66 dominant subnational pattern (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end), matching the precedent set by ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CA-1833-2025 even though Chile has zero existing subnational codes to date. Magallanes y de la Antartica Chilena is a standard first-order administrative region, not a historical territory with a name of its own like Alaska or Hyderabad, so the bespoke-code pattern (3 of 66 rows) does not apply here. MA is the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for this region (CL-MA).

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-chile-missing

**Local GADM 4.1 admin-1 file lacks Chile entirely**

The registered source gadm-4.1-adm1 is the correct polygon source in principle (schema and id_column GID_1 already match how this repo uses GADM elsewhere for admin-1 regions), but the locally staged gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg carries only a curated 81-country subset that excludes Chile, so there are zero features to select a polygon_feature_id from. Fetching the full global GADM 4.1 release, or specifically the CHL slice of it, would resolve this without a new source registration. Until then polygon_status stays unassigned.

### oq-pre1976-magallanes

**Pre-1976 Magallanes territory (1900-1975) is unrouted**

Chile's Regiones system, and this specific administrative form of Magallanes, only became operative in 1976 under DL 573/575. Before that, the area existed under earlier administrative labels (e.g. Territorio de Magallanes, later Provincia de Magallanes), but no predecessor polity with a confirmed stable and distinct territory across 1900-1975 is in evidence here. Any data reporting on 'Magallanes' before 1976 is therefore left unrouted rather than folded into either the national CHL total (which would misattribute subnational data as national) or this 1976-defined region (whose administrative boundaries did not yet exist in that form). If a stable pre-1976 predecessor territory is confirmed, a separate earlier polity should be proposed with its own predecessor edge into this one.

### oq-ohiggins-consistency

**Consistency with the O'Higgins region fix**

This unit's original routing was rejected for the same reason as Chile's O'Higgins unit (CHL-LI): both made a double-claim on the national container CHL-1902-2025 as if they were the same territory as the national total. This page resolves Magallanes; whether the O'Higgins page uses an equivalent 1976-based span and the same gadm-4.1-adm1 unfetched-source treatment should be checked for consistency once both exist, so the two sibling regions do not diverge in convention.
