# Sardinia

## Summary

This row tracks the **Kingdom of Sardinia** (Piedmont-Sardinia) from
**1800** (WHEP database floor) to **1860** [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
The CSV has `notes = NA` and `verification_status = VERIFIED`.

The Kingdom of Sardinia was the legal continuator state of Italian
unification. At 1800 it included Piedmont, Savoy, Nice, and the island
of Sardinia. Lost to France during the Napoleonic Wars, the kingdom was
restored at the Congress of Vienna in 1815. Under Cavour's diplomacy
and in alliance with France against Austria (1859), Sardinia drove
Italian unification.

The CSV row ends at 1860, one year before Italian unification on
17 March 1861. The likely end-of-row event is the Treaty of Turin
(24 March 1860), in which Sardinia ceded Nice and Savoy to France as
payment for French military support against Austria
[biger-1995 §france-italy](../sources/biger-1995.md#france-italy).

Predecessor: NA (database floor).
Successor: [ita-1861-1919](ita-1861-1919.md) -- Italian unification,
17 March 1861. Victor Emmanuel II of Sardinia became King of Italy.

  Italy (using Sardinia as continuator) from 1816-01-01. The

  Sardinia as the pre-unification entity and Italy as its successor

  PIE-1816-1861 (Piedmont) with `cow=338` -- potential overlap. See
  [oq-sar-pie-overlap](#oq-sar-pie-overlap).

## Open questions

### oq-sar-pie-overlap

SAR-1800-1860 and PIE-1816-1861 share `cow=338` and overlap
1816-1860. These are candidate duplicate rows. Both refer to the
same political entity (the Kingdom of Sardinia / Piedmont-Sardinia).
The CSV may be using "SAR" for the pre-1816 period and "PIE" for
1816-1861 to align with COW's start date, but the 44-year overlap
(1816-1860) is unexplained. Tier X -- requires human decision on
whether these are duplicates.

### oq-1860-vs-1861

The CSV ends SAR at 1860 but Italy (ITA-1861-1919) starts at 1861.
Is there a 1-year gap? The likely explanation: 1860 is the year of
the Treaty of Turin (Nice/Savoy cession, March 1860) and the
plebiscites in central Italy (March 1860), while 1861 is the formal
proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy (17 March 1861). The "gap" is
the 11-month window between the last major Sardinian territorial
event (Nice/Savoy cession) and the formal Italian proclamation.
This is a coding convention rather than a real gap -- during this
period the entity was still the Kingdom of Sardinia, just without
Nice and Savoy. But the CSV does not have a row covering Sardinia
in 1860-1861.

### oq-nice-savoy-cession

Nice and Savoy were ceded to France in March 1860 (Treaty of Turin,
24 March 1860). This is the CSV end-of-row event for SAR but also
appears on [fra-1800-1919](fra-1800-1919.md) as a mid-row territorial
gain. The asymmetry (row-ending event for SAR, mid-row for FRA) is
a recurring pattern in the WHEP CSV and not specific to this case.
