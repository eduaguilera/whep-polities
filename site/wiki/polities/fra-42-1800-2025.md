# Loire (department, France)

## Summary

Loire is a French departement (INSEE code 42, administrative seat Saint-Etienne, NUTS-3 code FRK25) in the Auvergne-Rhone-Alpes region, one of the roughly 83 original departements created by the Revolutionary territorial reform of December 1789/January 1790 as part of France's replacement of the old provinces with a rationalized administrative grid. This entry is `type: subnational`: France held sovereignty over Loire throughout, and the departement is a sub-national reporting and administrative tier, not an independent state, so it must sit below the national FRA chain rather than compete with it. The routing decision that created this page found the unit unremarkable within the country's documented departement conventions -- no name collision with an existing polity, and none of the four known departures from the default rule (the 1871-1919 Belfort/Alsace-Lorraine split, the 1960s Corse split, or the overseas departements) apply to Loire. Loire briefly merged with the neighboring Rhone departement in 1793 to form Rhone-et-Loire before the two were separated again later that year; aside from that early adjustment its boundaries have been stable since creation, which is why the modern GADM ADM1 shape is expected to be a workable eventual proxy once fetched (see territorial extent and open questions).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures departement-level data for Loire (France), covering the 1862-2023 span as identified in the routing decision (admin_name FRK25, source country "France"). That data was previously being routed to the national row FRA-1919-2025 (or its predecessor national rows) by default, which is wrong because national France aggregates all ~96 metropolitan departements plus overseas territories -- roughly 20x the geographic and reporting scope of Loire alone -- collapsing a departement-level reporting series into a country total it was never measured as. The routing decision's own reasoning establishes this is a standard, unremarkable departement with no existing polity match, triggering creation under the documented country-level policy for French departements: sub-national administrative/statistical units that predate and outlive whatever regime governs France nationally get their own row so that departement-level series are not silently folded into national totals. Historically, Loire's status as a distinct, continuously named administrative unit since 1790 (with the brief 1793 Rhone-et-Loire merger noted in territorial extent) is itself the confirmation of distinctness -- it has been tabulated as its own unit by INSEE and predecessor French statistical agencies for over two centuries, independent of this specific routing decision's data.

## Territorial extent

No polygon is available locally for this period. `polygon_source` is `gadm-4.1-adm1`, a registered source whose schema covers exactly this kind of unit (country ADM1/department-level features), but the routing decision found the locally-fetched GADM 4.1 ADM1 file has zero features for France -- the global download that includes FRA has not been fetched into this repository yet. `polygon_status` is therefore `unassigned` (registered_source_unfetched route), not `none`: the correct source is known and named, it simply is not present on disk, so no `polygon_area_km2` is recorded and none should be inferred.

**Territory description:** Loire departement sits in east-central France, in the Auvergne-Rhone-Alpes region, centered on the industrial city of Saint-Etienne (the departement seat) with the town of Roanne in its north. It borders the departements of Rhone (west, historically once united with Loire as Rhone-et-Loire in 1793), Saone-et-Loire, Allier, Haute-Loire, and Ardeche. On a modern map it is the area running roughly from the Roanne plain in the north down through the Monts du Forez and the Saint-Etienne basin to the department's southern tip near the source region of the Loire river, from which it takes its name. Its area today is approximately **4,780 km²** (a commonly cited modern INSEE figure for departement 42) -- this is a reference figure from general geographic knowledge, not a measurement of any polygon attached to this entry, since no shape has been fetched. Once gadm-4.1-adm1 (or an INSEE/IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS layer) is fetched for France, the actual measured polygon area should replace this estimate and populate `polygon_area_km2`.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Start year set to 1800, not the 1790 default from the France convention**

The routing decision's span_basis applies this country's documented default for original Revolutionary departements: start_year = 1790, the year of creation. That default cannot be honored here because the container gate requires the container chain to tile the polity's span with no gap, and the earliest French national polity row in this database, FRA-1800-1871, begins in 1800 -- there is no row at all covering 1790-1800. Rather than leave a decade uncontained (which the containment gate rejects), this entry's start_year is set to 1800 to match the earliest available container edge. The departement itself is administratively continuous from 1790; the discrepancy is a gap in the national polity table, not a claim that Loire did not exist as a unit before 1800. Fixing it properly would mean adding a FRA row for 1790-1800 (or extending FRA-1800-1871 back to 1790), which is outside the scope of authoring one subnational page and is flagged as an open question below.

### d-subunit-code-42-not-loi

**Subunit token is the INSEE department number 42, not a letter abbreviation**

The dominant pattern for subnational codes in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the Spanish examples (ESP-AS, ESP-CA) use short letter abbreviations. French departements, however, are officially and unambiguously identified by a two-digit INSEE number (Loire = 42), which is how the source datasets (mitchell, INSEE, IGN) key departement-level records, including the NUTS code FRK25 cited in the routing decision. Using '42' avoids inventing an abbreviation with no standard form for this specific departement -- several other French departements would collide on a 3-letter truncation of 'Loire' (Haute-Loire, Loire-Atlantique) -- and instead reuses an identifier already stable across sources since 1790. Resulting code: FRA-42-1800-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-fra-1790-1800-container-gap

**No FRA national polity row exists for 1790-1800**

This entry's start_year had to move from the country convention's default of 1790 to 1800 solely because the container chain has no FRA row covering that decade (data_gap: FRA-1800-1871 is currently the earliest French national polity). If a future ingest adds an earlier FRA row (e.g. FRA-1790-1800 or an extension of FRA-1800-1871 back to 1790), this entry's start_year and container edges should be revisited so Loire's span matches its true creation date rather than being truncated by an unrelated gap in the national chain.

### oq-loire-boundary-stability-unverified

**Loire's 1790 boundaries have not been checked against its present-day INSEE 42 boundaries**

The routing decision's own routing_concerns flag this directly: 'Confirm Loire is indeed an original 1790 departement and not a later split/renamed unit.' France's departement map has had several 19th-century adjustments (e.g. Belfort split from Haut-Rhin in 1871, Savoie/Haute-Savoie added in 1860), and Loire itself was reduced in 1793 when the short-lived Rhone-et-Loire department was split back into Rhone and Loire. Whether that 1793 split changed Loire's own boundary, or only affected the neighboring Rhone department, has not been verified here and should be checked before treating the modern GADM ADM1 boundary as a safe proxy back to 1800.

### oq-polygon-not-fetched

**No polygon is attached; gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered but the local file excludes France**

polygon_status is unassigned because the registered source gadm-4.1-adm1 does not yet include FRA in its locally-fetched subset (0 features for this country), per the routing decision's polygon_reasoning. Fetching the full global GADM 4.1 ADM1 download (or substituting French IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS, which would need registering as a new source) would resolve this.
