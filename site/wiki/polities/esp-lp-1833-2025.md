# Las Palmas (province of Spain)

## Summary

Las Palmas is a Spanish province in the eastern Canary Islands, comprising the islands of Gran Canaria, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura (plus minor islets such as the Chinijo archipelago). It corresponds to NUTS unit ES705 and is the source of the mitchell/statistical extract that motivated this entry's creation. The territory has been under continuous Spanish provincial administration since the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, first as part of a single undivided Canary Islands province shared with the western islands (Tenerife, La Palma, La Gomera, El Hierro), and from 1927 onward as its own province, Las Palmas, after the Canary Islands were split into two provinces. type is set to subnational because this is a first-order administrative division of the sovereign Spanish state, not an independent country: it is contained within ESP-1800-2025 for its entire span and does not compete with the national ESP row for data routing. The entry was created because 20+ years of data keyed to NUTS ES705 exist back to 1865 -- before the province even had its current name and borders -- and no existing polity in the table represents this island group; the nearest province candidates (A Coruna, Madrid) are unrelated mainland territories, and routing to Spain-national would erase a reporting distinction the source data itself preserves.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: at minimum a mitchell-family extract keyed to NUTS unit ES705 ("Las Palmas"/Gran Canaria), Spain, with a stated data range beginning 1865 (per the routing decision's justification for starting the span before 1927) through the modern period; exact row count was not itemized in the routing payload but the decision explicitly treats this as a genuine reporting-territory series, not a one-off value. Previously this territory had no matching polity row at all: the routing reasoning states that the country's existing province candidates (A Coruna, Madrid) are unrelated mainland territories and the Spain-national row (ESP-1800-2025) is too broad to carry province-level series without conflating it with 49 other Spanish provinces already routed to their own rows. What confirms this is a distinct reporting entity is the country's own administrative convention: Spain has organized statistics by province since 1833, Las Palmas/Canary Islands among them, and the source data's persistence back to 1865 demonstrates the unit was measured under the pre-1927 administration too, not invented by the later name change. The 49 other existing ESP-<SUBUNIT>-1833-2025 rows already establish province-level entries as the correct granularity for this country, so this entry brings Las Palmas into parity with siblings like ESP-B (Barcelona) and ESP-BI (Bizkaia) rather than leaving its data unmatched or misrouted to the national total.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is attached in this page: the routing decision names `mapspain-ign` (a registered Spanish province boundary source keyed by `cpro` province code, already used for 7 other ESP provinces including Asturias, Cadiz and Cordoba) as the correct source, but its file (`data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg`) is not yet fetched locally (`present_locally: False` in `scripts/sources.yaml`). No area figure is given on this page, measured or otherwise, because no geometry is attached yet.

**Territory description (locatable on a modern map):** Las Palmas province occupies the eastern Canary Islands: Gran Canaria (~1,560 km2), Lanzarote (~846 km2), Fuerteventura (~1,660 km2), and the small Chinijo archipelago (La Graciosa, Alegranza, Montana Clara), for a combined land area of approximately 4,065 km2 (published INE/IGN figure for the current province, not measured from any attached polygon on this page). The provincial capital is Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. It sits roughly 100-150 km off the coast of Western Sahara/southern Morocco in the Atlantic Ocean, and is bounded to the west by the sibling province of Santa Cruz de Tenerife (covering the remaining Canary Islands: Tenerife, La Palma, La Gomera, El Hierro). Before 1927 the same territory (Gran Canaria, Lanzarote, Fuerteventura) was administered as the eastern half of a single, larger Canary Islands province that also included the western islands; the 1927 reform redrew the internal boundary between the two new provinces along the existing island groupings rather than creating new territory.

## Sourced claims

- Spain's modern provincial system, including the Canary Islands province, dates to the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833, which divided the country into 49 provinces.
- The Canary Islands province was split into two provinces, Las Palmas and Santa Cruz de Tenerife, by a decree of 23 September 1927, dividing the archipelago along its existing eastern/western island groupings rather than redrawing island boundaries.
- Las Palmas province corresponds to NUTS-3 code ES705 and comprises the islands of Gran Canaria, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura, with a combined land area of approximately 4,065 km2 per INE/IGN published figures.
- mitchell-family and related statistical series keyed to this unit report data as far back as 1865, predating the 1927 provincial split by over six decades.

## Decisions

### d-start-1833-not-1927

**Span starts at 1833 (system floor), not 1927 when the Las Palmas name/border took effect**

The country convention lists Las Palmas as a named departure beginning in 1927, the year the Canary Islands province was split into Las Palmas and Santa Cruz de Tenerife. This page departs from that literal marker and starts at 1833 instead, because the underlying territory -- the island of Gran Canaria plus Lanzarote and Fuerteventura -- was already administered continuously as part of the single, undivided Canary Islands province from the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform onward, and mitchell-family data keyed to this unit's NUTS code (ES705) runs back to 1865, i.e. before the 1927 split existed. 1927 marks a rename/border-drawing event inside a continuous administrative lineage, not the creation of new territory or new administration where none existed. The alternative -- creating a separate 'Canary Islands 1833-1927' predecessor polity and starting this entry only at 1927 -- was considered and rejected for now because no sibling row for the Santa Cruz de Tenerife half of that split has been processed in this pass; splitting unilaterally risks creating an orphan lineage that a future Santa Cruz de Tenerife entry would have to reconcile against. This is flagged as the open question oq-canary-islands-predecessor below, per the routing_concerns passed into this page.

### d-subunit-code-lp

**Subunit code LP follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern used by other Spanish provinces**

419 of 433 subnational rows in the table follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and all 50 existing ESP subnational codes (ESP-17, ESP-A, ESP-AB, ESP-B, ESP-BI, ESP-BU, etc.) already follow it for Spanish provinces specifically. LP is the standard vehicle-plate/postal abbreviation for Las Palmas province in Spain, matching the two-letter style already used for other provinces in this table (e.g. BI for Bizkaia, BU for Burgos). No bespoke code is used here because, unlike Alaska or Hawaii, Las Palmas province has no distinct pre-Spanish or pre-provincial historical identity that would justify departing from the ISO3-SUBUNIT convention -- it is a standard province within the ordinary Spanish administrative hierarchy.

## Open questions

### oq-canary-islands-predecessor

**Should the pre-1927 undivided Canary Islands province be split out as its own polity?**

This entry currently spans 1833-2025 as a single continuous polity, treating the pre-1927 undivided Canary Islands province and the post-1927 Las Palmas province as one lineage. An alternative structure -- adopted if a sibling row is created for Santa Cruz de Tenerife (the other half of the 1927 split) -- would instead create a separate 'Canary Islands 1833-1927' predecessor polity shared as a common ancestor by both post-1927 provinces, with this entry's span shortened to 1927-2025 and predecessor set to that shared polity. Whether to make that change should be decided together with, not independently of, whatever the Santa Cruz de Tenerife routing pass proposes; if that sibling pass has already run or runs later and creates such a predecessor, this page's frontmatter (start_year, predecessor, container) needs to be revised to match rather than leaving two independent unreconciled proposals for the same 1833-1927 territory.
