# Borgu Confederacy

## Summary

Borgu was a loose confederation of kingdoms — principally Bussa, Nikki, Illo, and Kaiama — straddling what is now the Nigeria-Benin border region. It was never a centralised state; the constituent polities shared cultural and dynastic traditions (the Kisra origin legend) but each maintained its own ruler. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1897, when Britain and France partitioned the territory between their spheres of influence. The eastern portion (Bussa, Kaiama) became part of the British Niger Coast Protectorate, later Nigeria; the western portion (Nikki) went to French Dahomey. The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). Borgu occupied the savanna and woodland zone between the Niger River to the east and the Atakora Mountains to the west, covering parts of modern north-central Benin and northwestern Nigeria.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** none recorded in WHEP; the Borgu polities predate the 1800 database floor.
- **Successor:** [NGA-1914-1960](nga-1914-1960.md) — the eastern Borgu territories passed into British Nigeria.
- **Successor (western portion):** not separately tracked in WHEP; the French Dahomey portion eventually becomes [BEN-1960-2025](ben-1960-2025.md) (modern Benin).

## Open questions

### oq-western-partition-successor

The CSV successor field lists only NGA-1914-1960, but the western (French) portion of Borgu (Nikki and surroundings) became part of Dahomey / Benin. A separate successor link to BEN-1898-1960 or equivalent is missing. Is there a WHEP row for French Dahomey that should absorb the western Borgu territory?

### oq-confederacy-unity

Borgu was not a unified state; treating it as a single WHEP polity implies a shared territorial unit. The Paine et al. polygon presumably captures the outer extent of all member states. Whether this approach is appropriate or whether Bussa, Nikki etc. should each be separate rows is an open methodological question for future review.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger covers Nigeria only at the independence/colonial level and does not discuss the Borgu partition. A specialist source (e.g., Lombard 1965 *Structures de type "féodal" en Afrique noire*, or Biobaku 1952) is needed.
