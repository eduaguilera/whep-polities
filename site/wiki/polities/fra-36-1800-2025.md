# Indre

## Summary

This entry represents Indre, a French département in the Centre-Val de Loire region, in the historic province of Berry, south-west of Bourges and centred on the town of Châteauroux. It is coded `subnational` because a département is an administrative subdivision of France, not a sovereign state — France's sovereignty over this area is continuous and undisputed throughout the span, and the national FRA rows already carry that sovereignty claim for the whole country. This row exists solely to carry a sub-national reporting territory: statistics keyed to "Indre" (NUTS3 code FRB03) rather than all-France totals need somewhere to route that is not the national polygon. The entry follows the country convention's default start rule (1790, the year of the Revolutionary départements reform), adjusted here to 1800 to match the earliest available French national container row (see decisions), and runs to the open end_year 2025 since the département remains an active administrative unit of France today with no successor.

**Why this entry exists.** This page implements the routing decision for unit FRA-FRB03 (admin_name "FRB03", country France), which resolved via create_new because no existing FRA polity IS this territory — the national FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) describe the whole country, and a département is not represented by its country's row. The routing reasoning explicitly notes Indre is one of the original ~83 Revolutionary départements created in 1790, retains its present-day form, and is not one of the four listed departure cases in the country convention (Belfort, the Alsace-Lorraine trio, the Corse split, or overseas départements), so no match_existing candidate was available and no bespoke span applies. Before this decision the data for this unit was unmatched by construction of the routing pipeline, not misrouted to a wrong polity. What confirms Indre as a distinct, statistically-reported entity is that it is one of the original départements created by the 1790 reform and has been tabulated ever since under the numeric code 36 in INSEE's statistical apparatus and under NUTS3 code FRB03 in Eurostat series — the same institutional status every other département in this table's sibling FRA entries (Cher/18, Seine-et-Marne/77) already carries. The data extract underlying the routing decision starts in 1862, but this entry's span (1790, adjusted to 1800-2025) covers the full administrative existence per the country convention rather than being trimmed to the extract's narrower data window, so a single create_new polity suffices with no back_cast or matched segments.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision already established this is a `new_source_needed` case: no source registered in scripts/sources.yaml digitises French départements at this resolution. I confirmed this directly by reading the locally-registered gadm-4.1-adm1 GeoPackage (data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg): its COUNTRY column lists only French overseas territories (French Southern Territories, Guadeloupe, French Guiana, Martinique, Mayotte, New Caledonia, French Polynesia) and omits metropolitan France entirely — 0 features for continental départements including Indre — even though GADM's global release does cover them. cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe are country-level only and cannot supply a sub-national boundary either. The standard remedy is to register GADM's global admin-1 layer or French IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product, neither of which is on disk in this repository today.

**Territory description:** Indre occupies the south-western part of the Centre-Val de Loire region, in the historic province of Berry. On a modern map it is bounded by Indre-et-Loire to the north-west, Loir-et-Cher and Cher to the north-east/east, Creuse to the south, Haute-Vienne to the south-west, and Vienne to the west; its préfecture is Châteauroux. It covers approximately 6,791 km2 — a published administrative-area statistic for the modern département, not a measurement from any attached polygon, since no polygon is assigned to this entry (see status above). The département is predominantly agricultural (cereals, sheep farming in the Brenne wetlands to the west) with the Indre and Creuse rivers crossing its territory, and includes the Parc naturel régional de la Brenne.

## Predecessors and successors

Indre has no predecessor or successor polity in this table: it was created directly by the 1790 Revolutionary départements reform (predating this database's 1800 coverage floor, see the open question below) and has continued as an administrative unit of France without interruption through 2025, so both fields are empty. It is not known to have been split off from, merged into, or ceded to any other polity tracked here, and it was not among the départements affected by the 1871 Alsace-Lorraine cession or its 1919 return, since it lies in the interior of the country far from that border region.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the proposed 1790, because no container exists for the earlier decade**

The routing decision's span_basis argues for 1790, the year of the Revolutionary départements reform that created Indre. That is very likely correct as administrative history, but this database's earliest French national row is FRA-1800-1871 — there is no FRA row of any kind covering 1790-1800, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's span. Rather than leave a decade uncontained, or invent a container edge citing a national polity that does not exist in the table, I moved start_year to 1800 to match the earliest available container, exactly as the sibling entries FRA-18-1800-2025 (Cher) and FRA-77-1800-2025 (Seine-et-Marne) already did for the same reason. The polity code year (1800) and the frontmatter start_year (1800) are kept equal, satisfying the gate that ties code years to frontmatter years. If a pre-1800 French national polity is added later, this entry's start_year should be revisited alongside the other original départements, per the open question below.

### d-subunit-code-36-not-nuts

**Subunit code is the INSEE/ISO 3166-2 numeric code 36, not the NUTS3 code from the routing decision**

The dominant pattern for subnational rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the routing decision's own admin_name for this unit is 'FRB03', a Eurostat NUTS3 code. French départements do not have ISO 3166-2 letter codes in general public use, and NUTS3 codes are not how French statistical sources or the public identify one. The standard identifier for Indre, used by INSEE, La Poste, and ISO 3166-2:FR alike, is the two-digit number 36. I used FRA-36-1800-2025 rather than the NUTS3 code, matching the convention already set by sibling entries FRA-18-1800-2025 (Cher) and FRA-77-1800-2025 (Seine-et-Marne), since 36 is the identifier a reader would actually recognise on a map, car plate, or postcode prefix (36xxx).

### d-polygon-unassigned-not-proxy

**Polygon left unassigned rather than proxied from a neighbouring département or the national polygon**

I checked the locally-registered gadm-4.1-adm1 GeoPackage directly and confirmed it excludes metropolitan France (only French overseas territories appear in its COUNTRY column), and cshapes-europe/cshapes-2.0 are country-level only. I did not substitute the national FRA polygon or a neighbouring département's shape as a coarse proxy, because the whole point of this row is to isolate Indre's own reporting territory from the national one; a substitute shape would defeat that purpose silently and misstate territorial extent. polygon_source is set to the 'none' sentinel used by other unassigned rows in this table (e.g. FRA-18-1800-2025, FRA-77-1800-2025), matching existing convention rather than inventing a new placeholder.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1800-gap-uncovered

**The 1790-1800 decade of Indre's existence has no container in this table**

Indre was created in 1790 by the Revolutionary départements reform, but this entry's start_year was moved to 1800 (see decisions) because no FRA national row covers 1790-1800 — the earliest is FRA-1800-1871. This mirrors the same open gap already flagged on sibling entries FRA-18-1800-2025 (Cher) and FRA-77-1800-2025 (Seine-et-Marne). If a pre-1800 French national polity is ever added to close this gap, Indre's start_year (and those of its siblings) should be revisited together rather than one at a time, to avoid re-litigating the same decision piecemeal.

### oq-no-source-data-checked

**No underlying data rows for FRA-FRB03 were inspected directly in authoring this page**

This page was authored from the routing decision object alone, which states a data extract exists starting in 1862 but does not give a row count, source name, or measure. Unlike the Manchuria exemplar, which quantifies its underlying data (13 mitchell rows, 7 fao1952 rows) and cross-checks a stated territory figure against two independent sources, no equivalent verification was performed here: it is unconfirmed which dataset(s) actually key to FRA-FRB03, how many rows, or whether any published source states Indre's own km² independently of the modern administrative figure quoted above. This should be checked against the actual ingest/routing pipeline output before treating this entry's 'why this entry exists' section as fully corroborated.

### oq-gadm-global-release-not-registered

**A full GADM 4.1 admin-1 release covering metropolitan France is known to exist but is not registered as a data source**

The routing decision states GADM's global release does include French départements, and I independently confirmed the locally-installed subset (data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg) omits them. It remains open whether registering the full global release (rather than IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS) is the intended remedy, what licence terms would apply, and whether doing so would also resolve the same gap for the other unmatched FRA départements (FRA-18, FRA-28, FRA-77, FRA-91) simultaneously — in which case this should probably be tracked as one shared follow-up rather than five separate per-entry footnotes.
