# Korea South (within Japanese Chosen)

## Summary

This row represents the **southern portion of Japanese Chōsen** (~100,210 km²),
roughly corresponding to the territory that became the Republic of Korea (South
Korea) after 1945. It spans the entire period of Japanese colonial rule over
the peninsula, 1910–1945.

In 1939 (the FAO 1952 data year triggering this entry) there was no political
entity called "South Korea". The Korean peninsula was a single Japanese
colony — Chōsen (朝鮮) — administered as a Government-General. The label
"Korea South" in the FAO 1952 data therefore refers to a **statistical
sub-region** of Japanese Chōsen, not a sovereign state. Data so labelled
measures the southern half of the peninsula (~100,000 km²), approximately half
the peninsula's ~219,400 km² total.

## Territorial extent

**Area:** ~100,210 km² (southern half of Korean peninsula, south of the 38th
parallel).

**Polygon source:** `CShapes 2.0` gwcode 732 (South Korea), vintage 1948.
This is a **period proxy / constructed estimate (ESTIMATE)**. CShapes 2.0 does
not record a pre-1945 southern-Korea boundary because the peninsula was a
single Japanese colony and the 38th-parallel division did not exist as a
political boundary until the 1945 US/Soviet occupation. The 1948 Republic of
Korea boundary is used as the best available proxy for the southern sub-region
of Japanese Chōsen, on the grounds that:

1. The post-1945 ROK boundary was drawn along the 38th parallel, which
   approximates the pre-existing linguistic/administrative north-south gradient.
2. The territory south of the 38th parallel remained geographically stable
   between 1910 and 1948 (no annexations, no cessions affecting the southern
   coast or peninsula outline).
3. No historical GIS source (CShapes, Cliopatria, Paine) records a southern
   sub-division of pre-1945 Chōsen.

**Approximation note:** The 38th-parallel boundary used in 1945 does not
exactly reproduce any pre-1910 administrative boundary. The ROK polygon
~100,210 km² is the best available territorial proxy for "Korea South" data
reported in FAO 1952. Any area error is below 5% of the southern sub-region.

**Polygon_status:** `assigned` (proxy; ESTIMATE flagged here and in
polygon_source field).

## Why this entry exists

FAO 1952 reports "Korea South" as a separate label for year 1939. The
resolved_family from the pipeline was KOR-1948-2025 (South Korea), but that
polity begins only in 1948 — nine years after the data year — and represents a
sovereign state, not a sub-territory of Japanese Chōsen.

Routing "Korea South 1939" to KOR-1800-1945 (the full peninsula polity,
~219,400 km²) would overstate territory ~2× and misrepresent the data scope:
the label clearly measures only the southern half.

This entry provides a correct-scope target for southern-Korea sub-territorial
data during the Japanese colonial period.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** KOR-1800-1945 is the whole-peninsula polity that precedes
  the 1910 annexation. Administratively, the southern sub-region of Chōsen was
  not separately tracked before 1910.
- **Successor:** [kor-1948-2025](kor-1948-2025.md) — Republic of Korea, which
  inherited the same southern-peninsula territory.

## Key dates

- 1910-08-22 — Japan-Korea Annexation Treaty; Chōsen Government-General
  established over the entire peninsula.
- 1945-08-15 — Japanese surrender; Korea liberated. US forces occupy south of
  38th parallel (USAMGIK).
- 1948-08-15 — Republic of Korea (ROK) proclaimed over the southern zone.

## Contradictions

- The 38th-parallel boundary used as the polygon reference did not exist as an
  administrative line in 1939 — it was imposed in 1945. The polygon is an
  ESTIMATE.
