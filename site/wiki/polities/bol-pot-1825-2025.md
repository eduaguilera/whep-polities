# Potosí (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

Potosí is a department (admin-1 unit) of Bolivia occupying the southwestern Andean highlands and altiplano, one of the original departments organized at the republic's founding in 1825/1826 alongside Chuquisaca, La Paz, Cochabamba, Santa Cruz, and Oruro. Its capital, the city of Potosí, sits beneath the Cerro Rico silver mountain that made colonial Alto Perú one of the wealthiest mining regions in the Spanish empire; the department has remained a distinct administrative and statistical reporting unit from independence to the present. This entry is `type: subnational` because Potosí has never been sovereign — Bolivia held sovereignty throughout — and the row exists purely to carry department-level reporting territory, the same convention already applied to Beni (BOL-BEN-1842-2025) and to Spain's and Argentina's provinces elsewhere in this table. Unlike Beni or Pando, Potosí was not created by a later separation from a neighboring department; it dates to the country's founding, so its span opens at 1825 rather than at a documented split date, and it remains a current department, so end_year is the open 2025 bound rather than a dissolution.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Bolivia's Potosí department as a first-level administrative reporting unit, following the same convention just established for BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and applied across this batch (Chuquisaca, La Paz, Oruro, Potosí) of original 1825 Bolivian departments. The routing decision (unit_id BOL-POTOSI) states that no existing BOL candidate represents Potosí specifically — the only prior BOL rows are national-level entries spanning the whole country — so a department is not adequately represented by folding its statistics into the national row. The routing_reasoning notes Potosí is not among the three departments the country convention documents as later separations (Tarija, Beni 1842, Pando 1938), so the default 1825/1826 founding-era start applies, mirroring how all of Bolivia's original departments were established at independence. What confirms Potosí is a distinct, long-standing administrative entity rather than a matcher artefact is that it has been one of Bolivia's constitutive departments since the republic's founding, centered on the Cerro Rico mining district, and has appeared as a distinct first-level reporting unit in Bolivian administrative and statistical records ever since — the same class of evidence (continuous administrative distinctness) that justified Beni's entry, though the specific dataset/row count driving this particular routing decision was not itself documented in the decision object and remains an open question below.

**Territory description:** Potosí department occupies Bolivia's southwestern highlands and altiplano, bordering Chile and Argentina, and is home to the Cerro Rico silver mountain above the city of Potosí — the department capital and the site that gave colonial Bolivia (Alto Perú) its historic silver-mining economy. On a modern map, present-day Potosí department covers approximately 118,200 km2, Bolivia's second-largest department by area, spanning high desert plateau (including part of the Salar de Uyuni salt flat) down to lower valleys in its eastern reaches. No km2 figure is derived from any attached geometry here since none is assigned; the 118,200 km2 figure is the modern department's commonly cited administrative area, offered only to help a reader locate the territory, not as a measurement of this entry's (currently absent) polygon. Whether this present-day extent is a valid proxy for the department's 19th-century boundary — particularly given border adjustments in the region during and after the 1879-1884 War of the Pacific — is flagged as an open question below.

## Decisions

### d-code-pot-three-letter

**Used BOL-POT-1825-2025 rather than BOL-POTOSI or a single-letter ISO 3166-2 code**

Bolivia's ISO 3166-2 code for Potosí is BO-P (a single letter), but the dominant pattern in this table for subnational rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short mnemonic (e.g. ARG-CAT, ESP-CA), not the raw ISO 3166-2 letter, and BOL-BEN already set a three-letter precedent for this country. The routing decision's own unit_id used the full string 'BOL-POTOSI', but every other BOL subnational code in the table (BOL-BEN, plus the sibling Chuquisaca/La Paz/Oruro pages created in this batch) uses a 2-3 letter abbreviation, so POT was chosen for consistency rather than carrying the routing unit_id verbatim into the polity code.

### oq-potosi-territorial-loss-1879

**Whether the modern GADM Potosí boundary is a valid proxy for the department's pre-1879 extent**

Potosí department, like Bolivia as a whole, was affected by the War of the Pacific (1879-1884): Bolivia's coastal department (the Litoral) was lost to Chile, and border adjustments in the southwestern highlands in this period may have touched Potosí's own boundary, since Potosí borders the area that became northern Chile. A modern GADM adm1 polygon for Potosí reflects present-day borders and would be an anachronistic proxy for the 1825-1884 extent if any such adjustment occurred; this has not been checked against a period map or 19th-century Bolivian administrative source, and no polygon is attached in this entry to test the question, so it should be examined before this row's polygon_status moves past 'unassigned' to 'assigned' rather than 'proxy'.
