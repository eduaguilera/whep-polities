# Chuquisaca (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

Chuquisaca is a department (admin-1 unit) of south-central Bolivia, in the eastern Andean valleys and foothills, whose capital Sucre was the seat of the 1825 congress that founded the Bolivian republic and remains the country's constitutional capital and seat of the Supreme Court, even though the executive and legislative branches have sat in La Paz since the early 20th century. Unlike three of Bolivia's other departments (Tarija in 1831, Beni in 1842, Pando in 1938), Chuquisaca was not created by a later separation from another territory: it was one of the original departments organized at the republic's founding, so its span here starts in 1825 rather than at a documented later departure date. This entry is `type: subnational` because Chuquisaca has never been sovereign in its own right — it is carried as a first-level administrative reporting unit inside Bolivia, following the same convention already applied to Beni (BOL-BEN-1842-2025) and to Spain's and Argentina's provinces elsewhere in this table: a department/province-level admin-1 unit that appears as a distinct reporting unit in agricultural, trade or census statistics gets its own polity row rather than being folded into the national total.

**Why this entry exists.** This row is created directly from a routing decision (unit_id `BOL-CHUQUISACA`) rather than from a specific data extract examined here: the routing stage identified Chuquisaca, a Bolivian department, as a first-level administrative unit that this repository's country convention for Bolivia requires to have its own polity row, following the precedent already set by BOL-BEN-1842-2025. The routing decision confirms no existing candidate polity represents Chuquisaca specifically — the only polities sharing the ISO3 BOL are the four national-era rows (BOL-1825-1903, BOL-1903-1909, BOL-1909-1938, BOL-1938-2025), which are whole-country totals, not the department. What confirms Chuquisaca is a distinct, long-standing administrative entity rather than an artefact of routing is Bolivia's own department history: it is one of the departments organized at the republic's founding in 1825/1826, is not among the three documented later departures (Tarija 1831, Beni 1842, Pando 1938), and has been administered separately from La Paz, Potosí and the other departments ever since, with its capital Sucre serving as the country's constitutional capital and seat of the Supreme Court — a distinction (executive/legislative seat vs. judicial seat) that itself confirms Chuquisaca is treated as administratively distinct in Bolivian governance, not merely a name on a map. As with Beni, no specific dataset or row count has yet been examined for this entry; it was created purely from the routing-stage convention, and what data (if any) currently routes here should be checked once the row exists (see open questions).
