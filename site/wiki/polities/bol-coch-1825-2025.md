# Cochabamba (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

Cochabamba is a department (admin-1 unit) of Bolivia in the central valleys region, straddling the transition between the Andean altiplano to the west and the Amazonian lowlands (the Chapare) to the east; its capital is the city of Cochabamba. It is one of the departments organized at the republic's founding in 1825/1826 — unlike Beni (1842), Pando (1938) or Tarija (which joined later), Cochabamba is not among the later-created departures from the original department structure, so its row runs the full national span from 1825. This entry is `type: subnational` because Cochabamba has never been sovereign: it is carried here as a first-level administrative reporting unit inside Bolivia, following the same convention already applied to Beni ([bol-ben-1842-2025](bol-ben-1842-2025.md)) and to Spain's and Argentina's provinces elsewhere in this table — department-level admin-1 units that appear as distinct reporting units in agricultural and trade statistics get their own polity row rather than being folded into the national row.

**Why this entry exists.** This row is created directly from a routing decision (unit_id `BOL-COCHABAMBA`) rather than from a specific data extract examined here: the routing stage identified Cochabamba, a Bolivian department, as a first-level administrative unit that the country's convention for Bolivia (established when [bol-ben-1842-2025](bol-ben-1842-2025.md) was created) requires to have its own polity row. The routing reasoning states that no existing candidate polity represents the department specifically: the four polities sharing ISO3 BOL in the table are all national-level entities (the four BOL-<span> country eras), none of which is a department-level unit, so there was no risk of a false subnational match to rule out here (unlike Beni, which had to be distinguished from the unrelated country Benin's BEN-* rows). What confirms Cochabamba is a distinct, long-standing administrative entity rather than an artefact of the matcher is Bolivia's own department history: Cochabamba was one of the departments established at independence in 1825/1826 and has been administered separately from La Paz, Santa Cruz, Potosí, Chuquisaca and the other original departments ever since, appearing as a first-level unit in Bolivian censuses and agricultural statistics from that date. As with Beni, no specific dataset or row count motivated this row directly — it implements Bolivia's departmental-routing convention rather than resolving an observed data-routing conflict, which is why an open question below flags that this should be checked once the row exists.

**Territory description:** Cochabamba department occupies the central valleys of Bolivia, a temperate, agriculturally productive basin (the Cochabamba valley proper) surrounded by Andean ranges to the west and descending into the tropical Chapare lowlands to the east and north; it borders La Paz, Oruro, Potosí, Chuquisaca, Santa Cruz and Beni departments. Its capital and largest city is Cochabamba. On a modern map the department covers roughly 55,600 km2 — modest in area compared with Bolivia's Amazonian departments (Beni, Santa Cruz, Pando) but historically significant as one of the country's principal grain- and coca-growing regions and a major population center since the colonial era. No km2 figure specific to this row's polygon is given because none is yet attached; the figure above is the department's present-day extent from general reference, not a measurement of any geometry in this table. Unlike Beni, Cochabamba's own boundaries have not been the site of a documented departmental split within this span (Pando's 1938 creation drew territory from Beni, not from Cochabamba), so no comparable pre/post-split vintage caveat applies here — though this has not been independently verified against a Bolivian administrative-history source, which is flagged as an open question below.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-coch-not-single-letter

**Used BOL-COCH-1825-2025 rather than a single-letter ISO 3166-2 subdivision code**

Bolivia's ISO 3166-2 code for Cochabamba is BO-C (a bare letter), but the dominant pattern in this table for subnational rows, already set for Bolivia by bol-ben-1842-2025.md (BEN, not B), is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short mnemonic abbreviation rather than the raw ISO 3166-2 letter. COCH was chosen (rather than reusing the routing decision's unit_id suffix COCHABAMBA verbatim, which is the full department name) to stay consistent with Beni's three-to-four-letter mnemonic length and keep the code compact; a single-letter subunit (BOL-C-1825-2025) would be harder to read and easier to confuse with another department's code.

### d-container-full-span-not-1825-1903-only

**Split the container into four edges tiling 1825-2025, not the single BOL-1825-1903 edge the routing decision proposed**

The routing decision's proposed container_code was BOL-1825-1903 alone, but this entry's end_year is 2025 (Cochabamba is a current department), and a single 1825-1903 edge would leave 1903-2025 uncontained, which the containment gate rejects. Reading the polity table's four BOL national eras (1825-1903, 1903-1909, 1909-1938, 1938-2025) and tiling Cochabamba's full 1825-2025 span across all four mirrors exactly how bol-ben-1842-2025.md was containered, and is the same fix already applied there for the same reason (the routing stage proposes only the first-overlapping national era, not the full chain).
