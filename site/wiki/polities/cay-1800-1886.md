# Kingdom of Cayor

## Summary

The Kingdom of Cayor (Kajoor) was a Wolof kingdom in western Senegal, occupying the coastal region between the French posts of Dakar and Saint-Louis. Founded c. 1549 as a breakaway from the Jolof Empire, Cayor was among the most powerful Wolof polities throughout the 19th century. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1886, when the last Damel (king) Lat Dior Ngoné Latyr Diop was killed in battle while resisting French construction of the Dakar–Saint-Louis railway. Biger notes that France oversaw the federation of French West Africa in 1895, incorporating the former Wolof territories; Senegal gained independence in 1960. The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). Cayor's territory lay in the Cap-Vert peninsula region and the western Sahel, roughly corresponding to the present-day regions of Thiès and Louga in Senegal. The kingdom bordered the French-controlled coastline and was repeatedly pressed by French expansion from the 1850s onward.

## Open questions

### oq-chain-gap

The chain jumps from CAY-1800-1886 directly to SEN-1960-2025. The French colonial period for the Cayor territory (1886–1960) is not represented as a separate WHEP row. The Senegalese colonial row (SEN-1960-2025 starts at independence) does not cover this gap. A French Senegal colonial row may be needed.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger discusses Senegal only at the independence/French West Africa federation level. A specialist source on the Wolof kingdoms (e.g., Klein 1968 *Islam and Imperialism in Senegal*; Diouf 2001 *Kajoor in the Nineteenth Century*) is needed to source boundary and political claims for Cayor specifically.
