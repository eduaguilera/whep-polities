# Distrito Federal (Rio de Janeiro)

## Summary

Distrito Federal (Rio de Janeiro) covers the period 1900-1959, during which the city of Rio de Janeiro held federal-capital status within the Brazilian republic. Unlike the modern Distrito Federal, which is a purpose-built territory carved out of the state of Goias in 1960 to house Brasilia, this earlier Distrito Federal was not a separately bounded territory at all -- it was the federal-capital designation attached to the pre-existing city of Rio de Janeiro, held continuously from the 1889 proclamation of the republic until the 1960 transfer of the capital inland. The entry is `type: subnational` because the city held first-order administrative status directly under the federal union (outside any state), analogous in rank to a state but without full statehood, exactly as its post-1960 namesake does today -- national sovereignty rested with Brazil (via the container chain BRA-1800-1903 / BRA-1903-1909 / BRA-1909-2025) throughout. The two 'Distrito Federal' entities share a label and an administrative category but not a territory, an area, or a lineage of physical succession: this is why the routing decision that created this page treats them as two independent create_new proposals rather than one continuous entry re-spanning 1889-2023.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry exists to receive Brazilian data reported under the admin-unit label 'Distrito Federal' for years before the 1960 capital relocation -- the routing verdict (unit_id BRA-DISTRITOFEDERAL) states that this unit's history spans multiple administrations requiring one polity per era, but does not itself give the row count or source dataset for the pre-1960 segment (flagged as open question oq-row-count-and-source-unconfirmed). Before this entry existed, such rows had no correct home: matching them to the national row BRA-1909-2025 would discard the sub-national reporting granularity, while matching them to the post-1960 BRA-DF-1960-2023 row would conflate two different territories under one shared label -- the mistake the routing reasoning explicitly guards against, noting 'no name matches, no boundary features' tie the pre-1960 data to any existing candidate. What confirms this is a genuinely distinct entity rather than a relabeling exercise is straightforward constitutional and administrative history: the 1889 proclamation of the republic fixed Rio de Janeiro's status as Distrito Federal, and the 1960 inauguration of Brasilia (recorded in the sibling page bra-df-1960-2023.md) ended it and created an unrelated, newly bounded territory elsewhere -- a change in both the seat of government and the underlying land in the same year, which is the dividing line between this page and its successor.

Territory description: the federal-capital city of Rio de Janeiro, on Guanabara Bay in what is now the state of Rio de Janeiro, at approximately 22.9 degrees S, 43.2 degrees W. Its municipal extent during this period is generally put at roughly 1,200 km2, comparable to the modern municipality of Rio de Janeiro (which has grown somewhat through subsequent annexations and land reclamation, so the historical figure is necessarily approximate); this is drawn from general historical-geography references on the city's municipal boundary, not measured from any geometry attached to this entry, since no geometry is attached.

## Decisions

### d-code-bra-dfrj

**Chose BRA-DFRJ-1900-1959 over the obvious 'DF' abbreviation, which is already taken**

The dominant pattern is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and this page follows it, but the natural subunit abbreviation 'DF' is already used by wiki/polities/bra-df-1960-2023.md for the post-1960 Brasilia-based Distrito Federal -- a different territory (a purpose-built federal district carved from Goias) sharing only the administrative label with this pre-1960 entity (federal-capital status held by the pre-existing city of Rio de Janeiro). Reusing 'DF' for both would visually suggest continuity where the routing decision explicitly found none. 'DFRJ' (Distrito Federal, Rio de Janeiro) keeps the ISO3-SUBUNIT convention while making the two entities' codes distinct, since IBGE has no standing abbreviation for a federal district that no longer exists.

### d-no-successor-claim

**Declared successor: [] rather than repeating the rejected edge to BRA-DF-1960-2023**

A prior version of this page declared successor: [BRA-DF-1960-2023], which validate_chain_integrity rejected because that page's own predecessor field is [] -- the gate counts edges against a baseline in both directions, and an edge only one side asserts fails. This page cannot edit bra-df-1960-2023.md, so it drops the claim rather than re-assert something already shown to fail, and instead documents the relationship and the exact fix needed in predecessors_and_successors and open question oq-backfill-successor-edge.

**Rio de Janeiro held Distrito Federal status from 1889, not 1900 -- why does this entry start at 1900?**

Brazil's federal-capital arrangement giving Rio de Janeiro Distrito Federal status dates to the 1889 proclamation of the republic (confirmed in the sibling page bra-df-1960-2023.md). This entry starts at 1900 rather than 1889, following the proposed start_year in the routing decision, but the decision gives no administrative-history reason tied to that specific year -- it may instead reflect the extract's data coverage rather than the unit's actual founding.
