# Benin (1898-1960)

## Summary

French Dahomey, the colonial polity that governed the territory of present-day Benin from 1898 until independence on 1 August 1960. The start year 1898 marks the consolidation of French control following the conquest of the Dahomey kingdom and the creation of the colony from the Etablissements du Golfe de Guinee. "The Beninese were ruled by the French from the beginning of the twentieth century, at which time the country was called Dahomey after the great kingdom that controlled the area in precolonial times" [biger-1995 benin](../sources/biger-1995.md#benin). The colony was renamed from the Etablissements in 1883 and became Dahomey one year later; a French decree of March 1901 specified its boundaries [biger-1995 benin](../sources/biger-1995.md#benin). CShapes codes it as a French colony (ruler=FRA) for this period.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from CShapes 2.0 (dependencies=TRUE). The territory corresponds to the borders of modern Benin, bounded by Togo (400 mi; 644 km), Niger (165 mi; 266 km), Burkina Faso (190 mi; 306 km), and Nigeria (480 mi; 773 km) [biger-1995 benin](../sources/biger-1995.md#benin).

## Sourced claims

- The colony of Benin was created from the Etablissements du Golfe de Guinee and renamed Dahomey. A French decree of March 1901 specified a boundary between Dahomey and its military territories to the north. [biger-1995 benin](../sources/biger-1995.md#benin)
- "Dahomey gained independence on 1 August 1960, followed by Upper Volta four days later, on 5 August 1960." [biger-1995 benin](../sources/biger-1995.md#benin)
- During the colonial period Dahomey served as a source of African administrators for large parts of French-ruled West Africa. [biger-1995 benin](../sources/biger-1995.md#benin)
