# Espírito Santo

## Summary

Espírito Santo is a Brazilian state on the Atlantic coast of the Sudeste (Southeast) region, north of Rio de Janeiro state, with its capital at Vitória. This entry covers Espírito Santo as a first-order administrative and statistical reporting unit from 1889, when the Proclamation of the Republic converted the former Imperial province of Espírito Santo into a state of the federation, through the present open end_year of 2025. `type: subnational` is used because sovereignty over Espírito Santo has rested with Portugal and then Brazil continuously — it was a captaincy from 1535, then an Imperial province from Brazilian independence in 1822, and has been a state of the federation without interruption since 1889. It is not among the six Brazilian departure states (Acre, Mato Grosso do Sul, Rondônia, Amapá, Roraima, Tocantins) that were carved out of federal territories or other states after the Republic's founding and so get a later start year under the country convention; Espírito Santo is the ordinary case the convention's default 1889 rule was written for, and no predecessor row is needed because there is no sovereignty break or federal-territory interlude to record.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is created to give Espírito Santo-labelled statistical data a subnational polity to route to, rather than folding it into the national BRA-1909-2025 row. The routing decision (unit_id BRA-ESPIRITOSANTO) records a data span of 1900-2023 for the underlying extract, which the decision itself does not further characterize by source name or row count beyond noting it falls entirely inside the proposed 1889-2025 span; that gap in the routing record is carried forward as an open question below rather than invented. Before this entry, the only BRA rows in the database were three national totals (BRA-1800-1903, BRA-1903-1909, BRA-1909-2025), none of which is state-scale; any Espírito Santo-specific series had nowhere to go except the national chain, which is correct for sovereignty but wrong for reporting granularity — the national row is roughly 18 times Espírito Santo's own land area (8.5 million km2 national vs ~46,000 km2 for the state). Espírito Santo's distinctness as a reporting and administrative unit is confirmed by its continuous existence as a first-order Brazilian administrative division (captaincy, then province, then state) since colonial times, its own IBGE statistical apparatus, and its listing as a standard ADM1 unit (abbrev_state "ES") in modern boundary sources such as geobr-ibge — the same source cited for sibling entries BRA-BAHIA and BRA-CE.

**Territory description.** Espírito Santo occupies the Atlantic coastal strip of Brazil's Sudeste region, bordered by Bahia to the north, Minas Gerais to the west, and Rio de Janeiro state to the south, with the state capital and largest city Vitória on a coastal island. The modern state covers approximately 46,000 km2 — one of Brazil's smaller states by area — and its boundaries have been broadly stable since it became a Republican state in 1889, without the territorial disputes or federal-territory carve-outs that affected states like Acre or Rondônia. No area figure is given here as measured from any attached geometry, since none is attached; the ~46,000 km2 figure is the modern state's commonly cited area, useful for locating the territory on a map, not a polygon measurement.

## Decisions

### d-code-abbrev-not-fullname

**Used the ES abbreviation in the polity code, matching BRA-CE not BRA-BAHIA**

Two sibling Brazilian state entries diverge on naming: BRA-BAHIA-1889-2025 uses the full state name as the subunit token, while BRA-CE-1889-2025 uses the two-letter postal/geobr abbreviation. Since geobr-ibge (the named polygon source for all these entries, including this one) keys states by `abbrev_state`, and since the abbreviation is shorter and unambiguous for a state with a distinctive two-word name, this entry follows the BRA-CE precedent and uses `BRA-ES-1889-2025` rather than a longer `BRA-ESPIRITOSANTO-1889-2025`. Either would satisfy the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern; this is a tie-break between two already-established siblings, not a departure from the pattern itself.

### d-three-segment-container

**Containered against the three-segment BRA chain, not the overlapping BRA-1800-2025 row**

The polity table has two candidate BRA containers spanning 1889-2025: a single row BRA-1800-2025, and a three-segment chain BRA-1800-1903 / BRA-1903-1909 / BRA-1909-2025. Both sibling entries examined (BRA-BAHIA, BRA-CE) use the three-segment chain, and the routing decision for this unit names BRA-1909-2025 specifically as the proposed container for the post-1909 era, implying the segmented chain is the one in active use for Brazilian subnational containment. This entry follows that precedent and adds the two earlier segments (1889-1903, 1903-1909) so the container edges tile the full 1889-2025 span with no gap, rather than leaving 1889-1909 uncontained as citing only BRA-1909-2025 would.
