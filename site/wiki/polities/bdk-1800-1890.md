# Kingdom of Burundi

## Summary

The Kingdom of Burundi was a pre-colonial kingdom in the Great Lakes region of East Africa, existing from c. the 17th century. The database tracks it from 1800 until c. 1890, when the region came under the German East Africa sphere. The kingdom continued as a monarchy under colonial rule and was paired with Rwanda as the Ruanda-Urundi mandate after World War I under Belgian administration. Biger references Burundi in the Tanzania-Uganda boundary context.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). The kingdom occupied the territory of modern Burundi, a small highland country in the Great Lakes region bordered by Rwanda, Tanzania, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

## Sourced claims

- "After World War I, German East Africa, consisting of Rwanda-Urundi and Tanganyika, was administered under League of Nations mandates with Belgium responsible for Ruanda-Urundi (later to become the two separate independent states of Rwanda and Burundi) and Britain administering Tanganyika until its independence in 1961." [biger-1995 §burundi](../sources/biger-1995.md#burundi)
- "In 1886, the British and German spheres of influence in East Africa lay respectively to the north and south of a line which ran from Vanga, on the coast of the Indian Ocean, to the eastern shore of Lake Victoria." The German sphere encompassed the Burundi region. [biger-1995 §uganda](../sources/biger-1995.md#uganda)

## Contradictions

None identified. Note that Biger's text (p.490) appears to conflate the Kingdom of Burundi with the Uganda Protectorate context. Burundi was in fact part of the German East Africa sphere, not the British Uganda Protectorate.

## Open questions

### oq-1890-end-date

The database uses 1890 as the end year, roughly corresponding to the establishment of German colonial authority in East Africa. The exact transition date is approximate; the Anglo-German Agreement of 1 July 1890 assigned spheres of influence but did not immediately end the kingdom's internal governance.
