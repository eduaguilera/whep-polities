# Sweden (1814-1905)

## Summary

Sweden as a distinct statistical unit during the Sweden-Norway personal union (1814-1905). Despite the union, Sweden and Norway maintained entirely separate governments, parliaments (Riksdag / Storting), budgets, and statistical offices. Agricultural data was compiled separately for each country throughout the union period. The union entity is tracked as [SNW-1814-1905](snw-1814-1905.md) with the combined CShapes polygon.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm0` feature `SWE` (modern Sweden's national boundary, ~450k km²), used as a **proxy** (`polygon_status: proxy`). Sweden's core territory didn't change materially between the 1809 loss of Finland and the 1905 union dissolution — the union with Norway (1814-1905) was personal, not territorial, and the two kingdoms kept separate borders, governments, and statistical offices. The only CShapes polygon for Sweden in this period (gwcode 380, 1886-1904, ~766k km²) is the combined Sweden-Norway union territory and is assigned to the union entity [SNW-1814-1905](snw-1814-1905.md); using it for Sweden-only data would double-count Norway.

**Why this entry exists separately from SNW-1814-1905:** The pre-1961 agricultural input data reports "Sweden" and "Norway" as separate countries throughout the union period. Matching Swedish-only data to the union polygon would incorrectly colour Norway on the choropleth map. This entry captures Sweden-only data; Norway data matches [NOR-1800-2025](nor-1800-2025.md).

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [SWE-1809-1814](swe-1809-1814.md) — Sweden after loss of Finland (1809), before union with Norway.
- **Successor:** [SWE-1905-2025](swe-1905-2025.md) — Sweden after dissolution of union (7 June 1905).

## Key dates

- 1814-01-14: Treaty of Kiel — Denmark cedes Norway to Sweden.
- 1814-08-14: Convention of Moss — Norway accepts personal union.
- 1866: Riksdag reformed (bicameral parliament replaces Estates).
- 1905-06-07: Norway declares union dissolved.
- 1905-10-26: Formal dissolution; Sweden accepts.
