# Arauca (department of Colombia)

## Summary

Arauca is a department in northeastern Colombia, on the Llanos plains bordering Venezuela along the Arauca River, with Arauca city as its capital. Administratively it began as one of the territorios nacionales -- remote lowland territories east of the Andes that Bogotá governed directly rather than through ordinary departmental institutions, later reorganized as the Intendencia de Arauca. It was not made a full department with its own elected government until the 1991 Constitution abolished the intendencia/comisaría tier entirely and converted the remaining national territories, Arauca among them, into departments. This entry is `type: subnational` because Arauca never held sovereignty of its own; it is a reporting/administrative unit within Colombia, and the row exists to carry statistics tabulated specifically for this department rather than for the country as a whole. The 1991 start date is not arbitrary -- it is the date the country's own convention for classifying departure territories assigns to Arauca, distinguishing it from departments that existed earlier (most of which trace to the 19th century) and from the small number of even later departmental creations.

**Why this entry exists.** The source data carries Arauca-specific figures spanning 1915-1990 that are overwhelmingly (roughly 98%) scaled or interpolated rather than directly observed, and a further set of directly-reported rows from 1991 onward. All of these rows had previously been routed to the national containers (COL-1830-1903, COL-1903-1922, COL-1922-2025), which cover the whole of Colombia and are roughly 45x the area of this one department -- a routing that discarded the sub-national precision the source actually provides. No existing polity row isolates Arauca as a reporting unit. Historically, Arauca's distinctness as an administrative entity is confirmed by its status first as a territorio nacional/intendencia (a category Colombia governed and reported on separately from the ordinary departments long before 1991) and then by the 1991 Constitution's explicit act of departmentalizing it by name, which is the same event that fixes this entry's start year. The pre-1991 rows (1915-1990) are treated as back-cast onto this later administrative footprint rather than as evidence the department existed then -- they are not routed here, consistent with the country convention's departure-list rule that data for a territorio nacional predating its department conversion belongs to the earlier administrative status, not to the department row.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The registered source `gadm-4.1-adm1` (GADM 4.1, administrative level 1) does digitize Colombian departments and includes an Arauca feature (GID_1 = COL.34_1) in its full global release, but the copy of that source currently on disk in this repository is a subset covering only 81 countries, and Colombia is not among them -- so no feature ID can be assigned today without re-fetching the unsubsetted GADM 4.1 ADM1 layer. This is a fetch gap, not a missing-source problem: no alternative registered source (cshapes-2.0, cliopatria) digitizes Colombian departments at all, only the country as a whole.

**Territory description:** Arauca department sits in northeastern Colombia on the Orinoquía/Llanos plains, bounded by the Arauca River and Venezuela to the north and east, Casanare department to the south, and Boyacá/Norte de Santander to the west, with the department capital at Arauca city. Modern Arauca covers approximately 23,800 km2 (Colombia's official statistics, DANE). No area figure is measured from the attached geometry in this entry, since no polygon is yet attached; the 23,800 km2 figure is an external, present-day administrative statistic, not a geometry measurement. Boundary continuity from 1991 to today is expected to be close, since Arauca's departmental boundaries have not been subject to the kind of subsequent splits or annexations documented for some other former territorios nacionales, but this has not been independently verified against a historical map for this entry.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity is recorded. Pre-1991 Arauca-specific data is deliberately NOT chained as a predecessor here: under the departure-list convention, the territorio nacional/intendencia period is treated as part of Colombia's national reporting (COL-1922-2025 and its predecessors) rather than as an earlier phase of this department, because the intendencia lacked the departmental government and reporting apparatus this entry is meant to capture. If a future pass finds intendencia-specific statistics distinct enough to warrant their own row, that row should be linked here as a predecessor rather than folded in.

## Sourced claims

- Colombia's 1991 Constitution (Article 309 and related transitional provisions) converted the remaining national territories -- including the Intendencia de Arauca -- into departments with full departmental status, which is checkable against the constitutional text and DANE's administrative history of the department.
- DANE (Colombia's national statistics agency) records Arauca department's current area as approximately 23,800 km2, checkable against DANE's official department-level territorial statistics.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-col-ara

**Used <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> despite COL having no prior subnational precedent**

COL has zero existing subnational rows, so there is no country-specific precedent to follow. I applied the dominant pattern across the whole table (57 of 66 subnational rows follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919) rather than treating Arauca as a bespoke-named historical territory. Arauca is a standard modern department created by a generic constitutional reclassification shared with several other former territorios nacionales, not a historical polity with a name and identity of its own comparable to Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State, so the bespoke pattern (3 of 66 rows) did not fit. I chose 'ARA' as a 3-letter subunit code since Colombia does not have an ISO 3166-2 subdivision code convention already in use elsewhere in this table to match against.

### d-polygon-unassigned-not-proxy

**Marked polygon_status as unassigned rather than proxy, since no feature can be identified yet**

The proxy status is for cases where a specific polygon feature exists and is being reused across periods with a documented rationale. Here, the correct registered source (gadm-4.1-adm1) is known but the on-disk file is a subset missing Colombia entirely, so no feature_id exists to either assign or treat as a proxy. This is why polygon_feature_id is null and polygon_status is 'unassigned' rather than pointing at a placeholder feature that doesn't exist in the local GeoPackage.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-colombia-refetch

**GADM 4.1 ADM1 needs re-fetching to include Colombia before a polygon can be assigned**

The registered source gadm-4.1-adm1 is present in scripts/sources.yaml with id_column=GID_1, and the full/global GADM 4.1 release does digitize Colombian departments (Arauca would be GID_1 = COL.34_1 or similar), but the local copy in this repository's data/geodata is a subset covering only 81 countries and Colombia is not among them. Someone needs to either re-fetch the global (unsubsetted) GADM 4.1 ADM1 file or add Colombia specifically to the subset, after which polygon_feature_id and polygon_status here should be updated to 'assigned'. Until then this entry has zero territorial extent in the GeoPackage, which likely affects every other Colombian department entry created alongside it (e.g. col-antioquia-1886-2025.md, per the git status of this session).

### oq-pre1991-intendencia-rows

**Whether the 1915-1990 back-cast rows should eventually get their own intendencia-era polity**

The source carries Arauca-labeled rows from 1915 onward, almost entirely scaled/interpolated rather than directly observed, that were routed to the national COL containers under the reasoning that the intendencia/territorio nacional was not yet a department. It remains an open question whether a distinct intendencia-era subnational entry (something like COL-ARA-<intendencia-start>-1991) would better capture this data than leaving it inside the national row, especially if the exact date Arauca was first constituted as a territorio nacional or intendencia (rather than unorganized national territory) can be pinned down -- that date is not established in the evidence reviewed for this entry.

### oq-boundary-continuity-1991-today

**No historical map check confirms 1991 Arauca boundaries match today's GADM feature**

The territory description assumes Arauca's departmental boundaries have been stable from 1991 to the present, based on the general absence of documented boundary disputes or municipal transfers affecting the department as a whole, but this has not been checked against any historical administrative map of Colombia for this entry. If a future GADM fetch reveals boundary changes (e.g., municipalities moved between Arauca and neighboring Casanare or Boyacá), the polygon_status and area figures here would need revision, and the 'copy from present-day GADM back to 1991' proxy reasoning noted in the routing decision would need to be reassessed rather than assumed valid.
