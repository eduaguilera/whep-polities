# Soria (province of Spain)

## Summary

Soria is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, in the sparsely populated northeastern part of the historic region of Old Castile / Castilla y Leon. This entry is type: subnational because Soria is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Soria separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES417, for over a century and a half. Unlike Asturias, Cantabria or Madrid, Soria's 1833 province boundary is not coextensive with any later autonomous community on its own: since 1983 it has been one of nine provinces making up the autonomous community of Castilla y Leon, so the province survives today purely as a statistical/administrative sub-division rather than as a self-standing regional government, which is exactly the kind of unit this row is built to carry without conflating it with the community-level or national aggregate.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES417) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES417 ("Soria"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES112 through ES241), following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Soria is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing distinct -- the routing_reasoning states explicitly that the three existing ESP candidates considered (A Coruna, Madrid, and the ESP national total) are each a different province or the national aggregate, none of which IS Soria; routing the data to any of them would have collapsed a province-level series into either the wrong province or the country-level total, a fallback rejected for the same reason it was rejected for the other Spanish province rows created under this same sweep (ES112-ES241). What confirms distinctness: Soria's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES417) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CA-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 49x the area of Soria.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE province numeric code), under which Soria is conventionally cpro 42 -- the anticipated feature_id is "Soria's cpro code (INE province code 42) in mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg" -- but the polygon_reasoning states plainly that mapspain-ign, while registered with the correct schema and id_column, "is not present_locally": the provinces.gpkg file has not been downloaded or placed on disk, so the route cannot be executed until that data is fetched, exactly as recorded for the sibling provinces ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CA-1833-2025. GADM adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP entirely (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, and Soria is one of nine provinces within Castilla y Leon, so adm1 does not carry province-level attribution), so it was correctly ruled out rather than substituted. Territory description: Soria province occupies the northeastern part of the autonomous community of Castilla y Leon, bordering La Rioja and Zaragoza (Aragon) to the north and east, Guadalajara to the south, and Segovia and Burgos to the west -- an inland, mountainous, and famously depopulated part of the Iberian Meseta, drained by the upper Duero river. Its capital, also named Soria, is one of the smallest provincial capitals in Spain. On a modern map its outline is the same as it has been since 1833, since Castilian provinces of this kind were not subsequently split or merged the way Galicia's provinces were reorganized. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 10,306 km2 -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling provinces.

## Predecessors and successors

Soria has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was fragmented across older Castilian administrative jurisdictions rather than organized as a single distinct unit with its own name and identity, and this database does not model sub-kingdom Castilian divisions at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025 and the other sibling provinces. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES417 records that Soria is one of the 1833 Javier de Burgos provinces and is not among the enumerated departures from the default span rule (the Canary Islands split), so the system default start_year of 1833 applies, consistent with sibling verdicts ES112-ES241 which all created new rows rather than matching the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-so-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation SO rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025 and the other sibling rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES417) or the INE province numeric code (42). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-SO-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. SO is both the everyday abbreviation for Soria and matches the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-SO), reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row.

## Open questions

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Soria is conventionally cpro 42 -- but the polygon_reasoning for this routing decision states the source file is not present_locally: the provinces.gpkg has not been downloaded or placed on disk, so the feature cannot be confirmed as Soria's actual 1833-2025 boundary. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Soria before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Soria province has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now forming Soria province belonged to the historic Crown of Castile's administrative structure (partly the old jurisdiction centered on the town of Soria, partly fragments of Old Castile and the former Kingdom of Aragon's Castilian marches), with no single Antiguo Regimen unit corresponding cleanly to the 1833 boundary. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings. Whether a pre-1833 predecessor should eventually be modeled is an open question for the ESP country convention generally rather than specific to Soria, since Soria (unlike Asturias) does not carry a well-known pre-1833 territorial name of its own that a future author might mistake for grounds to add a bespoke predecessor code.
