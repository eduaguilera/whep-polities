# Imamate of Futa Jallon

## Summary

The Imamate of Futa Jallon (Fouta Djallon) was a Fula theocratic state in the highlands of modern Guinea. It was one of the earliest and most influential jihad states in West Africa, founded in 1727 after the Fulani Muslim uprising against the Jalunka rulers. The state was governed by an Almami (Imam) under Islamic law. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1896, when France conquered the imamate and incorporated it into French Guinea. Biger notes that "France established themselves in Guinea in 1849 when they proclaimed the Boke region a French protectorate and then began its expansion eastward," and that "Guinea became independent on 2 October 1958." The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). The Futa Jallon highlands cover approximately 60,000 km² of the Guinea interior, a region of rounded hills and plateaus. The imamate controlled the headwaters of the Gambia, Senegal, and Niger rivers and dominated trans-Saharan and Atlantic trade routes passing through the region.

## Open questions

### oq-conquest-date

The end year 1896 corresponds to the French conquest, but French encroachment on the Futa Jallon began in the 1880s and the final French military operations occurred 1896–1898. The exact date of effective French control needs verification.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger discusses Guinea only at the coastal French protectorate and independence level, not the Futa Jallon imamate specifically. A specialist source (e.g., Barry 1998 *Senegambia and the Atlantic Slave Trade*; Weil 1984 on the Futa Jallon) is needed for territorial and political claims.
