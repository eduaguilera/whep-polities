# West Berlin (1949-1990)

## Summary

West Berlin covers the three Western Allied sectors (American, British, French) of Berlin from the establishment of the Federal Republic of Germany on 23 May 1949 to German reunification on 3 October 1990. West Berlin had a unique constitutional status: it was formally a Land of the Federal Republic of Germany but was not legally integrated into the FRG under the Basic Law. It was administered by the Western Allied Commandants and governed by a Senate under their supervision, maintaining close ties with West Germany while remaining technically under Allied occupation law.

This entry exists because the FAO 1952 yearbook reports agricultural statistics (butter, cattle, horses, meat, milk) for 'Germany Berlin' as a distinct geographic unit for 1949-1951. After the 1949 division, the FAO label most plausibly refers to West Berlin, as Western-bloc statistical agencies tracked the three Western sectors. At approximately 480 km², West Berlin is roughly 520x smaller than West Germany (F78-1949-1990, ~249,000 km²). Folding West Berlin's agricultural output into F78-1949-1990 would misstate the territorial base ~520-fold. A separate polity entry is required.

## Territorial extent

The sector boundaries were established at the Allied Kommandatura and remained unchanged until reunification in 1990.

## Why this entry was created

The FAO 1952 yearbook reports 'Germany Berlin' as a distinct reporting unit for 1949-1951. After the 1949 division, this label refers to the Western-controlled portion of Berlin. The territorial magnitude (480 km²) is incompatible with routing to F78-1949-1990 (West Germany, ~249,000 km²), which is ~520x larger. Per WHEP rules, sub-territory data must not be folded into parent aggregates. A coverage_gap polity is required for the post-division Berlin data.

## Relationship to F78-1949-1990 (West Germany)

West Berlin was constitutionally associated with but not legally part of the FRG. The FRG's Basic Law (Article 23) designated West Berlin as a Land, but the Allied reservation meant this was suspended. The FRG's Bundestag included non-voting West Berlin delegates. Economically, West Berlin used the Deutsche Mark and was integrated into the West German economy. For WHEP's purposes, the spatial distinction is paramount: West Berlin's ~480 km² must not be conflated with West Germany's ~249,000 km².
