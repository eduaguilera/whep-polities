# Faroe Islands

## Summary

This row tracks the Faroe Islands as a territorial-economic unit for
**226 years**, from the **1800** WHEP database floor to the **2025**
database ceiling [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
The Faroe Islands are a self-governing archipelago of 18 volcanic
islands in the North Atlantic between Norway and Iceland, covering
approximately **1,399 km²**. Throughout the entire WHEP window the
archipelago has been administered by Denmark, first as a county
(amt) and since 1948 as a self-governing territory (hjemmestyre).
No territorial change occurred across these status transitions.

This polity was created to route FAO 1952 yearbook sub-entry
**"Denmark The Faeroes"**, which records agricultural data
(horses/mules, poultry, agricultural area, population) for the
Faroe Islands specifically. Routing this data to
[dnk-1920-2025](dnk-1920-2025.md) (~43,000 km²) would overstate the
territory roughly **30x** — a sub-territory/parent conflation error
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition).

- **COW = NA.** The Faroe Islands have never been listed in the COW
  state system as an independent state.
- **Not in CShapes 2.0.** CShapes covers states meeting the COW
  recognition threshold. The Faroe Islands are a Danish autonomous
  territory and do not appear as a separate CShapes unit.
- **Polygon source: GADM 4.1.** Because CShapes does not include the
  Faroe Islands as a separate unit, the polygon is sourced from GADM
  4.1 ADM0 (ISO3 `FRO`) which provides the archipelago boundary
  (~1,399 km²)
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- **Relationship to DNK chain.** The Denmark chain
  ([dnk-1800-1864](dnk-1800-1864.md), [dnk-1864-1920](dnk-1864-1920.md),
  [dnk-1920-2025](dnk-1920-2025.md)) tracks metropolitan Denmark only
  (~43,000 km²). The Faroe Islands are a distinct WHEP row because the
  archipelago has its own agricultural and population statistics reported
  separately in FAO and other international datasets.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `GADM 4.1` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). CShapes 2.0 does
  not cover the Faroe Islands as a separate unit. GADM 4.1 provides the
  administrative boundary polygon for ISO3 `FRO`.
- **Area.** Approximately **1,399 km²** (18 main islands plus
  numerous islets). This is the polygon_area_km2 recorded in the CSV.
  The contrast with mainland Denmark (~43,000 km²) confirms that any
  FAO sub-entry labelled "Denmark The Faeroes" or equivalent refers
  to this archipelago, not Denmark proper (a ~30x magnitude difference).
- **Island geography.** The Faroe Islands have no land boundaries;
  borders are defined by coastline and have not changed over the WHEP
  period.
- **Area stability.** No territorial changes occurred between 1800
  and 2025. The archipelago's geographic extent is fixed.
- **Polygon confidence.** The GADM 4.1 boundary represents the modern
  administrative extent of the Faroe Islands; since the archipelago
  has fixed island geography with no land-border changes, this polygon
  is appropriate as a back-projection for the full 1800–2025 period.

## Sourced claims

- **Self-governing Danish territory.** The Faroe Islands are a
  self-governing archipelago of Denmark in the North Atlantic.
  Home Rule was granted in 1948 under the Faroese Home Rule Act
  (Hjemmestyreloven), giving the islands control over most internal
  affairs while defence and foreign policy remain with Denmark.
- **Area ~1,399 km².** The GADM 4.1 ADM0 polygon for ISO3 `FRO`
  yields approximately 1,399 km² total land area across the 18 main
  islands and associated islets.
- **Not in CShapes 2.0 / COW state system.** The Faroe Islands
  (`FRO`, iso3) do not appear in the COW state-system membership list
  (v2024). They have never been recognised as an independent state
  by the COW project.
- **GADM polygon.** The CSV records `GADM 4.1` as the polygon source
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). GADM provides
  administrative boundary polygons for territories not covered by
  CShapes.
- **FAO 1952 data.** The FAO 1952 yearbook sub-entry "Denmark The
  Faeroes" records 7 rows of data (horses/mules, poultry, agricultural
  area, population) for years 1935–1951. These statistics are
  categorically distinct from Denmark proper data: a territory of
  ~1,399 km² vs ~43,000 km² for metropolitan Denmark.

## Decisions

- **[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition)** —
  the territorial-change rule. Applied here: no territorial changes
  across multiple status transitions (county to home rule territory)
  -> one WHEP row for 1800–2025.
- **Sub-territory routing rule.** FAO data labelled "Denmark The
  Faeroes" must NOT be routed to DNK-1920-2025. The Faroe Islands
  (~1,399 km²) are a sub-territory of Denmark (~43,000 km²); routing
  upward would overstate territory ~30x. A separate WHEP row is
  required to avoid this conflation.
- **Polygon choice.** GADM 4.1 ADM0 is used because CShapes 2.0 does
  not include the Faroe Islands as a separate unit (below the COW/CShapes
  recognition threshold for autonomous territories). The island
  geography is stable, so the modern GADM boundary is appropriate
  as a back-projection for the full WHEP period.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1948-data-availability

**Data reporting before 1948 Home Rule.** The FAO 1952 data covers
1935–1951. Whether any WHEP source reports Faroe Islands data
separately before 1935 (during the county/amt period) is not yet
audited. The polity span is set to 1800–2025 to match the database
floor/ceiling and avoid artificial truncation; if earlier data emerge
they can be incorporated without a row split (no territorial change).

### oq-independence-trajectory

**Independence referendum trajectory.** The Faroese government has
at times discussed independence from Denmark. If the Faroe Islands
become independent within the WHEP project's active period, this row
would need to be split and a successor row created. No action needed
now (still active at 2025 ceiling), but flagged for monitoring.
