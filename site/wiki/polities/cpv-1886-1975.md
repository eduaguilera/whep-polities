# Cape Verde (Portuguese colony)

## Summary

Portuguese Cape Verde from the earliest CShapes 2.0 coverage (1886) through independence on 5 July 1975. The Cape Verde archipelago was a Portuguese overseas territory administered as a province of Portugal. Cape Verde gained independence following the 1974 Carnation Revolution in Portugal, with the PAIGC/PAICV leading the independence movement. The territory is geographically unchanged at independence — the same 10 inhabited islands and 5 uninhabited islands of the archipelago.

## Territorial extent

Cape Verde Islands (area ~4,033 sq. km). The archipelago lies in the North Atlantic Ocean, approximately 570 km west of the African coast (Senegal/Mauritania). No land boundaries. Territory identical to the post-independence republic (CPV-1975-2025) — the political transition did not alter the archipelago's extent. Polygon from CShapes 2.0, feature id 402, vintage 1886.

The CShapes 2.0 polygon records Cape Verde as status=colony, ruler=PRT over this window. The same geographic islands form the post-independence state, so the polygon is equally valid for both periods; polygon_vintage_drift is not a concern here because the territory is an isolated archipelago with no land boundary changes possible.

### fao1952-coffee-1934-1938-cell-is-800x-too-large

Found 2026-08-18 while clearing the wiki notes queue.

FAO 1952 carries exactly **two** coffee rows for `Cape Verde Islands`:

| row | value | reading |
|---|---|---|
| period `1934-1938` | **80.2** (1000 t) = 80,200 t | impossible |
| year `1951` | **0.1** (1000 t) = 100 t | correct |

Cape Verdean coffee output was on the order of 100 t, so the **1951 figure is
right** and the 1934-1938 period cell is roughly **800x** too large. For scale,
the smallest coffee producers in the same FAO table all sit at 0.1: Paraguay,
British North Borneo, Eritrea, Sierra Leone — and Cape Verde's own 1951 row is
among them.

**The defect is one cell, not the series**, and that distinction matters for how
it is repaired: the 1951 value needs nothing done to it.

**A note in the queue described this as a series-level defect** on the strength of
a median of 40 (1000 t). That median is the mean of the two rows above —
(80.2 + 0.1) / 2 ≈ 40 — so it describes neither of them. A two-value series with
one correct and one defective figure has no meaningful median, which is the same
trap that produced a false ×100 reading for IIA tobacco and a false "mostly Togo"
reading for Ghana.

Also worth recording: the defective cell is a **period average** and the sound one
is a dated year. `state/item_axis_aggregates.csv` and the period-average routing
fix in #383 both concern that column; this is a third reason to treat
period-average rows as a distinct population rather than as data with a coarse
date.
