# Chaco (national territory of Argentina)

## Summary

The Territorio Nacional del Chaco was created by Ley 1532 of 16 October 1884, one of the national territories carved from lands taken from indigenous groups (Qom, Mocoví, Wichí) during and after the Campaña del Chaco. It was administered directly from Buenos Aires by a territorial governor appointed by the national executive, without provincial autonomy, elected legislature, or full representation in the national Congress — the standard status of an Argentine "territorio nacional" under Ley 1532's framework, which also created Formosa, Misiones (until 1953), Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego, La Pampa and Los Andes. This entry is `type: subnational` because sovereignty was always Argentina's; what the entry carries is the distinct reporting/administrative territory of the pre-province Chaco, which statistical sources tabulate separately from the national total and from the later province. The territory was elevated to provincial status by Ley 14037, promulgated 8 August 1951, becoming the Province of Chaco — captured by a sibling entry for the province era. The 1884 start marks the legal creation of the territory; the 1951 end marks its abolition as a national territory upon provincialization, not any change in physical extent.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry was created by the agent-harness routing pipeline to carry Argentine subnational statistics tabulated under "Chaco" for years before provincial status existed. The routing decision (unit_id ARG-CHACO) found that treating 1884-1951 Chaco data as an undifferentiated part of the national ARG total is wrong: Ley 1532 (1884) established Chaco as a legally and administratively distinct national territory, directly governed rather than folded into an existing province, and Ley 14037 (1951) marks a clean administrative break to provincial status. No prior polity row represented the Territorio Nacional del Chaco era specifically — the existing ARG national rows (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025) cover the sovereign state at large and would absorb Chaco-labelled data into a container roughly fifteen times its area. The historical/administrative record itself (Ley 1532's creation and Ley 14037's provincialization) is the source confirming this territory was governed as a distinct unit throughout the span, independent of any single statistical publisher's treatment.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is attached at this entry (`polygon_status: unassigned`, `polygon_feature_id: null`). The intended source is `gadm-4.1-adm1`, which is registered but whose locally-fetched extract is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Argentina — so the slug is recorded as the intended registered source without a fetched feature (`registered_source_unfetched`), rather than `new_source_needed`/`none_available` with `polygon_source: none`.

**Territory description:** The Territorio Nacional del Chaco corresponds closely to the modern Argentine province of Chaco, in the northeast of the country between the Bermejo and Paraná/Paraguay rivers, bordering Paraguay to the north. Modern Chaco province covers approximately 99,633 km². The historical territorio nacional's internal boundaries relative to neighboring territories (Formosa, Santiago del Estero, Salta) were subject to at least one decree adjustment during 1884-1951 that has not been checked here. A GADM/IGN modern Chaco province boundary is a reasonable but unverified proxy; it has not been fetched or attached at this entry.

## Sourced claims

- Ley 1532 (16 October 1884) created the Territorio Nacional del Chaco, along with Formosa, Misiones, Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego, La Pampa and Los Andes, under a unified national-territory administrative framework directly governed from Buenos Aires.
- Ley 14037 (promulgated 8 August 1951) converted the Territorio Nacional del Chaco into the Province of Chaco, ending its status as a directly-administered national territory.

## Decisions

### d-container-era-split

**Split the container into three ARG national-boundary eras instead of one edge to ARG-1902-2025**

The prior submission attached a single container edge to ARG-1902-2025 spanning 1884-1951, but ARG-1902-2025 itself only spans 1902-2025 — an edge must sit inside both parties' spans. The ARG national chain in the table has three consecutive boundary eras covering the full 1884-1951 window: ARG-1800-1899 (to 1899), ARG-1899-1902 (1899-1902), and ARG-1902-2025 (1902 onward). This entry now attaches one edge per era, tiling 1884-1899, 1899-1902 and 1902-1951 with no gap, matching the container gate's requirement that each edge fall inside both the member's and the container's own span.

### oq-internal-boundary-adjustments

**Unverified internal boundary changes to Chaco territorio nacional between 1884 and 1951**

Ley 1532 fixed an initial boundary for the Chaco territorio, but Argentine national-territory limits were periodically adjusted by decree relative to neighboring territories (Formosa to the north, Santiago del Estero and Salta to the west) during the following decades. It is not confirmed whether the 1884 boundary matches the boundary at the 1951 hand-off to provincial status, or whether a modern Chaco province polygon (once fetched) would misrepresent an earlier sub-period of this span. Check against Ley 14037's own boundary description and any intervening decrees before assigning a polygon.
