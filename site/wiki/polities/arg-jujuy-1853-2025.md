# Jujuy (province of Argentina)

## Summary

Jujuy is Argentina's northernmost province, in the far northwest bordering Bolivia and Chile, split off from Salta province in 1834 and recognized as one of the provinces acceding to the 1853 Constitution. `type` is `subnational` because this row does not claim sovereignty — Argentina held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1853-2025, and that is already carried by the national ARG chain (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025). What this entry carries instead is the reporting/administrative unit "Jujuy province," the level at which statistical sources (INDEC and its provincial predecessors) tabulate population, production, and land-area figures separately from national Argentina totals and from its 22 sibling provinces. Jujuy is not one of the routing convention's four departure cases for Argentina (the ex-territorios nacionales Chaco/Formosa/La Pampa/Misiones/Neuquén/Río Negro, the Patagonian pair Chubut/Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego, or Ciudad de Buenos Aires) — it is an original constitutional province, so the default start rule of 1853 applies, and since it remains a current first-order administrative division, end_year is the open convention date of 2025 rather than a data-driven cutoff.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the routing decision for unit_id ARG-JUJUY (admin_name "Jujuy", country Argentina) produced by the agent-harness routing pipeline, which found zero existing candidate polities — no name candidates and no boundary matches — meaning no wiki page previously represented Jujuy as a distinct reporting unit. The routing decision records that the data span it must contain is 1900-2023, though it does not enumerate the underlying source/label/row-count triples the way the Manchuria exemplar does; before this decision, any data keyed to a "Jujuy" province label would have had nowhere specific to route except the national ARG rows, roughly 70 times Jujuy's own area and conflating its statistics with those of all other provinces. The historical source confirming this is a persistently distinct, separately tabulated unit is administrative continuity itself: Jujuy separated from Salta in 1834, was one of the fourteen provinces organizing under the 1853 Constitution, and has since maintained its own governor, legislature, and statistical apparatus reporting under the "Jujuy" label continuously to the present — the same class of justification the exemplar uses for a region two publishers tabulate separately, except here the "second publisher" is simply Jujuy's uninterrupted 172-year existence as a first-order administrative division.
