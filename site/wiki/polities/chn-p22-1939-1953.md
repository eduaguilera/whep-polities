# China proper (the 22 provinces, 1939-1953)

## Summary

The Republic of China's provinces of China proper as FAO counted them in 1947: its 35 provinces
("the Republic of China in 1946 had 35 provinces") minus the nine north-eastern provinces
(Manchuria), Jehol (Rehe), Sikang (Xikang), Sinkiang (Xinjiang) and Taiwan, which FAO reports
separately. That leaves 22: Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Jiangxi, Hubei, Hunan, Sichuan, Hebei,
Shandong, Shanxi, Henan, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Fujian, Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan, Guizhou,
Chahar, Suiyuan and Ningxia.
[wikipedia-geodata-batch-2-2026-09-25 §roc-provinces](../sources/wikipedia-geodata-batch-2-2026-09-25.md#roc-provinces)

The span starts in 1939, when Sikang became "a nominal province formed by the Republic of China in
1939", the last change to the set of provinces before FAO's 1947 figures; it ends in 1953 because the
People's Republic began dissolving the 22 after FAO's last reported year, 1951: Chahar "was abolished
and divided into parts of Inner Mongolia, Beijing Municipality and Hebei" in 1952. It
is typed `subnational`, inside the China rows of these years, beside
[man-1945-1950](man-1945-1950.md) (Manchuria) and [chn-xj-1884-2025](chn-xj-1884-2025.md)
(Sinkiang).

## Why this entry exists

FAO 1952 reports `China` (the whole, 9,736,290 km²) beside `China 22 provinces`, `China Manchuria`,
`China Sinkiang`, `China Sikang`, `China Jehol` and `Tibet`, and the parts add up: this unit's 1947
`use total` is 507,182 thousand ha, i.e. **5,071,820 km²**, and with Manchuria (106,930 thousand ha),
Sinkiang (171,193), Sikang (45,152) and Jehol (17,998) the five parts are 87.1% of the whole's
973,629; the residual, 1,251,740 km², is Tibet to within 1.9% (FAO prints `Tibet` on its own). The
identity is recorded as `fao1952-china-whole-and-five-parts` in `state/data_errors.csv`.
Issue 449 found the parts landing on the same national polity as the whole, where a consumer
taking the mean averages them, and in two cells the "part" exceeding the whole by hundreds of times.

Before this row:

- `China 22 provinces` 1947-1951: **56 rows** resolved to no polity.
- `China 22 provinces` 1934-1938 averages and 1937-1939 dated rows: **31 rows** on
  [chn-1932-1945](chn-1932-1945.md), beside `China`'s own 1937 rows, on a polygon (6,710,153 km²:
  China without Manchukuo, but with Sinkiang, Sikang and Qinghai's plateau) 1.32x this unit's
  stated area.

Both now route here. The pre-1939 rows are `back_cast`: FAO's pre-war columns are stated on the
post-war boundaries (`source_conventions.csv`, fao1952 `*`/`*`), so the 1934-1938 figures are this
territory's, not the 1930s Republic's.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status: `proxy`.** `constructed`, from GADM 4.1 modern units, since no fetched source
draws the 1947 provinces:

- 25 modern adm1 units: Anhui, Beijing, Chongqing, Fujian, Gansu, Guangdong, Guangxi, Guizhou,
  Hainan, Hebei, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Jiangsu, Jiangxi, Ningxia, Qinghai, Shaanxi, Shandong,
  Shanghai, Shanxi, Sichuan, Tianjin, Yunnan, Zhejiang (4,684,575 km²);
- **minus** Sikang's eastern prefectures in modern Sichuan (Garzê, Ya'an, Liangshan, Panzhihua;
  230,164 km²) and Jehol's Chengde (39,492 km²; Jehol's Chaoyang and Chifeng are in Liaoning and
  Inner Mongolia, which are not included);
- **plus** the Inner Mongolian units that were Chahar (Xilingol, Ulanqab), Suiyuan ("today's
  prefecture-level cities of Hohhot, Baotou, Wuhai, Ordos, Bayan Nur, and parts of Ulanqab") and
  the Alxa banners of the Republic's Ningxia (696,181 km²).

The result measures **5,111,099 km²** (ESRI:54034), **0.8% above FAO's stated 5,071,820**.
[gadm-4.1 §what-it-adds](../sources/gadm-4.1.md#what-it-adds)

- **`proxy`**, because modern prefecture lines only approximate the 1947 provincial lines (Suiyuan
  held only "parts of Ulanqab"). The net error measured against FAO's figure is +39,000 km².
- **Outside CShapes' China.** 674,208 km² of this outline (13.2%) lies outside the China rows' CShapes polygons for 1913-1950, which draw that ground as
  part of Tibet (gwcode 711): 643,838 km² of it is in modern Qinghai, and the rest is border and coastline
  slivers of 1,100-4,700 km² per province. FAO's area includes the Qinghai ground, because the Republic's Qinghai did, so the
  outline follows FAO. The China rows' polygons are not changed here.

## Decisions

- **Span 1939-1953**, for the reason under *Summary*.
- **Nested in the China rows**, registered in `polity_composition.csv` as `sum_risk` wherever FAO's
  `China` feeds the same years: summing the whole and this part double-counts.
