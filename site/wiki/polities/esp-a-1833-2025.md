# Alicante/Alacant (province of Spain)

## Summary

Alicante/Alacant is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and since 1982 one of the three constituent provinces (with Valencia and Castellon) of the Valencian Community autonomous region on Spain's south-eastern Mediterranean coast. Unlike Asturias or Madrid, Alicante was never coextensive with its autonomous community: it is one of three provinces sharing that region, so the province and community boundaries have never been identical. This entry is type: subnational because Alicante is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right -- Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Alicante separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES521, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES521) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES521 ("Alicante/Alacant"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (A Coruna/ES111, Madrid/ES-MD, Asturias/ES120, and the rest of the ES112-ES243 batch), all following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) since there is no evidence this province departs from the default -- it is not one of the two Canary Islands provinces created by special exception in 1927. Previously matched to: nothing. Per the routing_reasoning, no existing polity in this database IS Alicante specifically; before this batch only A Coruna and Madrid existed among ESP subnational polities, and neither resembles Alicante geographically or administratively. The fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for every other ESP province in this batch. What confirms distinctness: Alicante's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES521) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 87x the area of Alicante.

## Predecessors and successors

Alicante/Alacant has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was administered under the Kingdom of Valencia's district structure (governaciones of Orihuela and Alicante), which the Nueva Planta decrees of 1707-1716 had already stripped of separate jurisdictional status over a century before the 1833 reform created the modern province, and this database does not model either the Kingdom of Valencia or its Nueva Planta successor arrangement at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and the other ESP province rows. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1982, also one of the three provinces of the Valencian Community autonomous region, unlike Asturias or Madrid it was never coextensive with its autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish provincial rows.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Alicante/Alacant as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES521, formerly ES-A code within ES52 Comunidad Valenciana) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES521 records that no existing polity in this database IS Alicante specifically -- only A Coruna and Madrid existed among ESP subnational rows before this batch -- and that the system default start_year of 1833 applies because Alicante is not one of the two Canary Islands provinces created in 1927 by special departure from the default.
- INE gives Alicante province's administrative area as approximately 5,816 km2, making it the smallest of the three provinces of the Comunidad Valenciana (Valencia and Castellon are both larger), a scale fact that distinguishes its statistical series from any national-Spain or whole-Comunidad-Valenciana aggregate.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-single-letter-plate

**Used the single-letter vehicle-plate/INE code A, not a two-letter abbreviation, matching the ESP house convention's per-province logic**

The existing seven ESP subnational rows (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO) each use the short conventional abbreviation for their province, and the untracked sibling batch being created alongside this one (esp-b, esp-t, esp-gi, esp-ab, esp-vi, esp-hu, esp-le, esp-ll, and others) shows that abbreviation is not fixed at two letters: it follows each province's own traditional vehicle-registration/INE alphabetic code, which is a single letter for a handful of provinces (Barcelona=B, Tarragona=T) and two or more letters for the rest. Alicante/Alacant's traditional code under this same system is the single letter A -- the historic vehicle-plate prefix for the province, still in everyday use on car plates, road signage, and INE provincial code tables. Adopting anything else (e.g. AL, which is actually Almeria's code, or ALI) would collide with an existing or predictable sibling code and break the one-code-per-province rule this batch depends on. No other Spanish province claims the letter A, so ESP-A-1833-2025 does not collide with any sibling in this creation batch or in the seven already-committed rows.

### oq-pre-1833-kingdom-valencia-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory (southern Kingdom of Valencia / Governacion de Orihuela-Alicante) has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory now forming Alicante province was part of the historic Kingdom of Valencia, administered through district-level governaciones (notably Orihuela and Alicante/Alacant) that do not map cleanly onto the 1833 province's boundary, and the Kingdom of Valencia itself was abolished as a distinct jurisdiction by the 1707-1716 Nueva Planta decrees, over a century before 1833. This database does not model either the Nueva Planta-era territorial division or the earlier Kingdom of Valencia as distinct polity rows, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent or ill-fitting row -- the same treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings for their own pre-1833 predecessor territories. Whether a Kingdom-of-Valencia-era or Nueva-Planta-era polity should eventually be added as a genuine predecessor (unlike a bare province, Valencia carries a well-documented distinct historical identity and boundary) is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
