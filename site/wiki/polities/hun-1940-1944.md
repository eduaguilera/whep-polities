This row tracks Hungary for **~4 years**, the peak-expansion WWII period
from the Second Vienna Award to the Soviet occupation and forced reversion.

The Second Vienna Award (30 August 1940) added northern Transylvania
(~43,492 km²) from Romania to Hungary. The April 1941 Yugoslav occupation
added the Backa and Baranya strips (~10,000 km²). At peak, Hungary
controlled ~172,000 km² — 58% larger than the Trianon-plus-First-Award
~109k km² baseline. From October 1944 Soviet forces began occupying
Hungary and stripping the annexations; the effective end of this regime is
placed at ~1944-10, when Soviet control made the enlarged borders
non-functional. The Treaty of Paris (10 February 1947) formally declared
the Vienna Awards null and void.

Predecessor: [hun-1938-1940](hun-1938-1940.md) — Second Vienna Award.
Successor: [hun-1944-1947](hun-1944-1947.md) — Soviet reversion.

  ~172,277 km² peak did not reconcile, and its error was the claim that
  Subcarpathian Ruthenia (~13,964 km²) was *"already in the CShapes 1938

  0 km² inside modern Ukraine** — Ruthenia (now Zakarpattia) is absent
  from it entirely. What the feature actually decomposes into is Trianon
  Hungary (**92,991 km²**, identical to the 1920-1938 and 1947-2019 steps)
  plus **15,795 km² inside modern Slovakia** — the First Vienna Award
  slice, and 3,965 km² larger than the ~11,830 the page had claimed.
  92,991 + 15,795 = 108,786, i.e. the whole feature.
  So this polygon is Trianon + First Vienna Award + Northern Transylvania
  proxy (92,991 + 15,795 + 43,685 − 47 km² of overlap = 152,423), and it
  is missing **two** components of the peak, not one: Subcarpathian
  Ruthenia (annexed March 1939, ~12,000 km²) and the April 1941 Bačka and
  Baranja strips (~10,000 km²). Adding both back gives ~174,500 against
  the stated peak of 172,277 — 1.3% high, which is the same First-Vienna
  discrepancy. Neither missing component is isolable from the fetched
  sources.
- **Data magnitude note.** The predecessor polity HUN-1938-1947's staple
  crop series (wheat ~1.82M t, maize ~1.81M t) showed no clear upward
  step at 1940-41, suggesting those source series may have excluded
  annexed territories. Researchers using production data for 1940-1944
  must verify whether the source publisher (FAO, USDA, national
  statistics) included or excluded northern Transylvania.
- **Confirmed with figures for the `juan` source, 2026-08-18.** Sown
  AREA in hectares across the Second Vienna Award boundary, from
  `whep_crops`:

  ```
  maize    1939  1,174,000 ha   1940  1,146,000 ha
  barley   1939    444,000 ha   1940    410,000 ha
  rye      1939    622,000 ha   1940    541,000 ha
  ```

  The award nominally added ~43,700 km², roughly 40% more land. Area
  does not rise at all — it drifts slightly down, and stays flat through
  1943. So `juan`'s Hungary figures for 1940-1944 are on the
  Trianon-plus-First-Vienna basis (~108,785 km²), not the enlarged
  ~152,000-172,000 km² state, and must not be read as representing the
  wartime territory. Area is the right series to test on: output falls in
  wartime for reasons unrelated to borders, while sown area tracks the
  land actually being counted.
- **This is Hungary's own evidence, NOT an instance of a general rule —
  and a proposed general rule was refuted.** A verification pass proposed
  registering, source-wide for `juan`, that "WWII-era European countries
  are reported on a CONSTANT pre-war territorial basis rather than
  adjusting for wartime border changes", generalising from Hungary plus
  the already-registered Finland and Czechoslovakia checks. Romania
  disproves it:

  ```
  juan romania, sown area (ha)     1939        1940      step
    barley                      1,096,000     588,000    -46%
    rye                           448,000      92,000    -79%
    maize                       4,932,000   3,567,000    -28%
  ```

  Romania LOST northern Transylvania, Bessarabia and southern Dobruja in
  1940 and the series drops with the territory; Bulgaria's maize rises
  674,000 → 806,000 ha in 1941 on gaining southern Dobruja. So `juan`
  does adjust for wartime borders in general, and this database's own
  splits (ROU-1940-1947) depend on it doing so. Hungary is flat for its
  own reasons — plausibly that Hungarian statistics kept reporting the
  pre-award area — and that conclusion stands only on the Hungarian
  figures above. The convention was dropped rather than registered.

actual features:

- Hungary gwcode 310, 1938-1947 feature = **108,785 km²** — Trianon
  (92,991 km²) plus the First Vienna Award only. It does **not** include
  northern Transylvania.
- Romania gwcode 360: the 1920-1940 feature is 296,087 km² and the 1940-2019

  **Bessarabia + northern Bukovina + southern Dobruja** — the territory

  includes northern Transylvania, which returned to Romania in 1947.

So that subtraction yields the wrong territory entirely. **CShapes 2.0 never
models the 1940-44 Hungarian holding of northern Transylvania** — the same gap
documented on [rou-1940-1947](rou-1940-1947.md) — so this row's ~167,492 km²
extent cannot be composed from it.

Resolving it needs a source with the Second Vienna Award boundary (or a
hand-digitised northern-Transylvania polygon) added as a builder in
`scripts/sources/constructed/build.py`. Until then the 201 layer-B rows matched
here have no geometry, which is the honest state rather than a wrong one.
