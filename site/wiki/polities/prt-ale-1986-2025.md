# Alentejo (NUTS II region of Portugal)

## Summary

Alentejo is the NUTS II statistical region covering southern-central Portugal south of the Tagus (roughly the districts of Portalegre, Évora, Beja, and parts of Setúbal), one of Portugal's official EU statistical divisions established after the 1986 EEC accession and the country's adoption of the NUTS (Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics) framework from 1989. `type: subnational` is used deliberately, mirroring the treatment of Portugal's other nine NUTS units (PT11 Norte, PT15 Algarve, PT16 Centro, PT17 Lisboa, PTAV Açores, PTBJ, PTEV, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG) already handled the same way: Portugal held unbroken sovereignty throughout, and this row exists purely to carry a reporting territory smaller than the national total, not to assert independent statehood. The region is the country's largest by area and one of its most sparsely populated, historically dominated by large cereal and cork-oak (montado) estates and, more recently, by irrigated agriculture fed by the Alqueva reservoir.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures livestock_stock series for the unit labelled PT18/Alentejo, with measured values reaching back to 1870 in the source even though the modern NUTS classification only exists from 1989. Following the established country convention for Portugal's other nine NUTS sibling units, pre-1989 years for this territory were left unrouted (marked unroutable) rather than force-matched to the national PRT row, because no earlier-period administrative unit sharing exactly Alentejo's boundary is named in the evidence — the pre-1989 territory was instead split across the historic districts of Portalegre, Évora and Beja, which the source does not report separately for this series. This row was previously implicitly absorbed into the national polity PRT-1800-2025 by default routing, which is wrong because the source's reporting unit is a specific sub-national region roughly one-seventh of Portugal's area, not the whole country. That Alentejo is treated as a distinct statistical/reporting entity is confirmed by Eurostat's own NUTS nomenclature, which assigns it the code PT18/PT2 and publishes region-specific statistics for it independently of the other eight Portuguese NUTS II divisions.

## Territorial extent


**Polygon status:** Assigned (2026-09-11). `polygon_source: eurostat-gisco-nuts2`, `polygon_feature_id: PT18`, `polygon_status: assigned`. The source is Eurostat GISCO's official NUTS 2021 level-2 boundary file, newly registered in `scripts/sources.yaml`, and the attached feature measures **31,519 km²** (projected, ESRI:54034). Registering it was necessary rather than convenient: a NUTS II region cannot be built by unioning whole districts, because the districts predate NUTS and straddle its boundaries — a district-built Alentejo falls 24.9% short and a district-built Norte 14.7% short of the official region. The five regions now sum to 88,798 km², against 88,801 km² for the eighteen districts covering the same mainland, a 0.003% difference.

**Polygon status (superseded 2026-09-11, kept as the record of what blocked it):** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The only source that plausibly digitises this exact boundary — Eurostat/GISCO's NUTS region boundary files — is not currently a registered source in sources.yaml (checked: gadm-4.1-adm1 excludes Portugal at admin-1 level entirely; cshapes, cliopatria, histogis, paine, reporting-areas and constructed carry no Portuguese sub-national geometry). Because no registered source or registrable-but-unfetched source exists, and no combination of already-registered features can be unioned to build Alentejo (construct_from_registered is not executable without a source that has Portuguese admin boundaries to union), this is a `new_source_needed` case: `polygon_source` is set to `none` and the desired source is recorded as an open question rather than declared here.

**Territory description:** Alentejo occupies the region south of the Tagus river and north of the Algarve, covering roughly a third of mainland Portugal — commonly cited at approximately 31,600 km² for the NUTS II region as currently delimited (post-2015 boundary, which folds in the Lezíria do Tejo sub-region). This can be located on a modern map as the area bounded by Spain to the east, the Atlantic coast around Sines/Alentejo Litoral to the west, the Tagus estuary/Lisbon region to the north, and the Algarve to the south. No figure is measured from an attached polygon here since none is attached; the ~31,600 km² figure is drawn from published Eurostat NUTS region statistics, not from this page's geometry.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor is assigned: pre-1989 Alentejo data is deliberately left unrouted under the sibling-consistent convention rather than retroactively backdating this polity or inventing an unattested earlier polity code. No successor is assigned since the NUTS classification remains current; end_year is set open at 2025 per the country convention even though the underlying livestock data series ends in 2020.

## Sourced claims

- Eurostat's NUTS nomenclature (2021 version) assigns Alentejo the code PT18 within PT2, distinct from Portugal's other eight NUTS II/III statistical divisions, confirming it is tabulated as a separate reporting unit.
- The livestock_stock source series for PT18/Alentejo reports measured values from 1870 through 2020, predating by over a century the 1989 introduction of the NUTS framework that currently names the unit.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Used <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> per the 57-row dominant pattern**

Alentejo is a standard EU statistical sub-national region, not a historical territory with a name and identity independent of its administrative classification (unlike the three bespoke-coded rows ALK, HYD, RYU). It therefore follows the dominant 57-row pattern ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end used for the other NUTS siblings (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025), giving PRT-ALE-1986-2025. Since PRT currently has zero existing subnational codes, this entry also sets the abbreviation convention (ALE for Alentejo) that future PRT NUTS entries should follow for consistency.

### d-prt-two-level-hierarchy

**Portugal is modelled as two levels — 18 districts, and from 1986 the five NUTS II regions that contain them**

The panel reports Portugal at two scales at once: five NUTS II regions and eighteen districts. Both sets tile mainland Portugal independently — measured on the committed geometry the regions sum to 88,798 km² and the districts to 88,801 km², a 0.003% difference — so treating them as one flat list of members would double-count every square kilometre. They are not a clean hierarchy either: the districts date from 1835 and NUTS II was delimited later across them, so five districts straddle a regional boundary (Lisboa is only 47% inside Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, Santarém 60%, Aveiro 63%, Viseu 65%, Setúbal 69%).

The resolution is a hierarchy **split in time**, which works because the regions postdate the districts. Until 1986 the districts are Portugal's direct members (17 of them, 18 once Setúbal exists in 1926) and no region competes with them. From 1986 the five regions become Portugal's only direct members and every district sits inside the region holding the largest share of its area, with that share recorded in the edge's own `basis`. A level-1 read is therefore exact in both periods; a level-2 read of a straddling district is approximate, and says so where it is.

For this row that means: inside Portugal (PRT-1800-2025) 1986-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-nuts-ii-geometry-needs-eurostat-gisco

**A union of whole districts is not an acceptable proxy for this NUTS II boundary, measured**

GADM's adm1 layer for Portugal is its 18 mainland DISTRICTS, and NUTS II boundaries cut across them, so a union of whole districts under-covers this region. Measured against the official NUTS II area:

| region | district union | official | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Norte | 18,156 km² | 21,278 km² | −14.7% |
| Centro | 27,397 km² | 28,199 km² | −2.8% |
| Alentejo | 23,740 km² | 31,605 km² | −24.9% |

For Alentejo that is -24.9%. Attaching it would publish a polygon missing a large share of the territory while `polygon_status` claimed it was real, so the geometry is left unassigned. The correct source is the Eurostat GISCO NUTS geometry at the matching vintage — the same release whose attribute table already supplies data/final/nuts_code_names.csv — which needs registering as a source and fetching. Algarve is the one exception and is already assigned: NUTS II Algarve is coterminous with the district of Faro.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** Eurostat GISCO is now registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` as `eurostat-gisco-nuts2` (NUTS 2021, level 2, EPSG:4326) and this row carries feature `PT18`, measuring **31,519 km²** projected (ESRI:54034). It was registered precisely because no union of whole districts reproduces a NUTS II region — a district-built Alentejo is 24.9% short and a district-built Norte 14.7% short — so there was nothing to attach until the regions' own geometry was fetched.

### oq-pre1989-alentejo-districts

**Did a historic pre-1989 administrative unit report Alentejo's livestock data separately?**

119 of 151 data years (79%) for this unit are pre-1989 and were marked unroutable under the sibling convention rather than matched. Pre-1989, the Alentejo area was administratively split across the districts (distritos) of Portalegre, Évora and Beja. If the underlying source actually reports a distrito-level or an informal 'Alentejo province' aggregate for those years (Portugal's traditional provinces, abolished administratively in 1976 but used informally in statistics well after), those years should be routed to a new predecessor polity spanning the relevant period, rather than left permanently unrouted. This mirrors the resolution already applied to Aveiro as a named departure from the sibling default, and should be checked before assuming the 1870-1989 gap is correctly left unfilled across all ten Portuguese NUTS rows.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** This row is a NUTS II region, and Portugal's NUTS II regions date from the 1986 accession to the European Communities, so the span now runs 1986-2025 rather than claiming the region existed earlier. The extract's 1870-2020 data therefore splits: 35 years (1986-2020) are carried here, and the earlier years are a **back-cast** of regional aggregates onto a boundary that did not yet exist, which is recorded as such rather than routed to this row. The districts that make up this region carry their own pre-1986 data directly under Portugal — Portugal's 1835 administrative reform created 17 mainland districts (distritos); Setúbal, the 18th, was carved out of Lisboa district in 1926. Ten of this table's Portuguese district rows already started in 1835, and the arithmetic closes exactly at 17 + 1 once the seven late-starting rows are corrected, which is the check that settled it.

### oq-eurostat-gisco-source-registration

**Should eurostat-gisco-nuts be registered as a polygon source for Portuguese and other EU NUTS regions?**

No registered source in sources.yaml currently carries Portuguese sub-national boundaries at any vintage, which leaves all ten Portuguese NUTS rows (this one included) with polygon_status unassigned. Eurostat/GISCO publishes versioned NUTS boundary files (e.g. NUTS 2016, NUTS 2021) that would resolve this for all ten rows at once, but licence terms have not been verified and no vintage has been checked for full 1989-2025 coverage under a single boundary (NUTS regions have been re-delimited at least once, in 2015, affecting Alentejo's own extent). This should be resolved as a batch registration decision rather than per-entry.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** Eurostat GISCO is now registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` as `eurostat-gisco-nuts2` (NUTS 2021, level 2, EPSG:4326) and this row carries feature `PT18`, measuring **31,519 km²** projected (ESRI:54034). It was registered precisely because no union of whole districts reproduces a NUTS II region — a district-built Alentejo is 24.9% short and a district-built Norte 14.7% short — so there was nothing to attach until the regions' own geometry was fetched.

### oq-nuts2015-boundary-change

**Does the 2015 NUTS boundary revision change what Alentejo covers across this entry's own span?**

The 2015 NUTS revision moved the Lezíria do Tejo sub-region into Alentejo, changing its area from earlier vintages. Since this entry spans 1989-2025 under one polity code, whichever polygon is eventually assigned needs to state which vintage it represents and whether a single feature can legitimately stand in for both the pre-2015 and post-2015 delimitation, or whether the entry needs to be split at 2015.
