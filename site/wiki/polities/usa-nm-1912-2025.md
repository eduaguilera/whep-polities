# New Mexico (state of the United States)

## Summary

New Mexico is one of the fifty United States, admitted to the Union on January 6, 1912 as the 47th state, the second-to-last of the contiguous states to be admitted (Arizona followed six weeks later). Before statehood the same territory was organized as New Mexico Territory, created in 1850 out of land ceded by Mexico after the Mexican-American War (with the 1853 Gadsden Purchase adding its southern strip), and progressively reduced in size as Colorado (1861), Arizona (1863), and other territories were split off from it over six decades. This database does not model that 62-year territorial phase as a distinct row: data from 1879-1911 that falls inside the routed unit is back-cast onto this polity rather than pointing at a separate territorial-era entity, matching the treatment given to sibling states with pre-admission data such as Colorado (USA-CO-1876-2025). This entry is type: subnational because New Mexico is a constituent state of the sovereign United States, not a sovereign polity itself: the USA held sovereignty over this territory throughout the routed span, and the row exists purely to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate. No existing polity represented New Mexico specifically before this decision -- the six USA-iso3 candidates in the table are either national-era totals (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) or the bespoke Territory of Alaska row (ALK-1867-1959), none of which is New Mexico.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id USA-NEWMEXICO) captures valued statistical rows for the administrative unit "New Mexico", country United States of America, under the default state-start rule of the USA country convention -- New Mexico is not one of the enumerated departures (Alaska, Hawaii, original thirteen colonies), so its start_year is its actual admission date, January 6, 1912. The routing_reasoning records that pre-1912 data years (1879-1911) are handled as back_cast onto this polity rather than a separate territorial-era row, consistent with the treatment of sibling states with pre-admission data (e.g. California, Colorado). Previously matched to: nothing dedicated -- absent this entry, New Mexico's rows would fall back to whichever USA national-era row spans the data year (USA-1867-1959 for 1879-1959 data, USA-1959-2025 thereafter), collapsing a single state into a country-level total roughly 25x New Mexico's own area for the contiguous-USA national row. What confirms distinctness: New Mexico has been continuously tabulated as a distinct first-order administrative unit by the U.S. Census Bureau and federal agricultural/economic statistical series since territorial days, at the same order of statistical granularity as other US states already present in this database (e.g. Colorado, USA-CO-1876-2025) -- a state is not its country, and federal series report at state granularity as a matter of course.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Copied from the registered gadm-4.1-adm1 source, feature USA.32_1 (a direct source-feature assignment, not a proxy copy from a sibling polity period). gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered in scripts/sources.yaml and the local extract at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_USA.gpkg includes a single ADM_ADM_1 feature with GID_1 "USA.32_1", NAME_1 "New Mexico": an unambiguous match. polygon_status is therefore assigned, not proxy, since this is the state's own modern GADM boundary, not one borrowed from a different period or unit. Vintage risk across the 1912-2025 span is low: New Mexico's boundary was fixed at admission in 1912 to the rectangular outline already established for the territory (apart from the 1911 Texas-New Mexico boundary survey settling the eastern line just before statehood), so the present-day GADM boundary is a good proxy for the state's boundary throughout its history as a state, and for the 1879-1911 back-cast territorial-era data.

Territory description: New Mexico occupies a roughly rectangular block of the southern Rocky Mountain and high-desert region of the southwestern United States, bordered by Colorado to the north, Oklahoma and the Texas panhandle to the east, Texas and Mexico (Chihuahua) to the south, and Arizona to the west, with a small strip (the "bootheel") extending south between Arizona and Texas to reach the Mexican border. Its capital is Santa Fe and its largest city Albuquerque; the terrain ranges from the southern Rocky Mountains and Rio Grande valley in the north-center to the Chihuahuan Desert in the south. Its area is approximately 314,917 km2 as measured from the attached gadm-4.1-adm1 geometry in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034) -- a measurement of the attached polygon, not an independently sourced official area, though consistent with commonly cited state-area figures (~314,900-315,200 km2 depending on land-vs-total-area convention), which corroborates rather than substitutes for the geometric measurement.

## Predecessors and successors

New Mexico has no predecessor polity in this database: before January 6, 1912, the same territory was organized as New Mexico Territory (established 1850, itself reduced over time as Colorado, Arizona and other territories were carved from parts of it), but that pre-statehood territorial phase is not modelled as a distinct row here. Data years 1879-1911 that fall inside the routed unit are back-cast onto this polity per the routing decision, rather than pointing at a separate territorial-era entity, matching the treatment given to sibling states such as Colorado. New Mexico has no successor either: it remains a current, active US state as of 2025, hence the open end_year of 2025 (exclusive), matching the convention used for other still-active USA subnational rows.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-state-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter postal abbreviation NM rather than a numeric FIPS code, matching the dominant subnational pattern**

The dominant subnational pattern measured from the polity table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, covering 57 of 66 subnational rows, using short letter abbreviations (already established for other USA state rows such as USA-CO-1876-2025 and the Spanish province rows ESP-AS, ESP-CA). This page follows that convention, minting USA-NM-1912-2025 with NM as the standard USPS/ISO 3166-2 postal abbreviation for New Mexico, rather than a FIPS numeric code (35) or a bespoke historical name. A bespoke code (the ALK/HYD/RYU pattern) was not used because New Mexico, unlike the Territory of Alaska, is not a historical territory with its own distinct name and identity separate from its role as a present-day US state -- it is simply one of the fifty states, so the standard <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT> pattern applies directly.

### d-two-container-edges-for-usa-national-eras

**Split the container into two edges to tile New Mexico's full 1912-2025 span across two USA national-era rows**

New Mexico's span (1912-2025) crosses the boundary between two USA national-era rows in the polity table: USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025. A single container edge pointing at only one of these would leave part of New Mexico's span uncontained and would be rejected by the containment gate, which requires an edge to fall entirely within both parties' spans. This page therefore uses two container edges -- USA-1867-1959 for 1912-1959 and USA-1959-2025 for 1959-2025 -- together tiling the full 1912-2025 span with no gap, following the same one-edge-per-containing-era practice used on sibling state pages such as USA-CO-1876-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1912-territorial-data-backcast

**1879-1911 pre-statehood data is back-cast onto the 1912-2025 state boundary without a distinct territorial-era row**

The routing decision states that data years 1879-1911 (predating the January 1912 admission) are handled as back_cast onto this polity, consistent with treatment of other states with pre-admission data. But New Mexico Territory (1850-1912) was not identical in boundary to the eventual state for most of that period -- it was reduced repeatedly as Colorado (1861) and Arizona (1863) were carved off, and its final territorial boundary was not settled until the 1911 Texas boundary survey, just before statehood. This entry does not verify that the 1879-1911 rows in fact refer to the post-1863 reduced territorial extent (already close to the modern state) rather than an earlier, larger unorganized-territory extent. Whoever next touches sourced data for this polity should confirm the 1879-1911 rows describe the New Mexico Territory boundary as it stood in that period before treating the back-cast as settled.

### oq-bootheel-boundary-precision

**The Gadsden Purchase / bootheel boundary segment has not been checked against the GADM feature for historical accuracy**

New Mexico's southern boundary includes the distinctive "bootheel" strip added by the 1853 Gadsden Purchase, and the exact alignment of this segment (along with the 1911 Texas-New Mexico line settlement) has not been independently verified against the gadm-4.1-adm1 USA.32_1 feature -- only the state's approximate area and general shape were checked against commonly cited figures. If a future author needs boundary precision along the Mexico border or the Texas panhandle line for a period-specific purpose, the present-day GADM vintage should be checked against a historical-vintage source rather than assumed identical.
