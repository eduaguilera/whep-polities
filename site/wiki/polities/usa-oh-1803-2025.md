# Ohio (state of the United States)

## Summary

This entry represents Ohio, a state of the United States, from its admission to the Union on March 1, 1803 to the present (2025). `type: subnational` because Ohio is a constituent state within the sovereign United States, not an independent polity — sovereignty and the national data chain remain with the USA rows (USA-1803-1848 through USA-1959-2025); this row exists to give state-level statistical series their own reporting territory, distinct from national USA totals. No prior polity row in the table represented Ohio specifically: the only USA-family candidates were national totals (which are roughly 85 times Ohio's land area) and the Territory of Alaska (an unrelated, non-contiguous U.S. territory). GADM 4.1 confirms Ohio as a real, distinct administrative unit (feature USA.36_1), and this entry mirrors the already-established precedent of USA-CALIFORNIA and USA-INDIANA in this table: an ordinary, non-original, non-Alaska/Hawaii state admitted after 1800, coded and dated the same way, with start_year set to its own 1803 admission date per the country convention's default rule rather than to the data extract's 1866 start year or to a container's start year.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: routing decision unit_id USA-OHIO, admin_name OHIO, verdict create_new, dated to this harness run against the country conventions state (pipelines/agent-harness/state/country_conventions.json) and routing_verdicts.csv; the proposed span is 1803-2025 with container USA-1959-2025 as the immediate era, iso3 USA. No specific dataset row count has been confirmed as routed to this code yet (see open question oq-data-coverage) — the entry is created by the harness's routing decision ahead of, and to enable, that matching, exactly as USA-IN-1816-2025 was. Previously, any Ohio-labelled statistical data would have matched only the national USA rows, which cover the entire United States (about 9.8 million km2 versus Ohio's 116,098 km2 — roughly 85x over-broad as a container for state-level figures), or would have gone unmatched. What confirms Ohio is a distinct entity worth its own row: it is one of the fifty United States, admitted in 1803 with its own government and statistical agencies, and GADM-recognized as a distinct administrative boundary (feature USA.36_1) separate from every neighboring state and from the national aggregate — the same standard already applied to sibling entries such as California, Indiana, and Alaska in this table.

## Territorial extent

Polygon: gadm-4.1-adm1, feature USA.36_1 (Ohio), polygon_status: assigned — a direct, non-proxy match, since GADM's present-day Ohio boundary is the same boundary the state has held since the 1836 Toledo War settlement. This is a registered source already used for sibling U.S. state entries in this table (e.g. ALK-1867-1959 and USA-IN-1816-2025 use the same GADM release). Area: approximately 116,098 km2 (44,826 sq mi), the state's well-known modern land+water area, consistent with the measured area of the attached GADM geometry (this figure is a property of the attached shape, not independent evidence about the historical territory). Ohio lies in the American Midwest, bordered by Lake Erie and Michigan to the north, Pennsylvania to the east, West Virginia to the southeast (across the Ohio River), Kentucky to the south (also across the Ohio River), and Indiana to the west — a shape and location any reader can locate on a modern U.S. map without difficulty. Why the polygon is a direct assignment, not a proxy: Ohio's borders were substantially set at admission in 1803 (the Ohio River line with Kentucky and West Virginia, the survey lines with Pennsylvania and Indiana) and reached their final form in 1836 when the Toledo Strip was transferred from Michigan Territory to Ohio in exchange for the Upper Peninsula. Since 1836 the external boundary has not changed. There is no analogous case here to the Manchuria-style proxy problem, where the reporting unit and the available geometry diverge — the unit this row is created to hold (the state of Ohio) and the geometry available (modern Ohio) are the same territory for all but the row's first 33 years.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: before March 1, 1803, the territory that became Ohio was part of the Northwest Territory (organized 1787), which is not separately represented in this polity table and is not this row's predecessor since the table currently has no pre-statehood Northwest Territory entry. No successor: Ohio remains a current U.S. state and this row's end_year (2025) is the table's open-ended present, matching the convention used for other still-existing subnational U.S. entries (e.g. USA-IN-1816-2025).

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern, not the bespoke form**

Ohio is an ordinary U.S. state admitted through the standard Northwest Ordinance territorial process, with no distinct historical name or contested-sovereignty period of its own (unlike Alaska Territory 1867-1959, coded bespoke as ALK-1867-1959, or historical named territories like Hyderabad or the Ryukyus). It therefore follows the dominant 57-of-66 subnational pattern, <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, giving USA-OH-1803-2025, rather than a bespoke code. This mirrors the sibling precedents USA-CALIFORNIA and USA-INDIANA (create_new), which the routing decision explicitly cites as analogous, and follows the same reasoning: Ohio's own start_year (1803, its admission date) is used rather than the data extract's start year (1866) or a container's start year, per the country convention's default state-admission start rule.

## Open questions

### oq-boundary-vintage

**Modern GADM boundary used as proxy back to 1803 admission**

The attached polygon (gadm-4.1-adm1, feature USA.36_1) reflects Ohio's present-day boundary. Ohio's external state lines were largely fixed at or shortly after admission in 1803 (the Ohio River line with Kentucky and (West) Virginia, the survey lines with Indiana and Pennsylvania), but the northern boundary with Michigan was not finally settled until the Toledo War of 1835-1836, which transferred the Toledo Strip to Ohio in exchange for Michigan's Upper Peninsula. No historical-boundary source has been checked to confirm the pre-1836 Ohio polygon differed materially from the modern GADM shape, so the 1803-1836 portion of this row's span carries an unquantified vintage risk that the 1836-2025 portion does not.
