# Tibet (1913-1950)

## Summary

Tibet under the Ganden Phodrang government in Lhasa, "a de facto independent state ... from the
collapse of the Qing dynasty in 1912 until its annexation by the People's Republic of China in
1951". The Lhasa government "controlled all of Ü-Tsang ... and western Kham ..., roughly coincident
with the borders of the Tibet Autonomous Region today", while eastern Kham, beyond the Yangtze, was
held by the Sichuan warlord Liu Wenhui.
[wikipedia-geodata-batch-2-2026-09-25 §tibet](../sources/wikipedia-geodata-batch-2-2026-09-25.md#tibet)

The span is **1913-1950**, the years every China row from [chn-1913-1914](chn-1913-1914.md) to
[chn-1949-1950](chn-1949-1950.md) leaves Tibet out of its polygon (CShapes codes Tibet as its own
state, gwcode 711, from 1913-01-01 to 1950-10-01). From 1950 [chn-1950-2025](chn-1950-2025.md)
contains it. Typed `national`, with `iso3: NA` so that the row cannot enter China's iso family and
take `china` labels by year.
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-states](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-states)

## Why this entry exists

FAO 1952 reports `Tibet` on its own in its 1947 land-use table: `use total` 121,578 thousand ha,
i.e. **1,215,780 km²**, beside `China` and its parts (issue 449's family of labels). **1 row**
resolved to no polity. Before this row Lhasa lay in no polygon for 1913-1950.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status: `proxy`.** GADM 4.1 adm1 `CHN.29_1` (Xizang, the modern Tibet Autonomous Region),
**1,125,949 km²** measured (ESRI:54034), against FAO's 1,215,780 (0.93).
[gadm-4.1 §what-it-adds](../sources/gadm-4.1.md#what-it-adds)

- **Why not CShapes 711.** CShapes' own Tibet (1914-1950 step) measures **1,811,596 km²**, 1.49x
  FAO's statement. It draws the whole Tibetan plateau outside China's polygon, eastern Kham and
  southern Qinghai included, which the Lhasa government did not hold. The article's description
  of the Lhasa government's reach, "roughly coincident with the borders of the Tibet Autonomous
  Region today", and FAO's area both point at the TAR outline.
- **Direction of the error.** The TAR outline is about 7% below FAO's figure; the difference is
  not located. Its eastern side runs along the Upper Yangtze for much of its length, the river the
  article gives as the line between Lhasa's western Kham and Liu Wenhui's eastern Kham.
- **The rest of CShapes' Tibet** (709,718 km² outside the TAR outline) belongs to no row for
  1913-1950, as it did before this row existed.
- **Overlap with the China rows.** 21,073 km² of the TAR outline (1.9%) lies inside the China rows'
  CShapes polygons for 1913-1950, where the two sources draw the line differently. 1,101,878 km² of it is inside CShapes' Tibet.
