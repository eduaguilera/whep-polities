# Albacete (province of Spain)

## Summary

Albacete is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today the sole province making up the Castilla-La Mancha autonomous community's easternmost territory, in the semi-arid plateau (the Mancha and, in the province's south, the Sierra de Alcaraz) of southeastern central Spain. It takes its name from its capital, the city of Albacete, historically a crossroads town on the route between Madrid and the Levante coast and known today for its cutlery-manufacturing tradition. This entry is type: subnational because Albacete is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Albacete separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES421, for over a century and a half. The routing decision for unit ES421 found no existing candidate matching Albacete by name (the only ESP subnational rows on file at the time were Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, and Pontevedra -- none of them Albacete, and none of them the province the source data actually measures), so this is a genuine create_new case, following the same pattern already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES421 ("Albacete"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES112 Lugo, ES113 Ourense, ES111 A Coruna, and onward through the ES1xx/ES4xx series), all following a system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing reasoning explicitly checked the ESP candidates on file and found none IS Albacete -- the existing rows were a different province (Madrid), a different region entirely (the Galician/northern provinces), and the national total, none of which represents Albacete's own reporting territory; folding its data into the national ESP-1800-2025 row would collapse a province-level reporting geography into an aggregate roughly 34x larger (505,000 km2 national vs Albacete's ~14,924 km2), losing the province-level distinction the source data actually carries. What confirms distinctness: Albacete's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES421) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entries ESP-LU-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025, and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national aggregate, and sibling units in the same NUTS3 batch are being uniformly created as new subnational polities under the country's default 1833-2025 span rule.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish province numeric code), under which Albacete is conventionally cpro 02 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False in scripts/sources.yaml (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository), so the feature cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet, exactly as ESP-LU-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025, and ESP-MD-1833-2025 do for the same reason. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level: gadm-4.1-adm1 has 18 ESP features and none match Albacete by name (Castilla-La Mancha is a single adm1 feature covering five provinces -- Albacete, Ciudad Real, Cuenca, Guadalajara, Toledo -- so an adm1 feature would be roughly 5x too large and the wrong granularity entirely). Territory description: Albacete province occupies the easternmost part of Castilla-La Mancha, in southeastern central Spain, bordered by Cuenca to the north, Valencia and Alicante (Comunitat Valenciana) to the east, Murcia to the south, and Ciudad Real and Jaen to the west. Its capital and largest city is Albacete, on the flat Mancha de Montearagon plain; the province's south rises into the Sierra de Alcaraz and the source of the Mundo and Guadalquivir tributaries. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 14,924 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform (Albacete was not among the units affected by the 1927 Canary Islands split or any other known province-level boundary revision, though it did absorb a small number of municipalities from neighbouring Murcia/Jaen in minor 19th-century adjustments not yet cross-checked here), so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling province entries.

## Predecessors and successors

Albacete has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was split across parts of the historic Kingdom of Murcia and the Kingdom of Toledo/La Mancha jurisdictions under the ancien-regime provincial system, which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-LU-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025, and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1982, the easternmost of the five provinces making up the Castilla-La Mancha autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for its Spanish NUTS3 sibling rows.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Albacete as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES421) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ES421 records that sibling Spanish NUTS3 units were routed create_new under the same 1833-start/2025-open-end country convention, which is the evidentiary basis for treating Albacete as a distinct, continuously-tabulated reporting geography rather than folding it into the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.
- mapspain-ign is the source already used to register the other Spanish province rows created in this convention pass (ESP-LU-1833-2025 for cpro=27, ESP-CO-1833-2025 for cpro=15, ESP-MD-1833-2025), and Albacete's own INE province code (cpro) is 02, the routing decision's basis for expecting a matching feature once the source is fetched.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-ab-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation AB, matching the already-decided ESP subnational house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name, and the existing ESP subnational rows follow it exactly: ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-OR, ESP-PO. Albacete is not a historical territory with a name and identity of its own independent of its status as a Spanish province (unlike the bespoke cases ALK-1867-1959, HYD-1724-1948, RYU-1937-1945), so the bespoke-code pattern does not apply here. AB is chosen as the abbreviation because it is the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for Albacete (ES-AB) and reads unambiguously against the sibling AS/CA/CO/LU/MD/OR/PO codes, giving ESP-AB-1833-2025. This also keeps the code independent of the INE province numeric code (02) and the NUTS3 code (ES421), neither of which appears in the sibling codes either.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and Albacete's cpro=02 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Albacete is conventionally cpro 02 (its INE province code) -- but the routing decision for ES421 could not confirm this because the source file is marked present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence for redistribution has not been checked. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=02 feature is confirmed to be Albacete (and confirmed to match the 1833-2025 province boundary rather than some other vintage), this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry, exactly as the sibling province entries do for the same reason. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Albacete before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Albacete province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Albacete province was split across parts of the historic Kingdom of Murcia's western districts and the La Mancha region of the Kingdom of Toledo, which do not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and are not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Albacete's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for the sibling province entries. Whether pre-1833 divisions should eventually be modeled at all remains an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.

### oq-boundary-adjustments-unchecked

**Minor 19th/20th-century municipal boundary adjustments with neighbouring provinces have not been cross-checked**

The routing reasoning asserts Albacete has no known 1927 Canary Islands-style span departure, and this is very likely correct for the province's start year, but Albacete's exact boundary has not been independently verified against a canonical list of Spanish provincial boundary adjustments since 1833 -- some municipalities near the Albacete/Murcia and Albacete/Jaen borders are known anecdotally to have shifted provincial affiliation in minor 19th-century reorganizations, distinct from the Canary Islands split. This does not affect start_year or end_year, but could affect the eventual polygon vintage once mapspain-ign is fetched, since a present-day IGN boundary might not exactly match the 1833-1900-era boundary if any such transfer occurred.
