# Aveiro (district of Portugal)

## Summary

Aveiro is one of the historic districts (distritos) of Portugal, located on the northwest Atlantic coast between Porto and Coimbra. The district was created in 1835 as part of the administrative reorganisation that established Portugal's mainland district system (replacing the old province structure), and it has persisted as the country's officially recognised sub-national reporting unit ever since, even as districts lost most administrative functions after Portugal's accession to the EU introduced NUTS statistical regions in 1986. This entry is `type: subnational` because Aveiro is a component of Portugal, not a sovereign polity of its own; it exists to carry data reported at the district level rather than to compete with the national PRT-1800-2025 row for sovereignty-based matching. Aveiro's district capital is the city of Aveiro, and the district roughly covers the modern municipalities of Aveiro, Águeda, Espinho, Ílhavo, Oliveira de Azeméis, Ovar, São João da Madeira, Santa Maria da Feira, Vagos, and several others along the Vouga river basin and Ria de Aveiro lagoon.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data reported against the label 'PT_AV' / Aveiro as a Portuguese sub-national reporting unit, per the routing decision for unit_id PRT-PTAV. That data was previously routed toward the national row PRT-1800-2025 by default matching logic, which is wrong because a district is a fraction of the country, not the whole of it -- national-scale figures cannot stand in for district-scale figures without inflating the implied population/area by roughly an order of magnitude. The country convention for Portugal explicitly lists 'Aveiro' as a named departure from the 1986 NUTS-default rule applied to sibling units (PT11, PT15, PT16, PT17, PT18, PTBJ, PTEV, PTFA, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG): because the official reporting name is exactly 'Aveiro' rather than a NUTS code, and Aveiro as a historic distrito predates the EU NUTS framework by over 150 years (creation in 1835), the district-era start year applies instead of the 1986 NUTS cutover used for its siblings. No historical trading-polity source (e.g. Federico-Tena) treats Aveiro separately, since districts are an internal Portuguese administrative unit rather than an independent customs or trading territory; the confirmation of distinctness here rests instead on Portugal's own administrative law creating and continuously maintaining Aveiro as a named district since 1835, distinct from its neighbouring districts (Porto, Coimbra, Viseu) and from the country as a whole.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. No registered source in scripts/sources.yaml carries Portuguese sub-national boundaries: gadm-4.1-adm1 is present locally but only for a curated 81-country subset that excludes Portugal, and no other registered source (cshapes-europe, paine-2024, cliopatria, histogis-1860-habsburg, constructed, reporting-areas) targets Portuguese sub-national units. The real boundary would come from Portugal's official CAOP (Carta Administrativa Oficial, DGT) or Eurostat GISCO NUTS III shapefiles, neither of which is registered or fetched here, so `polygon_route: new_source_needed` rather than `registered_source_unfetched` (no slug exists for this at all) -- accordingly `polygon_source` is `none` here and the candidate source is recorded as an open question rather than declared as a value that would fail validate_declared_sources. **Territory description:** Aveiro district covers roughly 2,808 km2 of coastal north-central Portugal, stretching from the Atlantic coast inland along the Vouga river valley, bounded by Porto district to the north, Viseu and Guarda districts to the east, and Coimbra district to the south. On a modern map it corresponds approximately to the present-day Aveiro sub-region/NUTS III area, including the Ria de Aveiro lagoon system and the cities of Aveiro, Ovar, and Santa Maria da Feira. No polygon-derived km2 figure is attached to this entry, since none is assigned; the ~2,808 km2 figure above is a territory-description estimate from the modern district's administrative extent, not a geometry measurement.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity is listed. Aveiro district's 1835 creation was part of a general national reorganisation replacing the earlier province system, so there is no single prior 'Aveiro' administrative unit to treat as a predecessor -- the pre-1835 territory was governed under overlapping province and comarca structures that do not map cleanly onto the new district. No successor is listed because the district, though administratively hollowed out after 1986, has not been formally abolished and remains the reporting unit referenced by the routing decision through the container chain's open end_year of 2025.

## Sourced claims

- Portugal's mainland district (distrito) system, including Aveiro, was established by the administrative reform of 1835 that replaced the earlier province-based structure, and Aveiro has been continuously maintained as a named district since that reform.
- Since Portugal's 1986 accession-driven adoption of the EU NUTS statistical framework, most Portuguese districts (including Aveiro's siblings PT11, PT15, etc.) are reported using NUTS codes rather than historic district names, but the country convention records Aveiro as an explicit exception reported under its historic name.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-container-span

**Use PRT-AV code form and a single container edge spanning 1835-2025**

Followed the dominant 57/66 subnational pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, using PRT-AV-1835-2025 rather than a bespoke code, since Aveiro is an ordinary administrative district and not a historical territory with its own separate identity comparable to Alaska Territory or Hyderabad. The container edge uses a single row (PRT-1800-2025, 1835-2025) because the polity table shows only one PRT national-era row spanning the country's whole 1800-2025 existence, so a single edge fully tiles the span with no gap, unlike cases where the container itself changed identity mid-span.

## Open questions

### oq-aveiro-polygon-source

**What source should eventually supply Aveiro's boundary polygon?**

No registered source currently carries Portuguese district-level boundaries. The two named candidates are Portugal's official CAOP (DGT) dataset and Eurostat GISCO NUTS III shapefiles; neither is registered in scripts/sources.yaml nor present in local geodata. Whichever is adopted, using a present-day CAOP/NUTS boundary to represent the district back to 1835 would be a real anachronism -- district boundaries have likely shifted at the margins (municipality reassignments) over 190 years -- and that vintage risk should be stated explicitly if/when a polygon is assigned, per the same caution already raised in the routing decision.

### oq-aveiro-pre1986-siblings-asymmetry

**Is Aveiro's departure from the sibling default correctly isolated, or do other sibling units also qualify?**

The routing decision notes that all other Portuguese sibling units (PT11, PT15, PT16, PT17, PT18, PTBJ, PTEV, PTFA, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG) were resolved using the 1986 NUTS-default rule, with PTFA matched to the national polity and the rest marked unroutable before 1989. It is not yet confirmed whether any of those siblings' official reporting names also coincide with another named departure entry in the Portugal country convention (the way Aveiro's does), which would mean this entry is not actually a one-off exception but the first of several district-level entries still to be created.
