# Asturias (province of Spain)

## Summary

Asturias is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and since 1981 also coextensive with the Principality of Asturias autonomous community on Spain's northern Cantabrian coast -- one of the few Spanish provinces (with Cantabria, Murcia, La Rioja, Madrid, Navarre, and the Balearic and Canary archipelagos) where the 1833 province boundary and the post-Franco autonomous community boundary are identical, since Asturias was never split among neighbouring communities the way Galicia's four provinces or Castile and Leon's nine were. This entry is type: subnational because Asturias is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Asturias separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES120, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES120) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES120 ("Asturias"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (A Coruna/ES111, Madrid/ES-MD, and onward through ES243), all following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Asturias is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units (data through 2021 confirms it is not extinct). Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states explicitly that "no existing polity represents Asturias specifically," so unlike A Coruna (which found a pre-existing but undocumented row) this unit had no candidate at all before this decision; the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for A Coruna and Madrid. What confirms distinctness: Asturias's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES120) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 47x the area of Asturias.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE province numeric code), under which Asturias is conventionally cpro 33 -- the routing decision even anticipates the resulting feature_id as "ESP-33-1833-2025" -- but the polygon_reasoning states plainly that mapspain-ign, while registered with the correct schema and id_column, "is not present_locally": the provinces.gpkg file has not been downloaded or placed on disk, so the route cannot be executed until that data is fetched, exactly as recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 for the same unmet precondition. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP entirely (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, and even where a community and province coincide, as they do for Asturias, adm1 does not carry province-level attribution), so it was correctly ruled out rather than substituted. Territory description: Asturias occupies a strip of Spain's northern Cantabrian coast, between Galicia to the west, Castile and Leon to the south, and Cantabria to the east, fronting the Bay of Biscay. Its capital is Oviedo; its largest city and main port is Gijon, with Aviles as the third major urban/industrial centre of the historically coal-and-steel Asturian basin. On a modern map it is the same outline as the Principality of Asturias autonomous community, since (unlike Galicia's four provinces or Castile and Leon's nine) Asturias was never subdivided among multiple provinces after 1833. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 10,604 km2 -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025.

## Predecessors and successors

Asturias has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized as the historic Principado de Asturias under the Antiguo Regimen's own partido/concejo structure, distinct from the 1833 province in administrative form even though the name and rough boundary persisted, and this database does not model sub-kingdom Asturian divisions at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1981, also the Principality of Asturias autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Asturias as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES120) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES120 records that Asturias is one of the 1833 Javier de Burgos provinces and, unlike Galicia's four provinces which were split among that community, Asturias's 1833 province boundary and its post-1981 autonomous-community boundary (Principality of Asturias) are identical -- the same class of one-province-equals-one-community exception already noted for Madrid.
- The polygon_reasoning for this decision records that mapspain-ign's id_column is cpro (INE province numeric codes) and that Asturias's INE province code is 33, giving the anticipated feature_id ESP-33-1833-2025 once the provinces.gpkg source file is fetched onto disk -- it is currently registered but not present_locally.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-as-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation AS rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES120) or the INE province numeric code (33). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-AS-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so that the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. AS was chosen over alternatives like OV (from the historic Oviedo provincial name still used in some older statistical series) because AS is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-AS) and the everyday abbreviation for the region, reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and Asturias's cpro=33 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Asturias is conventionally cpro 33 -- but the polygon_reasoning for this decision states the source file is not present_locally: the provinces.gpkg has not been downloaded or placed on disk, so the feature cannot be confirmed as Asturias's actual 1833-2025 boundary. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=33 feature is confirmed, this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry, exactly as ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 do for the same unmet precondition. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Asturias, matching the modern Principality of Asturias autonomous-community outline, before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Principado de Asturias has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the same broad territory was organized as the historic Principado de Asturias, an Antiguo Regimen jurisdiction with its own concejo/partido structure that does not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province's administrative form even though the name and rough boundary persisted across the transition. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. Whether the pre-1833 Principado should eventually be modeled as its own polity (given it has a genuine historical name and identity, unlike a bare province number) is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry -- and it is a live question here specifically because Asturias, unlike A Coruna or Madrid, carries a well-documented pre-1833 name of its own that a future author might mistake for grounds to add a predecessor row without checking whether the harness's bespoke-code precedent (used for ALK, HYD, RYU) should apply instead.
