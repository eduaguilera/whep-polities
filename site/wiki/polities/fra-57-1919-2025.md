# Moselle

## Summary

Moselle is a French département in Lorraine (INSEE code 57, prefecture Metz), in its present French administrative form dating from 1919. It is one of the three départements the FRA country convention explicitly names as a departure from the default 1790 start rule (with Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin): all three were annexed by the German Empire under the 1871 Treaty of Frankfurt and did not return to France until the Treaty of Versailles took effect in 1919. `type: subnational` is used because a département is an administrative subdivision, never a sovereign polity; sovereignty over this territory belongs throughout to the FRA national chain, which is why the entry is containered against FRA-1919-2025 alone rather than against the earlier FRA-1871-1919 row (the territory was outside France, under German administration, for that whole span). The entry exists to receive department-scale historical statistics reported under the modern NUTS code FRF33 that would otherwise have to be forced onto the whole-country FRA-1919-2025 row, overstating the reporting territory by roughly two orders of magnitude.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: a historical statistical series (per the routing decision) reporting values for the administrative unit labelled FRF33 / "Moselle", spanning 1862-2023; no row count is given in the routing decision payload itself, and 97.6%-scale observed/back-cast splits are not stated for this specific unit (unlike the FRA-54 sibling, whose decision quoted that figure). Previously this data had no dedicated polity: the routing decision found zero existing name candidates for Moselle in this table, so absent this entry the rows would have to be forced onto one of the whole-country FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), each of which covers all of France and would misattribute a single département's output to the entire country. What confirms the entity is distinct: Moselle is one of France's 96 metropolitan départements, independently enumerated in French national statistics (INSEE) since its creation, and its own administrative history is discontinuous from the rest of France for 1871-1918 -- a fact recorded in the FRA country convention's own list of explicit departures from the default start rule, which is external corroboration (beyond this pipeline) that this territory's French administrative identity has a genuine 1919 origin, distinct from a mere data-routing convenience.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. **Proxy deliberately not copied from a department-shaped source because none exists in this repository** -- the whole-country France polygon (cshapes-2.0, feature FRA-1919-2025) is attached as a stand-in, exactly as done for the FRA-54 (Meurthe-et-Moselle) sibling, because no registered source carries French département-level boundaries locally (gadm-4.1-adm1 excludes FRA entirely; gadm-3.6, which would carry French departments at adm2 level, is not present on disk). Recorded as `polygon_status: proxy` rather than `assigned` because the attached geometry is not Moselle's boundary at any scale.

Territory description: Moselle sits in the Lorraine part of modern Grand Est, on France's northeastern border. On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE department 57: prefecture Metz, bordered by Luxembourg and Germany (Saarland/Rhineland-Palatinate) to the north and east, Meurthe-et-Moselle to the south and west, and a short frontier with Bas-Rhin. Its actual area is approximately 6,216 km2 (INSEE). The polygon attached to this entry instead measures the whole of metropolitan France: 551,695 km2 as computed from the attached cshapes-2.0 geometry in an equal-area projection -- this is a measurement of the substitute polygon, not evidence about Moselle's own size, and must not be read as the department's territory.

## Predecessors and successors

Moselle has no predecessor row and no successor row in this table. A French département also named Moselle existed before 1871 with a different boundary (it did not exclude the territory later annexed by Germany, and its relationship to the modern department's gains from old Meurthe is unresolved -- see open questions), but that pre-1871 unit is not itself represented as a row here, so no predecessor code exists to cite. The department has continued under this name and essentially this extent from 1919 to the present, so it has no successor either; it remains an active French département today, part of the Grand Est administrative region since 2016 (a change above the department that does not alter its own boundary or code).

## Sourced claims

- The Treaty of Frankfurt (1871) ceded most of the historic Moselle département, along with Alsace, to the newly formed German Empire, where it was administered as part of Alsace-Lorraine (Elsass-Lothringen) until 1918.
- The Treaty of Versailles (1919) returned Alsace-Lorraine to France, at which point the modern French département of Moselle (INSEE code 57, prefecture Metz) was reconstituted in its present form.
- INSEE assigns Moselle department code 57 and gives its area as approximately 6,216 km2, with prefecture Metz.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1919-not-1790

**Start year set to 1919, the Versailles restitution, not 1790**

The routing decision's own span_basis already argues for 1919 rather than the country's default 1790 start rule, and it is right to do so: modern Moselle is one of the three départements the FRA country convention explicitly lists as a departure from the default (alongside Bas-Rhin and Haut-Rhin), because all three were ceded to the German Empire in 1871 and did not return to France until the Treaty of Versailles took effect in 1919. There is no continuous French administration of this territory across 1871-1918 to anchor a 1790 start on, so 1919 is used both as start_year and as the year cited in the polity code. This also matches where the FRA container chain itself splits from FRA-1871-1919 to FRA-1919-2025, so the span tiles cleanly against the existing container row with no gap.

### d-code-uses-insee-number-57

**Polity code uses INSEE department number 57, following the FRA-54 sibling precedent**

FRA-54-1871-2025 (Meurthe-et-Moselle) established, as the first FRA subnational entry, that French département rows use the INSEE department number rather than an invented letter abbreviation, specifically to avoid collisions between similarly named neighbours (Moselle vs Meurthe-et-Moselle vs Meuse). Moselle's INSEE number is 57, giving FRA-57-1919-2025. This departs from the routing decision's own generic label 'Moselle' with no numeric suffix, but follows the sibling precedent this repository has already set rather than introducing a second naming scheme for the same country.

### d-pre1919-rows-not-back-cast-to-this-entry

**Pre-1919 rows (1862-1918) are not folded into this entry's span**

The routing decision proposes treating 1862-1918 data reported under the modern NUTS code FRF33 as a back_cast onto this new polity, reasoning that the source is reporting a value for the same territory before its administrative identity in French hands existed. This page does not extend start_year to cover that back-cast rationale, because the decision's own routing_concerns flag that 1862-1918 is not one uniform prior reality: 1862-1870 sat under a French département also called Moselle but with a different boundary (it still included territory lost to Germany in 1871, and its relationship to the modern department's gains from old Meurthe is unclear), while 1871-1918 sat under German administration with no French departmental identity at all. Rather than pick one territorial referent for a back_cast label, this entry's own span is kept to the years modern French Moselle actually existed (1919-2025), leaving the back-cast routing question to the pipeline's aggregation layer instead of encoding it silently into this page's boundaries.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-status-downgraded

**polygon_status was `proxy` with no geometry attached**

the previous binding was cshapes-2.0 / FRA-1919-2025, which is the outline of FRANCE. A département cannot have its country's boundary as its own geometry — every sibling could claim it equally — so the binding is dropped rather than restated as a proxy.


### oq-pre1919-back-cast-territorial-referent

**What territory do the back-cast 1862-1918 rows actually describe?**

The routing decision proposes routing 1862-1918 rows (reported under NUTS code FRF33, i.e. the modern Moselle footprint) to this polity as a back_cast. But those years span two different prior realities with two different boundaries: 1862-1870, under an old French département also named Moselle whose boundary is not the modern one (it still included territory later lost to Germany in 1871, and it is unclear whether it already included territory the modern department gained from old Meurthe); and 1871-1918, under German administration as part of Alsace-Lorraine, with no French departmental identity at all. Whether the Mediterranean Subnational source is reporting these years by re-projecting onto the modern NUTS footprint (a dasymetric back-cast) or by citing the historical French département's own contemporary reported figures has not been checked against that source's methodology notes. This matters for whether the 1862-1870 segment belongs here, on an old-Moselle predecessor row that does not currently exist, or should be dropped as untraceable to a single consistent boundary.

### oq-department-polygon-still-missing

**No French department-level polygon source exists yet; this entry inherits the same whole-country proxy as FRA-54**

As with FRA-54-1871-2025, no registered source in this repository carries French département boundaries (gadm-4.1-adm1 excludes FRA; gadm-3.6, which would carry them at adm2 level, is not present on disk). The polygon attached here (cshapes-2.0, feature FRA-1919-2025) measures the whole of metropolitan France, roughly 89x Moselle's actual ~6,216 km2. Any spatial join or area-based check against this row will silently treat Moselle as the entire country until a French department-level source (GADM full/global ADM2 or IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS) is vetted and registered. This is now the second FRA subnational entry with this exact gap, reinforcing that it is a repository-wide gap rather than a one-off.

### oq-moselle-1919-boundary-vs-old-lothringen

**Does the 1919 restitution boundary of Moselle exactly match the pre-1871 French Moselle, or was it redrawn?**

This page assumes the department restored to France in 1919 as 'Moselle' is essentially the same territorial unit continuously since, aside from the 1871-1918 interruption. It has not been verified whether the 1919 boundary was an exact restoration of the pre-1871 French Moselle département's own boundary, or whether the post-Versailles French administration redrew the department line differently from the old Moselle/Meurthe split (e.g. some communes assigned to Meurthe-et-Moselle instead of Moselle, or vice versa). If the boundaries differ, the whole-France proxy is equally uninformative either way, but the historical narrative in this page's summary ('reconstituted in its present, French form') would need qualification.
