# Zacatecas

## Summary

Zacatecas is one of the states of the United Mexican States, located on the north-central plateau (Altiplano), historically built around the silver-mining economy of the city of Zacatecas (founded 1546) and, since the colonial era, a major producer of silver, and in the modern period also of agricultural goods on its plateau grasslands. This entry represents Zacatecas as a subnational administrative unit (`type: subnational`) rather than a sovereign polity, mirroring the pattern used for other federal states such as Spain's autonomous communities (ESP-AS, ESP-CA): it must not compete with the national MEX chain for sovereignty-based matching, but it exists because statistical and administrative sources report data at the state level distinct from national Mexican totals. Zacatecas was one of the original states admitted to the Mexican federation under the 1824 Constitution — it was not one of the three federal territories (Baja California, Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo) that were converted to statehood much later — so its start_year follows the country's default founding-state rule of 1824 rather than a later territory-to-state conversion date. No existing row in the polities database represents Zacatecas at admin-1 level; MEX-1800-1848 and MEX-1848-2025 are national aggregates that do not resolve to the state.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** this entry is created to host state-level statistics for Zacatecas, Mexico — the routing pipeline flagged a unit labeled `MEX-ZACATECAS` (admin_name "Zacatecas", country Mexico) with no year range or row count of its own supplied in the decision payload (the payload describes only the routing/container/polygon logic, not the underlying data rows); the entry's span is 1824 (Zacatecas's admission to the Mexican federation under the 1824 Constitution) to 2025 (open, current state).

**Previous matching / why it was wrong:** prior to this entry, any Zacatecas-labeled data would have had nowhere to route except the national aggregates MEX-1800-1848 or MEX-1848-2025, both of which represent the whole of Mexico rather than this one state — a mismatch of roughly 25-30x in territory (Mexico's ~2 million km² national extent vs. Zacatecas's ~75,000 km²), the same category of error the exemplar MAN-1950-1955 page documents for Manchuria routed to all-China totals.

**Confirmation the entity is distinct:** Zacatecas is one of the 19 original states enumerated in Mexico's 1824 federal constitution (Acta Constitutiva de la Federación / Constitución de 1824), with continuous administrative existence as a first-order political division of Mexico since then — it is not a territory that was later upgraded to statehood (unlike Baja California, Baja California Sur, and Quintana Roo, which the country's own routing convention lists as later conversions), and it has published its own state-level statistical apparatus (state congress, state government, and today INEGI state-level series) for the entire span, distinguishing it clearly from a purely notional subdivision of the national total.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is currently available in the GeoPackage for this entry. The natural boundary source is GADM's ADM1 layer (`gadm-4.1-adm1`, registered in scripts/sources.yaml with id_column GID_1), which does include Mexican states globally, but the local gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg file checked into this repository's shared geodata is an 81-country subset that excludes Mexico entirely (0 features). This is the registered_source_unfetched case: the source is registered and known to cover this territory, but the Mexico-inclusive file has not yet been fetched, so `polygon_source` is set to the slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` and `polygon_status` is `unassigned` rather than `assigned` or `proxy`, with `polygon_feature_id` left null pending that fetch (expected GID_1 = MEX.32_1 once available).

**Territory description (for a reader locating it today):** Zacatecas state sits on Mexico's north-central plateau, bordered by Durango and Aguascalientes to the west, Jalisco and Nayarit to the southwest, Guanajuato and San Luis Potosí to the east/southeast, and Coahuila to the north. Its capital, Zacatecas City, lies roughly 600 km northwest of Mexico City. The state covers approximately **75,000 km²** in its modern configuration (Mexico's national statistics agency INEGI states 75,416 km²), making it one of Mexico's larger, sparsely populated interior states. No polygon area is attached to this entry yet, so no measured km² figure is reported here — the 75,000 km² figure above is the modern state's own published area, not a measurement of any geometry in this database.

**Boundary caveat:** as with the GADM-sourced Mexican states generally, the ADM1 boundary that will eventually be attached reflects the present-day (c. 2020s) administrative boundary. Zacatecas's borders have shifted at least once in a way that matters for the earlier portion of this entry's span: modern Aguascalientes was originally Zacatecas territory before its 1835 separation (see open question below), so using a present-day boundary for the full 1824-2025 span is a standing approximation, more pronounced before 1835 than after.

## Predecessors and successors

Zacatecas has no predecessor or successor polity rows: it was admitted directly as a founding state of the Mexican federation under the 1824 Constitution (not converted from an earlier federal territory, unlike Baja California, Baja California Sur, or Quintana Roo, which each have their own territory-to-state transition), and it remains an active, current Mexican state with no dissolution or renaming in view, so end_year is the open default 2025 and successor is empty.

## Sourced claims

- Mexico's 1824 Federal Constitution (Constitución Federal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos, 4 October 1824) enumerated Zacatecas among the original constituent states of the federation, distinct from the later federal territories.
- INEGI (Mexico's national statistics institute) publishes the state of Zacatecas's official land area as 75,416 km², used here as the modern reference figure for locating the territory rather than as a measurement of any attached polygon.
- Aguascalientes was originally part of Zacatecas territory and was separated as its own political entity in 1835 (comarca status), later becoming a full state in 1857 — documented in standard Mexican state-formation histories and relevant to the boundary-vintage caveat on this entry's pre-1835 years.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

57 of 66 subnational rows in the polity table follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025). Zacatecas is an ordinary Mexican federal state with no independent historical identity outside the Mexican federation (unlike ALK or HYD, which were sovereign or quasi-sovereign entities before absorption), so the bespoke pattern does not apply. I chose the subunit token ZAC, the standard postal/ISO 3166-2:MX code for the state (MX-ZAC), giving polity_code MEX-ZAC-1824-2025. This keeps the code legible against Mexico's other future state entries and matches how ESP subnational codes use short subunit abbreviations rather than full names.

### d-container-two-edges

**Split the container into two edges to tile the full 1824-2025 span**

The routing decision only proposed MEX-1848-2025 as container, which would leave 1824-1848 uncontained. The polity table lists MEX-1800-1848 as an earlier national row covering pre-Mexican-American-War Mexico. Zacatecas existed as a state within that earlier national territory from its 1824 admission, so I added a first container edge MEX-1800-1848 for 1824-1848, and kept MEX-1848-2025 for 1848-2025, together tiling the full span with no gap, per the containment gate's requirement that every year be covered by exactly the container that held sovereignty then.

### d-polygon-unassigned-unfetched

**polygon_status set to unassigned, not proxy or assigned**

The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_unfetched: GADM's ADM1 layer is a registered source (scripts/sources.yaml, id_column GID_1) that does carry Mexico state boundaries including Zacatecas (GID_1 pattern MEX.32_1), but the local gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg on disk is a 81-country subset that excludes Mexico. Per the task instructions, when the route is registered_source_unfetched the slug itself (gadm-4.1-adm1) is the polygon_source and polygon_status must be unassigned, with polygon_feature_id left null until the Mexico-inclusive GADM file is fetched and the GID_1 feature is looked up.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-mexico-fetch

**Mexico ADM1 boundaries are not yet present in the local GADM file**

The registered source gadm-4.1-adm1 is a global layer that does include Mexican states, but the copy checked into this repository's geodata is a subset covering only 81 countries and Mexico is not among them (0 features for MEX). Until a Mexico-inclusive GADM 4.1 ADM1 file (or a Mexico-specific GADM extract) is fetched and added to the shared geodata symlink, polygon_feature_id cannot be assigned and polygon_status must remain unassigned. The expected feature_id once fetched is GID_1 = MEX.32_1 (Zacatecas), based on GADM's standard state ordering, but this has not been verified against an actual downloaded file.

### oq-1824-admission-date

**Whether 1824 is the correct admission year rather than a slightly later date**

The routing_concerns field in the decision itself flags this: Zacatecas is assumed to be one of the original states admitted under the 1824 Federal Constitution because it is not listed among the country convention's departures (Baja California, Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo, which were later territory-to-state conversions). However, this was inferred by absence from the exclusion list rather than confirmed against a primary source enumerating the 19 original 1824 states. If Zacatecas's admission was in fact later, start_year may need revision, and the polity_code and container start_year would both need to change to match.

### oq-aguascalientes-boundary-history

**Aguascalientes was carved out of Zacatecas territory in 1835 and this page does not model that split**

Modern Aguascalientes state was originally part of Zacatecas and was separated as its own territory in 1835 (later a state in 1857). This entry uses present-day GADM boundaries for Zacatecas for its entire 1824-2025 span (per the vintage_risk note in the routing decision), which means the pre-1835 portion of this entry's span (1824-1835) is measured against a smaller territory than Zacatecas actually held at the time. No separate Aguascalientes polity row is known to exist yet in the table; whether one should be created for the pre-1835 period, and whether Zacatecas's early years should instead be flagged as a distinct boundary-vintage case, is unresolved here.
