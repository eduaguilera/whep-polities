# Cuenca (province of Spain)

## Summary

Cuenca is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, in the historic region of La Mancha/New Castile and, since 1982, part of the Castilla-La Mancha autonomous community (alongside Albacete, Ciudad Real, Guadalajara and Toledo) -- one of the community's five constituent provinces, none of which is coextensive with the community itself, unlike the one-province-equals-one-community cases already recorded for Asturias, Cantabria or Madrid. This entry is type: subnational because Cuenca is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Cuenca separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES423, since the 1833 reform.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES423) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES423 ("Cuenca"), country Spain, under the juan-subnational source, covering years 1865-2021 per the routing_reasoning, following the same country convention already applied to sibling Castilla-La Mancha units ES112-ES241 in this batch (all create_new) and the default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform; Cuenca is not one of the Canary Islands departure cases so no exception applies) with an open end_year of 2025 for a still-current unit. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states there are "0 name candidates" and that the only existing ESP siblings before this decision were A Coruna, Madrid and the national total ESP-1800-2025, none of which is Cuenca; the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one covering roughly 30x Cuenca's own area and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for every other ESP province row in this batch. What confirms distinctness: Cuenca's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES423) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the other ESP-<SUBUNIT>-1833-2025 rows -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from both the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate and from its Castilla-La Mancha siblings (Albacete, Ciudad Real, Guadalajara, Toledo), which report as distinct NUTS3 units of their own.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE province numeric code), under which Cuenca is conventionally cpro 16, but the polygon_reasoning states plainly that mapspain-ign "should be verified against the actual file at data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg" -- sources.yaml lists it as present_locally=False even though 7 other ESP province rows (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra) already carry mapspain-ign as their registered polygon_source, so either the shapefile has since been fetched for those and this entry simply has not been reconciled against it yet, or all 8 rows share the same unmet precondition. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP entirely (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, so Castilla-La Mancha's five constituent provinces, including Cuenca, are not separately attributed there), so it was correctly ruled out rather than substituted. Territory description: Cuenca is Spain's third-largest province by area, occupying the eastern portion of Castilla-La Mancha on the plateau east of Madrid, bordered by Guadalajara and Teruel to the north, Valencia to the east, Albacete to the south, and Toledo and Ciudad Real to the west. Its capital, also called Cuenca, sits at the confluence of the Jucar and Huecar rivers and is a UNESCO World Heritage old town known for its hanging houses; the province also contains the Serrania de Cuenca uplands and the Alcarria conquense to the north. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 17,141 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is confirmed present, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the other ESP province rows.

## Predecessors and successors

Cuenca has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers fell within the historic Kingdom of Toledo/La Mancha jurisdiction of the Antiguo Regimen, organized by partido and corregimiento rather than by the 1833 province boundary, and this database does not model sub-kingdom Castilian divisions at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025 and the other ESP province rows. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1982, part of the Castilla-La Mancha autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES423 records 0 name candidates for Cuenca before this decision and identifies it as matching the pattern of the other Castilla-La Mancha/Spain sibling units in the batch (ES112-ES241), all of which were create_new.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-cu-as-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation CU rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and the other ESP-<SUBUNIT>-1833-2025 rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES423) or the INE province numeric code (16). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-CU-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so that the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. CU is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-CU) and the everyday abbreviation for the province, reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source's local presence is unconfirmed for Cuenca despite 7 other ESP rows already citing it**

sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, under which Cuenca is conventionally cpro 16, and the polygon_reasoning for this decision explicitly flags that the route 'should be verified against the actual file at data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg before running the harness on Cuenca' because sources.yaml shows present_locally=False. But 7 other ESP province rows (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra) already carry polygon_source: mapspain-ign in the database, which means either the shapefile was fetched at some point after those rows were created and this entry just has not been reconciled against it, or all 8 rows share the same unmet precondition and none has an actual geometry yet despite the source field being populated. Whoever next touches the mapspain-ign source should check whether data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg exists on disk, and if so assign cpro=16 to this entry (and audit the other 7 for the same gap) rather than leaving polygon_status: unassigned indefinitely.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Antiguo Regimen jurisdiction covering this territory has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory now forming Cuenca province fell within the Kingdom of Toledo/La Mancha's partido and corregimiento structure, which does not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province boundary even though much of the name and rough territory persisted. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and the other ESP province rows. Whether any pre-1833 Castilian jurisdiction should eventually be modeled as its own polity is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
