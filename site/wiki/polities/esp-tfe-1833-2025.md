# Tenerife (island of Spain)

## Summary

Tenerife is the largest of the Canary Islands, about 2,034 km² (INE's published island area). It is an island, not a province. From the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform to 1927 it belonged to the single Canary Islands province. Since the decree of 23 September 1927 it has belonged to the province of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, which it shares with La Palma, La Gomera and El Hierro. `type: subnational`, because Tenerife has always been part of Spain.

**Why this entry exists.** The entry gives the official NUTS code `ES709` a polity that means what the code means. In the Eurostat NUTS 2013 and 2016 classifications, ES70 Canarias is divided by island: ES703 El Hierro, ES704 Fuerteventura, ES705 Gran Canaria, ES706 La Gomera, ES707 La Palma, ES708 Lanzarote and ES709 Tenerife. The alias registry was inconsistent on this. In the `eurostat-nuts2013`, `eurostat-nuts2016` and `jrc-nuts2016` code systems, ES705 resolved to the island polity [ESP-GC-1833-2025](esp-gc-1833-2025.md) and ES531 to the island polity [ESP-IBZ-1833-2025](esp-ibz-1833-2025.md). ES709 instead resolved to the whole province [ESP-38-1927-2025](esp-38-1927-2025.md), and to a 1865-1926 back_cast onto it. So a figure that Eurostat coded to Tenerife landed on a territory about 1.7 times larger. The province is 3,381 km²; the other three islands make up the remaining ~1,350 km². Those rows of the official code systems now resolve here, 1865-2020, the same span the registry gives ES705. No other polity represented the island. The province is a strict superset of it, and Spain is a further superset.

**Which code system means the province.** The repository's own subnational panel uses ES705 and ES709 differently: there they are the capital-island codes standing for the two provinces. The #735 audit measured exactly 50 Spanish units per indicator family, and ES705/ES709 replace the province codes ES701/ES702 only in the livestock family. The panel's own slugs therefore keep routing ES709 to the province: `juan-subnational` and `whep-lab-spain-provinces`, unchanged. This page and its aliases cover only the official code systems, where data genuinely coded to ES709 is Tenerife. That is the same slug scoping commit d4f0c20 introduced for ES531 and the 2026-09-24 repoint applied to ES705.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. `polygon_status: unassigned`, `polygon_source: none`. None of this repository's registered polygon sources has a Tenerife feature. `mapspain-ign` serves provinces, and its feature 38 is the four-island province. `eurostat-gisco-nuts2` is registered at NUTS level 2 only, where ES70 is the whole archipelago. GISCO's NUTS level-3 layer carries ES709 Tenerife, so registering that layer is the natural source. Until then, no proxy is attached. The nearest candidate polygon is the province, and it would overstate the island by about 66% (3,381 against 2,034 km²). Adopting it would claim the other three islands' territory for this row. No area is measured on this page, because no geometry is attached. The 2,034 km² is a published figure.

**Territory description (locatable on a modern map):** Tenerife is the central island of the Canary archipelago, in the Atlantic about 300 km west of the Moroccan coast, between Gran Canaria to the east and La Gomera to the west. Its capital is Santa Cruz de Tenerife, and its summit is Teide (3,715 m). The island's extent has not changed over the span. Only the province above it has: the single Canary Islands province before 1927, Santa Cruz de Tenerife since.

## Predecessors and successors

None recorded. The island is one continuous territory across 1833-2025. The 1927 split changed the province above it, which the container edges record, and not the island itself.

## Sourced claims

- The 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial division made the Canary Islands a single province.
- The decree of 23 September 1927 split it into the provinces of Las Palmas (Gran Canaria, Lanzarote, Fuerteventura) and Santa Cruz de Tenerife (Tenerife, La Palma, La Gomera, El Hierro).
- In the Eurostat NUTS 2013 and 2016 classifications, ES709 is Tenerife. `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv` cites the GISCO attribute tables (`NUTS_AT_2013.csv`, `NUTS_AT_2016.csv`) for that name, and `data/final/nuts_code_names.csv` records it.

## Decisions

### d-official-code-system-only

**Only the official NUTS code systems route ES709 here.**

`eurostat-nuts2013`, `eurostat-nuts2016` and `jrc-nuts2016` resolve ES709 to this row for 1865-2020. Their earlier rows sent it to ESP-38-1927-2025 for 1927-2020, plus 1865 and 1880-1926 as a back_cast, and were removed. `juan-subnational` and `whep-lab-spain-provinces`, the panel's own slugs, still route ES709 to the province, because in that panel the code stands for the province (#735). No in-repository data moves. None of the three official code systems has a corpus in this repository, so the alias map leaves `observed_rows` empty for them, and the layer-B panel is national. The change is in what the published `label_alias_map.csv` tells a consumer holding Eurostat-coded data.

### d-subunit-code-tfe

**Subunit `TFE`, not `TF`.**

An island has no ISO 3166-2 code, so the subunit is a documented abbreviation of at most four characters (`scripts/validate_subunit_codes.py`). `TF` is ruled out: ISO 3166-2 `ES-TF` is the province of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, the territory this row is distinguished from. `TFE` is the common abbreviation for the island and collides with no `ESP-*` code.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-gisco-nuts3

**Register GISCO NUTS level 3 to give the island a boundary.**

Registering GISCO NUTS level 3 would give the island its ES709 polygon. It would serve ESP-GC-1833-2025 (ES705) and ESP-IBZ-1833-2025 (ES531) the same way. All three island rows are `unassigned` today for the same reason. This is polygon work, outside the alias fix that created the row.
