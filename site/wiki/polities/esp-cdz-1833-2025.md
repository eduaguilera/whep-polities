# Cádiz (province of Spain)

## Summary

Cádiz is one of the 50 provinces of Spain established in 1833 by the Javier de Burgos territorial reform, which redrew the country's historical kingdoms and regions into a uniform grid of provinces for administrative and statistical purposes. This entry is `type: subnational` because Cádiz never held sovereignty of its own — it is, and always has been, a first-level administrative subdivision of the Spanish state, created by and reporting through Spain's national government from 1833 to the present. The province occupies the southwestern tip of the Iberian Peninsula on the Atlantic coast of Andalusia, bordering the provinces of Málaga, Sevilla, and Huelva, the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar, and facing Morocco across the Strait of Gibraltar. Its capital is the city of Cádiz, and its largest cities include Jerez de la Frontera, Algeciras, and San Fernando. No prior WHEP entry represented this unit: before this pipeline ran, only the national ESP polity and two other ESP provinces (A Coruña, Madrid) existed, and Cádiz-labelled statistical rows had no dedicated home. The province has existed under this name and with essentially this boundary since 1833, making 1833-2025 (open) the correct span, matching the pattern used for all other Spanish provinces in this database.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures 20+ statistical rows (per the routing decision for unit_id ES612, NUTS3 code for Cádiz, country Spain) that had no dedicated polity to route to. Before this pipeline ran, the only existing ESP candidates were A Coruña, Madrid, and the national Spain polity (ESP-1800-2025), none of which IS Cádiz — those rows were previously either left unmatched or misrouted to the national aggregate, which is roughly 68x Cádiz's own area and would badly overstate the geographic precision of any Cádiz-labelled figure. Cádiz is confirmed as a distinct, statistically-reported entity by its continuous status as one of Spain's 50 provinces since the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, and by INE's ongoing publication of provincial-level statistics under cpro code 11 / NUTS3 ES612, the same identifier basis used by the other 49 Spanish province rows already in this database. Following the identical pattern already applied to sibling NUTS units (ES112, ES113, etc.), a new polity was created spanning 1833-2025.

It has no successor: the province remains a current first-level administrative division of Spain as of 2025, with end_year 2025 reflecting an open-ended, still-active unit rather than a dissolution. ## Sourced claims

- The 1833 Javier de Burgos royal decree (subdivision of the Spanish territory into 49 provinces, later 50 with the Canary Islands split) created Cádiz province with boundaries drawn from the former Kingdom of Seville's coastal districts.
- The Spanish National Statistics Institute (INE) still lists Cádiz under provincial code 11, the same code used in NUTS3 classification as ES612, confirming administrative continuity of this exact unit from creation to the present.

## Decisions

### d-code-choice-cdz

**Chose ESP-CDZ over the two-letter ESP-CA pattern because ESP-CA is already taken**

The dominant sibling pattern for Spanish provinces is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using the INE/postal two-letter province abbreviation (e.g. ESP-A for A Coruña, ESP-AB for Albacete). Cádiz's conventional two-letter abbreviation is CA, but ESP-CA-1833-2025 already exists as the page for Cantabria (province of Spain), a different unit (ES612 is Cádiz, not Cantabria). A prior answer for this same routing decision used ESP-CA and was rejected for exactly this collision. Rather than inventing a bespoke non-conforming code, this entry keeps the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern but uses CDZ, the three-letter abbreviation commonly used to disambiguate Cádiz from Cantabria, which avoids the existing collision while remaining recognizable and consistent with the 50-province ESP subnational family.
