# Meuse

## Summary

Meuse is a French département in the Grand Est region of northeastern France (INSEE code 55, prefecture Bar-le-Duc), one of the original ~83 départements created by the March 1790 Revolutionary administrative reform. This entry is `type: subnational` because a département is an administrative subdivision of France, never a sovereign polity: sovereignty over this territory belongs throughout to the FRA national chain, which is why the entry is containered against three successive FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) rather than standing alone. Meuse is distinguished from its eastern Lorraine neighbours by what did NOT happen to it in 1871: the Treaty of Frankfurt ceded the Alsace départements and the eastern arrondissements of Moselle to the new German Empire, producing the Meurthe/Meurthe-et-Moselle split recorded on FRA-54-1871-2025, but Meuse itself was untouched by that cession and stayed inside French national territory across 1871-1919 and beyond. The entry exists to receive département-level historical statistics for the administrative unit labelled FRF32/\"Meuse\" that are too fine-grained to route to any of the whole-country FRA rows, each of which would overstate the reporting territory by roughly two orders of magnitude.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision, a historical statistical series (juan-subnational or a comparable compilation) reporting values for the administrative unit labelled FRF32/\"Meuse\", drawn from Eurostat GISCO's NUTS 2021 naming (data/final/nuts_code_names.csv records FRF32 = Meuse). Previously this data had no dedicated polity: the routing decision found zero existing candidates matching this specific département by name -- the FRA rows in the table (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) are all national totals for the whole of France, and Meuse would only ever be contained by them, never equal to any, so routing its rows there would conflate one département's output with that of the entire country. What confirms the entity is distinct: Meuse is one of France's metropolitan départements, independently administered and independently enumerated in French national statistics (INSEE) since its 1790 creation, and Eurostat's own NUTS classification assigns it the distinct code FRF32 separate from every neighbouring département -- it is not a subdivision that shares reporting with Ardennes, Marne, Haute-Marne, Vosges, or Meurthe-et-Moselle.

## Territorial extent

On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE department 55, bordered by Ardennes to the northwest, Marne and Haute-Marne to the south/southwest, Vosges to the southeast, Meurthe-et-Moselle and Moselle to the east, and a short stretch of Belgium to the north. Its administrative seat (préfecture) is Bar-le-Duc, with Verdun (site of the 1916 battle) as its other major town. Its actual area is approximately 6,200 km2, consistent with the general pattern that 1790-era départements were drawn to a broadly similar size (comparable to neighbouring Haute-Marne at ~6,211 km2). The polygon attached to this entry instead measures the whole of France: **551,695 km2** as computed from the attached cshapes-2.0 geometry in an equal-area projection -- this figure is a measurement of the substitute polygon, not evidence about Meuse's own size, and should not be read as the département's territory.

## Predecessors and successors

Meuse has no predecessor row and no successor row in this table. It has been continuously constituted as a French département since its creation in March 1790 by the Revolutionary administrative reform, and it remains an active département of France today, part of the Grand Est region since 2016 (a regional-tier change above the department that does not alter Meuse's own boundary, number, or code). Unlike its eastern neighbours in the historic Trois-Évêchés/Lorraine area, Meuse was never ceded to Germany under the Treaty of Frankfurt in 1871 and so has no analogue to the Meurthe/Meurthe-et-Moselle split recorded on FRA-54-1871-2025 -- there is no earlier or later Meuse polity to link to, only the three successive FRA national container rows this entry sits inside as France's own national-level polity changes around it.

## Sourced claims

- Meuse was created in March 1790 as one of the original ~83 French départements established by the Revolutionary administrative reform, taking its name from the Meuse river which crosses it.
- Unlike Bas-Rhin, Haut-Rhin, and the eastern arrondissements of Moselle, Meuse was not part of the territory ceded to the German Empire under the 1871 Treaty of Frankfurt, so it remained inside French national territory continuously through the 1871-1919 period when Alsace-Lorraine was under German administration.
- INSEE assigns Meuse the official department code 55, with prefecture Bar-le-Duc; the department borders Ardennes, Marne, Haute-Marne, Vosges, Meurthe-et-Moselle, Moselle and (a short stretch of) Belgium.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Start year set to 1800, matching the earliest FRA container row, not 1790**

The routing decision this page implements proposed start_year 1790, citing Meuse's creation as one of the original Revolutionary départements. That founding date is correct as administrative history, but every existing FRA container row in this table begins no earlier than 1800 (FRA-1800-1871 is the earliest national row; there is no FRA-1790-1800 or equivalent). Setting start_year to 1790 would leave 1790-1800 uncontained by any row in the chain, which the containment gate rejects since it checks that a container edge falls inside both parties' spans. Every sibling French département page created so far (fra-08-1800-2025, fra-52-1800-2025, fra-mrn-1800-2025, and others) resolves the same tension the same way: they use 1800 as start_year, not 1790, precisely because the container chain's floor is 1800. This page follows that established precedent rather than reopening the question per-page. The 1790-1800 gap in the underlying administrative history (ten years of Meuse's existence with no French national polity row above it in this table) is recorded as an open question below rather than silently absorbed.

### d-polygon-code-department-number

**Polity code uses the INSEE department number (55), not a name-derived abbreviation**

The dominant subnational pattern for France is FRA-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. Following the precedent set by FRA-54-1871-2025 (Meurthe-et-Moselle) and repeated by every subsequent French département page in this table (fra-08, fra-52, fra-mrn, etc.), the subunit identifier used is the official INSEE department number rather than an invented two-or-three-letter abbreviation. Meuse is INSEE department 55, giving the code FRA-55-1800-2025. A name-derived abbreviation risks collision with neighbouring départements that share letters with Meuse (Meurthe, Meurthe-et-Moselle, Marne), which is exactly the collision risk the FRA-54 page's own decision flagged when it adopted numbering over abbreviation.

### d-polygon-proxy-whole-country

**Whole-country France polygon attached as a declared proxy, not a department-shaped source**

As with every other French département row in this table, no registered source in this repository (scripts/sources.yaml) carries French département-level boundaries: gadm-4.1-adm1 excludes France entirely from its curated subset, and gadm-3.6 (which would carry French ADM2 boundaries) is not present on disk. Rather than leave the polygon fields as bare `none`, this page follows the FRA-54-1871-2025 precedent of attaching the whole-country cshapes-2.0 FRA-1919-2025 feature as an explicitly declared, massively over-covering proxy, flagged `polygon_status: proxy`. This keeps the geometry field populated with a named, registered source (satisfying the requirement that polygon_source name an actual slug) while the body text makes unambiguous that the attached shape is not Meuse's boundary at any scale.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-status-downgraded

**polygon_status was `proxy` with no geometry attached**

the previous binding was cshapes-2.0 / FRA-1919-2025, which is the outline of FRANCE. A département cannot have its country's boundary as its own geometry — every sibling could claim it equally — so the binding is dropped rather than restated as a proxy.


### oq-1790-1800-gap-uncontained

**Meuse existed from 1790, but no container row covers 1790-1800**

Meuse was created in March 1790 as one of the original Revolutionary départements, ten years before this page's start_year of 1800. The FRA national chain in this table has no row earlier than FRA-1800-1871, so there is currently no way to container a Meuse entry for 1790-1800 without either creating a new pre-1800 FRA national row or accepting that decade as genuinely absent from the table. If a FRA-1790-1800 (or similarly named) national row is ever added, this page's start_year and predecessor field should be revisited. Until then, any data for Meuse dated 1790-1799 has nowhere correct to be routed.

### oq-pre1800-and-boundary-stability-unverified

**Boundary stability from 1790/1800 to 2025 has not been checked against a primary source**

The routing decision asserts Meuse's boundaries have not changed materially since 1790, citing only 'minor commune-level adjustments' as a caveat. This has not been independently verified here against INSEE's own administrative-history record or against a GISCO/IGN boundary-vintage comparison. If Meuse's boundary underwent any arrondissement-level reorganisation (as happened to several neighbouring Lorraine départements around 1871), a single span to 2025 with a single proxy polygon reference would be wrong in the same way flagged for other FRA département pages -- it just has not been surfaced because no department-shaped polygon exists to compare against in the first place.

### oq-department-polygon-never-built

**No department-level polygon source is registered for France; the whole-France proxy massively over-covers**

The attached polygon (cshapes-2.0, feature FRA-1919-2025) is the entire country of France, roughly 551,695 km2 in metropolitan extent, used only because no registered source in this repository carries French département-level boundaries. Meuse's actual extent is on the order of a few thousand km2 (comparable Grand Est départements such as Haute-Marne and Ardennes measure 5,200-6,200 km2), so the attached geometry over-covers by roughly two orders of magnitude. Any area-based check, choropleth, or spatial join against this row's geometry will silently represent Meuse as the whole of France until a real French département boundary source (e.g. IGN Admin Express or a locally-registered GADM ADM2 layer) is added to scripts/sources.yaml and this polygon is replaced. This is the same gap already flagged on FRA-54-1871-2025 and will recur on every French département page until fixed once, upstream.
