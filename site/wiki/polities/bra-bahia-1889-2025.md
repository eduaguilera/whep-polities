# Bahia

## Summary

Bahia is a Brazilian state on the Atlantic coast of the Nordeste (Northeast) region, one of the oldest and largest of Brazil's original captaincies, with Salvador — Brazil's first colonial capital — as its capital and largest city. This entry covers Bahia from 1889, when the Proclamation of the Republic converted the former Imperial province of Bahia into a state of the federation, to the present. `type: subnational` is used because Bahia has never been a sovereign polity in its own right: it was a captaincy and then a province under Portuguese and Imperial Brazilian rule, and has been a first-order administrative division (state) of the Brazilian federation continuously since 1889, with no territorial or sovereignty break of the kind that would justify a predecessor row (unlike BRA-ACRE, which passed through Bolivian sovereignty and federal-territory status before statehood). Bahia is not among the small set of listed BRA departures (Acre, Mato Grosso do Sul, Rondonia, Amapa, Roraima, Tocantins), so the country convention's default republic-founding start year of 1889 applies, with an open end_year of 2025 since Bahia remains a current state.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision this page implements (unit_id BRA-BAHIA) found no existing polity in the database representing Bahia itself: the only BRA rows are three national totals (BRA-1800-1903, BRA-1903-1909, BRA-1909-2025), none of which is state-scale. Source rows describing Bahia specifically — a state-level Brazilian subnational dataset spanning the full republican era, following the same pattern as the sibling BRA-ALAGOAS and BRA-AMAZONAS verdicts — had previously nowhere correct to route except the national BRA-1909-2025 row, a container many times Bahia's own territory, which would misattribute state-level agricultural/production data to the whole country. What confirms Bahia is a distinct reporting unit rather than folded into national Brazil figures is the same practice documented for the sibling entries: the source reports Bahia as a named first-order administrative unit (a Brazilian state), consistent with IBGE's own state-level statistical reporting since the founding of the Republic. This entry is one of a batch of Brazilian state-level polities created under the same default start-year rule, with no departure listed for Bahia in the country convention. The routing decision itself gives no exact row count or year range for the underlying source data (see open question below), only that it follows the established sibling-verdict pattern.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct source is `geobr-ibge` (Brazilian state boundaries, id_column `abbrev_state`), which is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` specifically to carry Brazilian ADM1 units but whose file (`data/geodata/geobr-ibge/states.gpkg`) is not present locally, per the routing decision's own polygon_reasoning. `gadm-4.1-adm1` excludes Brazil entirely from its locally curated subset, and the only BRA source currently present, `cshapes-2.0`, digitises Brazil at whole-country scale only. Once `geobr-ibge` is fetched, this entry should match `abbrev_state='BA'`.

**Territory description:** modern Bahia covers approximately 564,700 km2, the fifth-largest Brazilian state by area — larger than France — occupying much of the northern Nordeste's interior sertao as well as a long Atlantic coastline running roughly from the Recôncavo around Salvador in the north to the Rio de Contas basin in the south. It borders Sergipe, Alagoas, Pernambuco and Piaui to the north, Tocantins and Goias to the west, and Minas Gerais and Espirito Santo to the south. Its capital, Salvador, was Brazil's colonial capital until 1763. The state's western and southern interior boundaries were subject to more adjustment during the 19th and early 20th centuries than the more settled coastal states (see open question below), so the modern administrative boundary carries somewhat more vintage risk as a proxy across the full 1889-2025 span than it does for a smaller, more stable neighbour like Alagoas. No `polygon_area_km2` is recorded here since no geometry is yet attached.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row is created by this decision: Bahia's status as a first-order Brazilian administrative unit runs continuously back to 1889 with no interruption of the kind that separates BRA-ACRE from its federal-territory and Bolivian-sovereignty predecessors, so no separate pre-1889 provincial entry is proposed (the pre-1889 Imperial province is left to the national BRA-1800-1903 row, consistent with the default convention). This entry has no successor — Bahia remains a Brazilian state to the present, so the span runs to 2025 as still-current.

## Sourced claims

- The Proclamation of the Republic (15 November 1889) converted the Imperial provinces, including Bahia — one of Brazil's original captaincies/provinces dating to the earliest Portuguese colonial administration — into states of the new federation, the event that fixes this entry's 1889 start under the country convention's default rule.
- IBGE (Brazil's national statistics institute) reports agricultural and demographic data at the state level for Bahia continuously since the early Republic, the same reporting practice cited for sibling entries BRA-ALAGOAS and BRA-AMAZONAS as evidence the unit is a distinct, statistically tracked reporting territory rather than a component folded into national totals.

## Decisions

### d-code-iso3-subunit-start-end-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

Used BRA-BAHIA-1889-2025 rather than a bespoke code, matching the pattern already established for sibling Brazilian state entries (BRA-ALAGOAS, BRA-AMAZONAS, BRA-ACRE) created under the same batch of routing verdicts. Bahia is a first-order administrative division (a Brazilian state) with no historical identity as a polity distinct from that administrative role: it was an Imperial province before 1889 and has been a Republic state since, with the same continuity as Alagoas, not the discontinuous federal-territory-then-state history of Acre. That places it in the 57/66 dominant subnational pattern, not among the three bespoke historical-territory codes. SUBUNIT is the plain state name 'BAHIA' rather than an IBGE abbreviation, for the same reason given on the Alagoas and Acre entries: Brazilian states lack a universally recognized postal-style short code the way US states do, so the full name is self-documenting and consistent with sibling pages.

### d-container-three-edges-tile-full-span

**Three container edges are needed, not one, because the national row itself changes across 1889-2025**

The routing decision's proposed container_code is BRA-1909-2025 alone (and its span_basis text even cites 1889 as the start, an internal inconsistency in the decision object), but BRA-1909-2025 only begins in 1909 — leaving 1889-1909 uncontained if a single edge were used, which the containment gate would reject as falling outside the container's own span. The polity table shows three successive national BRA rows: BRA-1800-1903 (to 1903), BRA-1903-1909, and BRA-1909-2025. This entry's container list therefore has three edges, one per national era, together tiling the full 1889-2025 span with no gap: 1889-1903 under BRA-1800-1903, 1903-1909 under BRA-1903-1909, and 1909-2025 under BRA-1909-2025 (the routing decision's own proposed container, correctly used only for its own portion of the span). This mirrors the identical fix already made on BRA-ALAGOAS.

## Open questions

### oq-geobr-ibge-not-fetched

polygon_status is 'unassigned' because geobr-ibge is named in scripts/sources.yaml as the correct source for Brazilian state boundaries (id_column abbrev_state) but present_locally=False per the routing decision's polygon_detail — the file data/geodata/geobr-ibge/states.gpkg does not exist locally, so no feature_id can be matched yet. This is the same blocking task recorded on the sibling BRA-ALAGOAS and BRA-ACRE entries. Once fetched, this entry should match abbrev_state='BA' and polygon_status should move to 'assigned', with the resulting measured area cross-checked against IBGE's own published figure for Bahia (commonly cited near 564,700 km2, not independently verified against a specific IBGE table in this pass).

### oq-1889-vs-1903-provincial-boundary-continuity

**Was the province-to-state boundary literally unchanged in 1889, or is this an assumed continuity?**

This entry treats the 1889 Imperial province of Bahia and the 1889-onward Republican state of Bahia as territorially identical, the standard assumption for the provinces-to-states conversion but unchecked here against any 19th-century boundary source. Bahia's western and southern boundaries with Minas Gerais, Espirito Santo, Goias and Piaui were subject to municipal-level adjustments and disputed sertao frontier surveys well into the 20th century (unlike Alagoas's comparatively settled coastal boundary), so this continuity assumption carries somewhat more risk here than on the Alagoas sibling entry. If a future source documents a specific boundary change, the geobr-ibge proxy (a present-day boundary) would need the same vintage-risk caveat already flagged for BRA-ACRE's federal-territory predecessor.
