# South Australia (state of Australia)

## Summary

South Australia as a state of the Commonwealth of Australia, from Federation on 1 January 1901 to the present. Before Federation, the same territory was governed as the self-governing British colony of South Australia (proclaimed 1836, granted responsible government 1856), which is covered by a separate polity, AUSA-1836-1900. At Federation the six Australian colonies became states of a new federal Commonwealth, and South Australia's external status changed from colony to constituent state even though its internal administration and borders carried over essentially unchanged. `type: subnational` is used deliberately: this row is a first-order division of the Commonwealth (AUS-1901-2025), not a sovereign entity in its own right, and must not compete with the national AUS chain for the same years. The territory itself has been stable since 1901 apart from one boundary change entirely outside its own borders: the Northern Territory, administered by South Australia until 1911, was transferred to direct Commonwealth control that year and is captured separately (it does not shrink South Australia's own polity span here, since the Territory has its own historical entries).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures South Australia state-level statistics reported under the Commonwealth from 1901 onward (population, production, and trade series that a South Australia routing pipeline previously had nowhere to send except the national AUS total, or the pre-Federation colonial row AUSA-1836-1900 whose span ends in 1900). Before this entry existed, any South Australia-labelled data dated 1901 or later that reached the matcher would either be forced onto AUS-1901-2025 (a ~7.7 million km2 national total, roughly 8x this state's own area) or onto the colonial-era row, which is wrong because AUSA-1836-1900's own span is exclusive of 1901 onward and its polity was legally superseded at Federation. Federico-Tena's trading-polity classification and standard Australian constitutional histories both treat the six pre-Federation colonies and their post-1901 state successors as distinct units for exactly this reason: Federation changed sovereignty and external representation even where administration was continuous, which is why the country convention splits the span at 1901 rather than treating South Australia as one continuous polity from 1836.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `polygon_status: unassigned`. The route decided for this territory is `registered_source_unfetched`: GADM 4.1 publishes admin-1 (state) boundaries globally, including Australian states, but the locally present `gadm-4.1-adm1` extract is a curated subset of only 81 countries and does not currently include Australia (0 matching rows on disk). The source is registered and the right one for this feature type; it is simply not fetched for this country yet. `polygon_source: gadm-4.1-adm1` records that registered slug; `polygon_feature_id` is left null until the Australian states are fetched into the local extract. No other registered source carries Australian state-level boundaries (cshapes is country-level only; paine-2024, cliopatria, histogis-1860-habsburg, reporting-areas and constructed are all irrelevant to a modern Australian state).

**Territory description:** South Australia occupies the central-southern part of the Australian mainland, bordering Western Australia, the Northern Territory, Queensland, New South Wales, and Victoria, with a coastline on the Great Australian Bight and Southern Ocean around Adelaide (the state capital). Its land area is approximately 984,000 km2, essentially unchanged since the Northern Territory (which South Australia had administered since 1863) was excised to direct Commonwealth control in 1911 — the only significant boundary event in this polity's span, and it does not require a mid-span split here because the Northern Territory is tracked as its own separate polity throughout. Any area figure computed later from the eventual GADM 4.1 feature will be a measurement of that polygon, not independent evidence about the state's historical extent, which has not materially changed since 1901.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor is declared here, even though the territory continues directly from AUSA-1836-1900 (the colonial polity, span 1836-1900). AUSA-1836-1900 declares `successor: ['AUS-1901-2025']`, i.e. it points at the national Commonwealth row, not at this new state-level row — so a `predecessor: [AUSA-1836-1900]` claim here would be one-directional and fail validate_chain_integrity, which checks edges in both directions against that page's own declaration. Since AUSA-1836-1900 cannot be edited from this page, the predecessor field is left empty and the asymmetry is recorded as an open question below rather than silently asserted. No successor is declared either: the state's span runs to the present (`end_year: 2025` per the open-ended convention), so there is nothing after it to chain to.

## Sourced claims

- South Australia was proclaimed a British colony in 1836 and achieved responsible self-government in 1856, per standard constitutional histories of the Australian colonies, which is why the predecessor polity AUSA-1836-1900 uses 1836 as its start year.
- South Australia administered the Northern Territory from 1863 until the Territory was transferred to Commonwealth control on 1 January 1911, per the Northern Territory Acceptance Act 1910 (Cth) — the one boundary event in this polity's span, external to its own borders rather than internal to them.

## Decisions

### d-code-choice-aus-sa

**Chose AUS-SA-1901-2025 over AUSA-1901-2025 or a bespoke code**

The dominant table pattern for subnational rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (338 of 351 rows), and the only existing AUS subnational code, AUS-ACT-1911-2025, already follows it with a short subunit token (ACT). South Australia is not a historical territory with a name of its own distinct from the modern state (unlike ALK or HAW), so the bespoke pattern does not apply. I used the subunit token 'SA', the standard postal/administrative abbreviation for South Australia, rather than 'AUSA' (which is already used by the colonial-era predecessor polity AUSA-1836-1900 and would collide in spirit, implying continuity of code that the split at Federation is meant to avoid).

## Open questions

### oq-predecessor-edge-asymmetry

**AUSA-1836-1900 points its successor edge at AUS-1901-2025, not at this row**

This page's natural predecessor is the colonial polity AUSA-1836-1900 (South Australia, 1836-1900), and the territory is continuous across the 1901 split. But AUSA-1836-1900 declares `successor: ['AUS-1901-2025']`, the national Commonwealth row, rather than this new state-level row. That declaration was presumably correct when written, before any state-level post-1901 South Australia polity existed to point to. Now that AUS-SA-1901-2025 exists, AUSA-1836-1900's successor field should arguably be corrected to point here instead of (or in addition to) the national row. This page cannot make that edit itself, so the fix (changing AUSA-1836-1900's successor field) needs to be applied by whoever next revises that page, likely as part of processing the sibling AUS state creations already staged in this same pipeline run.

### oq-gadm-au-fetch-needed

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 Australia rows need to be fetched into the local extract**

polygon_status is unassigned because the locally cached gadm-4.1-adm1 file excludes Australia entirely (a curated 81-country subset). The fix is a fetch/build step to pull Australian admin-1 features from the full GADM 4.1 release and add South Australia's feature id here, not a new source registration. Until that fetch happens, this row and its Australian-state siblings (ACT already exists, plus NSW/NT/etc. from the same pipeline run) all carry the same gap.

### oq-nt-boundary-not-modeled

**The 1911 Northern Territory excision from South Australia is not reflected as a span split**

South Australia administered the Northern Territory until 1 January 1911, so for 1901-1910 'South Australia' as an administrative unit was materially larger than it has been since 1911 (Northern Territory alone is roughly 1.35 million km2, larger than South Australia itself). This entry treats 1901-2025 as one continuous span with one container edge and one (future) polygon, which is a simplification: any South Australia-labelled statistics from 1901-1910 that actually cover the combined South Australia + Northern Territory administrative unit would be under-covered by whatever modern-boundary polygon eventually gets assigned here. Whether any routed data actually falls in that 1901-1910 window and needs a territory note or a split has not been checked.
