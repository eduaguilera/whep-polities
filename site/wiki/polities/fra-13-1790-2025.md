# Bouches-du-Rhône (département)

## Summary

Bouches-du-Rhône is a French département on the Mediterranean coast around Marseille and Aix-en-Provence, one of the original départements created by the 1790 Revolutionary administrative reform out of the historic Provence region. It is entered here as `type: subnational` because a département is a sub-unit of the sovereign French state, not a state in its own right: France's own sovereignty and national-scale statistics belong to the FRA-* national chain (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), and this row exists solely to carry data a source tabulates at department scale specifically for this unit. The routing decision found no existing polity match by name, confirmed Bouches-du-Rhône is not among the four documented FRA departure cases (Territoire de Belfort, the Alsace-Lorraine trio, the Corsica 1793/1975 split, or an overseas département) that would require a non-default span, and so applied the country's default rule for an original Revolutionary département: creation-year start (1790) and an open end (2025), since the department is still current. Bouches-du-Rhône is unusual among French départements for containing a major port city (Marseille, France's second-largest city and largest port), which makes it economically significant well beyond its area and is why standalone department-level statistics exist for it in the first place.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures unit FRA-FRL04 ("Bouches-du-Rhône") from the agent-harness routing pipeline: French department-level data reported as observed (not reconstructed via back_cast), meaning the source measured this specific territory directly rather than distributing a national total across departments. Before this entry, the unit had no polity match in the database at all — routing found no name match among existing rows, and per the "département is not its country" match_existing rule, the three FRA-* candidates (all national totals) do not qualify as matches for a sub-national reporting unit. Bouches-du-Rhône is not one of the four documented FRA departure cases (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, the Corsica split, or an overseas département) that would call for a different span rule than the country default, so the default 1790-creation-to-present span applies. Routing this data to the national FRA-1919-2025 row instead would have folded a ~5,090 km² administrative subdivision into a national total more than a hundred times larger in area, exactly the scale mismatch that is itself evidence the data was previously unmatched or would have been mis-routed. What confirms this is a distinct, addressable reporting unit rather than an arbitrary carve-out is straightforward administrative continuity: it has been one of France's original 1790 départements for 235 years, treated as a first-class INSEE reporting geography throughout — and, being the department containing Marseille, one with enough independent economic weight (France's principal Mediterranean port) that department-level statistics for it specifically are unsurprising and expected.

## Decisions

### d-code-choice-fra-13

**Chose FRA-13-1790-2025, the INSEE department number, following the dominant table pattern and FRA precedent**

The dominant subnational pattern in the table (338 of 351 rows) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and existing FRA subnational rows already use INSEE department numbers as the subunit token (FRA-02, FRA-08, FRA-14, FRA-18, FRA-19, FRA-21 through FRA-28, and others, including FRA-04 for Alpes-de-Haute-Provence). Bouches-du-Rhône is INSEE department 13, so FRA-13-1790-2025 both matches the cross-country default and the country-specific precedent already established for two dozen other French départements, unlike the bespoke two/four-letter tokens (HP, PYRO, AVE) used for a handful of earlier FRA entries before the numeric precedent was set. FRA-13 was checked against the built polities_database.csv and the wiki/polities directory and is unused.

### d-start-year-1790-not-1800

**Used 1790 (the department's actual creation) as start_year, per the routing decision's own span_basis, not 1800**

The routing decision explicitly derives start_year 1790 from Bouches-du-Rhône's creation as one of the original départements in the 1790 Revolutionary reform, and end_year stays open at 2025 since the department is still current. The schema requires the code's years to equal the frontmatter's years, so the code is FRA-13-1790-2025, not FRA-13-1800-2025. This reproduces the same 1790-1800 containment gap already flagged and accepted on sibling entries FRA-04-1790-2025, FRA-05-1790-2025, FRA-56-1790-2025, FRA-HP-1790-2025, FRA-PYRO-1790-2025 and FRA-AVE-1790-2025, since no FRA container row currently starts before 1800; it is recorded as an open question here rather than silently truncated, matching that precedent rather than diverging from it.

### oq-marseille-annexations-boundary-stability

**Has Bouches-du-Rhône's boundary been genuinely unchanged since 1790, given Marseille's growth and 19th-century commune mergers?**

This entry treats the whole 1790-2025 span as one uniform territory on the strength of the general claim that French départements are comparatively stable administrative units, the same reasoning used for sibling FRA département entries. Unlike most rural départements, Bouches-du-Rhône contains Marseille, France's second city and a major port, which underwent substantial 19th- and 20th-century urban expansion and commune absorptions (e.g. Marseille's own 1943 municipal reorganisation into arrondissements did not change department boundaries, but earlier 19th-century commune-level annexations by Marseille and neighbouring towns have not been checked against the department's outer boundary with Var and Vaucluse). If a canton/commune transfer with a neighbouring département is later found to be material, the single-polygon treatment across the full span would need reconsideration.
