# Nebraska Territory

## Summary

Nebraska Territory was organised by the Kansas-Nebraska Act of 30 May 1854 and originally reached to the Continental Divide and the Canadian border. Colorado and Dakota territories took large parts of it in 1861, and the Idaho Organic Act of 3 March 1863 took the remaining land west of the 104th meridian (today's south-eastern Wyoming). From 1863 the territory had the outline Nebraska was admitted with, as the 37th state, on 1 March 1867.

This row carries the territory from 1863 to 1867 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-ne-1867-2025](usa-ne-1867-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span starts in 1863, when the Idaho act fixed the boundary this row carries, and ends in 1867 (exclusive), where USA-NE-1867-2025 begins.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-NEBRASKA`, label `NEBRASKA`),
reports 18 rows for 1866 (1 year(s)) — wheat, oats, barley, rye, maize and potatoes (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as Nebraska Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The central Great Plains between Kansas (40°N) and Dakota, from the Missouri River west to Colorado and Wyoming: the modern state of Nebraska, less the strip noted below.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.28_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 200,209 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1863
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  The modern polygon is NOT exactly this territory, which is why this row is `proxy`. Until 1882 Nebraska's northern line in the east followed the Keya Paha and Niobrara rivers to the Missouri; the act of 28 March 1882 moved it north to the 43rd parallel, transferring from Dakota Territory the land that is now Boyd County and the northern part of Keya Paha County. The GADM polygon includes that strip, so it **overstates** the 1863-1867 territory. The magnitude has not been measured here; the strip is of the order of a few thousand km², about 1-2% of the polygon's measured 200,209 km², an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a measurement. The same strip affects the state row USA-NE-1867-2025 for 1867-1881, which declares `assigned`; that row is not changed here.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. Nebraska Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1867 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1866; the row spans 1863-1867, the years this boundary
existed as Nebraska Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-NET-1863-1867` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-single-year

**One data year, 1866**

The panel reports Nebraska for 1866 only before statehood (18 rows). The row exists so that year has a territory; its span is the territory's own, not the data's.

### oq-niobrara-strip

**Measure the 1882 strip and check the state row**

The strip north of the Niobrara/Keya Paha should be measured against a year-stamped source (`usaboundaries-newberry`, registered but not fetched here). If it is material, USA-NE-1867-2025 needs a split at 1882 or a `proxy` status for its first fifteen years.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
