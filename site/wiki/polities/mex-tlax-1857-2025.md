# Tlaxcala

## Summary

This entry captures Tlaxcala, one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico, as a subnational reporting unit from its recognition as a full state under the 1857 Constitution through the present. `type: subnational` because Tlaxcala is not, and has never been, a sovereign state in its own right — it is a constituent unit of the Mexican federation, and this row exists purely to hold state-level statistics that the two national MEX rows (which cover the whole country) cannot represent at the correct territorial resolution. It was created because no existing polity in the database is Tlaxcala specifically: the national MEX rows merely contain it as one of 32 states, and no prior entry, national or subnational, matched or bounded Tlaxcala's own territory. Administratively, Tlaxcala began as one of several federal territories carved out after independence (1824), too small and sparsely populated at the time to be trusted with full statehood, and was upgraded to a state under the 1857 Constitution alongside a wave of other territorial promotions. Its boundaries have been essentially stable since then — it is bordered almost entirely by Puebla, which nearly surrounds it, with a short frontier against the State of Mexico. No polygon is currently attached, since the registered global boundary source that would cover Mexican states (GADM 4.1 admin-1) is only locally available for a curated subset of countries that excludes Mexico.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** the pipeline's routing decision for unit_id `MEX-TLAXCALA` (admin_name "Tlaxcala", country Mexico) found data keyed to Tlaxcala at Mexican-state resolution; the decision record does not give a row count or source name because this page authors the polity shell that the ledger will attach those rows to, but the unit was flagged for routing precisely because such state-level rows exist and could not be placed. **Previous match:** absent a Tlaxcala-specific polity, any Tlaxcala-labelled data would have fallen through to one of the two national MEX rows (spanning all of Mexico's ~2 million km²), which is wrong by roughly three orders of magnitude of territory and would silently blend a state-level series into a national aggregate context. **Confirmation of distinctness:** Tlaxcala is a first-order administrative division of Mexico (an INEGI/ISO 3166-2 state, code MX-TLA) with its own government, legislature, and statistical apparatus (part of Mexico's national statistical system, INEGI) — it is treated as a distinct reporting and administrative unit by every modern Mexican and international statistical source that disaggregates below the national level, exactly the kind of "reporting unit with data collected on it" standard used elsewhere in this database (e.g. the Manchuria exemplar) to justify a subnational row even where the unit is not sovereign.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision classified this as `new_source_needed`: GADM 4.1 admin-1 boundaries would in principle carry Tlaxcala as a feature, and the `gadm-4.1-adm1` slug is registered in this repository, but the locally fetched copy of that source is an 81-country subset that does not include Mexico. Since the source is not actually present for this country, `polygon_source` is set to `none` and `polygon_status` to `unassigned`, per the harness rule that `new_source_needed` may never be written as a fake source slug. `polygon_area_km2` is left unset — there is no attached geometry to measure.

**Territory description:** Tlaxcala state occupies the eastern flank of Mexico's central plateau, roughly 120 km east of Mexico City. It is the smallest of Mexico's 32 states by land area, at approximately **4,060 km²** (a commonly cited modern figure, not a GIS measurement, since no polygon is attached here). It is bounded on the north, east, and south by the state of Puebla, which nearly encircles it, and has a short western border with the State of Mexico. Its capital is Tlaxcala de Xicohténcatl. On a modern map it sits between the cities of Puebla (immediately southeast) and Mexico City (west), in the shadow of the La Malinche volcano, which straddles its border with Puebla. No large territorial change to the state's boundaries is documented between 1857 and the present, which is why a present-day boundary source (once fetched) is expected to serve as a reasonable proxy for the full span — a claim recorded as an open question below rather than assumed.

## Predecessors and successors

Tlaxcala has no predecessor or successor polity entries: it was carved out of the federation's own territorial structure rather than succeeding a prior distinct polity, and it has not been merged, split, or renamed since 1857. Before 1857 it existed as the Federal Territory of Tlaxcala (from 1824), which is not separately modelled here because no data series in this database is routed to it; the pre-1857 period is covered only implicitly by the national MEX rows. If pre-1857 Tlaxcala-specific data is ever found (e.g., a 19th-century agricultural census reporting the territory separately), a predecessor entry such as MEX-TLAX-1824-1857 could be created and linked.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-subunit

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end code pattern**

57 of 66 existing subnational rows use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. Tlaxcala is not a historical territory with a name distinct from its role as a modern federal-subject state (unlike ALK or HYD), so the bespoke pattern does not apply. MEX has zero existing subnational rows, so there is no country-specific precedent to depart from; MEX-TLAX-1857-2025 was chosen using the state's common three-to-four letter abbreviation (TLAX) rather than an official ISO 3166-2 code, since Mexico's ISO 3166-2 codes for states are numeric-suffixed (MX-TLA) and the repo's existing subunit codes (AS, CA, CVD) are short alphabetic tags rather than ISO subdivision codes.

### d-polygon-none

**polygon_source set to none, not new_source_needed**

The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed: GADM 4.1 adm1 is registered globally in principle but the locally fetched copy is an 81-country subset that excludes Mexico, so no registered feature exists to attach today. Per the harness instructions, new_source_needed must resolve to polygon_source: none with polygon_status: unassigned, and the desired future source (a fetched GADM 4.1 adm1 extract covering Mexico, or an INEGI state-boundary layer) is recorded as an open question rather than fabricated as a source slug.

### d-start-year-1857

**Used the 1857 Constitution statehood date, not the data's first year (1900)**

The country convention for Mexico defaults new state entries to their historical admission-to-statehood year rather than the first year data happens to appear, except for three listed departures (Baja California, Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo) which are territories admitted much later. Tlaxcala is not among those exceptions, so start_year=1857 was used, matching when the 1857 Constitution recognized Tlaxcala as a full state after 33 years as a federal territory (1824-1857).

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-mexico-states

**No registered polygon source currently covers Mexican states**

The registered gadm-4.1-adm1 slug only has a locally fetched subset of 81 countries, and Mexico is not among them. To assign a real polygon to MEX-TLAX-1857-2025 (and to any other Mexican state entry created later), someone needs to either fetch the full global GADM 4.1 admin-1 dataset and register Mexico's states from it, or source an INEGI (Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía) state-boundary shapefile, which would be the more authoritative domestic source. Until then this entry carries polygon_status: unassigned and no measured area.

### oq-1857-boundary-vintage

**Tlaxcala's 1857 boundary may not equal its present-day boundary**

Tlaxcala is Mexico's smallest state and its municipal boundaries have seen minor adjustments through the 19th and 20th centuries (including boundary disputes with Puebla, which surrounds it on three sides). A present-day GADM or INEGI polygon, once fetched, would be a reasonable proxy for most of the 1857-2025 span since no large territorial change is documented, but this should be stated explicitly as an approximation rather than assumed exact, and any known 19th-century boundary adjustment with Puebla should be checked before declaring the proxy valid.

### oq-statehood-date-source

**1857 admission date not verified against a primary constitutional source**

The routing decision itself flags this: the 1857 date for Tlaxcala's elevation from federal territory to full state rests on general historical consensus (the 1857 Constitution's state list) rather than a primary source consulted in this session. If the repository's Mexican-states convention is later found to derive admission dates from a different reference (e.g., the actual decree date rather than the constitution's promulgation year), this entry's start_year may need to be corrected, and predecessor/successor edges elsewhere in the graph that assume 1857 would need to be checked too.
