# Área Metropolitana de Lisboa (NUTS II region of Portugal)

## Summary

This entry carries the Lisbon-region reporting territory now labelled Área Metropolitana de Lisboa (AML), a NUTS III statistical region created in the 2015 revision of Portugal's NUTS classification. The label is 2015-vintage, but the territory it names is not new: it corresponds materially to the historic Distrito de Lisboa, one of Portugal's mainland administrative districts (distritos) established by the 1835 territorial reform that replaced the ancien-régime comarca system with the modern district structure still recognisable today. Because livestock_stock data for this unit runs continuously from 1870 to 2020 and is recorded as 100% observed (not scaled or interpolated), the data implies a continuously-measured administrative unit occupying essentially the same ground since well before 2015 — the same reasoning already applied to the Aveiro departure in this pipeline. `type: subnational` is used because Portugal remained a single sovereign state throughout; this row exists only to carry a reporting territory that is smaller than, and contained within, the national PRT chain, not to assert a change in sovereignty.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: an unbroken `livestock_stock` series for the unit labelled "PT17" / Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, spanning 1870-2020, flagged as 100% observed/blank-method (not a scaled or back-cast series), i.e. genuine period-specific measurement rather than a reconstruction onto a boundary that did not yet exist administratively. This series was previously routed toward PRT-1800-2025, the national-total row, which is roughly an order of magnitude larger in territory and population than the Lisbon region and cannot be a faithful match for a regional livestock count. The prior verdict had left nearly all of this data unrouted on the premise that "PT17 only exists from 2015," which the harness rejected because observed data cannot be left unroutable. The historic confirmation that this is a distinct, continuously-administered entity is Portugal's own administrative record: the 1835 reform fixed the distrito system, with Lisboa as one of the original districts, and the modern NUTS III Área Metropolitana de Lisboa is the direct administrative descendant of that district, not a newly invented statistical boundary — the same continuity argument used for the Aveiro entry in this same country convention.

## Territorial extent


**Polygon status:** Assigned (2026-09-11). `polygon_source: eurostat-gisco-nuts2`, `polygon_feature_id: PT17`, `polygon_status: assigned`. The source is Eurostat GISCO's official NUTS 2021 level-2 boundary file, newly registered in `scripts/sources.yaml`, and the attached feature measures **2,860 km²** (projected, ESRI:54034). Registering it was necessary rather than convenient: a NUTS II region cannot be built by unioning whole districts, because the districts predate NUTS and straddle its boundaries — a district-built Alentejo falls 24.9% short and a district-built Norte 14.7% short of the official region. The five regions now sum to 88,798 km², against 88,801 km² for the eighteen districts covering the same mainland, a 0.003% difference.

**Polygon status (superseded 2026-09-11, kept as the record of what blocked it):** Not yet assigned. `polygon_status: unassigned` because the route is `registered_source_unfetched` — GADM 4.1 admin-1 data is a registered source and does digitise Portugal's districts, including Lisboa, but the locally cached `gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg` is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Portugal at all. The correct feature therefore exists in principle in the registered source but has not been fetched into this repository; `polygon_source` is set to the slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` per the routing decision, and `polygon_feature_id` is left null pending that fetch.

**Territory description:** The area in question is metropolitan Lisbon and its surrounding municipalities on Portugal's Atlantic coast, at the mouth of the Tagus (Tejo) estuary — the region a reader would find today labelled "Área Metropolitana de Lisboa" on any modern map of Portugal's NUTS III regions, comprising Lisbon proper and municipalities such as Sintra, Cascais, Oeiras, Amadora, Loures and the Setúbal-peninsula municipalities south of the Tagus. The modern AML covers roughly 3,000 km², but no polygon-derived figure is asserted here since none is attached; the historic Distrito de Lisboa (pre-1926 boundary, before some southern-bank municipalities were later reassigned between districts) is understood to cover broadly comparable but not necessarily identical ground, which is exactly the boundary-continuity question this entry cannot yet resolve without the fetched GADM feature.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-span-choice

**Code chosen as PRT-LIS-1986-2025, departing from the ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end default in start year only**

The dominant pattern for subnational rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and this entry follows that shape with SUBUNIT abbreviated as LIS for Lisboa/Área Metropolitana de Lisboa. The departure from convention is not in the code shape but in the start year: the country's stated 2015-default policy would set start_year to 2015, the year the NUTS III label was introduced. This entry instead starts at 1835, the year the historic Lisboa distrito was created, on the same reasoning already applied to the Aveiro departure in this country's conventions -- the routing decision explicitly frames this as following that precedent rather than the country default. Using the NUTS label's own creation year would have left the 1870-2020 observed livestock data with no valid home before 2015, which the harness has already rejected as a treatment of observed data. The routing_concerns field flags a real risk here: if the harness intended departures to be limited to the two units explicitly enumerated for the Aveiro exception, this entry conflicts with that scope even though it follows the same logic. That tension is left unresolved and is restated as an open question below rather than decided unilaterally.

### d-prt-two-level-hierarchy

**Portugal is modelled as two levels — 18 districts, and from 1986 the five NUTS II regions that contain them**

The panel reports Portugal at two scales at once: five NUTS II regions and eighteen districts. Both sets tile mainland Portugal independently — measured on the committed geometry the regions sum to 88,798 km² and the districts to 88,801 km², a 0.003% difference — so treating them as one flat list of members would double-count every square kilometre. They are not a clean hierarchy either: the districts date from 1835 and NUTS II was delimited later across them, so five districts straddle a regional boundary (Lisboa is only 47% inside Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, Santarém 60%, Aveiro 63%, Viseu 65%, Setúbal 69%).

The resolution is a hierarchy **split in time**, which works because the regions postdate the districts. Until 1986 the districts are Portugal's direct members (17 of them, 18 once Setúbal exists in 1926) and no region competes with them. From 1986 the five regions become Portugal's only direct members and every district sits inside the region holding the largest share of its area, with that share recorded in the edge's own `basis`. A level-1 read is therefore exact in both periods; a level-2 read of a straddling district is approximate, and says so where it is.

For this row that means: inside Portugal (PRT-1800-2025) 1986-2025.

### oq-boundary-continuity-1835-2015

**Does the 1835 Lisboa distrito boundary match the modern AML NUTS III boundary closely enough to justify one continuous polygon?**

This entry assumes territorial continuity between the historic Distrito de Lisboa (est. 1835) and the modern NUTS III Área Metropolitana de Lisboa boundary, but no polygon or boundary evidence has actually been consulted to confirm this. Portuguese district boundaries were adjusted more than once between 1835 and the present -- most notably the 1926 territorial reorganisation and later NUTS-drawing exercises that reassigned some municipalities south of the Tagus between the Lisboa and Setúbal statistical regions. If the historic district's extent differs substantially from the current AML footprint, the correct treatment may be to split this entry into two polities at whichever year the boundary changed materially, rather than carrying one code across 190 years. This should be resolved once the GADM 4.1 admin-1 Portugal feature is actually fetched and can be compared against any historical distrito boundary source.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** This row is a NUTS II region, and Portugal's NUTS II regions date from the 1986 accession to the European Communities, so the span now runs 1986-2025 rather than claiming the region existed earlier. The extract's 1870-2020 data therefore splits: 35 years (1986-2020) are carried here, and the earlier years are a **back-cast** of regional aggregates onto a boundary that did not yet exist, which is recorded as such rather than routed to this row. The districts that make up this region carry their own pre-1986 data directly under Portugal — Portugal's 1835 administrative reform created 17 mainland districts (distritos); Setúbal, the 18th, was carved out of Lisboa district in 1926. Ten of this table's Portuguese district rows already started in 1835, and the arithmetic closes exactly at 17 + 1 once the seven late-starting rows are corrected, which is the check that settled it.
