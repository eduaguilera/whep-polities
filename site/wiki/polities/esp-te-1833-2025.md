# Teruel (province of Spain)

## Summary

Teruel is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today the southernmost of the three provinces making up the Aragon autonomous community, in the northeast interior of the Iberian Peninsula. It takes its name from its capital, the city of Teruel, and occupies a mountainous, sparsely populated stretch bordered by Zaragoza province to the north, Castellon and Valencia to the south and east, Cuenca and Guadalajara to the west, and Cuenca/Guadalajara/Valencia further south. Teruel is Spain's least densely populated province and has become a byword for rural depopulation ("Espana vaciada"). This entry is type: subnational because Teruel is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Teruel separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES242, since the province's creation. The routing decision for unit ES242 found no existing ESP candidate matching Teruel (only A Coruna, Madrid, and the national Spain row existed at the time this batch of NUTS3 units began being processed), so this is a genuine create_new case following the same pattern already applied to the other Spanish province rows in this database (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra, Madrid).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES242 ("Teruel"), country Spain, following the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES111 A Coruna through ES243), which uses a default start year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open, exclusive end_year of 2025 for still-current units, since Teruel is not one of the documented departure cases (the 1927 Canary Islands split). Previously matched to / why wrong: prior to this routing decision, no ESP subnational row represented Teruel specifically; the only ESP candidates on file were A Coruna, Madrid, and the national Spain row, so any data for unit ES242 that predates this batch would have had to fall back to the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, collapsing a single interior Aragonese province of roughly 14,785 km2 into a national total of about 505,000 km2 -- a roughly 34x aggregation that would erase the province-level distinction the source data actually carries. What confirms distinctness: Teruel's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES242) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity continuously since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the six other ESP-XX-1833-2025 sibling entries created in this same convention pass -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from both the national aggregate and from its Aragonese sibling provinces Zaragoza and Huesca.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE/IGN province numeric code), under which Teruel is cpro 44 -- but this source is marked present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not yet been fetched into this repository), so the feature cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet, exactly as the other unassigned ESP-XX-1833-2025 rows (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra, Madrid) do for the same reason. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and Teruel does not coincide with the Aragon autonomous community boundary (Aragon also contains Zaragoza and Huesca provinces), so an adm1 feature would be the wrong granularity. Territory description: Teruel province occupies the southern part of Aragon, a mountainous, high-plateau interior region traversed by the Sistema Iberico, bordered by Zaragoza to the north, Castellon and Valencia (Comunitat Valenciana) to the south and east, and Cuenca and Guadalajara (Castilla-La Mancha) to the west. Its capital and largest city is Teruel, a small city on the Turia and Alfambra rivers known for its Mudejar architecture. On a modern map, Teruel province's approximate area per INE is 14,785 km2, making it the largest of Aragon's three provinces by area despite being its least populous -- this is the province's own official administrative area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform (Teruel was not among the units affected by the 1927 Canary Islands split or any other known province-level boundary revision), so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling rows.

## Predecessors and successors

Teruel has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the Kingdom of Aragon's older territorial divisions (corregimientos and historic Aragonese partidos that predate the unified 1833 provincial map), which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to the sibling ESP-XX-1833-2025 rows. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1978, one of the three provinces making up the Aragon autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for its Spanish NUTS3 sibling rows.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Teruel as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES242) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ES242 records that sibling Spanish NUTS3 units (ES111 A Coruna through ES243) were routed create_new under the same 1833-start/2025-open-end country convention, mirroring verdicts already recorded for provinces ES112 through ES241, which is the evidentiary basis for treating Teruel as a distinct, continuously-tabulated reporting geography rather than folding it into the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.
- mapspain-ign is the source already used to register the seven other Spanish province rows created in this convention pass, and Teruel's own INE province code (cpro) is 44, the routing decision's basis for expecting a matching feature once the source is fetched.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-te-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation TE, matching the already-decided ESP subnational house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name, and all seven existing ESP subnational rows follow it exactly (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO). Teruel is not a historical territory with a name and identity of its own independent of its status as a Spanish province (unlike the bespoke cases ALK-1867-1959, HYD-1724-1948, RYU-1937-1945), so the bespoke-code pattern does not apply here. TE is chosen as the abbreviation because it is the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for Teruel (ES-TE) and reads unambiguously against the sibling AS/CA/CO/LU/MD/OR/PO codes, giving ESP-TE-1833-2025. This also keeps the code independent of the INE province numeric code (44) and the NUTS3 code (ES242), neither of which appears in the sibling codes either.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and Teruel's cpro=44 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Teruel is conventionally cpro 44 (its INE province code) -- but the routing decision for ES242 could not confirm this because the source file is marked present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence for redistribution has not been checked. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=44 feature is confirmed to be Teruel (and confirmed to match the 1833-2025 province boundary rather than some other vintage), this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry, exactly as the other unassigned ESP-XX-1833-2025 rows do for the same reason. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Teruel, distinct from Aragon's other two provinces (Zaragoza, Huesca), before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Teruel province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Teruel province was part of the Kingdom of Aragon's older territorial divisions (corregimientos and historic partidos that predate 1833), which do not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and are not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Teruel's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for the sibling ESP-XX-1833-2025 rows. Whether pre-1833 Aragonese administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all remains an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.

### oq-canary-islands-departure-check

**Confirm Teruel genuinely has no 1927 Canary Islands-style span departure**

The routing reasoning asserts Teruel is not one of the Canary Islands departure units and therefore uses the default 1833 start with no departure, but this has not been independently cross-checked against a canonical list of all Spanish provincial boundary or span revisions since 1833 (the only documented departure case in the country convention is the 1927 Canary Islands split into Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas). Teruel, as a mainland Aragonese province untouched by that or any other known 19th/20th-century provincial reorganization, is very likely a clean 1833-2025 case, but if a fuller list of Spanish provincial revisions surfaces a Teruel-specific departure, start_year or end_year here -- and the code itself -- would need to change.
