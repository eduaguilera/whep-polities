# Zamora (province of Spain)

## Summary

Zamora is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the nine provinces making up the Castilla y Leon autonomous community in the northwestern interior of the country. It takes its name from its capital, the city of Zamora, on the Duero river. The province sits on the border with Portugal in the far west of Castilla y Leon, bordered by Ourense and Leon provinces to the north, Valladolid to the east, Salamanca to the south, and Portugal (Braganca district) to the west. This entry is type: subnational because Zamora is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Zamora separately from the national total for over a century and a half. No polity row for Zamora existed in the database before this routing decision, so this page both allocates the code ESP-ZM-1833-2025 and documents it, following the same convention already established for its sibling ESP entries (A Coruna, Madrid, Pontevedra, and the other 1833-reform provinces already in this table).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES419 ("Zamora"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to the sibling batch of Spanish NUTS3 provincial units (ES112 through ES243 and beyond), all following a system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: no existing polity row represented Zamora specifically -- the routing decision evaluated only A Coruna (ESP-CO-1833-2025), Madrid (ESP-MD-1833-2025), and national Spain (ESP-1800-2025) as ESP candidates, none of which IS Zamora; routing Zamora's series to the national aggregate would have collapsed a province-level series (~10,559 km2) into a territory roughly 48x larger than the province and orders of magnitude coarser than the reporting geography, and no departure such as the Canary Islands split applies to a standard mainland Castilian province. What confirms distinctness: Zamora's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025 and ESP-PO-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish province numeric code), under which Zamora is conventionally cpro 49 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository), matching the same open state already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025 and ESP-PO-1833-2025, so the feature_id cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level (Castilla y Leon as a whole), not the 50-province level, and Zamora province does not coincide with that boundary (Castilla y Leon contains eight other provinces: Avila, Burgos, Leon, Palencia, Salamanca, Segovia, Soria, Valladolid). Territory description: Zamora province occupies the westernmost part of Castilla y Leon, straddling the Duero river valley, bordered by Portugal's Braganca district to the west, Ourense and Leon provinces to the north, Valladolid to the east, and Salamanca to the south. Its capital is the city of Zamora, known for its concentration of Romanesque churches; the province also contains the Sanabria lake district and Lago de Sanabria natural park in its mountainous northwest. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 10,559 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for sibling entries.

## Predecessors and successors

Zamora has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the Kingdom of Leon's older partido and jurisdiction system (including the historic Toro jurisdiction, whose namesake town lies in the eastern part of the modern province), which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025 and ESP-PO-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and one of the nine provinces making up the Castilla y Leon autonomous community since 1983) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish provincial units.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-zm-not-za

**Used ZM rather than Zamora's real vehicle-plate code ZA, because ZA is already taken by Zaragoza in this table**

The dominant subnational code pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and existing ESP subnational rows use a short letter abbreviation of the unit (CO for A Coruna, MD for Madrid, PO for Pontevedra). Zamora's standard Spanish vehicle-plate and INE-informal abbreviation is ZA -- but ESP-ZA-1833-2025 already exists in this repository's untracked wiki set, allocated to Zaragoza province (a different, unrelated Aragonese province whose own vehicle-plate code is Z, not ZA). Reusing ZA for Zamora would collide with an already-created polity code, which the containment/uniqueness gates would reject outright. This entry therefore uses ZM instead: still a two-letter abbreviation in the same house style, still legible as short for Zamora, but not the true plate code. Whoever next touches ESP-ZA-1833-2025 (Zaragoza) should be aware this collision exists and that Zamora's natural abbreviation was already displaced.

This page works around the collision by using ZM for Zamora, but it is worth checking, once both entries land, whether Zaragoza's code should instead have been ESP-Z-1833-2025 or some other form, freeing ZA for its more natural referent and avoiding a lasting asymmetry where one province's code matches its plate abbreviation and a neighboring one does not.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Zamora province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Zamora province was part of the Kingdom of Leon's older partido and jurisdiction system, including the historic Toro jurisdiction, which does not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and is not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Zamora's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025 and ESP-PO-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Leonese/Castilian administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all remains an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
