# Azerbaijan SSR (1920-1991)

## Summary

The Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic (Azerbaijan SSR) was a
constituent union republic of the USSR from 1920 to 1991, covering
the South Caucasus territory of modern Azerbaijan (~87,000 km²).
The Red Army conquered Azerbaijan on 28 April 1920. From 1922 to
1936 it was subsumed within the Transcaucasian Soviet Federative
Socialist Republic (TSFSR) before becoming a direct constituent
republic of the USSR. It declared independence on 30 August 1991
and formally left the USSR on 25 December 1991.

~87,000 km² (including the Nakhchivan exclave; Nagorno-Karabakh
was an autonomous oblast within the Azerbaijan SSR).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** CShapes 2.0 `cowcode=373`, vintage 1991.
  This is the modern Azerbaijan polygon, used as a proxy for the
  entire 1920-1991 period. Azerbaijan SSR borders were largely
  coterminous with modern Azerbaijan; the main difference is that
  Nagorno-Karabakh was an autonomous oblast within the Azerbaijan
  SSR but is captured in the same polygon footprint.
- **Area.** ~87,000 km². This approximates the 1920-1991 territorial
  extent well; only minor boundary demarcation shifts occurred
  internally within the USSR.
- **Polygon status: proxy.** CShapes 2.0 does not carry sub-republic
  polygons for the Soviet period (it records only the USSR aggregate
  at cowcode=365). The 1991 Azerbaijan CShapes polygon (cowcode=373)
  is used as a period proxy. The polygon_vintage_drift is
  approximately 71 years (1920 to 1991 vintage), but the territorial
  extent is a reasonable approximation because Azerbaijan SSR borders
  remained essentially stable from 1936 onward. The main
  approximation concern is the 1920-1936 TSFSR period, when
  Azerbaijan, Armenia, and Georgia were federated — but the
  individual republic boundaries within the TSFSR were maintained
  throughout, so the polygon remains valid.
- **Direction of approximation.** Slight understatement possible
  for very early years (1920-1922) before Soviet borders fully
  consolidated; slight overstatement possible if Nagorno-Karabakh
  administrative transfers are considered, but these are sub-1%
  variations on ~87,000 km².
