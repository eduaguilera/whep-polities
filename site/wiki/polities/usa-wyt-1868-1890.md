# Wyoming Territory

## Summary

Wyoming Territory was created by the Organic Act of 25 July 1868 from parts of Dakota, Idaho and Utah territories along the line of the Union Pacific railroad; its government was organised in 1869, and in the same year it became the first US jurisdiction to grant women the vote. Its rectangle of meridians and parallels (41°-45°N, 104°03'-111°03'W) never changed, and it was admitted as the 44th state on 10 July 1890.

This row carries the territory from 1868 to 1890 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-wy-1890-2025](usa-wy-1890-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span starts in 1868 with the Organic Act — not 1869, when the government was organised — because the act fixed the boundary and it is the boundary this row carries. It ends in 1890 (exclusive), where USA-WY-1890-2025 begins.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-WYOMING`, label `WYOMING`),
reports 30 rows for 1882-1889 (8 year(s)) — maize, oats and potatoes (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as Wyoming Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The rectangle between Montana (45°N) and Colorado/Utah (41°N), from the Dakota and Nebraska lines (104°03'W) west to Idaho and Utah (111°03'W): the modern state of Wyoming.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.51_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 253,297 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1868
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  Yellowstone National Park (1872) lies mostly inside the rectangle and was federal land throughout; that is tenure, not a boundary difference. The polygon is recorded as `assigned`.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

## Sourced claims

- Organic acts, boundary changes and admission dates as stated in the summary; these are the
  statutory dates recorded in the Statutes at Large for each act and in the admission acts and
  proclamations.
- USDA NASS Quick Stats (https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov/) reports `WYOMING` for
  1882-1889 as `SURVEY` data; in the harmonised panel every one of the 30 rows is
  lane `observational`, derivation family `none` — none is flagged as interpolated, scaled or
  back-cast by the panel itself.
- Continuity check across admission: the year-on-year change in harvested area at statehood is
  of the same size as the year-to-year changes around it, with no step, which is what a series
  on an unchanged frame looks like (measured on the panel, 2026-09-24).

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. Wyoming Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1890 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1882-1889; the row spans 1868-1890, the years this boundary
existed as Wyoming Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-WYT-1868-1890` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-thin-series

**Only 30 pre-statehood rows, and no wheat**

The pre-1890 series covers maize, oats and potatoes for 1882-1889. The absence of wheat is the source's, not a routing gap; whether Quick Stats omits territorial wheat or USDA never estimated it is not established.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
