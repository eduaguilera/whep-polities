# Kingdom of Kazembe

## Summary

The Kingdom of Kazembe (Mwata Kazembe) was an eastern Lunda kingdom in the Luapula valley, straddling the modern Zambia–DRC border. It was established in the 18th century as an offshoot of the Lunda Empire (Mwata Yamvo) when Lunda warriors conquered the Luapula valley. Kazembe became a major centre of the Central African trade network, exchanging copper and salt from the interior for cloth and beads from the Indian Ocean coast. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1899, when the British South Africa Company (BSAC) established administrative control. Biger notes that "the central part of Africa remained free of European occupation until the 1880s." The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). Kazembe's core territory was the Luapula valley between Lake Mweru and the Luapula River, in what is now Luapula Province of Zambia and adjacent Katanga (DRC). The kingdom exercised suzerainty over tributary chieftaincies across a wider catchment area.

## Open questions

### oq-drc-extent

Kazembe's territory straddled the modern Zambia–DRC border. The WHEP polygon presumably captures one side; the DRC portion may be unaccounted for in the successor chain (no DRC pre-colonial row is linked). This is a potential spatial coverage gap.

### oq-chain-gap

Same chain gap as BMB (Bemba Kingdom): both jump directly from the pre-colonial kingdom to ZMB-1964-2025, skipping the BSAC (1899–1924) and Northern Rhodesia (1924–1964) periods.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger covers Zambia only at independence. A specialist source on Kazembe (e.g., Cunnison 1959 *The Luapula Peoples of Northern Rhodesia*; Schecter 1976) is needed to source territorial and political claims.
