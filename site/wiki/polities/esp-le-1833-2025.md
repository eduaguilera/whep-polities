# León (province of Spain)

## Summary

León is one of the 50 provinces of Spain created simultaneously on 30 November 1833 by Javier de Burgos's territorial reform, which replaced the old kingdoms and lordships of the Crown of Castile with a uniform grid of provinces for centralized administration. Before 1833 the León area was governed as part of the old Kingdom of León, one of the historic territorial divisions of the Crown of Castile, with no separate statistical apparatus of its own; the 1833 reform is the point at which "León" begins to exist as a discrete administrative and statistical unit that can be matched to data. `type: subnational` is used, not `national`, because León never held sovereignty — it is, and always has been, a province inside Spain, and this entry exists purely to carry a reporting territory that is smaller than the national ESP row. This entry follows the same pattern already established for 49 sibling Spanish provinces in this repository (ESP-A-1833-2025, ESP-AB-1833-2025, ESP-BU-1833-2025, etc.), all created by the same 1833 reform and all coded `ESP-<subunit>-1833-2025`.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: statistics reported at the level of the Spanish province of León, matching the standard Spanish provincial breakdowns used across this repository's sibling ESP-XX-1833-2025 entries (agricultural and demographic series tabulated by INE and its historical predecessors at province granularity, spanning the province's existence from 1833 to the present). Previously, any province-level León data not yet routed to a dedicated polity code would have fallen back to the national ESP row, which is roughly 35x too large in area and conflates León's statistics with those of Spain's other 49 provinces plus its autonomous cities — a routing that discards the sub-national resolution the source actually reports at. What confirms León is a distinct, separately-tabulated entity rather than a homonym or a sub-unit folded into a larger one: it is one of the 50 provinces fixed by the 1833 Javier de Burgos decree, a legally and administratively distinct unit from that date forward, still current today as a NUTS3-equivalent province of the autonomous community of Castile and León, and it is administratively and statistically distinguished from same-named or similar-named entities (Sierra Leone, the modern autonomous community of Castile and León as a whole, and neighboring provinces such as Zamora, Palencia, and Asturias).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. `polygon_source: mapspain-ign` is the correct registered source — this repository already uses mapspain-ign's `provinces.gpkg` as the polygon source for the other 49 Spanish provinces created by the same 1833 reform, following the identical ESP-XX-1833-2025 code pattern — but `sources.yaml` lists mapspain-ign as `present_locally: False`: the file `data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg` is not on disk in this environment, so no feature can be extracted right now. `polygon_status` is therefore `unassigned` rather than `assigned` or `proxy`, per the routing decision's `registered_source_unfetched` route: the source is registered and its correctness for this territory type is already established by 49 sibling rows, it simply has not been fetched into this checkout yet. `gadm-4.1-adm1` was checked as an alternative and rejected — it carries only 18 ESP features, which read as autonomous communities rather than the 50-province breakdown, so it has no León feature to substitute.

**Territory description:** The province of León occupies the northwestern part of the Castile and León autonomous community, in northwestern Spain, bordered by Asturias to the north, Cantabria and Palencia to the east, Zamora to the south, and Ourense and Lugo (Galicia) to the west. Its capital is the city of León. The province covers approximately 15,581 km², making it the third-largest of Spain's 50 provinces by area, and includes the Cordillera Cantábrica mountains in the north (the Picos de Europa massif partly falls within it) descending into the plateau of the Tierra de Campos in the south. Its boundary has been essentially stable since the 1833 creation, aside from minor municipal boundary adjustments that do not affect the province's outline at the resolution this repository works at.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: León as a statistical/administrative unit begins at its creation by the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, and no prior polity row in this repository carries the pre-1833 Kingdom of León (that territory, when it needs representation, would belong to a pre-1833 Castile/León polity not yet modeled here — noted as an open question). No successor: León remains a current province of Spain as of this repository's 2025 open-ended horizon, unchanged in status, so `end_year: 2025` is the open boundary shared by all Spanish provinces in this table, not a terminal event for León itself.

## Sourced claims

- León is one of the 50 provinces created by the Javier de Burgos territorial division decree of 30 November 1833, which replaced the prior division into historic kingdoms and señoríos with a uniform administrative grid — a fact already the stated basis for the 49 sibling ESP-XX-1833-2025 entries in this repository.
- The province of León has a land area of approximately 15,581 km² according to Spain's National Statistics Institute (INE) provincial area tables, verifiable against modern administrative sources independent of any geometry attached to this entry (no polygon is attached yet).

## Decisions

### d-follow-provincial-convention

**Follow the dominant ESP-XX-1833-2025 convention rather than a bespoke code**

León is a standard administrative province, not a historical territory with a name and identity independent of the province system (unlike bespoke cases such as ALK-1867-1959 or HAW-1959-2025). It was created by the same 1833 reform as the 49 other Spanish provincial entries already in this table, uses the same start/end years, and is routed to the same national container. Departing from the dominant pattern here would create an inconsistency with no basis: there is nothing distinct about León's administrative history relative to its 49 siblings that would justify a different code shape. The subunit token LE was chosen as the standard two-letter abbreviation consistent with sibling codes like ESP-BU-1833-2025 (Burgos) and ESP-AS-1833-2025 (Asturias).
