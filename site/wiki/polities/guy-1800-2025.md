# Guyana (British Guiana, 1800–2025)

## Summary

Guyana (formerly British Guiana), a territory on the northern coast of South America. The three Dutch colonies of Berbice, Demerara, and Essequibo were held alternately by the Dutch and British between 1781 and 1814, then permanently ceded to Britain; in 1831 they were united as the colony of British Guiana [biger-1995 §guyana]. Guyana became independent on 26 May 1966. The polity uses a single CShapes 2.0 polygon (feature 110, vintage 1886) across the full 1800–2025 span.

## Territorial extent

The Guyana–Venezuela boundary runs for 462 miles (743 km) from the Atlantic coast to the tripoint with Brazil on Mount Roraima [biger-1995 §guyana]. The territory presently disputed between Venezuela and Guyana (the Essequibo) covers approximately 50,000 sq. miles (130,000 km²) [biger-1995 §guyana].

### 1899 Paris Arbitration boundary change (polygon approximation)

**Border change within polity span.** The Guyana–Venezuela western boundary shifted materially in 1899. Prior to the Paris Arbitration Award of 3 October 1899, the operative line was the Schomburgk Line, surveyed in 1840–44. The arbitration tribunal awarded a revised boundary to British Guiana that broadly followed the Schomburgk Line but with modifications; the net effect was that approximately **30,000–50,000 km²** of sparsely populated western jungle and savanna (the upper Cuyuni and Mazaruni basins) was confirmed or transferred to British Guiana relative to Venezuela's claim.

The CShapes 2.0 polygon used here has a vintage of **1886**, which predates the 1899 award. It therefore:

- **Understates** post-1899 British Guiana territory by roughly 30,000–50,000 km² on the western frontier;
- **Overstates** pre-1899 territory if the 1886 feature already reflects a forward projection of later borders (direction uncertain without inspecting the CShapes geometry in detail).

**Why this approximation is acceptable for WHEP agricultural data.** The commodities recorded for this polity (sugar, rice, and related plantation crops) were produced entirely on the Atlantic coastal strip and the lower river valleys (Demerara, Berbice, Essequibo). The disputed western territory was uninhabited forest and savanna with no recorded agricultural output. The production data are therefore **unaffected** by the 1899 boundary change, and routing data labeled "Guyana" or "British Guiana" to this polity is correct regardless of which polygon vintage is used.

**Recommended future action.** If a GIS polygon for post-1899 British Guiana becomes available (e.g., from a CShapes 1902 or 1905 feature, or a purpose-built historical boundary dataset), the polity should be split at 1899 into:

- `GUY-1800-1899` — pre-arbitration extent (Schomburgk Line; 1886 CShapes polygon as proxy);
- `GUY-1899-2025` — post-arbitration extent (Paris Award boundary; later CShapes feature or constructed polygon).

Until that polygon is available, the single polity with the 1886 polygon is retained and this note serves as the required documentation of the approximation and its direction.
