# Alpes-Maritimes

## Summary

Alpes-Maritimes is a French department occupying the Cote d'Azur in the far south-east of metropolitan France, corresponding almost entirely to the historic County of Nice. Unlike the great majority of French departments, which trace to the 1790 Revolutionary reorganisation, this territory was not French at that date: it remained part of the Kingdom of Sardinia (House of Savoy) until the Treaty of Turin of 24 March 1860, when Victor Emmanuel II ceded the County of Nice to Napoleon III's France in exchange for French support of Italian unification, ratified by an April 1860 plebiscite. The department was then formed from the ceded county (with a small strip around the Col de Tende retained by Sardinia/Italy until a further 1947 transfer). `type: subnational` because the department has always been governed as part of the French state and was never a sovereign polity in its own right after 1860; the row exists purely to carry a reporting/administrative territory far smaller than the national France rows, not to claim separate sovereignty. Its start year (1860) departs from the country convention's 1790 default-start for French departments precisely because, like Savoie and Haute-Savoie (the other 1860-annexation-wave departments already in this table), its own annexation postdates the Revolutionary reorganisation by seven decades, and the routed data begins in 1862, two years after annexation, consistent with the department needing time to be folded into French administrative statistics.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures statistical rows for the unit labelled 'FRL03' / Alpes-Maritimes in the France country dataset (production/trade pipeline unit inventory, admin_name FRL03), a real NUTS3 department with zero prior name or boundary matches in this table. Before this entry, any Alpes-Maritimes-labelled row had no distinct home: the only existing FRA candidates were the national-level rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), each an entire-country total roughly 125-150x the department's own area, which would silently misattribute departmental figures to the whole of France. That the County of Nice/Alpes-Maritimes was a distinct entity before becoming French is confirmed by mainstream political history, not modelling convenience: it was a sovereign Sardinian holding ceded by treaty and plebiscite in 1860, the same annexation event and evidentiary basis already used to justify the sibling FRA-SAV-1860-2025 and FRA-HSAV-1860-2025 rows for the Duchy of Savoy's two departments.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. Neither registered France source (cshapes-europe, cshapes-2.0) carries anything below whole-country level, and the one registered adm1 source (gadm-4.1-adm1) omits France entirely from its locally fetched 81-country subset -- and even fully fetched, GADM adm1 corresponds to French regions, not departments, so it would not carry an Alpes-Maritimes feature either way. No union/difference of existing registered FRA features can produce a department-level shape, since none of them subdivides the national territory below the whole country. The routing decision's `polygon_route: new_source_needed` reflects this correctly: the polygon that would actually serve this entry is a French department-level (adm2) layer -- IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS or a full, untruncated GADM adm2 fetch -- neither of which is currently registered in this repository. `polygon_source` is therefore `none` and the missing source is recorded as an open question rather than declared here.

**Territory description:** The department of Alpes-Maritimes occupies the far south-eastern corner of metropolitan France on the Mediterranean coast, bordering Italy (Piedmont, Liguria) to the east, the principality of Monaco (which it surrounds on three sides), and the Var department to the west; its prefecture is Nice, with Cannes, Antibes, Grasse, and Menton among its other principal towns. It corresponds to the historic County of Nice after the 1860 cession, plus a small further addition (Tende and La Brigue, ~160 km2) transferred from Italy under the 1947 Paris Peace Treaties. The modern department covers approximately **4,299 km2** (INSEE figure for the current department), a figure drawn from its administrative definition, not measured from any geometry attached to this entry -- no geometry is attached, per the polygon status above.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row: before 1860 this territory was the County of Nice under the Kingdom of Sardinia, not carried in this database as a distinct polity, so predecessor is empty rather than pointing at a non-existent Sardinian row. No successor: Alpes-Maritimes remains a live French department, so end_year is open at 2025 (exclusive) with successor empty; its only post-creation boundary change (the 1947 Tende/La Brigue addition) was an enlargement, not a succession, and is noted as an open question above rather than as a new row.

## Sourced claims

- The Treaty of Turin (24 March 1860) ceded the County of Nice from the Kingdom of Sardinia to France, ratified by a plebiscite of the Nicois population in April 1860, after which the ceded territory (minus a small strip retained by Sardinia/Italy around the Col de Tende) was organized as the department of Alpes-Maritimes, per standard accounts of the unification of Italy (e.g. Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Nice, County of').
- The department's area is approximately 4,299 km2 per INSEE's administrative department statistics, with Nice as its prefecture and Cannes, Antibes, and Grasse among its other principal towns; a further ~160 km2 (Tende and La Brigue) was added from Italy under the 1947 Paris Peace Treaties, after which the boundary has been stable.

## Decisions

### d-code-am-not-numeric-insee

**Used subunit abbreviation AM rather than the INSEE numeric code 06**

France's departments carry a two-digit INSEE numeric code (06 for Alpes-Maritimes), and some existing FRA department rows in this table use that numeric form (e.g. FRA-02, FRA-08, FRA-14). But the two most directly comparable precedents -- FRA-SAV-1860-2025 and FRA-HSAV-1860-2025, the sibling departments created by the same 1860 annexation wave -- both use a readable letter abbreviation rather than an INSEE number, and FRA-HSAV's own decisions section explicitly asked that a future Savoie-annexation-era department 'use a parallel convention'. AM (Alpes-Maritimes) follows that instruction rather than introducing a third convention (INSEE-numeric) into the same annexation cluster. This departs from the majority numeric pattern used by the pre-1860, 1790-default departments, but those rows were not created under the same routing decision lineage and a numeric 06 would read inconsistently next to SAV/HSAV in any listing of 1860-annexation departments.

### d-three-container-edges

**Container chain split into three national eras, not the single FRA-1919-2025 the routing decision proposed**

The routing decision's proposed container was FRA-1919-2025 alone, with end_year 2025 -- but that row only starts in 1919, and the department's own span starts in 1860 (see below). Using FRA-1919-2025 as the sole container would leave 1860-1919 (59 years) uncontained, which the containment gate rejects since a container edge cannot fall outside either party's span. Checking the polity table shows three non-overlapping FRA national eras that together tile 1860-2025 exactly: FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, and FRA-1919-2025. FRA-1800-1919 (a fourth national row spanning 1800-1919) was not used because it overlaps in time with the first two edges and would create redundant containment. This is the identical fix already applied on FRA-SAV-1860-2025 and FRA-HSAV-1860-2025, both created from routing decisions with the same single-edge proposal defect.

### d-start-year-1860-not-1862

**Start year set to the 1860 annexation, not the data's 1862 first-observed year**

The routing decision proposes start_year 1860 with span_basis citing the Treaty of Turin, while separately noting the located data begins in 1862. As on the Savoie/Haute-Savoie sibling pages, 1860 (the department's creation date) is used rather than 1862 (when statistical series happen to begin): the two-year gap plausibly reflects the lag in folding a newly annexed territory into national administrative statistics, not a claim that the department didn't exist yet. The polity's span should describe when Alpes-Maritimes existed as a territory, not when a particular data extract starts.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-department

**No French department-level polygon source is registered in this repository**

Alpes-Maritimes is a real INSEE department (code 06) with a fixed boundary since 1860, but no registered source here carries French department-level (adm2) boundaries: cshapes-europe and cshapes-2.0 only carry France at whole-country resolution, and the locally fetched gadm-4.1-adm1 file is truncated to an 81-country subset that excludes France entirely (and is adm1/region level regardless, one tier too coarse for a department). No union or difference of existing registered FRA features can produce a department shape, since none of them subdivide the national territory. The two remedies identified but not executed here are: (1) a full, untruncated fetch of GADM's global adm2 layer, or (2) registering IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product, the French national mapping agency's authoritative department-level layer -- whose licence terms have not been checked. This is the same gap already open on the sibling FRA-SAV-1860-2025 and FRA-HSAV-1860-2025 pages; resolving it once would likely unblock all three plus any other 1860-cluster department rows.

### oq-nice-vs-department-boundary-drift

**Whether the 1860-1947 department boundary matches the present-day one is unverified**

The 1860 Treaty of Turin annexed the County of Nice (with a small strip retained by Sardinia/Italy for military reasons around the Col de Tende) to form the department. In 1947, the Paris Peace Treaties transferred a further small area (Tende and La Brigue communes, roughly 160 km2) from Italy to France, enlarging the department slightly. Any future polygon assigned to this entry needs to state which vintage it represents: a present-day GADM/IGN boundary would silently include Tende/La Brigue for the entire 1860-2025 span, overstating the pre-1947 department by that margin. This has not been quantified here because no polygon is attached yet (see polygon status above), but it should be checked before any future polygon_status: assigned or proxy declaration on this row.

### oq-annexation-date-not-independently-verified

**The 1860 Treaty of Turin / County of Nice creation date is stated from general knowledge, not verified against a file in this repository**

As with the Savoie/Haute-Savoie sibling pages, this entry repeats the 1860 annexation and department-creation date from standard historical knowledge (Treaty of Turin, 24 March 1860, ratified by an April 1860 plebiscite in the County of Nice) rather than from a citation checked against an authoritative French administrative-history source held in this repository. If a future pass finds a different formal creation date for the department itself (as distinct from the annexation treaty date), start_year may need to move.
