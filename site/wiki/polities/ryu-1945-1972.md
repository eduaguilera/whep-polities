# Ryukyu Islands (US Administration)

## Summary

This row tracks the **Ryukyu Islands** under **US administration**,
from Japan's 1945 surrender through to the **reversion to Japan on
15 May 1972**. The period is conventionally divided into:

- **1945–1950:** US Military Government of the Ryukyu Islands (USMGRI)
- **1950–1972:** US Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands (USCAR)

The territory was placed under US control by the San Francisco Peace
Treaty (signed 8 September 1951, in force 28 April 1952), which
designated the US as administering authority under a UN trusteeship
arrangement. The islands were formally kept separate from occupied and
then sovereign Japan — [jpn-1945-1952](jpn-1945-1952.md) and
[jpn-1952-2025](jpn-1952-2025.md) cover the Japanese home islands
without the Ryukyus. Reversion to Japan was effected by the Okinawa
Reversion Agreement, signed 17 June 1971, in force 15 May 1972.

**Why this entry exists.** The FAO 1952 dataset contains 58 rows
labeled "Ryukyu Islands" for years 1937–1951, of which the 1945–1951
rows represent production under US military administration. These rows
cannot route to [jpn-1945-1952](jpn-1945-1952.md) (covers home islands
only, ~377,000 km²) because the Ryukyus were explicitly excluded from
that polity. A distinct WHEP polity is required for the US-administered
period.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** GADM 4.1 administrative level-1 feature `JPN.32_1`
(Okinawa Prefecture, Japan). Used as a **period proxy** for the
Ryukyu Islands under US administration. The islands' geographic extent
was unchanged from the Japanese-prefecture period; the GADM boundary
is spatially identical to the historical territory.

**Area.** ~2,271 km² (Okinawa Prefecture land area). This is the
core Ryukyu chain (Okinawa group, Miyako Islands, Yaeyama Islands).
The Amami Islands (Amami Oshima group) were administered separately
from the main Ryukyus: Amami was returned to Japan in December 1953
under a separate reversion agreement, while the rest of the Ryukyus
remained under USCAR until 1972. The GADM Okinawa Prefecture polygon
excludes Amami (which is administratively in Kagoshima Prefecture today),
so the proxy is appropriate for the post-1953 territory.

**Polygon status: proxy.** Modern GADM Okinawa Prefecture boundary
used for the historical US-administered Ryukyu Islands. No territorial
change of the islands themselves occurred during 1945–1972 (the islands
did not grow or shrink). The proxy is valid; "proxy" rather than
"estimate" reflects that the spatial extent is the same, only the
administrative status changed.

**Relationship to other Japan rows.** The Ryukyu Islands are excluded
from [jpn-1945-1952](jpn-1945-1952.md) (occupied Japan, home islands)
and [jpn-1952-2025](jpn-1952-2025.md) (modern Japan, which explicitly
notes the 1972 Okinawa reversion). This RYU-1945-1972 entry fills the
coverage gap for the islands during the US administration period.

## Key dates

- **1945-06-22:** Battle of Okinawa ends; US military government
  (USMGRI) established.
- **1945-08-15:** Japan's surrender; Ryukyu Islands formally separated
  from Japanese administration.
- **1950-12-15:** USCAR (US Civil Administration of the Ryukyu Islands)
  established, replacing USMGRI.
- **1951-09-08:** San Francisco Peace Treaty signed; Article 3
  designates Ryukyu Islands as US-administered territory with US
  option to seek UN trusteeship.
- **1952-04-28:** San Francisco Peace Treaty in force; Ryukyu Islands
  formally under US trusteeship (GRI — Government of the Ryukyu
  Islands — established).
- **1953-12-25:** Amami Islands returned to Japan; remaining Ryukyus
  continue under USCAR.
- **1972-05-15:** Ryukyu Islands reverted to Japan (Okinawa Reversion).
  End-of-row event.

## Decisions

- **End year 1972** (Okinawa Reversion), not 1952 (San Francisco Peace
  Treaty in force). The administrative and territorial reality of
  US control of the Ryukyus continued until 1972 regardless of the
  change in legal framework (military government -> civil administration
  -> formal trusteeship). WHEP's territory-change rule drives the
  split; the islands remained under US administration throughout,
  so there is no 1952 territorial change.
- **iso3: NA.** During 1945–1972 the Ryukyu Islands had no ISO 3166-1
  assignment; the territory was neither Japan (JPN) nor the US (USA).
  ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 `RYU` is not an active code. `NA` is appropriate.
- **Polygon: GADM 4.1 `JPN.32_1` (proxy).** Same polygon as
  [ryu-1937-1945](ryu-1937-1945.md). The modern Okinawa Prefecture
  boundary is the appropriate proxy; the islands did not change extent
  during the US administration period.
- **Successor set to JPN-1952-2025**, not a separate JPN-1972-2025
  polity. The 1972 reversion is a territorial addition to Japan but
  the main Japan polity row already spans 1952–2025; splitting it at
  1972 would require a rebuild of that entry. The JPN-1952-2025 page
  already notes the 1972 reversion. An open question flags this.

## Open questions

### oq-ryu-amami-1953

The Amami Islands were returned to Japan in December 1953. Strictly,
the USCAR-administered area shrank at that point. If Amami-specific
data exists in FAO or other sources for 1945–1953, a separate Amami
polity would be needed. This row approximates the 1953 territorial
reduction by using the Okinawa Prefecture polygon (which already
excludes Amami); no correction is needed for the proxy polygon, but
the Amami reversion should be noted for data users.

### oq-ryu-jpn-1952-2025-split

Strictly, the Ryukyu Islands' reversion in 1972 made the territory of
[jpn-1952-2025](jpn-1952-2025.md) change at that date. This could
justify splitting JPN-1952-2025 into JPN-1952-1972 (without Ryukyus)
and JPN-1972-2025 (with Ryukyus). For WHEP's purposes, the
polygon_vintage_drift is modest (~2,271 km² on ~378,000 km², less than
1%) and the JPN-1952-2025 page already documents the 1972 reversion.
The split is deferred unless data-magnitude evidence requires it.
