# Guarda (district/NUTS region of Portugal)

## Summary

Guarda is one of the historic mainland districts (distritos) of Portugal, in the country's northeastern interior along the border with Spain, encompassing the Serra da Estrela's eastern slopes and the high Beira plateau. The district was created as part of the 1835 administrative reorganisation that replaced Portugal's old province structure with the distrito system, and it has persisted as an officially recognised sub-national reporting unit ever since, even after districts lost most governing functions when Portugal's 1986 EU accession introduced NUTS statistical regions. This entry is `type: subnational` because Guarda is a component of Portugal, not a sovereign polity: it exists to carry data reported at the district level so it does not compete with the national PRT-1800-2025 row in sovereignty-based matching. Guarda's district capital is the city of Guarda, the highest-altitude city in Portugal, and the district covers the modern municipalities of Guarda, Seia, Gouveia, Trancoso, Sabugal, Pinhel, Almeida, and others along the upper Mondego and Côa river basins.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data reported against the label 'PT_GU' / Guarda as a Portuguese sub-national reporting unit, per the routing decision for unit_id PRT-PTGU: the source reports observed (not scaled/allocated) values for Guarda across 1880-2023 at 96.2% observed, meaning an actual territory called Guarda was measured continuously rather than a NUTS classification invented only in 1986. That data was previously headed toward a create_new/unroutable split treating pre-1986 Guarda as uncovered, which was wrong because Portugal's mainland districts date to the 1835 reform and 'Guarda' as a NUTS region is simply the modern label for the historic Distrito de Guarda -- the same logic already applied to the Aveiro departure recorded in the country convention, where an exact historic-name match overrides the 1986 NUTS-default cutover used for sibling units. No historical trading-polity source (e.g. Federico-Tena) treats Guarda separately, since districts are an internal Portuguese administrative unit rather than an independent customs or trading territory; the confirmation of distinctness rests instead on Portugal's own administrative law creating and continuously maintaining Guarda as a named district since 1835, distinct from its neighbouring districts (Castelo Branco, Coimbra, Viseu, Bragança) and from the country as a whole.
