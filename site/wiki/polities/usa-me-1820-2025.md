# Maine (state of the United States)

## Summary

Maine, one of the fifty states of the United States, admitted to the Union on March 15, 1820 as the free-state counterpart to Missouri under the Missouri Compromise, separated from the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, of which it had been the non-contiguous District of Maine since 1652. This entry is `type: subnational` because it does not compete with the national USA chain for sovereignty: Maine has never been independently sovereign, and the row exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory for statistics tabulated at the state rather than the national level. The span 1820-2025 tracks continuous statehood, with no interruption, through four successive eras of the national USA polity chain (USA-1803-1848 through USA-1959-2025). Unlike some New England or original-colony states, Maine's external boundary was not fully settled at admission: the northeastern frontier with New Brunswick was disputed through the 1838-1839 Aroostook War and only fixed by the 1842 Webster-Ashburton Treaty, a boundary-vintage issue this page flags rather than glosses over.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Maine as a distinct subnational reporting unit within the United States, created by the agent-harness pipeline processing the administrative unit USA-MAINE ("Maine", country USA), following the same systematic state-by-state pattern as sibling entries usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co, usa-ct, usa-de, usa-fl, usa-ga, usa-ia, usa-id, usa-il, usa-in, usa-ks, usa-ky, and usa-la created in this run. No prior polity row existed for Maine at the state level -- the existing USA chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) covers the country as a whole, so any Maine-specific statistics would previously have had no state-level home and would either be absent from the database or folded into national USA (or, before 1820, Massachusetts) totals, a mismatch whenever a source reports Maine separately. The routing decision does not name a specific source, row count, or year range of data driving this creation (unlike the Manchuria exemplar, which cites 20 rows across two named sources); it instead frames this as adding the state as a real, distinct administrative entity with no existing candidate polity, consistent with a systematic pass creating one row per U.S. state. What confirms Maine is a distinct reporting/administrative entity is uncontroversial: it is one of the fifty states, admitted by act of Congress with its own government since 1820, and long used as a state-level reporting unit in economic and demographic series (e.g., USDA, Census Bureau, and Mitchell-style historical compilations). The open question below flags that the specific data motivating this row's creation was not supplied in the routing decision and should be traced.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.20_1`, assigned directly (not a proxy) because this is a 1:1 match between the administrative unit and a GADM admin-1 feature already registered and locally present in this database's GeoPackage. `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Area:** measured from the attached geometry at approximately 91,633 km2 (Maine's commonly cited total area is about 91,633 km2 / 35,385 sq mi, the largest state in New England). This is a measurement of the attached polygon, consistent with Maine's real-world extent, not an independent territorial claim about the 1820-1842 period specifically.

**Territory description:** Maine occupies the northeasternmost corner of the contiguous United States, bordered by New Hampshire to the west, the Canadian provinces of Quebec to the northwest and New Brunswick to the north and east, and the Atlantic Ocean and Gulf of Maine along its long southeastern coastline -- readily locatable on any modern map as the state forming the "top" of New England, east of the White Mountains. Its modern external boundary, however, is a post-1842 artefact along the New Brunswick frontier: the Webster-Ashburton Treaty resolved the Aroostook War dispute by splitting the contested territory roughly in half between Maine and New Brunswick, so the GADM boundary used here should not be treated as valid back to the 1820 admission date without the caveat recorded in the open questions.

## Predecessors and successors

Maine has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database: it enters as a full U.S. state in 1820 via separation from Massachusetts, and per the routing decision its span runs open/current through 2025 with no successor. The pre-1820 District of Maine, a district of Massachusetts since 1652, is not itself represented as a polity row, so no predecessor edge is recorded; see the open question on whether that district should be added given separate pre-1820 reporting.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

Maine is a standard U.S. state with no distinct historical name of its own (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad), so it follows the pattern used by 57 of 66 subnational rows: ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end. The subunit token chosen is ME, the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US code for Maine, matching the two-letter convention already used for the sibling USA states added in this same harness run (usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co, usa-ct, usa-de, usa-fl, usa-ga, usa-ia, usa-id, usa-il, usa-in, usa-ks, usa-ky, usa-la). The full code is USA-ME-1820-2025.

### d-start-year-admission

**Start year is 1820 admission to the Union, per the country default rule**

The country convention's default rule for U.S. states is to start at formal admission to the Union unless the state is a departure case (Alaska, Hawaii, or a territory with a distinct pre-statehood identity already carrying its own polity row). The pre-1820 District of Maine was a district of Massachusetts, not a separately governed territory of the federal government, and is not itself a polity row in this database, so 1820 is used directly rather than back-cast to an earlier colonial period, mirroring the treatment of Alabama Territory in usa-al-1819-2025.

### d-four-container-edges

**Four container edges tile 1820-2025 across the successive national USA rows**

The national USA chain in the polity table is split into five rows by era: USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025. Maine's 1820 start falls inside the second of these, so the container list uses four edges (skipping USA-1800-1803 entirely) to cover the full 1820-2025 span with no gap, each edge trimmed to the intersection of Maine's span and that national row's own span.

## Open questions

### oq-district-of-maine-predecessor

**Should the pre-1820 District of Maine (part of Massachusetts) be represented as a predecessor row?**

Maine existed as the District of Maine, a non-contiguous exclave district of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, from 1652 (Massachusetts Bay Colony purchase of the Gorges claim) through separation in 1820. No polity row in this database currently represents that district separately from Massachusetts's own state-level figures. If Mitchell-style or Census-era statistics report the District of Maine separately from Massachusetts proper prior to 1820, a predecessor entry analogous to Alabama Territory's treatment (left unrepresented per usa-al-1819-2025) would need to be added, and this page's predecessor field revisited.

### oq-vintage-risk-boundary-stability

**Does the GADM present-day boundary match Maine's boundary at admission in 1820, including the 1842 Webster-Ashburton settlement?**

The polygon assigned here (GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.20_1) captures Maine's current boundary. Maine's northern and eastern boundary with New Brunswick was disputed after 1820 (the Aroostook War) and was only settled by the 1842 Webster-Ashburton Treaty, which adjusted the border from earlier claims. This means the modern GADM boundary is not a safe proxy for Maine's territory in the 1820-1842 window without further check, unlike the assumption made for Alabama (usa-al-1819-2025), whose borders were treated as stable since admission with lower boundary risk. No independent verification against an 1820-era map or the treaty's boundary description was done in authoring this page.
