# Madrid (province/region of Spain)

## Summary

Madrid is the Spanish province and, since 1978, coextensive Comunidad Autonoma covering the capital city and its surrounding region in the center of the Iberian Peninsula. It was created as one of Spain's 50 provinces by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833, which replaced the country's historic kingdoms and territories with a uniform grid of provinces for tax collection, justice, and statistical reporting. Madrid province is landlocked, bordered by Castile-and-Leon provinces (Avila, Segovia) to the north and west and by Castile-La Mancha provinces (Guadalajara, Cuenca, Toledo) to the east and south. Unlike most of Spain's autonomous communities, which group several provinces, Madrid is a single-province community: the modern Comunidad de Madrid and the historic province share the same boundary. This entry is type: subnational because Madrid is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right -- Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography that is smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish statistical agencies have tabulated Madrid separately from the national total for over 150 years.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES300, NUTS2 code ES300, "Madrid", country Spain) captures valued statistical rows for the Comunidad de Madrid / Madrid province under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS2/NUTS3 Spanish units ES111 through ES243, all routed to create_new because Spanish provincial/regional statistics have been collected continuously since the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform established provinces as the stable administrative basis for these reporting units. Previously, no polity row represented Madrid specifically: the only candidate in the database was ESP-1800-2025, the national aggregate, which contains Madrid but covers the whole of Spain (roughly 505,000 km2 against Madrid's ~8,028 km2, about 63x larger) and cannot stand in for province-level statistics without collapsing Madrid's own reporting series into the national total. The routing decision found no existing polity by name for this unit. Madrid's administrative distinctness as a reporting geography is confirmed by its status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS2 unit (ES300) and an INE statistical division reporting at province/region granularity since the 1833 reform, the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entry ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña).

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish province numeric code), under which Madrid is conventionally cpro 28 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False (the shapefile has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence has not been checked, so the feature_id cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet. gadm-4.1-adm1 was considered and rejected because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and while Madrid's autonomous community and province boundaries happen to coincide, using gadm-4.1-adm1 was not the route chosen in the routing decision, which specifically named mapspain-ign as the correct registered source for Spanish province-level statistics. Territory description: Madrid province/region occupies the center of the Iberian Peninsula on the northern Meseta Central, dominated by the metropolitan area of the city of Madrid (Spain's capital and largest city) and extending north into the Sierra de Guadarrama mountains. On a modern map it is bounded by Segovia and Avila (Castile and Leon) to the north and west, and by Guadalajara, Cuenca, and Toledo (Castile-La Mancha) to the east and south. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 8,028 km2 -- this is the province/community's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform (the 1978 creation of the Comunidad de Madrid autonomous community simply promoted the existing province boundary to a new administrative tier rather than redrawing it), so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low.

## Predecessors and successors

Madrid has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was part of the Kingdom of Castile's administrative structure, which this database does not currently model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment applied to the sibling entry ESP-CO-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province/region remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish NUTS units (including ESP-CO-1833-2025) of treating 2025 as the open end for still-current entries rather than tying the terminus to any historical event.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Madrid as a NUTS2/NUTS3 statistical unit (ES300) with continuous data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its 1833 creation as one of the Javier de Burgos provinces.
- The routing decision's underlying dataset records valued rows for unit ES300 under the country convention applied to sibling NUTS2 units ES111-ES243, all routed to create_new for the same reason: no existing polity row represents any Spanish province specifically, only the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-md-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation MD rather than a numeric province code**

The dominant subnational code pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, e.g. DZA-CVD-1902-1919, and the sibling entry ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña) already set the house convention for ESP: a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS digits (ES300) or the INE province numeric code (28). Following that precedent exactly, this entry uses MD for Madrid rather than mixing in a numeric scheme. I considered ES300 (the NUTS2 code cited in the routing decision) but rejected it because it would make Madrid the only ESP subnational entry using digits while A Coruña, and presumably future provinces (Lugo -> LU, Pontevedra -> PO), use letters; internal consistency within ESP's own convention outweighs matching the routing decision's NUTS label verbatim.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and its licence is unchecked**

sources.yaml already lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Madrid is conventionally cpro 28 -- but the routing decision could not confirm this because the source file (data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg) is not present locally (present_locally=False) and its licence for redistribution in this project's GeoPackage has not been checked. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=28 feature is confirmed to be Madrid, this entry carries polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry. Whoever acquires the source should also verify there is exactly one feature for Madrid (the province and the modern Comunidad de Madrid autonomous community share the same boundary, unlike most of Spain's other 16 autonomous communities, which are subdivided into multiple provinces -- so no merge/union step should be needed, but this should be checked rather than assumed).

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Madrid province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Madrid province was administered under the old Castilian territorial system (the historic Villa y Tierra de Madrid within the Kingdom of Castile), which does not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and is not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Madrid's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the same unresolved question already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Castilian administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all, and if so at what granularity, is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
