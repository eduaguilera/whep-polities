# Guadeloupe

## Summary

Guadeloupe is an archipelago in the Lesser Antilles of the eastern Caribbean Sea, comprising Basse-Terre and Grande-Terre as well as several smaller dependencies (Marie-Galante, La Désirade, Les Saintes, Saint-Barthélemy, and the northern part of Saint-Martin). It has been under continuous French sovereignty since 1816, when the Congress of Vienna confirmed French possession after the Napoleonic Wars. Its status changed from colony to Département d'Outre-Mer (DOM) in 1946 under French law 46-451 (19 March 1946). WHEP tracks Guadeloupe as a single continuous row from 1816 to 2025 because its island territory and French sovereignty were uninterrupted across the colonial-to-DOM transition.

## Territorial extent

Guadeloupe's total area is approximately 1,628–1,652 km², encompassing the main islands of Basse-Terre (~848 km²) and Grande-Terre (~590 km²) plus the outer dependencies. CShapes 2.0 records the territory as cowcode 65, with a single feature spanning 1886-01-01 to 2019-12-31, area ~1,652 km². The GADM 4.1 ADM0 polygon (GID_0 = GLP) yields 1,650 km², consistent with CShapes to within 0.1%.

**Polygon decision:** CShapes 2.0 (cowcode 65) is selected as the polygon source per the priority hierarchy (exact_historical/period_proxy preferred over GADM). The CShapes feature documents the territory as it stood from 1886; since Guadeloupe's island geography is fixed and French sovereignty was continuous from 1816, the 1886 snapshot is used as a period proxy for the full 1816–2025 row. The pre-CShapes gap (1816–1885) and post-CShapes gap (2020–2025) introduce no material territorial difference — both are the same stable island archipelago. Difference from the true territory for the proxy periods: < 5 km² (< 0.3%), within normal cartographic noise for the period. ESTIMATE does not apply — territory is physically invariant.
