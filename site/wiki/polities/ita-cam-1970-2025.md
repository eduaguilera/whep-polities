# Campania (region of Italy)

## Summary

Campania is one of Italy's 15 ordinary-statute regions (regioni a statuto ordinario), located in southern Italy on the Tyrrhenian coast, centered on the Bay of Naples, with Naples (Napoli) as its capital. This entry is `type: subnational` because Campania never held sovereignty of its own in this period -- Italy held sovereignty throughout 1970-2025 -- the row exists purely to carry a reporting/administrative territory below the national level, the same rationale already documented for the sibling entries ITA-LIG-1970-2025 (Liguria) and ITA-PIE-1970-2025 (Piemonte). Campania became an operative administrative and statistical unit in 1970, when Italy's ordinary-statute regions held their first regional council elections mandated by the 1948 Constitution; before that date regional government existed on paper only and statistics were not compiled at this level, so this row's span begins in 1970 rather than at unification (1861) as the routing decision's own span_basis argued -- see decisions below for why 1861 was rejected in favor of the 1970 convention already established by the two prior sibling entries. The end_year of 2025 is open -- the region remains current. This row is created to receive statistical series reported under Italian administrative unit `ITA-ITF3` (admin_name ITF3, i.e. Campania) in NUTS/GADM-style subnational classifications, which the routing pipeline identified as having no existing polity row (0 name candidates), previously falling to the national ITA rows and thereby folding a distinct regional reporting territory into an all-Italy total.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: this row is created to receive statistical series reported under Italian administrative unit `ITA-ITF3` (admin_name ITF3, i.e. Campania) in NUTS/GADM-style subnational classifications; the routing decision names the unit with no row count given in the decision object itself, flagged for creation because the routing pipeline identified Campania-labelled data with no existing polity representing it (0 name candidates). Previously matched to: all such data was being matched to the national ITA polities (ITA-1919-2025 and its predecessors), which is wrong for any year from 1970 onward because Campania-level reporting is a real, distinct statistical/administrative practice that folding into the national total obscures -- a regional figure folded into a national total cannot be recovered or distinguished from other regions' contributions. What confirms distinctness: Campania is a constitutionally recognized ordinary-statute region of Italy with its own directly elected regional council since 1970, its own regional government (Giunta regionale), and its own budget and statistical reporting apparatus (e.g. Istat, Eurostat under NUTS2 code ITF3), reported separately from national totals -- the same standing already established for Liguria's ITC3 in ITA-LIG-1970-2025 and Piemonte's ITC1 in ITA-PIE-1970-2025, which set the working precedent for how Italy's ordinary-statute regions are entered into this table.

**Territory description:** Campania occupies the western part of southern Italy on the Tyrrhenian Sea, centered on the Bay of Naples, bordered by Lazio to the north, Molise and Puglia to the east/northeast, and Basilicata to the southeast. It comprises five provinces -- Napoli, Caserta, Benevento, Avellino, and Salerno -- and includes the islands of Capri and Ischia. Its present-day administrative area is approximately **13,671 km²**, making it one of the more densely populated but smaller regions of Italy by area relative to its population. This figure is a widely cited present-day administrative-area statistic, not a measurement of any geometry attached to this entry (none is attached yet) -- once the GADM ADM1 feature is fetched, `polygon_area_km2` should be populated from that geometry directly and can be checked against this figure.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-subunit

**Use ITA-CAM-1970-2025, the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern**

57 of 66 existing subnational rows follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. DZA-CVD-1902-1919, ESP-AS-1833-2025). Campania is an ordinary Italian administrative region (NUTS2 ITF3) with no name-of-its-own historical identity distinct from being a region of Italy -- unlike Manchuria or Hyderabad, which earned bespoke codes as historical territories in their own right -- so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. ITA already has two subnational rows created under this pipeline, ITA-LIG-1970-2025 (Liguria) and ITA-PIE-1970-2025 (Piemonte), which set the country's precedent; CAM is the natural three-letter abbreviation for Campania and is consistent with that precedent.

### d-start-year-1970-not-1861

**Start year is 1970, rejecting the routing decision's proposed 1861**

The routing decision for unit ITA-ITF3 proposed start_year 1861, reasoning that Campania was part of the original 1861 Kingdom of Italy and so should inherit the earliest container national row's start_year under a stated 'default start rule'. This entry departs from that proposal and instead uses 1970, following the convention already established (and documented in its own decisions section) by the two prior Italian regional entries created under this same pipeline: ITA-LIG-1970-2025 (Liguria) and ITA-PIE-1970-2025 (Piemonte). Both of those entries rejected an 1861-based span for the identical reason that applies here: Italy's ordinary-statute regions (regioni a statuto ordinario), including Campania, existed only on paper between the 1948 Constitution and 1970, when the first regional council elections were held and regional government and statistical reporting became operative. No region-level Campania data exists, and no distinct regional administrative apparatus existed, before 1970; treating the span as 1861-2025 would misrepresent nearly 110 years during which 'Campania' was not a reporting or governing unit at all, only a geographic label folded into national Italian data. Using 1970 also keeps the three Italian regional entries mutually consistent, which the routing decision's own routing_concerns flagged as a live risk for exactly this reason (inconsistency with sibling units ITC1, ITC3, ITF1, etc.).

### oq-pre1970-campania-data-fate

**What happens to any Campania-labelled data dated before 1970?**

This entry's span begins in 1970 on the grounds that Campania was not an operative statistical/administrative reporting unit before then. If the routing pipeline's underlying data (the unit ITA-ITF3 records referenced in the routing decision) actually includes rows dated before 1970 under a Campania-equivalent label (e.g. a former Kingdom of Naples province grouping, or an earlier compartimento-era classification), those rows have nowhere to go under this entry and would currently fall to whichever national ITA row is current for that year. It is not verified here whether any such pre-1970 rows exist in the source data; if they do, a back_cast segment or a decision to accept the national-total fallback for that period should be made explicitly rather than left implicit, mirroring the routing decision's own routing_concerns note about this exact risk ('if a source shows Campania ... had a distinct earlier or discontinuous history, the span ... should be revisited').

### oq-nuts-boundary-vintage

**Boundary vintage: does a present-day GADM feature match Campania's 1970 extent?**

Campania's five provinces (Napoli, Caserta, Benevento, Avellino, Salerno) have been stable since well before 1970, but Italy has periodically created new provinces by splitting existing ones (e.g. nationally, provinces created in the 1990s-2000s). It has not been verified whether any province-level boundary change within Campania after 1970 would make a present-day GADM ADM1 polygon differ from the region's 1970 extent -- Italian regions themselves (as opposed to their constituent provinces) have not changed boundaries since unification, so the risk is likely low, but this has not been explicitly checked against an authoritative source the way it was for, e.g., Liguria's four-province composition.
