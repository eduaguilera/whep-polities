# Pontevedra (province of Spain)

## Summary

Pontevedra is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the four provinces making up the Galicia autonomous community in the northwestern corner of the Iberian Peninsula. It takes its name from its capital, the city of Pontevedra, though its largest city and economic center is the Atlantic port of Vigo. The province occupies Galicia's southwestern corner, along the Rias Baixas coastline, bordered by A Coruna province to the north, Ourense province to the east, Portugal across the Mino river to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west. This entry is type: subnational because Pontevedra is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Pontevedra separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES114, for over a century and a half. Unlike A Coruna and Madrid, no polity row for Pontevedra existed in the database before this routing decision, so this page both allocates the code ESP-PO-1833-2025 and documents it, following the same convention already established for its two Galician-and-central sibling entries.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES114 ("Pontevedra"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES112 Lugo, ES113 Ourense, and onward through ES243), all following a system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing decision found no existing polity row for Pontevedra -- the three ESP candidates it evaluated were A Coruna (ESP-CO-1833-2025), Madrid (ESP-MD-1833-2025), and national Spain (ESP-1800-2025), none of which IS Pontevedra; routing rows for this unit to the national aggregate would have collapsed a province-level series (~4,495 km2) into a territory roughly 112x larger. What confirms distinctness: Pontevedra's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES114) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entries ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish province numeric code), under which Pontevedra is conventionally cpro 36 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence has not been checked, so the feature_id cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet, exactly as ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 do for the same reason. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and Pontevedra province does not coincide with the Galicia autonomous community boundary (Galicia contains three other provinces: A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense), so an adm1 feature would be the wrong granularity entirely. Territory description: Pontevedra province occupies the southwestern corner of Galicia, on the Rias Baixas ("lower estuaries") Atlantic coast. Its nominal capital is the city of Pontevedra, but its largest city and main economic center is Vigo, one of Spain's principal Atlantic ports and a major fishing and shipbuilding hub; the province also contains the resort town of Sanxenxo and the Cies Islands nature reserve at the mouth of the Vigo estuary. On a modern map it is bounded by A Coruna province to the north, Ourense province to the east, the Mino river and Portugal to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 4,495 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling entries.

## Predecessors and successors

Pontevedra has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the Kingdom of Galicia's historic partido and jurisdiction system (including the old Tuy jurisdiction along the Portuguese border), which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1978, one of the four provinces making up the Galicia autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish NUTS3 units of treating 2025 as the open end for still-current entries.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Pontevedra as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES114) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ES114 records no existing polity row representing Pontevedra specifically -- the three ESP candidates evaluated (A Coruna, Madrid, national Spain) are all distinct territories -- and assigns the default 1833-2025 country-convention span rather than a departure span such as the one used for the Canary Islands split, since Pontevedra is one of the original 1833 provinces rather than a later territorial division.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-pv-abbreviation

**Used a two-letter subunit abbreviation (PO) rather than a NUTS or numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational code pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the two existing ESP subnational rows (ESP-CO-1833-2025 for A Coruna, ESP-MD-1833-2025 for Madrid) both use a short letter abbreviation of the unit rather than the NUTS3 code (ES111, ES130) or the INE province numeric code (15, 28). Pontevedra's own INE province code is 36 and its NUTS3 code is ES114, but following the sibling precedent this entry uses ESP-PO-1833-2025, where PO is Pontevedra's standard Spanish vehicle-plate/province abbreviation (also used informally in INE tables), not a bespoke invention. This is not a departure from the dominant pattern -- it is the same subunit-abbreviation convention already used twice for the same country -- so no new code shape is introduced here.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, so Pontevedra's cpro=36 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Pontevedra is conventionally cpro 36 -- but the routing decision for unit ES114 could not confirm this because the source file is marked present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence for redistribution has not been checked, exactly the same open state recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=36 feature is confirmed to be Pontevedra (and confirmed to match the 1833-2025 province boundary rather than some other vintage), this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Pontevedra, distinct from Galicia's other three provinces (A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense), before assigning it here.

### oq-present-locally-flag-inconsistent

**mapspain-ign is flagged present_locally=False even though two sibling ESP province entries already cite it**

Both ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 already route through mapspain-ign, yet sources.yaml still marks the source as not present locally. Either the on-disk flag is stale (the geopackage was fetched or cached separately when those two entries were routed, and the manifest was never updated), or those two entries are themselves still sitting with unconfirmed geometry despite being routed. This entry inherits the same ambiguity rather than resolving it: it is unclear whether acquiring the source for Pontevedra is a fresh fetch or simply a matter of pointing at a cache that already exists from the A Coruna/Madrid work. This should be resolved once, at the country-convention level, rather than re-investigated per province.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Pontevedra province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Pontevedra province was part of the Kingdom of Galicia's older partido and jurisdiction system (including the historic Tuy and Pontevedra jurisdictions), which does not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and is not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Pontevedra's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Galician administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all remains an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
