# Huelva (province of Spain)

## Summary

Huelva is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today also forms one of the four provinces of the Andalusia autonomous community (with Sevilla, Cadiz and Cordoba to its east), in the far southwest of Spain on the Portuguese border and the Gulf of Cadiz coast. This entry is type: subnational because Huelva is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, distinguishable from it because INE and Eurostat have tabulated Huelva separately at NUTS3 code ES618 since the province's creation. Unlike Asturias or Madrid, Huelva is not coextensive with its autonomous community: Andalusia comprises eight provinces, so the province boundary and the community boundary diverge sharply, and Huelva-specific data cannot be treated as a proxy for all of Andalusia or vice versa.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES618) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES618 ("Huelva"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units, following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Huelva is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units.

Previously matched to: nothing -- no existing polity represented Huelva before this decision (only the national ESP-1800-2025 row and the other already-created Spanish province rows existed for ESP), so the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, collapsing a province-level series into a country-level one, which is rejected for the same reason it was rejected for every other Spanish province row created this session.

What confirms distinctness: Huelva's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES618) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling ESP province rows -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 50x the area of Huelva.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the INE province numeric code), under which Huelva is conventionally cpro 21; the polygon_reasoning flags that sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign as present_locally=False even though several sibling province pages (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra, Huesca) already cite it this session -- that discrepancy is unresolved and is recorded as an open question below rather than assumed away. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP entirely (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, and Huelva is one of eight provinces within Andalusia, so adm1 could not carry province-level attribution), so it was correctly ruled out.

Territory description: Huelva province occupies the far southwestern corner of continental Spain, bordered by Portugal (the Algarve and Alentejo regions) across the Guadiana river to the west, the Gulf of Cadiz (Atlantic coast) to the south, Sevilla province to the east, and Badajoz province (Extremadura) to the north. Its terrain ranges from the Atlantic coastal plain and the wetlands of the Donana National Park in the southeast, through the Condado agricultural district, up to the Sierra de Aracena y Picos de Aroche in the north near the Portuguese and Extremaduran borders. Its capital is the city of Huelva, at the confluence of the Odiel and Tinto rivers; other notable towns include Ayamonte on the Portuguese border and Aracena in the mountainous north. On a modern map its outline matches the current INE/IGN provincial boundary. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 10,148 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is confirmed present and fetched, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low.

## Predecessors and successors

Huelva has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was organized under the Antiguo Regimen partido structure of the Kingdom of Sevilla, not as a bounded province, so predecessor is left empty, matching the treatment given to every other 1833-reform province entry (ESP-AS, ESP-CO, ESP-HU, etc.). It has no successor either: Huelva remains a current, active province and NUTS3 statistical unit of Spain (ES618) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), consistent with the ESP country convention for still-current units.

## Decisions

### d-code-uses-h-not-hu

**Code is ESP-H, not ESP-HU, because ESP-HU is already Huesca**

The dominant pattern for these Spanish province rows is not the INE province code (cpro) but the historical vehicle-registration plate letter(s): ESP-AS=Asturias(?), ESP-CA=Cantabria, ESP-CO=A Coruna, ESP-LU=Lugo, ESP-OR=Ourense, ESP-PO=Pontevedra, ESP-CDZ=Cadiz, ESP-COR=Cordoba, and critically ESP-HU is already taken by Huesca (whose plate code is HU). Huelva's own historical plate code is the single letter H, distinct from Huesca's HU, so this entry uses ESP-H-1833-2025 rather than colliding with or inventing a variant of the already-created ESP-HU row. This departs from a naive assumption that 'Huelva -> HU' would be correct; it is Huesca's code, not Huelva's. Sibling precedent for single-letter plate codes already exists in this same batch: ESP-A (Alicante), ESP-B (Barcelona), ESP-T (Tarragona), ESP-V (Valencia).

## Open questions

### oq-mapspain-ign-present-locally-flag

**mapspain-ign source flagged present_locally=False despite many sibling pages citing it**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning notes that sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign as present_locally=False, yet at least seven sibling ESP province pages created this same session (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra, and now Huesca) already cite mapspain-ign as their intended polygon_source and record polygon_status: unassigned pending that file's confirmed presence. This entry follows the same convention: polygon_status is left as unassigned rather than assigned, and no feature_id is guessed beyond noting Huelva's cpro code for future use. Whether the source is actually present on disk, or whether the sources.yaml flag is stale, needs resolving once for all these provinces rather than page by page -- fixing it here would not by itself validate the other six pages that share the same unresolved flag.

### oq-huelva-boundary-changes-since-1833

**Whether Huelva's provincial boundary has shifted internally since 1833**

This page assumes, per the general 1833 Javier de Burgos reform precedent applied to every sibling province, that Huelva's boundary has been essentially stable since creation and that a present-day IGN polygon will be a low-risk proxy for the full 1833-2025 span. Unlike some provinces, Huelva's eastern boundary with Sevilla province and its coastal/estuary boundary near the Guadiana river (the Portugal frontier) have had minor municipal-level adjustments historically (e.g. disputes over the Ayamonte/Guadiana estuary and a few municipality transfers between Huelva and Sevilla in the 19th century); whether any of these were significant enough to matter at province-level statistical geography, or are sub-province noise below the resolution this row needs to track, has not been checked against a dedicated boundary-history source and is left open.
