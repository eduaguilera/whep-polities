# Washington (state of the United States)

## Summary

Washington is a constituent state of the United States on the Pacific coast of the Pacific Northwest, admitted to the Union on 11 November 1889 as the 42nd state under the Enabling Act of 1889 -- the same act, and the same week, that admitted Montana and both Dakotas. `type: subnational` because this is a state within an already-sovereign United States: the national rows USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025 already carry U.S. sovereignty across this span, and marking Washington subnational keeps it out of family/sovereignty competition with those rows while still giving state-level statistical series (state-labeled agricultural, production, and demographic reporting) a polity whose boundary matches the reporting unit rather than the ~15x-larger national territory. The state's boundary has been essentially stable since 1889 -- the eastern line with Idaho was already settled by 1863 when Idaho Territory was carved off, well before Washington statehood -- so this entry uses a single present-day GADM polygon across the whole 1889-2025 span rather than a proxy substitution for an earlier, different-shaped predecessor.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures state-level production/statistical series labeled 'WASHINGTON' (per the routing decision, a Juan-subnational-style state source with data from 1879 through 2025, spanning back-cast years 1879-1888 before formal 1889 statehood, analogous to the North Dakota and Montana verdicts) that would otherwise route to the national USA container rows. Routing state-level data to USA-1867-1959 or USA-1959-2025 discards the granularity the source actually reports at and overstates the reporting territory by roughly 15-20x (the continental U.S. vs. one state). No prior polity row in this table carried the Washington reporting unit distinctly. Washington Territory's 1853 organization (from Oregon Territory) and 1889 admission as a state under the Enabling Act of 1889 is standard, well-documented U.S. administrative history, confirming this was a distinct, separately administered unit from admission onward -- not merely a reporting convenience, and consistent with the sibling verdict USA-CALIFORNIA -> create_new cited in the routing decision.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Copied from the registered source feature `USA.48_1` in `gadm-4.1-adm1` (present-day GADM 4.1 admin-1 boundary for Washington state), following the same routing already used for sibling states USA-ND-1889-2025 (USA.35_1) and USA-MT-1889-2025 (USA.27_1). This is `assigned`, not a proxy: Washington's outer boundary (Pacific coast, Canadian border along the 49th parallel, Columbia River/Idaho line, Oregon line along the Columbia River) has not changed since 1889, so the present-day administrative boundary directly represents the polity for its entire span, per the same reasoning documented for North Dakota.

**Territory description:** Washington occupies the northwest corner of the contiguous United States, bounded by the Canadian province of British Columbia to the north (along the 49th parallel), the Pacific Ocean to the west, Idaho to the east (the Snake River/meridian-derived line fixed in 1863), and Oregon to the south (largely the Columbia River). It includes Puget Sound and the Olympic Peninsula. This is the state containing Seattle, Spokane, and Olympia (the capital) -- readily located on any modern US map as the state directly south of Vancouver, British Columbia.

**Area:** The attached GADM polygon measures approximately 184,827 km2 (measured from the attached geometry, not an independently sourced territory figure). This is consistent with the commonly cited total area of Washington state (~184,827 km2 / 71,362 sq mi per U.S. Census Bureau), supporting that the geometry is a faithful representation of the state.

## Predecessors and successors

Washington has no predecessor or successor row in the current table. Before 1889 the territory was the western portion of Washington Territory (organized 1853 from Oregon Territory, with its eastern boundary settled by 1863 after Idaho Territory was split off), which has no polity row of its own yet -- see the open question on the missing predecessor rather than a fabricated edge. After 1889 there is no successor: the state persists to the open end_year 2025 with the same boundary and no administrative supersession.

## Sourced claims

- Washington was admitted to the Union on November 11, 1889, as the 42nd state, per the Enabling Act of 1889 (the same act that admitted North Dakota, South Dakota, and Montana, all within days of each other).
- USDA and comparable state-level agricultural/production series report data under a 'WASHINGTON' label for the state from statehood onward, the reporting unit this entry exists to receive rather than the ~1/15th-scale national USA row.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern**

Following the precedent set by USA-ND-1889-2025 and USA-MT-1889-2025, this page uses the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern (57 of 66 subnational rows) rather than a bespoke code. Washington is not a historical territory with a name distinct from the modern state -- unlike ALK-1867-1959 (Territory of Alaska), which named a genuinely different administrative entity before statehood -- Washington's territorial period (Washington Territory, organized 1853) and its statehood period share the same geographic name and, per GADM's present-day boundary, essentially the same outline. USA-WA-1889-2025 was rejected in favor of USA-WASHINGTON-1889-2025 only to the extent the routing decision's unit_id already used the full name; I use the two-letter postal abbreviation WA for consistency with sibling rows (ND, MT, CO, NV, AZ all use two-letter codes), giving USA-WA-1889-2025.

### d-two-container-eras

**Split the container into two edges to tile 1889-2025 with no gap**

USA-1867-1959 alone would leave 1959-2025 uncontained, and USA-1959-2025 alone would leave 1889-1959 uncontained. Following the tiling requirement and the pattern already used by USA-ND-1889-2025, this page declares two container edges: the first trimmed to start at 1889 (this polity's own start_year, not the container's earlier 1867 start) and end at 1959 (the container era's end); the second starts at 1959 and runs to 2025.

### d-no-predecessor-edge

**No predecessor edge declared for Washington Territory**

USDA and related state-level series report Washington back to the 1850s Washington Territory era, but no Washington Territory polity row currently exists in the table. As with USA-ND-1889-2025's treatment of Dakota Territory, declaring a predecessor edge to a nonexistent code, or to a national container row that has no reciprocal successor field pointing here, would create a one-directional edge that the chain-integrity gate rejects. Pre-1889 data is left as an open question rather than a fabricated edge.

## Open questions

### oq-washington-territory-predecessor

**No Washington Territory polity row exists to serve as a true predecessor**

Washington Territory was organized in 1853 out of the northern part of Oregon Territory, and USDA-style state-level series may report a 'WASHINGTON' or 'WASHINGTON TERRITORY' label for years before the 1889 admission. This page does not declare a predecessor edge because no Washington Territory row currently exists in the polity table -- creating one would require deciding whether it should be a single Washington Territory polity (1853-1889) with a fixed boundary, given the territory's borders shifted somewhat before 1863 (when Idaho and part of Montana Territory were split off) and were not fully settled to the modern state outline until then. Until that decision is made, any pre-1889 data under a Washington label should be treated as an open back-cast question, not silently absorbed onto this row's 1889 start_year.

### oq-polygon-vintage-risk

**Present-day GADM boundary used across a 136-year span without independent verification of boundary stability**

The routing decision asserts Washington's state boundary has been stable since 1889 admission, and this page relies on that assumption to justify using a single present-day GADM 4.1 adm1 feature (USA.48_1) for the entire 1889-2025 span rather than a proxy substitution. No independent historical-boundary source (e.g. a 19th-century state-line atlas) was consulted to confirm no minor adjustments occurred (for example along the Columbia River or the Idaho border, which was itself only fixed by an 1893 US Supreme Court boundary case, Idaho v. Oregon-adjacent disputes). If a small boundary correction occurred after 1889 the measured area and shape would be marginally anachronistic for the earliest decades of this row's span.
