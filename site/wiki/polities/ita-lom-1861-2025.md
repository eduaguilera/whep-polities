# Lombardia (region of Italy)

## Summary

Lombardia is one of Italy's twenty first-level administrative regions (regioni), located in the north of the country, bordered by Switzerland to the north, Piedmont to the west, Emilia-Romagna to the south, and Veneto and Trentino-Alto Adige to the east. Its capital is Milan. This entry is `type: subnational` because Lombardia has never been a sovereign state in this period: it entered the Kingdom of Italy at national unification in 1861 (having been ceded by Austria in 1859) and has remained an administrative subdivision of Italy ever since, through the Kingdom, the Fascist period, and the post-1946 Republic. The entry exists to carry a *reporting territory* distinct from the national ITA rows: regional statistical series (agricultural production, population, and similar NUTS2-level data spanning 1861-2023) report Lombardia specifically, at roughly 1/13 the area and with a very different economic profile (Italy's most industrialized and populous region) from national Italy-wide totals, so routing this data to the national ITA-1919-2025 row would misattribute a regional figure to the whole country. No prior polity row represented Lombardia on its own; the data was previously unrouted or would otherwise have fallen to the national chain, which contains but is not this region.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: a regional-level statistical series for Lombardia (NUTS2 code ITC4), spanning 1861-2023, routed under the country convention for Italy's subnational regions (pipelines/agent-harness/state/country_conventions.json). The routing decision does not give an exact row count for this specific unit, but treats it as a standard "region of Italy" case like eleven sibling NUTS2 units already processed (ITC1, ITC3, ITF1, ITF3-ITF6, ITG1-ITG2, ITH1, ITH3). Previously, absent a polity row for Lombardia, this data had no distinct home other than the national ITA rows (ITA-1861-1866 through ITA-1919-2025) — which is wrong because those rows represent the whole of Italy (roughly 13x Lombardia's area at any given date) and mixing a regional series into a national row would either overstate national totals if double-counted, or misattribute a distinctly regional figure (e.g. Lombardia's agricultural output or population) to the nation as a whole. What confirms Lombardia is a distinct reporting entity rather than an artefact of routing convenience: it is Eurostat's own standard NUTS2 statistical division of Italy, used by that publisher specifically because Italy's own official statistics (ISTAT) report at the regional level as a first-class unit, separate from national totals — the same reasoning applied to its eleven already-created NUTS2 siblings.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The only registered source type that digitises country subdivisions at Lombardia's level (ADM1/NUTS2) is `gadm-4.1-adm1`, but the copy of that source present in this repository is an explicit curated subset of 81 countries that does not include Italy at all (zero ITA features). No other registered source (cshapes, cshapes-europe, paine-2024, cliopatria, histogis-1860-habsburg, constructed, reporting-areas) covers modern Italian regional boundaries. A correct polygon almost certainly exists — either in GADM's full global release (a superset of the local subset) or in ISTAT's official regional cartography — but neither has been fetched or licence-checked here, so `polygon_source` is `none` and `polygon_status` is `unassigned` rather than a placeholder route name. See open question oq-polygon-source-unresolved.

**Territory description:** Lombardia covers approximately 23,864 km2 in northern Italy, making it about 7.9% of Italy's total land area. It is bounded by Lake Maggiore and the Swiss border to the north, the Po river forming much of its southern edge, and includes the cities of Milan, Bergamo, Brescia, Como, Cremona, Mantua, Pavia, and Sondrio. A modern-map reader can locate it as the region containing Milan, immediately south of the Alps and Switzerland's Ticino canton, and immediately west of Lake Garda. The region's own boundary has been stable since unification in 1861: administrative reorganizations in this period (e.g., the 1990s creation of the province of Lecco, or 2004 creation of Monza e Brianza) redrew *provincial* boundaries within Lombardia, not the region's own outer boundary, so a single present-day regional outline should be a reasonably safe proxy back to 1861 once a source is fetched — a vintage-drift caveat that belongs on that future polygon assignment, not a reason to fabricate one now.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: this is the first polity row created for Lombardia specifically, and it starts exactly at the region's incorporation into the unified Kingdom of Italy in 1861, so there is no distinct prior polity to chain from. No successor: the end_year of 2025 is open, matching the still-current status of Lombardia as a first-level administrative region of the Italian Republic; if a future administrative reorganization abolished or redrew Lombardia, a successor row would be added then. The four container edges (ITA-1861-1866, ITA-1866-1870, ITA-1870-1919, ITA-1919-2025) tile the whole span without gap or overlap, reflecting that Lombardia's own territory was unaffected by any of the national-level changes (Veneto 1866, Rome 1870, WWI settlement 1919) that motivate those national row boundaries.

## Sourced claims

- Lombardia is one of Italy's twenty regioni and corresponds to the Eurostat NUTS2 statistical unit ITC4, per the routing decision's admin_name field.
- The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in 1861 with Lombardy already part of it (annexed from Austrian rule in 1859, formalized at unification), which is why the region's polity span begins in 1861 rather than at a later national milestone like 1866 (Veneto) or 1870 (Rome).
- Italy's NUTS2 boundaries, including Lombardia's, have been redrawn at the province level in various reforms (e.g. the 1990s-2000s creation/abolition of some provinces), but the region-level (NUTS2) outline of Lombardia itself has not changed since unification.

## Decisions

### d-code-subunit-lom

**Code uses ITA-LOM, not the source's NUTS id ITC4**

The routing decision names this unit by its Eurostat NUTS2 code ITC4, but NUTS codes are a statistical classification, not a name a reader of this wiki would recognize, and they have been reassigned across NUTS revisions (Lombardia has carried ITC4 since the 2010 revision but different strings before it). Following the dominant 57-of-66 pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, the subunit token is set to LOM, a readable abbreviation of the region's own name, matching how other ISO3-SUBUNIT codes in the table use the unit's own name or a standard abbreviation (e.g. ESP-CA for Cataluña) rather than a codelist id. iso3 stays ITA per the dominant pattern; polity_code is therefore ITA-LOM-1861-2025.

### d-start-year-1861

**start_year set to 1861 per the country's default rule, contradicting some ITA sibling verdicts**

The routing verdict flags that sibling Italian regions (ITC1, ITC3, ITF1, ITF3, ITF4, ITF5, ITF6, ITG1, ITG2, ITH1, ITH3) were recorded with segmented pre-1970 coverage or marked unroutable rather than given a single 1861 start, even though the country convention's stated default rule would set every region's start at 1861 unless it is one of the two enumerated departures (Trentino-Alto Adige/Friuli-Venezia Giulia at 1919, Molise at 1963). Lombardia is not one of those departures, its data (1861-2023) fits entirely inside 1861-2025, and there is no dramatic territorial change to segment around (Lombardia's borders have been stable since unification), so this entry follows the convention text as written. The conflict with sibling precedent is left as an open question below rather than resolved here, since resolving it would mean re-litigating verdicts already banked for other regions.

### d-container-tiling

**Container edges use the four fine-grained ITA eras, not the coarse ITA-1861-1919 aggregate**

The polity table carries both a fine partition of the Kingdom of Italy (ITA-1861-1866, ITA-1866-1870, ITA-1870-1919) and a coarse aggregate row (ITA-1861-1919) covering the same span. Using the aggregate would double-count the interval against the fine rows if both are ever queried together, and the routing verdict itself pointed at ITA-1861-1866 specifically as the basis for the 1861 start. The four container edges here (1861-1866, 1866-1870, 1870-1919, 1919-2025) tile 1861-2025 with no gap and no overlap using the finer, non-aggregate rows.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-unresolved

**No registered polygon source actually covers Lombardia yet**

The locally present gadm-4.1-adm1 file is an explicit 81-country subset that excludes Italy entirely (0 features for ITA), and no other registered source in sources.yaml digitises Italian ADM1 boundaries; cshapes/cshapes-europe are country-level only. Two real candidates exist but neither has been fetched or licence-checked: (1) GADM's full global release (non-subset), which would need re-fetching since the local copy was deliberately curated to 81 countries; (2) ISTAT's official regional boundary shapefiles (Confini amministrativi), which would need translation from ISTAT's projection/format into the repository's polygon pipeline. Until one is fetched, polygon_source stays none and polygon_status stays unassigned; whichever is chosen should also settle whether it needs different vintages for pre- and post-1970s provincial reorganizations within Lombardia, or whether the modern regional outline is stable enough to back-cast to 1861 with a single vintage-drift note.

### oq-sibling-precedent-conflict

**This verdict's 1861 start conflicts with how sibling Italian regions were routed**

Per decision d-start-year-1861, at least eleven other Italian NUTS2 regions (ITC1, ITC3, ITF1, ITF3, ITF4, ITF5, ITF6, ITG1, ITG2, ITH1, ITH3) were previously routed with segmented coverage tying pre-1970 years to the national ITA rows, or marked unroutable, rather than given their own polity row from 1861 as this entry does for Lombardia. Either those sibling verdicts predate a later revision of the country convention (in which case they should be re-run against the current convention text and likely gain their own 1861-start pages), or the convention text was intended more narrowly than its literal wording and this entry is the outlier that should instead have been segmented. This should be checked against pipelines/agent-harness/state/country_conventions.json's history/versioning for ITA before more Italian regions are processed, since it determines whether ~11 more pages need to be created or this one needs to be re-scoped.

### oq-data-coverage-vs-span

**Underlying data covers 1861-2023, not 1861-2025**

The routing verdict states the routed data spans 1861-2023, but end_year is set to 2025 (open/current) matching the container's own open end. It is not yet confirmed whether 2024-2025 will ever be populated for this unit or whether the 2023 cutoff is a permanent feature of the source (e.g. an annual regional-statistics series with a two-year publication lag). If the source is confirmed to have a structural lag rather than a temporary gap, that should be documented here rather than left implicit in an open end_year that suggests ongoing coverage.
