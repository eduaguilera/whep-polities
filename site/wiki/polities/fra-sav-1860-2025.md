# Savoie (department)

## Summary

Savoie is a French department in the eastern Alps, corresponding to the northern part of the historic Duchy of Savoy. Unlike the great majority of metropolitan French departments, which trace to the 1790 Revolutionary reorganisation, Savoie was not French territory at that date: it remained part of the Kingdom of Sardinia (House of Savoy) until the Treaty of Turin of 24 March 1860, when Victor Emmanuel II ceded Savoy to Napoleon III's France in exchange for French support in the unification of Italy, ratified by a regional plebiscite in April 1860. The historic duchy was then split into two French departments, Savoie (capital Chambery) and Haute-Savoie (capital Annecy); this entry covers Savoie only. `type: subnational` because the department has always been governed as part of the French state, never a sovereign polity in its own right; the row exists purely to carry a reporting/administrative territory smaller than the national France rows, not to claim separate sovereignty. Its start year (1860) departs from the country convention's 1790 default-start for French departments precisely because Savoie's own annexation history postdates the Revolutionary reorganisation by seven decades, and the data motivating this entry begins in 1862, two years after annexation, consistent with the department needing time to be folded into French administrative statistics.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures statistical rows for a unit labelled 'FRK27' / 'Savoie' in the France country dataset (source: production/trade pipeline unit inventory, admin_name FRK27), with data beginning in 1862. Before this entry, rows for this unit had no distinct home: the routing pipeline found no existing FRA subnational polity by name, so the only available candidates were the four national-level FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), each of which is an entire-country total rather than a single department, differing from each other only in whether Alsace-Lorraine is included. Matching Savoie-level department data to any of those national totals would silently misattribute a departmental figure to the whole of France, inflating or diluting per-capita and territorial statistics for the department by roughly two orders of magnitude relative to national area. That Savoie was a distinct entity before becoming French is confirmed by mainstream political history, not merely a modelling convenience: it was a sovereign holding of the House of Savoy and part of the Kingdom of Sardinia until the 1860 Treaty of Turin, at which point it was partitioned into the Savoie and Haute-Savoie departments — a partition itself evidence that 'Savoie' pre-1860 and 'Savoie' the French department are related but administratively distinct units.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. Neither registered France source (cshapes-europe, cshapes-2.0) carries anything below country level, and the one registered adm1 source (gadm-4.1-adm1) omits France entirely from its locally fetched subset — and even if fetched, GADM adm1 for France corresponds to regions, not departments, so it would not carry a Savoie feature either way. No union/difference of existing registered FRA features can produce a department-level shape, since none of them subdivides the national territory. `polygon_route: new_source_needed`: the polygon that would actually serve this entry is a French department-level layer — IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS or GADM's global adm2 tier — which is not currently registered in this repository for France. `polygon_source` is therefore left as `none` and the missing source is recorded as an open question rather than declared here.

**Territory description:** The department of Savoie occupies the northern Graian and Vanoise Alps of eastern France, bordering Italy to the east and the Haute-Savoie department to the north; its administrative seat is Chambery. It corresponds to the northern arrondissements of the historic Duchy of Savoy after the 1860 partition (the southern arrondissements became Haute-Savoie). The modern department covers approximately 6,028 km2 (INSEE figure for the current department of Savoie), a boundary that has been stable since the 1860 partition with no subsequent territorial changes, so vintage risk for a future department-level polygon would be low despite the long span this entry covers (1860-2025).

## Sourced claims

- The Treaty of Turin (24 March 1860) transferred Savoy from the Kingdom of Sardinia to France, ratified by a plebiscite in April 1860, per standard accounts of the unification of Italy (e.g. Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Savoy').
- INSEE lists the department of Savoie's area as approximately 6,028 km2, and records its creation (alongside Haute-Savoie) from the 1860 partition of the annexed Duchy of Savoy.

## Decisions

### d-departed-from-1790-default-start

**Start year set to 1860 (annexation), departing from the 1790 department-start convention**

The country convention's default rule starts French departments in 1790, matching the Revolutionary administrative reorganisation. Savoie is not one of the original 1790 departments and is not among the convention's four listed departures (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, Corsica split, overseas departements). Its own history is decisive here: it remained part of the Kingdom of Sardinia until the Treaty of Turin ceded it to France on 24 March 1860. Using 1790 would assert Savoie was French seventy years before it was, and would leave a stretch of the code's span (1790-1860) with no meaningful French administrative reality to describe. 1860 is used instead, which also lines up closely with the routed data's own start of 1862 — a two-year gap plausibly reflecting the time needed to fold a newly annexed territory into French national statistical series.

### d-three-container-edges-not-one

**Container chain split into three edges to tile 1860-2025 without gaps**

The routing decision's proposal listed only FRA-1919-2025 as the container, with a flagged concern that earlier years might need a different parent. Checking the polity table confirms this: FRA-1919-2025 only starts in 1919, and using it alone would leave 1860-1919 (59 years) uncontained, which the containment gate rejects since an edge cannot fall outside either party's span. Instead this page declares three edges — FRA-1800-1871 for 1860-1871, FRA-1871-1919 for 1871-1919, and FRA-1919-2025 for 1919-2025 — which exactly tile the full 1860-2025 span using the three non-overlapping national eras present in the table. FRA-1800-1919 (the fourth national row, 1800-1919) was not used because it overlaps in time with FRA-1800-1871 and FRA-1871-1919, and using it alongside them would create redundant, overlapping containment rather than a clean tiling.

## Open questions

### oq-department-polygon-source

**No department-level polygon source is registered for France**

This entry needs a France department-level (adm2) boundary layer to assign a real polygon, and none is currently registered in this repository. IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product is the standard authoritative digitisation of French department boundaries and would be the natural first candidate; GADM's global adm2 layer (distinct from the locally fetched gadm-4.1-adm1 subset, which is adm1/region level and also happens to omit France) is a fallback if licensing on IGN data is a blocker. Until one of these is registered and fetched, this entry and any other French department-level entries will remain `polygon_status: unassigned`. Licensing terms for IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS have not been checked.

### oq-pre1860-savoy-not-modeled

**Pre-1860 Savoy (Kingdom of Sardinia era) has no corresponding polity row**

This entry starts at 1860 because that is when Savoy became French, but the territory obviously existed and was administered (as part of the Kingdom of Sardinia) before that date. If production/trade or other datasets ever carry pre-1860 statistics labelled by Savoy or by the Duchy of Savoy specifically, they currently have no home in this table — the Kingdom of Sardinia is not represented here as covering this specific sub-territory. Whether such a predecessor row is worth creating depends on whether any dataset actually reports at that sub-national level for the pre-annexation period; none has been identified during this pass.

### oq-haute-savoie-companion-entry

**Haute-Savoie, the sibling department from the same 1860 partition, is not yet a row**

The Duchy of Savoy was split into Savoie and Haute-Savoie in 1860, both undergoing the identical annexation history and administrative transition. If the France unit inventory contains a distinct Haute-Savoie unit (as opposed to it being folded into a different aggregate), it would warrant a directly analogous entry with the same 1860 start year and the same container chain and polygon gap. This has not been checked in this pass; if a FRK-code for Haute-Savoie exists in the same inventory that produced FRK27, it should be routed consistently with this entry rather than independently re-deriving the 1860 start rationale.
