# Goiás

## Summary

Goiás is a landlocked Brazilian state on the central plateau (Planalto Central), one of the original captaincies/provinces of Portuguese and Imperial Brazil, whose territory once included what is now Tocantins to the north (split off as a separate state only in 1988) and the enclave that became the Federal District (Brasília, carved out in 1960). This entry covers Goiás from 1889, when the Proclamation of the Republic converted the Imperial province of Goiás into a state of the federation, to the present. `type: subnational` is used because Goiás has never been a sovereign polity: it was a captaincy and then a province under Portuguese and Imperial rule, and has been a first-order administrative division (state) of the Brazilian federation continuously since 1889. Goiás is not among the six listed BRA departures from the default rule (Acre, Mato Grosso do Sul, Rondônia, Amapá, Roraima, Tocantins), so the country convention's default republic-founding start year of 1889 applies, with an open end_year of 2025 since Goiás remains a current state. Tocantins itself IS one of the listed departures and gets its own polity row once created — this entry does not attempt to split its own span at 1988, since the routing decision gave no basis for that and the sibling verdicts (BRA-BAHIA, BRA-ALAGOAS) establish the pattern of a single unbroken 1889-2025 span even where the modern boundary is smaller than the historical one.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision this page implements (unit_id BRA-GOIAS) found no existing polity in the database representing Goiás itself: the only BRA rows are three national totals (BRA-1800-1903, BRA-1903-1909, BRA-1909-2025), none of which is state-scale. Source rows describing Goiás specifically — a state-level Brazilian subnational dataset, following the same pattern already established for sibling verdicts BRA-BAHIA, BRA-ALAGOAS and BRA-AMAZONAS — had previously nowhere correct to route except the national BRA-1909-2025 row, a container many times Goiás's own territory, which would misattribute state-level agricultural/production data to the whole country. The routing decision's own text ("Data 1900-1888(within republic era)-2023 is covered... route all observed years within the new polity's span (1889-2025) as matched") indicates the source spans roughly 1900-2023, though it gives no exact row count. What confirms Goiás is a distinct reporting unit rather than folded into national Brazil figures is the practice documented for the sibling entries: the source reports Goiás as a named first-order administrative unit (a Brazilian state), consistent with IBGE's own state-level statistical reporting since the founding of the Republic. This entry is one of a batch of Brazilian state-level polities created under the same default start-year rule, with no departure listed for Goiás in the country convention.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct source is `geobr-ibge` (Brazilian state boundaries, id_column `abbrev_state`), registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` specifically to carry Brazilian ADM1 units, but its file (`data/geodata/geobr-ibge/states.gpkg`) is not present locally, per the routing decision's own polygon_reasoning. `gadm-4.1-adm1` excludes Brazil from its locally curated subset, and the only BRA source currently present, `cshapes-2.0`, digitises Brazil at whole-country scale only. Once `geobr-ibge` is fetched, this entry should match `abbrev_state='GO'`. **The modern boundary is a materially incomplete proxy for most of this span**: present-day Goiás covers approximately 340,100 km2, but until 1988 its territory also included the area that became the state of Tocantins (roughly 278,000 km2), so pre-1988 Goiás was closer to 620,000 km2 combined — nearly twice the modern state's area — and a small triangular area was separately excised in 1960 to form the Federal District (Brasília, ~5,800 km2). Modern Goiás sits entirely inland on the central plateau, bordered by Tocantins to the north, Bahia and Minas Gerais to the east, Mato Grosso do Sul and Minas Gerais to the south, and Mato Grosso to the west; its capital is Goiânia (founded 1933, replacing the old colonial capital Goiás Velho/Vila Boa). No `polygon_area_km2` is recorded here since no geometry is yet attached.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row is created by this decision: Goiás's status as a first-order Brazilian administrative unit runs continuously back to 1889 with no sovereignty break, so no separate pre-1889 provincial entry is proposed (the pre-1889 Imperial province is left to the national BRA-1800-1903 row, consistent with the default convention). This entry has no successor row of its own — Goiás remains a Brazilian state to the present, so the span runs to 2025 as still-current — but Tocantins (split off 1988) and the Federal District (carved out 1960) are territorial descendants of undivided Goiás that, once created as their own polities, will need predecessor/successor or container links back to this entry; this page does not create those links since the routing decision provides no basis for it.

## Sourced claims

- The Proclamation of the Republic (15 November 1889) converted the Imperial provinces, including Goiás — one of Brazil's original captaincies/provinces dating to 18th-century Portuguese colonial administration — into states of the new federation, the event that fixes this entry's 1889 start under the country convention's default rule.
- The Federal District (Brasília) was carved out of Goiás's territory in 1960 when the national capital moved there, and the state of Tocantins was split off from northern Goiás by the 1988 Constitution, both reducing modern Goiás to roughly half its pre-1960 undivided area — a fact relevant to reading any 20th-century territorial figures against this entry's proxy polygon.

## Decisions

### d-code-iso3-subunit-start-end-pattern

**Use the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> code pattern, not a bespoke code**

Goiás is a first-order Brazilian state, structurally identical to the 57 of 66 subnational rows in the table that follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919), including the sibling BRA states already created this session (BRA-BAHIA-1889-2025, BRA-ALAGOAS-1889-2025, BRA-AMAZONAS-1889-2025, BRA-CE-1889-2025, BRA-ES-1889-2025). It is not a historical territory with a name and identity distinct from its status as an administrative division, so it does not qualify for bespoke-code treatment like ALK-1867-1959 or HYD-1724-1948. BRA-1909-2025 is the container across almost the entire span, consistent with all sibling BRA-state entries.

### d-single-span-despite-1988-partition

**One unbroken 1889-2025 span, not split at the 1988 Tocantins partition**

Goiás lost roughly 45% of its undivided territory when Tocantins was split off in 1988, a change at least as dramatic as some cases the spec's proxy rules flag for rejection (e.g. post-Trianon Hungary, post-1918 Poland). Despite that, this entry does not split into pre-1988/post-1988 rows, because: (1) the routing decision gives no basis for a mid-span split and explicitly instructs routing all observed years 1889-2025 to a single new polity; (2) the sibling verdicts (BRA-BAHIA et al.) establish an unbroken single-span pattern for BRA states even where boundaries changed; (3) Tocantins is itself one of the six listed BRA departures and will receive its own polity row with its own start year, at which point the territorial overlap/transfer can be handled via container edges or predecessor/successor links on that entry rather than by fragmenting this one. The territorial discontinuity is instead documented plainly in Territorial extent so a future polygon-fetch step does not treat the modern geobr-ibge boundary as valid back to 1889 without qualification.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1988-boundary-proxy-validity

**Is the modern geobr-ibge Goiás boundary a valid proxy before 1988, or does it need a constructed pre-partition polygon?**

Once geobr-ibge is fetched, the default action would be to match `abbrev_state='GO'` for the whole 1889-2025 span. But that modern boundary excludes the ~278,000 km2 that became Tocantins in 1988, so for 1889-1988 (the majority of this entry's span) it under-covers Goiás's actual historical territory by nearly half. A constructed union polygon (modern Goiás + modern Tocantins, analogous to how MAN-1932-1945 unions three modern Chinese provinces) may be more correct for the pre-1988 portion, via `constructed/build.py`. This needs a decision before any pre-1988 data routed here is treated as territorially accurate, and before `polygon_status` is upgraded from `unassigned`.

### oq-federal-district-1960-carveout-not-modeled

**Does the 1960 Federal District carve-out need its own container/predecessor edge on this entry?**

Brasília and the Federal District were excised from Goiás in 1960, a smaller but still real territorial loss (~5,800 km2) that this entry's single container chain (BRA-1800-1903 / BRA-1903-1909 / BRA-1909-2025) does not distinguish from the larger 1988 Tocantins change. If a BRA-DF polity row is created (wiki/polities/bra-df-1960-2023.md is already present in this working tree per git status), it may be worth adding an explicit note or link between the two entries so a reader does not assume Goiás's reporting territory was constant 1889-2025 without the DF excision. Left unresolved here since it depends on how that DF entry itself frames the split.

### oq-underlying-source-row-count-unknown

**Exact row count and year coverage of the source data routed to this entry is not stated in the routing decision**

The routing decision states the data span is roughly 1900-2023 ("Data 1900-1888(within republic era)-2023 is covered") but gives no row count, unlike some sibling verdicts. Before this entry's `last_ingest`/`sources` metadata is finalized, the actual source file and row count for Goiás-labeled subnational data should be confirmed against the ingest pipeline (likely the same `juan-subnational` dataset used for BRA-BAHIA and other BRA-state siblings), since a wiki page asserting specific years without checking the source risks the kind of unverified pin flagged elsewhere in this project's history.
