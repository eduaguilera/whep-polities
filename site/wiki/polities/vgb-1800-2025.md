# British Virgin Islands

## Summary

The British Virgin Islands (BVI) is an archipelago of approximately 60 islands, islets, and cays in the northeastern Caribbean Sea, east of Puerto Rico and the United States Virgin Islands. The principal island is Tortola (~56 km²); the total land area is approximately 153 km². Britain established a permanent settlement in 1672 and the islands have remained under continuous British administration ever since. WHEP tracks BVI as a single continuous row VGB-1800-2025, covering the period from 1800 through the present. The islands were administered under the Leeward Islands federation (1871-1956), became a separate Crown Colony in 1956, and remain a British Overseas Territory (BOT) as of 2025. BVI has never become independent and has no COW state-system entry.

The WHEP row is created to absorb IIA data for 'british virgin islands' covering 1909-1925 (29 rows of cotton lint and cotton seed data). The territory is a distinct statistical unit, approximately 153 km², unambiguously separate from the broader British West Indies (~600k km²) aggregate.

## Administrative History

- **1672:** Britain establishes a permanent settlement on Tortola; islands come under British Crown sovereignty.
- **1800-1871:** Administered as a separate British Crown Colony with its own presidency.
- **1871-1956:** Part of the Leeward Islands federation, grouped with Antigua, St. Kitts-Nevis-Anguilla, Dominica, and Montserrat under a single governor; BVI retained its own legislature and administrative identity throughout.
- **1956:** Leeward Islands federation dissolved; BVI becomes a separate Crown Colony.
- **1967:** New constitution granting greater internal self-government.
- **2007:** New constitution providing for further autonomy (Premier replaces Chief Minister).
- **2025:** Remains a British Overseas Territory (BOT); never became independent.
