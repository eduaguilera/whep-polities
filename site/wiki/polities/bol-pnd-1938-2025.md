# Pando (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

Pando is one of Bolivia's nine departments, occupying the country's northernmost Amazonian lowlands on the borders with Brazil and Peru. It is the youngest and least populous Bolivian department, created in 1938 out of territory previously administered directly from La Paz as part of the country's northern Amazonian holdings (the same rubber-boom frontier region that also produced the Acre dispute with Brazil decades earlier). This entry is `type: subnational` because Pando is a first-order administrative division of a sovereign state (Bolivia), not an independent polity — it exists in this database because at least one production/trade data source tabulates statistics at the department level and Pando's territory is not equivalent to any existing polity row: the only candidates that could have absorbed this data are Bolivia's national rows, which contain Pando but are roughly twelve times its size and would misattribute department-level statistics to the whole country. Its span starts in 1938, the year of its administrative creation, rather than at Bolivia's 1825/1826 independence date used as the default department start elsewhere in this database, because no department named or bounded as Pando existed before that year.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: a production/trade dataset (per the routing decision that created this page) carries rows labeled for the Bolivian unit "Pando" spanning roughly 1900-2023, at department granularity, distinct from Bolivia's national totals. Previously, absent a Pando-specific row, this data had no correctly-scoped destination: the only existing candidates in the polity table are Bolivia's national entries, which are approximately 12x Pando's territory (Bolivia is ~1,098,581 km2; Pando is ~63,827 km2), so routing department-level Pando statistics to a national row would silently misrepresent their territorial scope to any downstream user. Historical confirmation of distinctness: Pando is one of Bolivia's official first-order administrative departments (departamento), created by law in 1938, with its own capital (Cobija) and continuous separate administrative existence since — it is listed as a distinct unit in Bolivia's own administrative structure and in any modern gazetteer or the INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadística de Bolivia), which is the same government body that would be the ultimate source of department-level Bolivian statistics feeding a dataset like this one. This satisfies the "explicitly listed departure from the default start rule" the routing decision calls for: rather than defaulting to 1825/1826, the entry's start_year is anchored to Pando's actual 1938 creation, with pre-creation data left for separate attribution (see open questions).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is currently available in this repository's GeoPackage for Pando specifically: the registered source `gadm-4.1-adm1` is the right source in principle (GADM publishes a global admin-1 layer including Bolivian departments), but the locally-fetched copy in this repo's `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/` directory was pre-filtered to a curated 81-country subset that does not include Bolivia at all (zero BOL features on disk). `polygon_status: unassigned` and `polygon_feature_id: null` reflect that the source is registered and appropriate but not yet fetched for this country, per the `registered_source_unfetched` route — this is not a case of no suitable source existing, but of the present local dataset lacking the country. Cshapes-2.0, the other geodata source already integrated here, only carries Bolivia at the country level (4 coarse national-boundary rows spanning 1825-2025) and has no sub-national department geometry, so it cannot substitute.

**Territory description:** Pando occupies Bolivia's extreme north, a department of roughly 63,827 km2 (about 5.8% of Bolivia's national territory) bordering the Brazilian state of Acre to the north and east and Peru's Madre de Dios region to the west, with the Beni department to its south. Its capital is Cobija, on the Acre River at the Brazilian border. The department is low-lying Amazonian rainforest, drained by the Madre de Dios, Orthon, and Tahuamanu rivers, and remains Bolivia's least populated department. Its 1938 creation split off territory that, together with neighboring areas, had been at the center of the early-20th-century Acre rubber boom and the associated 1899-1903 Acre dispute settled by the Treaty of Petrópolis, after which Bolivia reorganized its residual northern Amazonian holdings; Pando was the last of Bolivia's departments to be created out of that reorganization.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity rows are declared. Before 1938, Pando's territory was part of Bolivia's national holdings administered directly from La Paz (not yet a department in its own right) — there is no existing polity row in this table that represents that pre-1938 administrative status specifically, which is why `predecessor` is left empty rather than pointing at a national BOL row that is not territorially equivalent; this gap is the subject of the open pre-1938 data-attribution question below. Pando has had no successor changes since 1938 and remains an active Bolivian department through the present, so `successor` is empty and `end_year: 2025` follows this database's convention of an exclusive end date for still-current polities.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Used <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> rather than a bespoke code**

Chose BOL-PND-1938-2025 over a bespoke historical-territory code because Pando is an ordinary, currently-existing first-order administrative department of Bolivia with no separate historical identity of its own (unlike bespoke cases such as Alaska Territory or Hawaii, which named genuinely distinct historical polities). This follows the dominant pattern used by 338 of 351 subnational rows in the table, including the existing sibling BOL-BEN-1842-2025 for Beni. The three-letter subunit code PND was chosen as a short, unambiguous abbreviation for Pando, following the style of the existing BOL-BEN code (first three letters of the department name).

### d-start-year-1938-not-default

**Start year set to 1938 (department creation) rather than the 1825/1826 default**

The routing decision explicitly flags this as a departure from the default Bolivian department start rule (1825/1826, Bolivia's independence). Pando did not exist as an administrative department until 1938, so backdating its span to independence would assert continuity that has no administrative basis. This is consistent with the routing_reasoning field in the decision that created this page, which states the same conclusion. Data prior to 1938 for the same geographic area is left unassigned to this entry and flagged as an open question rather than silently included.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1938-data-attribution

**What polity absorbs the 1900-1937 rows currently pointing at this unit?**

The source data for this unit runs from 1900, thirty-eight years before the department of Pando existed. Those pre-1938 rows describe production or trade in the Amazonian territory Pando was carved from in 1938 (then part of the Beni department or a national territory administered directly from La Paz — the exact administrative status circa 1900-1937 needs checking against a Bolivian administrative-history source). Folding those rows into this polity's span would misrepresent a pre-existing department as having existed 38 years before its creation. They should instead route to whichever polity carried that territory before 1938 — most likely BOL-BEN-1842-2025 (Beni) if Pando's territory was administratively part of Beni, but this has not been confirmed and needs a dedicated check against a Bolivian territorial-history source before the pre-1938 rows are re-routed.

### oq-gadm-boundary-vintage

**Does a present-day GADM adm1 Pando boundary match the department's 1938-2025 extent, or did internal boundaries shift?**

The polygon route is registered_source_unfetched: gadm-4.1-adm1 is a registered source but the local geodata file was pre-filtered to 81 countries and excludes Bolivia entirely, so no feature currently exists to check. Once a full GADM Bolivia adm1 layer is fetched, it should be checked against Bolivian sources for any post-1938 boundary adjustments between Pando and its neighboring departments (Beni, La Paz) — provincial-level border disputes and adjustments in Bolivia's Amazonian departments are not rare, and using a 2025-vintage boundary as a proxy for 1938 assumes none occurred.

### oq-fao-mitchell-overlap

**Do any of the routed rows for Pando overlap in year/item with rows already routed to BOL-BEN or national BOL entries?**

Because Pando was carved from Beni-administered or nationally-administered Amazonian territory, there is a risk that some production series double-count the same physical territory once under Pando and once under an overlapping Beni or national Bolivia entry for the same year, if a source's unit boundaries lagged the 1938 administrative change. This has not been checked against BOL-BEN-1842-2025's row inventory.
