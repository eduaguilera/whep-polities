# Cáceres (province of Spain)

## Summary

Cáceres is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833, and today one of the two provinces (with Badajoz) making up the autonomous community of Extremadura, formed in 1983. Unlike Asturias or Madrid, Cáceres was never coextensive with its autonomous community on its own -- Extremadura is a two-province community, so the 1833 province boundary and the 1983 autonomous-community boundary diverge, the same pattern as Galicia's four provinces or Castile and León's nine, only here split just once rather than many times. This entry is type: subnational because Cáceres is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Cáceres separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES432, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES432) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES432 ("Cáceres"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (Asturias/ES120, A Coruña/ES111, Madrid/ES-MD, and other units spanning ES112-ES241), all following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Cáceres is not one of the enumerated departures, i.e. the Canary Islands split) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units (data through 2021 confirms it is not extinct). Previously matched to: nothing distinct -- the routing_reasoning records 0 name matches among existing ESP candidates, which cover a different province, Madrid, and the national total, none of which IS Cáceres; the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for Asturias, A Coruña, and Madrid. What confirms distinctness: Cáceres's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES432) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the other Spanish province rows -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 25x the area of Cáceres.

## Territorial extent

Territory description: Cáceres occupies the northern half of Extremadura, in west-central Spain, bordering Portugal to the west, Salamanca (Castile and León) to the north, Badajoz to the south, and Toledo and Ciudad Real (Castile-La Mancha) to the east. Its capital is the city of Cáceres; other notable towns include Plasencia and Trujillo. On a modern map it is a large, sparsely populated interior province of the Extremaduran plateau. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 19,868 km2, making it Spain's second-largest province by area after Badajoz -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the other Spanish province entries.

## Predecessors and successors

Cáceres has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the Antiguo Régimen's partido structure within the historic region of Extremadura, distinct from the 1833 province in administrative form, and this database does not model sub-kingdom Extremaduran divisions at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1983, one of the two provinces of the Extremadura autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-cc-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation CC rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña), and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES432) or the INE province numeric code (10). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-CC-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so that the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. CC was chosen because it is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-CC) and the vehicle-registration/everyday abbreviation for the province, avoiding collision with the two-letter code AC (Alicante or A Coruña variants) and reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-registered-locally

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is not yet present on disk, unlike for other Spanish province rows**

scripts/sources.yaml registers mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Cáceres is conventionally cpro 10 -- but the polygon_detail for this routing decision states the shapefile is absent from data/geodata/mapspain-ign entirely, a weaker starting position than the sibling entries (Asturias, A Coruña, Madrid) where the source is at least registered as a known-but-unfetched dataset. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=10 feature is confirmed, this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Cáceres, distinct from neighbouring Badajoz within Extremadura, before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Extremaduran partido structure has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory now covered by Cáceres was organized under the Antiguo Régimen's own partido structure within the historic region of Extremadura, which does not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province's administrative form. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. Whether a pre-1833 Extremaduran entity should eventually be modeled is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry, and it is also unclear whether the 1983 formation of the Extremadura autonomous community (splitting reporting between Cáceres and Badajoz) needs its own container edge or note, since some EU/regional statistics may report at the two-province Extremadura level rather than per-province from that date forward -- this page assumes per-province reporting continues throughout, but that assumption is unverified against the actual source rows.
