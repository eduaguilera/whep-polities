# British Cameroons

## Summary

This row tracks **British Cameroons** from **British military occupation
of the German Kamerun colony (1916)** to **partition at independence
plebiscites (1961-10-01)**, when Northern British Cameroons voted to join
Nigeria and Southern British Cameroons voted to join the Republic of
Cameroon.

The row exists because IIA/FAO agricultural yearbook **footnotes** for
**1953** report this territory's data as **included in (composed_union
with) Nigeria**: the footnote folds British Cameroons figures into the
Nigeria aggregate, requiring a distinct WHEP entity to track the
boundary correctly. Without this row the host country (Nigeria) aggregate
silently absorbs Cameroonian territory for the period prior to partition.

German Kamerun was partitioned between Britain and France following
World War I under a League of Nations mandate (1922), with Britain
receiving the narrower western strip along the Nigerian border. The
territory was administered in two non-contiguous sections — Northern
Cameroons (attached administratively to Nigeria) and Southern Cameroons
(initially to Nigeria, later to the British Cameroons High Commission).
The United Nations Trusteeship Agreement (1946) replaced the League
mandate without changing the boundary. A UN-supervised plebiscite held
on 1961-02-11 determined that Northern British Cameroons would join
Nigeria (effective 1961-06-01) and Southern British Cameroons would join
the Republic of Cameroon (effective 1961-10-01).

No predecessor row for the German Kamerun colony (1884–1916) currently
exists in the database; the predecessor field is left blank pending
creation of that row.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` (Schvitz et al. 2022), feature
  `gwcode=472`, "British Cameroons", FID 303, dated `1922-07-20` to
  `1961-09-30`. A second feature (FID 302) covers 1919-06-28 to
  1922-07-19 (the period between the Versailles Treaty and the formal
  League mandate agreement). Both have identical area (87,489.22 km²),
  confirming no boundary change between the two records. FID 303 is
  selected as the primary feature because it covers the bulk of the
  polity period and spans the 1953 footnote year.
- **Method.** `exact_historical` — CShapes 2.0 explicitly records
  British Cameroons as a distinct colonial dependency (owner: United
  Kingdom) for the mandate/trusteeship period. The polygon matches the
  entity, geographic extent, and period with no proxy needed.
- **Area.** 87,489 km² (CShapes 2.0 projected area). This is
  consistent with published estimates of approximately 88,000 km² for
  the British Cameroons mandate territory (Northern ~72,000 km²,
  Southern ~16,000 km²; minor variation by source reflects different
  projection methods).
- **Confidence.** High. CShapes 2.0 is the authoritative GIS source
  for colonial dependencies from 1886 onward. The British Cameroons
  feature covers the mandate period exactly and is consistent with
  published area figures.
- **Period adjustment note.** The polity is coded `start_year: 1916`
  (date of British military takeover from Germany) but CShapes 2.0
  begins the feature at 1919-06-28 (Versailles Treaty, when German
  sovereignty formally lapsed). The 1916–1919 gap is a de-facto
  British administration period. The `polygon_feature_year: 1953`
  is set to the IIA footnote year, which falls well within the
  feature's 1922–1961 span.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor.** No WHEP row currently exists for **German Kamerun**
  (1884–1916), the German colony whose western strip became British
  Cameroons. When created, BCM-1916-1961 should be updated to
  reference it. The territory was seized by Anglo-French forces in
  the Kamerun Campaign (August 1914 – February 1916).
- **Successor (North).** [nga-1961-2025](nga-1961-2025.md) — Northern
  British Cameroons (approximately the present-day Adamawa and Taraba
  States of Nigeria) joined Nigeria on 1961-06-01 following the
  plebiscite result.
- **Successor (South).** [cmr-1961-2025](cmr-1961-2025.md) — Southern
  British Cameroons joined the Republic of Cameroon on 1961-10-01,
  forming the Federal Republic of Cameroon. The Southern Cameroons
  region is today's North West and South West Regions of Cameroon.

## Contradictions

- CShapes 2.0 starts the British Cameroons feature at 1919-06-28
  (Versailles Treaty), not 1916 (military occupation). The polity
  `start_year` is set to 1916 to reflect the de-facto beginning of
  British administration; the polygon cannot cover 1916–1919 because
  CShapes only records the formal sovereign transition. This is a known
  limitation of the CShapes temporal model.
- Northern British Cameroons formally joined Nigeria on 1961-06-01,
  while Southern Cameroons joined Cameroon on 1961-10-01. CShapes 2.0
  uses a single `gwedate=1961-09-30` for the combined polygon, consistent
  with the later of the two partition dates. The WHEP `end_year` follows
  CShapes (1961).

## Decisions

- **Polygon selection.** CShapes 2.0 gwcode=472 FID 303 is an exact
  historical match covering 1922–1961. FID 302 (1919–1922) is not
  selected as primary because FID 303 spans the footnote year (1953)
  and the majority of the polity period; both features have identical
  area.
- **polity_code.** `BCM` chosen as a three-letter abbreviation for
  "British CaMeroons" (no ISO 3166 code exists for this historical
  entity; `CMR` is reserved for modern Cameroon / French Cameroun).
- **iso3.** `CMR` assigned because the larger portion of the territory
  (Southern Cameroons) became part of modern Cameroon (CMR). This is
  a pragmatic approximation; Northern Cameroons is today part of NGA.
  Reviewed against HAWI template: iso3 assigned to the predominant
  modern successor state.
- **cow.** CShapes 2.0 assigns gwcode=472 to this entity; this is
  used as the COW code field. British Cameroons did not have its own
  COW state system membership (it was a colony, not an independent
  state).
- **type.** `colonial` — British Cameroons was a League of Nations
  mandate / UN Trust Territory under British administration, not a
  sovereign state.
- **Two successors.** The polity split at independence; both
  NGA-1961-2025 and CMR-1961-2025 are listed as successors. No
  intermediate polity rows exist for the transitional period
  (Northern Cameroons transitional administration June–October 1961).

## Open questions

### oq-predecessor-german-kamerun

**German Kamerun** (1884–1916) is not currently in the WHEP database.
British Cameroons directly succeeded the German colony's western strip.
When a German Kamerun row is created, the predecessor field here should
be updated. CShapes 2.0 likely includes a German Kamerun polygon
(gwcode range to verify); Cliopatria may also have an entry.

### oq-northern-cameroons-partition-date

Northern British Cameroons joined Nigeria on 1961-06-01, not 1961-10-01.
The NGA-1961-2025 polity's start_year is 1961. A strict treatment would
set BCM end_year=1961 and note both partition dates; alternatively a
transitional row for "Northern British Cameroons (administered by Nigeria,
1961-06 to 1961-10)" could be created if data precision requires it.
Current treatment accepts 1961 as end_year for both successor events.

### oq-area-north-south-split

The combined CShapes polygon (87,489 km²) does not distinguish Northern
from Southern Cameroons. Published figures suggest Northern Cameroons was
approximately 72,000 km² and Southern Cameroons approximately 16,000 km².
If sub-territory granularity is needed for production/trade data
disaggregation, two sub-rows could be created; for current purposes the
combined polygon is the appropriate WHEP unit.
