# China (to 1860)

## Summary

Qing China from the 1800 database floor to the Convention of Peking (1860), before the cession of
Outer Manchuria, the land north of the Amur and east of the Ussuri, to Russia under the Treaty of Aigun
(1858) and the Convention of Peking (1860) [wikipedia-border-eras-2026-10-07 §china](../sources/wikipedia-border-eras-2026-10-07.md#china). Created on 2026-10-07 by splitting the old
`CHN-1800-1895` row at 1860 (issue 686); the later years are [chn-1860-1895](chn-1860-1895.md).

**Why this entry exists.** The old row carried CShapes' 1886 polygon back to 1800, so for 1800-1859 it
left out about 900,000 km² that was Qing territory. No layer-B data reaches these years; the split is
for the territorial record and for any pre-1860 source.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** proxy. Constructed (`build_chn_1800_1860` in
`scripts/sources/constructed/build.py`): CShapes 2.0 gwcode 710's 1886-1895 step plus **Outer
Manchuria**, taken as CShapes 365's 1886 step (Russia) intersected with Cliopatria's "Qing Dynasty" at
1857 [cliopatria-v0.1.3](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md), keeping only the part east of 119 E.
Cliopatria is used only for the ground no CShapes step gives to China, and its outline is cut to
CShapes' Russian coast and border.

**km² reasoning (Mollweide).** Outer Manchuria measures 896,569 km², the "~1 million km²" usually
given; the result is 12,064,041 km² against 11,165,400 km² for the CShapes step. The intersection also
returns 40,067 km² around Lake Zaysan and three small pieces on the Mongolian border; those were ceded in
1864 (Tarbagatai), after this row ends, so they are not added, and the row is short by them.

**Why proxy, and what is not split.** Cliopatria moves all of Outer Manchuria at once between its 1859
and 1860 steps, so Aigun (1858), which gave Russia the left bank of the Amur while the Ussuri region
stayed under joint possession until 1860, cannot be drawn from any fetched source. The years 1858-1859
therefore keep the whole of Outer Manchuria here. Hong Kong Island (1842, about 80 km²) is a separate
row and is not cut out of this polygon.
