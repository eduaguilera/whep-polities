# Iraq

## Summary

Iraq as tracked by WHEP from 1921, when the British mandate was formally established from three former Ottoman provinces. Iraq was the first mandate to graduate to independence in 1932. Its boundaries with Iran, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Syria, Turkey, and Jordan were all shaped by post-WWI arrangements.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is CShapes 2.0. Iraq is bounded by Turkey (219 mi; 352 km), Iran (906 mi; 1,458 km), Kuwait (149 mi; 240 km), Saudi Arabia (502 mi; 808 km), Jordan (111 mi; 179 km), and Syria (376 mi; 605 km).

## Sourced claims

- Iraq as a modern state came only into being as a British mandate after World War I, created from three former Ottoman provinces. [biger-1995 §iraq](../sources/biger-1995.md#iraq)
- In 1932 Iraq was the first mandate to graduate to independence. [biger-1995 §iraq](../sources/biger-1995.md#iraq)
- The Iraq--Kuwait boundary demarcation was only carried out after the defeat of Iraq by UN-sponsored forces in the Gulf War of 1991. [biger-1995 §iraq](../sources/biger-1995.md#iraq)
- The Iraq--Syria boundary is essentially artificial, resulting from the division of British and French spheres of influence after WWI. [biger-1995 §iraq](../sources/biger-1995.md#iraq)

### oq-mosul-vilayet

The Mosul vilayet was contested between Turkey and Iraq in the 1920s. Biger notes Turkey's claim was rejected by the League of Nations, which established the Brussels Line as the northern boundary. The next ingest should verify whether this affected WHEP boundary dates.
