# Baja California Sur

## Summary

Baja California Sur is the state occupying the southern half of the Baja California peninsula in northwestern Mexico. It was created in 1931 when the original, undivided Territorio de la Baja California — held by the federal government as a territory since the 1850s — was split into two federal territories, Norte and Sur, along a line near the 28th parallel. The southern territory kept that boundary through statehood: it was admitted as Mexico's 30th state, Estado Libre y Soberano de Baja California Sur, on 8 October 1974, with no change to its external limits. Because the unit's shape has been continuous since 1931 regardless of its territory/state legal status, this entry is coded `type: subnational` and spans the whole 1931-2025 window as a single reporting geography inside the national MEX chain, rather than being split into a pre-1974 territory row and a post-1974 state row. Statehood in 1974 is a change in legal status and representation in Congress, not a change in the reporting boundary that the routed data measures, so one row is the correct model here.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures a Mexican agricultural/production reporting unit labelled 'Baja California Sur' (unit_id MEX-BAJACALIFORNIASUR) with continuous data from 1931 onward (the harness's routing decision notes the underlying extract actually starts at 1900, but the 1900-1930 portion predates the 1931 territorial split and is left unrouted since no polity represents the undivided pre-1931 Baja California Territory). Before this entry existed there was no candidate polity at all for this unit — the routing search returned 0 name candidates and 0 boundary features — so the data would otherwise have had nowhere correct to go; routing it to the national MEX-1848-2025 row would have overstated the reporting territory by roughly 30x (Mexico's ~1.96 million km2 vs. this state's ~73,900 km2) and merged a distinct sub-national statistical series into the national total. That Baja California Sur is tracked as its own administrative and statistical entity, separate from Baja California (Norte) and from the national aggregate, is standard in Mexican federal administrative history from 1931 (territory) and 1974 (state) onward — it appears as its own unit in INEGI's state-level statistics and in every Mexican census and agricultural census since its creation.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct source is GADM 4.1 admin-1 (feature GID_1 likely `MEX.2_1`), which is the registered source for Mexican first-level subdivisions, but the locally cached `gadm-4.1-adm1` GeoPackage only covers a subset of 81 countries and Mexico is not among them — 0 features are available on disk for MEX at any admin level. No other registered source (cshapes, cliopatria, paine, histogis, reporting-areas, constructed) carries Mexican state boundaries. Fetching the global GADM 4.1 layer (or a Mexico-specific extract) is required before a `polygon_feature_id` can be assigned; until then `polygon_status` is `unassigned` and no area figure is given here, since none has been measured. **Territory description:** Baja California Sur occupies the southern ~45% of the Baja California peninsula, south of a line running roughly from Bahía San Bartolomé/Guerrero Negro on the Pacific to San Felipe's southern edge on the Gulf of California, and includes La Paz (the state capital), Cabo San Lucas, and the peninsula's entire southern tip. Its modern land area is approximately 73,900 km2 (INEGI), making it roughly the size of Ireland's Republic and Northern Ireland combined, and its boundary has been unchanged since the 1931 territorial partition.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor entry exists: the undivided Territorio de la Baja California that preceded the 1931 split is not currently represented anywhere in the polity table, so the 1900-1930 portion of the source extract for this reporting unit is left unrouted rather than assigned a fabricated predecessor code. No successor entry is needed either, since the state's boundary and reporting identity have not changed since 1931 and the entry runs to the database's present horizon (2025) under the continuing MEX-1848-2025 national container.

## Decisions

### d-single-span-not-split

**One 1931-2025 row instead of separate territory-era and state-era rows**

The routing decision's routing_concerns field explicitly raised whether the repository's convention prefers splitting a territory-era row from a state-era row (mirroring the implicit 1974 boundary in the country conventions' departure list) rather than one continuous span. I chose one continuous row because the boundary and the reporting unit that generates the underlying data (state/territory-level agricultural and demographic statistics) did not change in 1974 — only the unit's constitutional status did, which is not something this database's type or container fields are built to encode at the subnational level (there is no 'territory' vs 'state' distinction in the schema's type enum). Splitting would have produced two rows with identical geometry and no data-driven reason for the seam, so I followed the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern as a single span. If a future reviewer decides the territory/state legal distinction should be modeled explicitly, this row should be split at 1974 and the polygon (once assigned) copied unchanged across the split, per the proxy-copy rule for territory that did not change.

## Open questions

### oq-1931-split-date-precision

**Exact date and decree of the 1931 territorial split is unverified against a primary source**

The routing decision's own routing_concerns flagged that the 1931 split date should be checked against a primary source on Mexican territorial history before being committed to a span boundary; this entry inherits that same open flag. If the actual decree date turns out to be a different year (some secondary sources cite the reorganization as taking effect in 1930 or being finalized administratively over 1931-1932), the start_year and the year embedded in polity_code would both need correcting, and the gate that checks code years against frontmatter years would need to be re-satisfied.

### oq-1900-1930-unrouted-predecessor

**1900-1930 data for this reporting unit is left unrouted with no predecessor polity**

The underlying data extract for unit MEX-BAJACALIFORNIASUR reportedly begins in 1900, three decades before this entry's start_year. That pre-1931 span belongs to the undivided Territorio de la Baja California, which has no polity row in this database and no boundary candidate was found for it either. Those rows remain unroutable until (a) a predecessor polity for the undivided territory is created and (b) this entry's predecessor field is updated to point to it. Whether that predecessor should itself be split from BCN's pre-1931 history or modeled as one shared row is also unresolved.

### oq-gadm-mexico-not-cached

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 does not currently include Mexico in the local cache, blocking polygon assignment**

The registered gadm-4.1-adm1 source is the correct polygon source for this entry per the routing decision, but the on-disk GeoPackage subset (81 countries) excludes Mexico entirely, so 0 features are retrievable for any Mexican state right now. Until the global GADM 4.1 layer or a Mexico-specific extract is fetched and added to the cached source, this entry and every other Mexican subnational entry created from this same pipeline run will stay at polygon_status: unassigned. This is a fetch/ingest gap, not a boundary-identification gap — the feature almost certainly exists in GADM under GID_1 MEX.2_1 or similar once the data is available.
