# Loir-et-Cher

## Summary

This entry represents Loir-et-Cher, a French departement in the Centre-Val de Loire region, formed in 1790 chiefly from the former province of Blesois (with parts of Orleanais, Vendomois and Touraine) and centred on the city of Blois on the Loire river. It is coded as a `subnational` polity because a departement is an administrative subdivision of France, not a sovereign state or a distinct internationally-recognised territory -- France's sovereignty over this area is continuous and undisputed throughout the entry's span, and the national FRA rows in this table already carry that sovereignty claim for the whole country. This row exists solely to carry a *sub-national reporting territory*: statistics keyed to "Loir-et-Cher" specifically, as opposed to all-France totals, need somewhere to route that isn't the national polygon. The entry follows the country convention's default start rule (1790, adjusted here to 1800 to match the earliest available French national container row, see decisions) and runs to the open end_year 2025 since the departement remains an active administrative unit of France today with no successor.

**Why this entry exists.** The input data is the routing decision for unit FRA-FRB05, admin_name "FRB05" (a Eurostat NUTS3-style code), country France -- source data keyed to this unit that the harness's routing pipeline could not match to any existing polity. It was previously effectively unmatched (not misrouted to a wrong polity) because the three existing FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) are national totals for the whole of France, so routing this unit's data there would silently conflate a sub-national reporting territory (Loir-et-Cher, ~6,343 km2) with the country total (~551,700 km2, roughly 87x larger). What confirms Loir-et-Cher as a distinct entity is that it is one of the ~83 original departements created by the 1790 Revolutionary reform, carved chiefly from the province of Blesois, and has been continuously tabulated as a discrete statistical unit by INSEE (code 41) ever since -- the same institutional basis already established for sibling entries FRA-18 (Cher), FRA-28 (Eure-et-Loir), FRA-36 (Indre), FRA-37 (Indre-et-Loire) and FRA-77 (Seine-et-Marne) created in this same pipeline run.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision already established this is a `new_source_needed` case: no source registered in scripts/sources.yaml digitises French departements at the needed resolution. gadm-4.1-adm1's local file is a curated subset that omits France entirely (even though GADM's global release does cover French departements), and cshapes-2.0/cshapes-europe are country-level only. The standard remedy would be to register GADM's global admin-1 layer or French IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product, neither of which is on disk in this repository today.

Territory description: Loir-et-Cher occupies the central part of the Centre-Val de Loire region, straddling the Loire, Cher and Loir rivers. On a modern map it is bounded by Eure-et-Loir to the north, Loiret to the northeast, Cher to the east, Indre to the south, Indre-et-Loire to the southwest, and Sarthe to the northwest; its administrative seat (prefecture) is Blois. It covers approximately 6,343 km2 -- this is a published administrative-area statistic for the modern departement, not a measurement from any polygon attached to this entry, since no polygon is yet assigned (see polygon status above). The departement includes the Sologne forest and wetland region in its south, several Loire valley chateaux (Chambord, Cheverny, Blois itself), and mixed cereal/viticulture agriculture (Cheverny and Cour-Cheverny AOC wines).

Why this entry exists: This page implements the routing decision for unit FRA-FRB05 (admin_name "FRB05", country France), which resolved via create_new because no existing FRA polity IS this territory -- the national FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) describe the whole country, roughly 87 times the area of this one departement, so routing this unit's data there would silently conflate a sub-national reporting territory with the country total. What confirms Loir-et-Cher as a distinct, statistically-reported entity is that it is one of the ~83 original departements created by the 1790 Revolutionary reform and has been continuously tabulated as a discrete statistical unit by INSEE under the numeric code 41 ever since -- the same institutional basis that justified creating sibling entries for Cher, Eure-et-Loir, Indre, Indre-et-Loire and Seine-et-Marne in this same pipeline run.

## Predecessors and successors

Loir-et-Cher has no predecessor or successor polity in this table: it was created directly by the 1790 Revolutionary departements reform (predating this database's coverage of France, see the open question on the 1790-1800 gap) and has continued as an administrative unit of France without interruption through 2025, so both fields are empty. It is not among the departements affected by the 1871 Alsace-Lorraine cession or its 1919 return, nor by the Corsican split or overseas departement departures noted in the country's routing convention -- it sits squarely in the Loire valley interior, unaffected by any of those national-boundary changes.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the proposed 1790, because no container exists for the earlier decade**

The routing decision's span_basis argues for 1790, the year of the Revolutionary departements reform that created Loir-et-Cher (chiefly from the province of Blesois, with slivers of Orleanais, Vendomois and Touraine). That is plausible administrative history, but this database's earliest French national row is FRA-1800-1871 -- there is no FRA row of any kind covering 1790-1800, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's span. Rather than leave a decade uncontained, or invent a container edge citing a national polity that does not exist in the table, I moved start_year to 1800 to match the earliest available container, exactly as sibling entries FRA-18-1800-2025 (Cher), FRA-28 (Eure-et-Loir), FRA-36 (Indre), FRA-37 (Indre-et-Loire) and FRA-77 (Seine-et-Marne) already did for the same reason. The polity code year (1800) and frontmatter start_year (1800) are kept equal, satisfying the gate that ties code years to frontmatter years. If a pre-1800 French national polity is added later, this entry's start_year should be revisited alongside it -- see the open question below.

### d-subunit-code-41-not-nuts

**Subunit code is the INSEE/ISO 3166-2 numeric code 41, not a NUTS code**

The dominant pattern for subnational rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. French departements do not carry ISO 3166-2 letter codes in general use, and NUTS3 codes are not how French statistical sources or the public identify a departement. The standard identifier for Loir-et-Cher, used by INSEE, La Poste, and ISO 3166-2:FR alike, is the two-digit number 41. I used FRA-41-1800-2025, matching the convention already set by sibling entries FRA-18, FRA-28, FRA-36, FRA-37 and FRA-77, since 41 is the identifier a reader would actually recognise on a map, car plate, or postcode prefix (41xxx). This departs from the routing decision's own proposed name only in that no bespoke code was warranted -- Loir-et-Cher is a standard departement, not a historical territory with a name of its own.

### d-polygon-unassigned-not-proxy

**Polygon left unassigned rather than proxied from a neighbouring departement or the national polygon**

The routing decision already established that no registered source in scripts/sources.yaml digitises French departements at the resolution this entry needs -- gadm-4.1-adm1's local file is a curated subset that excludes FRA entirely (even though GADM's global release does cover French departements), and cshapes-europe/cshapes-2.0 are country-level only. I did not attempt a coarser proxy (e.g. the national FRA polygon, or a hand-drawn union of neighbouring shapes) because the whole point of this row is to isolate Loir-et-Cher's own reporting territory from the national one; substituting the national shape would defeat that purpose silently. polygon_source is set to the 'none' sentinel used by other unassigned rows in this table (e.g. sibling FRA-18, FRA-28, FRA-36, FRA-37, FRA-77) rather than a placeholder string, matching existing convention.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap-uncovered

**The 1790-1800 decade of Loir-et-Cher's actual administrative existence has no container and is not represented anywhere in this table**

Loir-et-Cher was created in 1790 by the Revolutionary departements reform, ten years before this entry's start_year of 1800. That decade is real administrative history -- the departement existed, was named, and had a boundary -- but no French national polity row in this database covers 1790-1800, so there is nothing to container an edge against. If a pre-1800 FRA national row is ever added (e.g. FRA-1790-1800), this entry's start_year and its first container edge should be revisited to close the gap. Until then, any source data keyed to Loir-et-Cher for 1790-1799 has nowhere to route in this table.

### oq-no-polygon-source-registered

**No registered data source in this repository can supply a Loir-et-Cher boundary, so the polygon question is open indefinitely absent a new source**

polygon_status is unassigned because gadm-4.1-adm1's local file omits France entirely and no other registered source (cshapes-2.0, cshapes-europe, paine-2024, cliopatria, histogis-1860-habsburg, reporting-areas, constructed) digitises French sub-national administrative divisions. The routing decision flagged GADM's global admin-1 layer or French IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS departements product as candidate fixes, neither of which is on disk today. Until one of those is registered and fetched, this entry (and its five siblings FRA-18, FRA-28, FRA-36, FRA-37, FRA-77) cannot receive a real polygon, and any area figure quoted in the territorial extent below remains a published administrative-area statistic rather than a geometry measurement.

### oq-boundary-stability-since-1790

**Whether Loir-et-Cher's boundary has been genuinely unchanged since 1790, or altered by any 19th/20th-century commune reassignments, is unverified**

This entry assumes territorial continuity across its full 1800-2025 span, consistent with the general pattern for French departements, but I have not checked for minor boundary adjustments (commune transfers between neighbouring departements, which have occurred elsewhere in France, e.g. around Paris in 1968). If such an adjustment affected Loir-et-Cher, a single polygon (once sourced) would not correctly represent the full span and a mid-period split might be needed, similar to how Seine-et-Marne's neighbours were affected by the 1968 Paris-region reorganisation.
