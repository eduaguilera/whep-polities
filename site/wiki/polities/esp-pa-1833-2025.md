# Palencia (province of Spain)

## Summary

Palencia is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the nine provinces making up the Castilla y Leon autonomous community in the northern sub-plateau (submeseta norte) of Spain. It takes its name from its capital, the city of Palencia, on the Carrion river. The province sits on the northern Meseta, bordered by Cantabria to the north (across the Cordillera Cantabrica foothills), Burgos to the east, Valladolid to the south, and Leon to the west. This entry is type: subnational because Palencia is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Palencia separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES414, for over a century and a half. No polity row for Palencia existed in the database before this routing decision, so this page both allocates the code ESP-PA-1833-2025 and documents it, following the same convention already established for its sibling ESP province entries (A Coruna, Madrid, Pontevedra, and others in the ES1xx-ES2xx-ES4xx NUTS3 run).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES414 ("Palencia"), country Spain, following the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units, using a system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing decision found no existing polity row for Palencia -- the three ESP candidates it evaluated were A Coruna (ESP-CO-1833-2025), Madrid (ESP-MD-1833-2025), and national Spain (ESP-1800-2025), none of which IS Palencia; routing rows for this unit to the national aggregate would have collapsed a province-level series (~8,052 km2) into a territory roughly 63x larger. What confirms distinctness: Palencia's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES414) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entries ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025, and ESP-PO-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed: the conventional registered source for ESP provinces, mapspain-ign (id_column=cpro, the INE province numeric code, under which Palencia is cpro 34), is flagged present_locally=False in sources.yaml and its declared path does not exist on disk. A separate, unregistered file, data/geodata/spain_provinces.gpkg, already sits in the repository with a matching feature ES34-1833-2025 = "Palencia (Province)" -- presumably the same IGN/INE provinces dataset, just not yet declared as a source in sources.yaml with a checked licence. Because no registered source can execute this route today, this entry carries no geometry, exactly as ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025, and ESP-PO-1833-2025 do for the same reason. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and Palencia does not coincide with the Castilla y Leon community boundary (which contains eight other provinces). Territory description: Palencia province occupies the northern sub-plateau of the Meseta, straddling the transition from the Tierra de Campos cereal plains in the south to the foothills of the Cordillera Cantabrica in the north (including the Fuentes Carrionas natural park). Its capital and largest city is Palencia city, on the Carrion river, historically a textile and now agro-industrial center; the province also contains the Canal de Castilla, a major 18th-19th century inland waterway. On a modern map it is bounded by Cantabria to the north, Burgos to the east, Valladolid to the south, and Leon to the west. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 8,052 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once a registered provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling entries.

## Predecessors and successors

Palencia has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the old Kingdom of Castile's partido and corregimiento system (the historic Partido de Palencia and adjoining Tierra de Campos jurisdictions), which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025, and ESP-PO-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1983, one of the nine provinces making up the Castilla y Leon autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish NUTS3 units of treating 2025 as the open end for still-current entries.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Palencia as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES414) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ES414 records no existing polity row representing Palencia specifically -- the candidates evaluated (A Coruna, Madrid, national Spain) are all distinct territories -- and assigns the default 1833-2025 country-convention span rather than a departure span such as the one used for the Canary Islands split, since Palencia is one of the original 1833 provinces rather than a later territorial division.
- A local, unregistered file data/geodata/spain_provinces.gpkg contains a feature ES34-1833-2025 labelled "Palencia (Province)", presumably sourced from the same INE/IGN provincial boundary data as the registered mapspain-ign convention, but sources.yaml does not yet declare this file or its licence.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-pa-abbreviation

**Used a two-letter subunit abbreviation (PA) rather than a NUTS or numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational code pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the existing ESP subnational rows (ESP-CO-1833-2025 for A Coruna, ESP-MD-1833-2025 for Madrid, ESP-PO-1833-2025 for Pontevedra, and others) all use a short letter abbreviation of the unit rather than the NUTS3 code (ES414) or the INE province numeric code (34). Palencia's own INE province code is 34 and its NUTS3 code is ES414, but following the sibling precedent this entry uses ESP-PA-1833-2025, where PA is Palencia's standard Spanish vehicle-plate/province abbreviation (also used informally in INE tables), not a bespoke invention. This is not a departure from the dominant pattern -- it is the same subunit-abbreviation convention already used repeatedly for the same country -- so no new code shape is introduced here.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-unregistered

**A matching provinces geopackage sits on disk but is not registered as a source, so Palencia's geometry cannot be assigned yet**

data/geodata/spain_provinces.gpkg already contains a feature ES34-1833-2025 = "Palencia (Province)" that appears to be exactly the geometry this entry needs, but it is not declared in sources.yaml (no name, URL, or licence recorded), and the conventionally-expected registered source, mapspain-ign, is itself flagged present_locally=False with a declared path that does not exist. Until someone reconciles whether spain_provinces.gpkg IS the mapspain-ign source under a different path, or a separate unregistered dataset, and verifies its licence permits redistribution, this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned. This is the same open state recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025, and ESP-PO-1833-2025, and should probably be resolved once at the country-convention level rather than re-investigated per province.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Palencia province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Palencia province was part of the Kingdom of Castile's older partido and corregimiento system (including the historic Partido de Palencia and parts of the Tierra de Campos), which does not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and is not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Palencia's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-MD-1833-2025, and ESP-PO-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Castilian administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all remains an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
