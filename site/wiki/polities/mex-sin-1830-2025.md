# Sinaloa

## Summary

Sinaloa is a state of the Mexican federation located on the Pacific coast of northwestern Mexico, established in 1830 when the former joint Estado de Occidente (shared with Sonora since 1824) was divided into two separate states. This entry is typed `subnational` rather than `national` because Sinaloa has never held sovereignty in its own right: it has existed continuously as a first-order administrative and political subdivision of Mexico, first under the pre-1848 Mexican federation and then under the post-1848 Mexican state, and the `subnational` type keeps it from competing with the national MEX chain in any downstream family or sovereignty ranking. The entry exists to receive state-level economic and production data -- an extract beginning around 1900 -- that had previously been folded into the national MEX totals, a routing that both mismeasures scale (Sinaloa is a small fraction of national territory and output) and risks double-counting once other Mexican states are also broken out as their own rows. The span 1830-2025 follows the country convention's default rule that a Mexican state's start_year is its year of admission to the federation as a state (not the year data for it happens to begin), with 1830 as Sinaloa's admission year and end_year left open at 2025 since the state remains current.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures a routed extract of Mexican state-level production/economic data for Sinaloa beginning around 1900, previously ingested and matched against the national MEX-1848-2025 row. That match was wrong at the scale of a factor of roughly 30 (national territory and output versus one of Mexico's 32 states), and risks double-counting against national totals as more Mexican states are broken out individually. What confirms Sinaloa is a distinct governed entity, independent of any single data source's treatment, is its own continuous constitutional history: it was created as a separate state of the federation in 1830 out of the former joint Estado de Occidente (shared with Sonora since 1824), and has held its own state congress and administrative boundaries ever since -- a status recognized in Mexican constitutional and administrative history regardless of whether a given economic-history compilation happens to tabulate it apart from the nation.

Why this entry exists: this entry captures Sinaloa as the reporting territory behind an extract of Mexican state-level data starting around 1900 that had been matched to the national MEX row -- a country roughly 30 times Sinaloa's area -- which is wrong because Sinaloa is one of 32 first-order administrative divisions of Mexico and its production/economic figures are not representative of, nor summable into, the nation as a whole without double-counting against other states. Sinaloa's distinctness as a governed unit is confirmed by its own constitutional and administrative history: it has held separate statehood, its own state congress, and its own boundaries since 1830, independent of whether any particular economic-history source (e.g. Federico-Tena) tabulates it separately.

Territory description: Sinaloa is a Mexican Pacific coastal state in the northwest of the country, bordered by Sonora to the north, Chihuahua and Durango to the east, Nayarit to the south, and the Gulf of California to the west -- readily located on a modern map as the coastal strip running roughly from Los Mochis through the state capital Culiacan down to Mazatlan. Its modern area is approximately 58,000 km2. No km2 figure is given here as a measurement of the attached geometry, since no polygon is attached to this entry; the 58,000 km2 figure is a general reference figure for the modern state, not derived from any geometry in this database.

## Predecessors and successors

Sinaloa has no predecessor or successor polity rows: it was carved out of the former Estado de Occidente (a joint Sonora-Sinaloa state, 1824-1830) directly into a standalone federal state in 1830, and it has remained a state of Mexico continuously since, with no independence, annexation, or dissolution event that would end this row before 2025. The Estado de Occidente itself is not currently represented as a polity row in this database, so no predecessor link is declared; if it is added later, this entry's predecessor field should be updated to point to it.

## Sourced claims

- Sinaloa was established as a separate state of the Mexican federation in 1830, having previously formed part of the joint Estado de Occidente together with Sonora since 1824 -- a split documented in standard accounts of Mexican federal state formation (e.g. Mexican government and historiographical sources on the division of the Estado de Occidente).
- Mexico's own national polity row splits at 1848 (MEX-1800-1848 / MEX-1848-2025) at the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo cession of its northern territories to the United States; Sinaloa, in the country's northwest interior, was not part of the territory ceded and its membership in the federation was unaffected by that split.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-span

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern; span extended to 1830 admission year rather than the 1900 data start**

MEX has no existing subnational rows, so there is no in-country precedent to match, but 57 of 66 subnational rows across the whole table use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and Sinaloa is an ordinary federal state with no bespoke historical identity of its own (unlike ALK or HYD), so the dominant pattern is followed: MEX-SIN-1830-2025. The routing decision's proposed start_year of 1830 (Sinaloa's admission to the Mexican federation as a state, per the country convention's default admission-year rule) is used rather than the 1900 start of the data extract that motivated this entry, since the convention's default rule is that a state's span begins at federal admission, not at first data availability. The 1830 date itself was flagged by the router as unverified against a primary source; that uncertainty is carried into the open questions below rather than silently accepted.

### d-two-container-edges

**Two container edges are required to tile 1830-2025, one per era of the Mexican national row**

The Mexican national chain splits into MEX-1800-1848 (Mexico to 1848) and MEX-1848-2025 (Mexico from 1848). Since Sinaloa's span starts in 1830, a single container edge citing only MEX-1848-2025 would leave 1830-1848 uncontained and would fail validation, because that edge falls outside MEX-1848-2025's own span. Two edges are declared instead, each clipped to the overlap between Sinaloa's span and the respective container row's own span, together tiling 1830-2025 with no gap.

## Open questions

### oq-1830-admission-date

**The 1830 admission-to-statehood date for Sinaloa is unverified against a primary source**

The routing decision proposed 1830 as Sinaloa's start_year on the basis of the country convention's default rule (a Mexican state's start_year is its admission year to the federation, unless it is one of three documented exceptions: Baja California, Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo). The router itself flagged that this date was not directly supplied in the evidence used and should be checked against a primary source on Mexican state formation -- e.g. Sinaloa's 1830 constitutive decree separating it from the former Estado de Occidente (which it had shared with Sonora since 1824). If the correct date turns out to differ, both start_year and the code MEX-SIN-1830-2025 would need to change together, and the first container edge's end_year would shift with it.

### oq-no-polygon-source

**No registered source currently carries a Mexican state-level (adm1) boundary layer**

The on-disk copy of gadm-4.1-adm1 is an 81-country subset that excludes Mexico entirely (0 features for MEX), even though GADM as a source does digitise Mexican state boundaries at the adm1 level and would be the natural fit once fetched. No other registered source (cshapes-2.0 is national-only, cliopatria covers pre-1848 Mexico only as a single national unit, paine-2024/histogis/reporting-areas/constructed carry nothing relevant to Mexican states) can produce a Sinaloa feature today. polygon_source is therefore left as none and polygon_status as unassigned rather than pointing at a source file that does not actually contain this feature. Fetching the global (non-subset) gadm-4.1-adm1 file would resolve this without registering a new source. Separately, any GADM adm1 feature obtained this way would reflect present-day boundaries, not those of an 1830s state carved out of the former Estado de Occidente -- a vintage mismatch across a ~195-year span that should be flagged when the polygon is eventually assigned.

### oq-territorial-changes-since-1830

**Whether Sinaloa's boundaries have shifted materially since 1830 is unresearched**

This page treats Sinaloa as a single continuous territorial unit from 1830 to the present, but no check has been made for boundary adjustments with neighbouring states (Sonora, Durango, Baja California Sur) over nearly two centuries -- for instance whether any municipios were transferred at the margins during 19th or 20th century reorganizations of northwestern Mexico. This matters once a polygon is assigned, since a modern GADM shape would silently assume no such changes occurred.
