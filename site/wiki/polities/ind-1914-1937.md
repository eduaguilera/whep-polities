# India (1914-1937)

## Summary

CSV row for India from **1914** to **1937** — a **24-year row**
covering British India through WWI and the interwar period
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW code 750
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

The 1937 end-of-row event: **Burma separated from India** under
the Government of India Act 1935 (effective 1 April 1937) — a
real territorial change satisfying the WHEP polity-definition rule.

## Territorial extent

This row is bound to CShapes 2.0, `cowcode=750`, `polygon_feature_year=1914`
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-states](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-states).
Direct measurement of that feature from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`
(reprojected to ESRI:54034, equal-area) gives **4,893,939 km²**, matching the
successor page's independently-stated pre-Burma-separation figure of
4,894,456 km² for the same polygon (small residual is a coordinate-precision
artifact, not a different feature).

Direct inspection of the raw CShapes 2.0 distribution
(`data/geodata/cshapes2_full.gpkg`, gitignored, not the source-file citation
above but the underlying dataset it documents) shows `cowcode=750` recorded
across **three consecutive time-steps that overlap this row's span**:

| start | end | area (km², ESRI:54034) | capname |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1899-01-01 | 1914-07-02 | 4,819,795 | Calcutta |
| 1914-07-03 | 1931-02-12 | 4,894,456 | Calcutta |
| 1931-02-13 | 1937-03-31 | 4,894,456 | New Delhi (Delhi) |
| 1937-04-01 | 1947-08-14 | 4,227,508 | New Delhi (Delhi) *(successor row)* |

Two things follow directly from this table:

1. **The only real area change inside this bracket falls right at the row's
   1914 start**, not inside it: area rises from 4,819,795 km² to 4,894,456 km²
   at the 1899-01-01→1914-07-02 / 1914-07-03 boundary, i.e. exactly where WHEP
   also starts a new row. (Uncited general history, not from an ingested
   source: 3 July 1914 is the date of the Simla Convention between British
   India and Tibet, which fixed the McMahon Line and shifted the
   India–Tibet/Assam frontier — a plausible but not source-confirmed
   explanation for the jump; flagged as such rather than asserted as fact.)
2. **The 1931-02-13 CShapes time-step is not a territorial change.** The area
   is bit-identical on both sides (4,894,456 km²); the only field that
   changes is `capname`, from "Calcutta" to "New Delhi (Delhi)". This is a
   direct instance of the rule in `wiki/README.md` §"What a WHEP polity is":
   *"CShapes splits are not automatically WHEP splits… the test is whether
   `ST_Area(geom)` changes between CShapes time-steps. Same area = same WHEP
   polity = one row."* No split is warranted at 1931. (Uncited general
   history: 13 February 1931 is the date New Delhi was formally inaugurated
   as the seat of government of British India, which is consistent with —
   though not, from an ingested source, proven to be — the cause of this
   step in the CShapes coding.)

Cross-checking against COW: `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv`
(the raw file the `cow-state-system-v2024` source documents) has exactly one
tenure row for India (`IND`, `ccode=750`): `1947-08-15 → 2024-12-31`. British
India never appears as a COW state-system member before independence, which
is consistent with COW's own inclusion criteria — diplomatic
recognition/League membership plus a population threshold that a colony by
definition cannot meet
[cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion).
This is expected, not a contradiction: per the WHEP polity-definition rule,
COW's state-system-membership gap says nothing about whether the *territory*
changed, and this row correctly does not use 1947 as an internal boundary.

**Bottom line: borders were effectively constant across the whole 1914–1937
span** (4,894,456 km² throughout), bookended by a frontier adjustment right
at the 1914 start and the well-documented Burma separation right at the 1937
end — both already reflected in the row boundaries, not inside them.

## The data may cover British India only, and this affects the whole IND chain (added 2026-08-18)

This page documents the polygon carefully — constant CShapes borders 1914-1937, the Burma separation at
1937 — and says nothing about whether the DATA routed here covers the same territory. It probably does
not, and the gap is systemic rather than local to this row.

**The scope question.** Colonial-era Indian agricultural statistics conventionally report the
directly-administered **British Provinces** only. The 1920-21 *Agricultural Statistics of India* covers
roughly 3.06M km2; George Blyn's standard reconstruction, which underlies much of the later literature,
is explicitly British-India-only. This row's polygon is the whole Indian Empire:

```
IND-1914-1937 polygon       4,889,739 km²   (measured from the committed geometry)
British Provinces           ~3,060,000 km²   (1920-21 Agricultural Statistics of India)
princely / Indian States    ~1,830,000 km²   ≈ 37% of this row
```

So an acreage or production total routed here is plausibly missing the output of some 500 princely
states covering over a third of the mapped area — a sub-territory fold-up, the direction the
verification prompt names as the one most often missed.

**It is not one assertion, and here is the count.** Thirteen assertions carry the bare `india` label
across three sources and 2,227 rows:

```
india|mitchell|1914-1936    595 rows   -> IND-1914-1937   (quarantined, and for a different reason)
india|mitchell|1893-1913    372        -> IND-1893-1914
india|iia|1914-1936         277        -> IND-1914-1937
india|mitchell|1937-1946    207        -> IND-1937-1947
india|iia|1909-1913          75        -> IND-1893-1914
india|mitchell|1947-1948     66        -> IND-1947-1949
india|fao1952|1937-1940      44        -> IND-1937-1947
india|iia|1937-1945          41        -> IND-1937-1947
india|mitchell|1886-1892     35        -> IND-1886-1893
india|mitchell|1885-1885      1        -> IND-1800-1886
                          -----
pre-independence subtotal  1,713 rows across six IND-* rows
```

The remaining three assertions — `india|mitchell|1949-1960` (350), `india|fao1952|1949-1951` (161) and
`india|fao1952|1948-1948` (3), 514 rows — route to IND-1949-2025 and are **not** exposed: the princely
states were integrated into the Republic by 1949, so there is no separate territory to omit.

**Not resolvable at this page's level.** No polity in the IND chain separates "British India, directly
administered" from the whole dependency, at this or any period, so there is nowhere to reroute to and
every affected assertion stays `best_available` at most. Creating such a row would be a substantial
change: it needs a British-Provinces polygon, and it would sit alongside rather than replace the
existing chain, since the Empire extent is the right territory for anything that genuinely covers it.

**What this is NOT.** The quarantine on `india|mitchell|1914-1936` is a separate and narrower finding —
Mitchell's rice-paddy series drops Burma at 1929, eight years before the political separation, visible
as a −18.6% one-step break with wheat and barley unaffected. That is a mid-span reporting change in one
commodity. This section is about the whole chain and every commodity, and neither subsumes the other.

## Polygon corrected 2026-08-13 (issue 84)

> **Polygon rebuilt on 2026-08-13 to remove Portuguese India and French India (issue 84).**
>
> This row used to publish CShapes 2.0 gwcode 750 unchanged, and CShapes does not carve foreign
> enclaves out of a colonial power's outline. So it claimed the same ground as two other rows:
>
> ```
> before   this row n PTIND-1816-1961    3,719 km2    98.4% of Portuguese India
>          this row n FRIN-1816-1954       520 km2    95.2% of French India
> after    this row n PTIND-1816-1961       13.6 km2
>          this row n FRIN-1816-1954        47.2 km2
> ```
>
> **Neither enclave was ever British.** Portuguese India (Goa, Daman, Diu) was Portuguese until
> India annexed it in **December 1961**; French India (Pondicherry, Karikal, Mahe, Yanam) was
> French until the **October 1954** cession. This row ends in 1936, so both were foreign
> sovereign territory for the whole of its span and the subtraction needs no judgement call —
> which is exactly what could not be said of `IND-1949-2025`, and why that row is untouched.
>
> **Polygon:** `constructed` — `IND-1914-1937` in `scripts/sources/constructed/build.py`, which is
> `_cshapes2_step(750, 1914, 1931)` MINUS `build_ptind_1816_1961()` MINUS
> `build_frin_1816_1954()`. The subtrahends are the *same geometries those two rows publish*, not
> re-derivations, so the pairs cannot drift apart. The CShapes step is named by its **bounds**
> rather than by a feature year because `polygon_feature_year` is ambiguous for part of this
> chain — 1914 matches two steps of gwcode 750 — and the bound form reproduces the step this row
> was already bound to, so nothing but the enclaves moved.
>
> **`polygon_feature_year` is kept** at its old value even though `polygon_source` is now
> `constructed`. The `constructed` source has no `temporal` key in `scripts/sources.yaml`, so the
> year plays no part in resolving the feature — but it still records which CShapes vintage the
> polygon is built from, which is what `04_territory_basis.py` reads to decide whether a row's
> border is `measured` or merely `assumed_constant`. Dropping it to `null` would have reclassified
> this row as "polygon present but no recorded vintage year", which would be false.
>
> ```
> CShapes 750 `1914-1931` step    4,894,456 km2
> minus Portuguese India and French India   -4,246
> after the cut              4,890,210 km2
> published                  4,889,739 km2   (write-time simplification, -0.011%)
> ```
>
> **The residual 13.6 + 47.2 km² is a simplification artefact, not the recipe.** Measured on the
> builder's own geometry the enclaves are gone to 0.00 km²; `scripts/build_database.py` then
> applies a Douglas-Peucker simplification on write, which moves every boundary — including the
> two new holes — and puts a thin rim back. That same step is why the published area is 0.011%
> under the computed one. 61 km² is 0.001% of this row; it is recorded rather than described as
> zero.
>
> **What this does not fix:** `IND-1949-2025` still contains both enclaves in full (3,719 and 520
> km²), because that row spans 1949–2025 and therefore straddles both annexation dates — for part
> of it the enclaves genuinely were Indian. Correcting it means splitting the row at 1954 and
> 1961, a periodisation change to a major national family. **That split was considered and
> declined on 2026-08-13** — it would rename the modern-India polity code, which is referenced
> outside this repository, for a 0.13% correction, and Sikkim sits in the same polygon at
> 6,988 km² untouched. The overstatement is documented on
> [ind-1949-2025](ind-1949-2025.md) instead.
