# Spanish Morocco (1912-1956)

## Summary

The Spanish Protectorate in Morocco from its establishment by the Franco-Spanish treaty of 27 November 1912 to the reunification of Morocco on 7 April 1956, when the northern Spanish zone was returned to the independent Kingdom of Morocco shortly after France relinquished its own protectorate. The Cape Juby / Tarfaya southern zone was ceded separately via the Treaty of Angra de Cintra on 1 April 1958, after the brief Ifni War — see [mor-1956-1958](mor-1956-1958.md).

## Territorial extent

Polygon from CShapes 2.0 (dependencies=TRUE). The Spanish Protectorate was a non-contiguous territory: a northern zone along the Mediterranean coast (facing the Strait of Gibraltar, including Larache and Tetuán) and a southern zone around Cape Juby (Tarfaya). The Spanish plazas de soberanía (Ceuta, Melilla, Peñón de Vélez, Peñón de Alhucemas, Chafarinas) and Ifni were distinct territories with distinct sovereignty claims and are not captured by this row.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor (CSV):** SWA-1884-1912 (Spanish West Africa). This predecessor is geographically inconsistent — Spanish West Africa refers to the Saharan protectorates (Río de Oro / Saguia el-Hamra), not to the Spanish Zone of Morocco. SWA's successor arrow to SMO is factually wrong; it should be treated as a CSV error. See [swa-1884-1912](swa-1884-1912.md) if it exists.
- **Successor:** None (CSV field NA). The correct successor is the reunified Kingdom of Morocco from 7 April 1956 (partial — northern zone) and Tarfaya-adjusted Morocco from 1 April 1958 (southern zone).

## Contradictions

**Predecessor is wrong.** SWA-1884-1912 (Spanish West Africa / the Saharan protectorate) did *not* have Spanish Morocco as its successor. Spanish West Africa's successor territory is the Spanish Sahara → Western Sahara / Morocco-Mauritania partition, not Spanish Morocco. This is a candidate CSV fix: SMO-1912-1956's predecessor should be the pre-protectorate Alawi-dynasty Morocco (MAR-* or MOR-* chain) rather than SWA.
