# China (1914-1921)

## Summary

An **8-year row** covering the early Warlord Era
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW code 710
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Yuan Shikai's
failed monarchical restoration (1915-1916) and death (1916) led to
fragmentation of central authority. Japan's Twenty-One Demands
(1915) imposed further concessions. The row likely reflects CShapes
polygon changes from concession-area adjustments during WWI and its
aftermath.

Predecessor per CSV: [chn-1913-1914](chn-1913-1914.md). Successor per
CSV: [chn-1921-1945](chn-1921-1945.md).

## Territorial extent

The polygon assigned to this row is `CShapes 2.0`, `gwcode 710`,
feature-year 1914 [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
Measured directly from the committed GeoPackage in an equal-area CRS
(ESRI:54034), the polygon is **9,059,518 km²**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.gpkg). The raw CShapes
2.0 shapefile (`data/geodata/cshapes-2.0/CShapes-2.0.shp`) carries the
matching value of 9,059,502 km² as a single, unbroken time-step running
**1914-04-17 → 1921-03-12** — i.e. CShapes codes no territorial change
of any kind for China across the entire span this row covers. CShapes
assigns one polygon per interval between coded changes and backdates
constant borders rather than re-drawing them
[cshapes-2.0 §geocoding](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#geocoding); a single
unbroken time-step spanning 1914–1921 is therefore direct evidence
that China's WHEP-relevant territory did not move during this row,
not merely an absence of recorded changes.

The two neighbouring time-steps show where the real changes are, and
both fall **outside** this row's span:

- **Before (predecessor):** 1913-01-01 → 1914-04-16, area 9,224,555
  km² — this belongs to [chn-1913-1914](chn-1913-1914.md); the ~165,000
  km² difference is a pre-existing boundary this row does not need to
  explain.
- **After (successor):** 1921-03-13 onward, area 7,496,578 km² — a
  drop of **~1,562,924 km²** at the exact date this row ends. That
  figure is almost exactly Outer Mongolia's area, 1,566,500 km² per
  [biger-1995 §mongolia](../sources/biger-1995.md#mongolia). Biger
  dates Outer Mongolia's original declaration of independence to the
  1911 Chinese Revolution and the Mongolian People's Republic
  proclamation to 1924, with nothing recorded for 1921
  [biger-1995 §mongolia](../sources/biger-1995.md#mongolia); CShapes'
  coded date of 1921-03-13 lines up instead with the 1921 Mongolian
  Revolution, in which Soviet-backed forces retook Urga from the
  White Russian occupier Ungern-Sternberg and restored the Bogd
  Khaganate as a Russian-aligned theocratic state — the change that
  CShapes' "made permanent by post-war agreement" rule
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)
  treats as the effective date of loss. This event is not covered by
  an ingested source beyond the area-matching inference above, so it
  is flagged here as researched-but-uncited-for-the-specific-date; it
  resolves **oq-1921-split-event** below by identifying which
  territorial change the 1921 row boundary corresponds to (Outer
  Mongolia's secession), even though the exact severance date rests on
  CShapes' coding rather than an ingested narrative source.

For state-system-membership context (which per WHEP methodology does
not drive row boundaries): COW lists China (ccode 710) as a single
continuous state-system member from 1860-10-25 to the present, with no
gap anywhere near 1914–1921
[cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion).
That continuity is consistent with — but does not by itself establish
— the CShapes finding that the 1914–1921 window itself is
territorially flat.

**Conclusion:** within 1914–1921, China's WHEP-relevant territory was
constant per CShapes' own time-step boundaries. No internal
territorial change was found, so no further split of this row is
warranted.

## Open questions

### oq-1921-split-event

Largely resolved by direct CShapes measurement (see *Territorial
extent* above): the polygon change at the row's end date, 1921-03-13,
is a loss of ~1,562,924 km², matching Outer Mongolia's area
(1,566,500 km² per
[biger-1995 §mongolia](../sources/biger-1995.md#mongolia)) far more
closely than any Washington Naval Conference concession would. The
remaining open point is that no ingested source narrates the
1921-03-13 date itself (it is inferred here from area-matching plus
general knowledge of the 1921 Mongolian Revolution); a source that
discusses that specific date directly would fully close this
question.
