# Porto (district of Portugal)

## Summary

Porto is one of the historic districts (distritos) of Portugal, on the northwest Atlantic coast, containing the country's second city (also called Porto) at the mouth of the Douro river. The district was created in 1835 as part of the administrative reorganisation that established Portugal's mainland district system, replacing the earlier province structure, and has persisted as the country's officially recognised sub-national reporting unit ever since, even after districts lost most administrative functions when Portugal's 1986 EU accession introduced NUTS statistical regions. This entry is `type: subnational` because Porto is a component of Portugal, not a sovereign polity of its own; it exists to carry data reported at the district level rather than to compete with the national PRT-1800-2025 row for sovereignty-based matching in the family/sovereignty ranking. The district's capital is the city of Porto, and it covers the lower Douro valley and surrounding coastal and inland municipalities, roughly corresponding to the modern Porto NUTS III sub-region.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data reported against the label 'PT_PO' / Porto as a Portuguese sub-national reporting unit, per the routing decision for unit_id PRT-PTPO (country Portugal). That data was previously implicitly headed toward routing under the national row PRT-1800-2025 by default matching logic, or would have been marked unroutable pre-1986 like the non-departure NUTS siblings (PT11, PT15, PTBJ, PTEV, PTFA, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG); both are wrong for the same reason given for Aveiro -- a district is a fraction of the country, not the whole of it, so national-scale figures cannot stand in for district-scale figures, and marking the pre-1986 span unroutable is wrong when the territory already existed under an earlier administration (the distrito, from 1835). The alternative candidate match PNV-1800-1882 is explicitly rejected in the routing decision as an unrelated West African kingdom that merely shares the name 'Porto'. The country convention's own listed departures from the 1986 NUTS-default rule include Aveiro on the basis that it is a historic district predating NUTS reporting under its own historic name rather than a NUTS code; nothing distinguishes Porto's administrative pedigree from Aveiro's, since both are original 1835 mainland districts, so Porto is created here as the second such departure. No historical trading-polity source (e.g. Federico-Tena) treats Porto separately as a customs/trading territory in its own right; the confirmation of distinctness rests instead on Portugal's own administrative law creating and continuously maintaining Porto as a named district since 1835, distinct from its neighbouring districts (Aveiro, Braga, Viseu, Vila Real) and from the country as a whole.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity is listed. Porto district's 1835 creation was part of the same general national reorganisation that replaced the earlier province system and created Aveiro and the other mainland districts; there is no single prior 'Porto' administrative unit distinct enough to record as a predecessor, since the pre-1835 territory was governed under overlapping province (Entre-Douro-e-Minho) and comarca structures that do not map cleanly onto the new district. No successor is listed because the district, though administratively hollowed out after Portugal's 1986 EU accession introduced NUTS statistical regions, has not been formally abolished and remains the reporting unit referenced by the routing decision through the container chain's open end_year of 2025.

## Sourced claims

- Portugal's mainland district (distrito) system, including Porto, was established by the administrative reform of 1835 that replaced the earlier province-based structure, and Porto has been continuously maintained as a named district since that reform.
- Since Portugal's 1986 accession-driven adoption of the EU NUTS statistical framework, most Portuguese districts (including siblings PT11, PT15, PTBJ, PTEV, PTFA, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG) are reported using NUTS codes rather than historic district names, but the country convention records both Aveiro and Porto as explicit exceptions reported under their historic names.

**Is Porto district's 1835 origin confirmed against a Porto-specific source, or only inferred by analogy with Aveiro?**

The routing decision's own routing_concerns flag this directly: the 1835 start year is assumed by analogy with the Aveiro departure recorded in the Portugal country convention, not confirmed against a source specific to Porto's own administrative history.
