# Texas (state of the United States)

## Summary

This entry captures the State of Texas as a subnational reporting unit within the United States, from its admission to the Union in 1845 through the present (2025, per the open-end convention). `type: subnational` because Texas has never held sovereignty independent of the U.S. federal government during this span — the Republic of Texas (1836-1845) predates this entry and is out of scope. The entry exists because no polity row in the table currently matches statistics reported under a Texas-specific label, and the sibling U.S. state California was independently created under the same default rule, establishing precedent for per-state subnational rows keyed to admission year rather than folding state-level data into the national USA chain. The default admission-year start rule applies because Texas is not one of the three enumerated departures from that rule (Alaska, Hawaii, and the 13 original states) — its start year is the year it joined the Union as a state, 1845, distinct from both its prior life as an independent republic and from the national USA polity's own era boundaries.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry exists because no polity row in the table currently matches statistics reported under a Texas-specific label: the routing decision this page implements found "no existing polity matches Texas by name or is Texas itself." The sibling U.S. state California was independently created under the same default create_new rule in the same pass, establishing precedent for per-state subnational rows keyed to admission year rather than folding state-level data into the national USA chain. Texas is a standard U.S. state with no prior polity-table entry to have been mismatched to; the closest wrong match would have been the national row (e.g. USA-1867-1959 or USA-1959-2025), which is roughly 4x Texas's own area and would blend Texas-labeled statistics into 49 other states' totals. The distinctness of Texas as a reporting/administrative unit is confirmed simply by its status as a constituent state of the federal union since 1845, with its own state government, borders, and (in the source data this entry is meant to receive) its own statistical reporting — the same logic already applied to California and, for the pre-1959 era, to Alaska Territory (ALK-1867-1959).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.44_1` ("Texas"), assigned directly rather than as a proxy — this is a `registered_source_feature` route, meaning GADM 4.1 already carries the exact present-day feature and no construction step was needed. `polygon_status: assigned` because Texas's external boundary has been stable since statehood in 1845: the only material change was the 1850 Compromise, in which Texas ceded its claims to parts of present-day New Mexico, Oklahoma, Kansas, Colorado, and Wyoming in exchange for federal assumption of Texas's public debt, plus subsequent minor river-boundary surveys (notably the Red River dispute with Oklahoma, resolved by the Supreme Court in the 1920s-30s). None of these approach the scale of a Trianon-style redrawing, so the same feature can stand for the whole 1845-2025 span; this is flagged as a vintage-risk item in open questions rather than treated as invalidating the assignment.

**Area:** measured from the attached GADM 4.1 polygon at roughly **695,662 km2** (Texas's commonly cited land+water area) — this is a measurement of the polygon, not independent evidence about the historical territory, since no separate stated-area source (e.g. a 19th-century census or yearbook figure specific to Texas) was consulted for this entry. A reader can locate the territory on a modern map as the state of Texas: bordered by Mexico (Rio Grande) to the south/southwest, the Gulf of Mexico to the southeast, Louisiana and Arkansas to the east, Oklahoma to the north (Red River), and New Mexico to the west.

## Predecessors and successors

Texas has no predecessor polity row: it enters the table directly as a new subnational unit at admission (1845), not as a continuation of an earlier entity such as the Republic of Texas (1836-1845), which is out of scope for this page since no polygon or data routing for that period is addressed here. It has no successor row either, since it remains an active U.S. state; end_year 2025 reflects the repository's open-end convention rather than a dissolution, cession, or reorganization event.

## Decisions

### d-code-iso3-subunit-years

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern, using postal abbreviation TX**

57 of 66 existing subnational rows follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, e.g. ESP-CA-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919. Texas is a standard, currently-existing US state with a stable external boundary since admission and no name distinct from the modern polity, so it does not qualify as a bespoke-code case like ALK-1867-1959 (a defunct historical territory whose name, 'Territory of Alaska', differs from the modern state and whose boundaries genuinely differ pre/post-statehood). The existing USA subnational precedent, ALK-1867-1959, used a bespoke code specifically because Alaska Territory (1867-1959) is administratively and geographically distinct from the State of Alaska. Texas has no such split: it was admitted directly as a state in 1845 and has been the same entity since. I therefore chose USA-TX-1845-2025 (postal code TX as SUBUNIT) over a bespoke name, following the dominant pattern rather than the Alaska exception.

### d-container-four-edges

**Container chain uses four edges to tile 1845-2025 with no gap**

The proposed decision only supplied USA-1867-1959 as container, but that single edge would leave 1845-1867 (22 years) uncontained, violating the tiling requirement. I read the polity table's USA national chain (USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025) and added edges for each era that overlaps Texas's 1845-2025 span, clipping the first edge to start at 1845 (Texas's admission year) rather than 1803 (the era's own start). This mirrors how any subnational unit whose span crosses multiple containing-national eras must be represented with one container edge per era rather than skipping intermediate reorganizations of the container itself.

## Open questions

### oq-end-year-open-convention

**end_year=2025 follows the open-end convention, not a real dissolution**

Texas has not dissolved or changed status; end_year 2025 (exclusive) is applied per the repository's open-end convention for still-existing subnational units, matching how the proposed decision treated it. If the harness's convention for 'still current' entries changes (e.g. to a rolling year or a different marker), this page's end_year and the polity_code's embedded year would both need to move in lockstep, since a gate checks that they match. Whether a still-active entry should instead carry no successor placeholder or a different sentinel end_year is unresolved across the polity set.

### oq-polygon-vintage-risk

**GADM 4.1 present-day boundary stands in for the full 1845-2025 span**

The assigned polygon (USA.44_1 from GADM 4.1 admin-1) is a present-day snapshot. Texas's external boundary has been stable since admission with only minor adjustments (the 1850 Compromise cession of claimed territory to New Mexico/Oklahoma/Kansas/Colorado/Wyoming, and the disputed Red River boundary with Oklahoma resolved in the 1920s-30s). These are considered immaterial to the modern polygon, but no measurement was made of how much area the 1845-1850 claimed-but-unceded territory would add if a boundary-accurate 1845 polygon were constructed instead. This is flagged as a vintage-risk gap rather than resolved.
