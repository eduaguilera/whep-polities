# Australian Capital Territory (state of Australia)

## Summary

The Australian Capital Territory (ACT) is the federal territory carved out of New South Wales in 1911 to house the national capital, Canberra, under direct Commonwealth administration rather than as a self-governing state — it did not gain its own elected legislative assembly until 1988/1989. This entry is `type: subnational` because the ACT is, and always has been, a division within the sovereign state of Australia: sovereignty is carried by the national row AUS-1901-2025, and this row exists purely to hold statistics that a source reports at ACT level specifically rather than folding into either New South Wales or the national Australian total. The span starts in 1911, the year of the formal transfer from NSW, and is left open to 2025 following this country's convention of using an open end_year for administrative units that are still current today. No predecessor or successor polity applies: the ACT was created from within a still-continuing NSW, not from a polity that ceased to exist, and it has not since been abolished, merged, or split.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision for unit AUS-AUSTRALIANCAPITALT ("Australian Capital Territory", country Australia), the underlying source(s) report a data span of 1911-2022 for this admin unit; the routing decision does not itemize row counts or which named sources contribute them, which is flagged as an open question here rather than asserted without evidence. Previous matching: no existing polity represented the ACT specifically — data for it would otherwise have had to be absorbed into the national row AUS-1901-2025, which is roughly 3,300x the ACT's own area (7.69M km² vs ~2,358 km²) and would silently blend a small federal enclave's statistics into national Australian totals, or misattributed to New South Wales, from which the ACT was excised but which no longer includes it. What confirms this entity is distinct: the ACT has been a legally and administratively separate Commonwealth territory, distinct from every Australian state, continuously since 1 January 1911 — it has its own postal abbreviation, was carried as its own reporting geography in Australian Bureau of Statistics collections from early in the 20th century (population, and later broader economic statistics), and today has its own legislative assembly (since 1988/1989) and its own seat(s) in the federal parliament, none of which apply to a mere sub-region of NSW.

**Territory description:** The ACT is a small enclave roughly 150 km southwest of Sydney, entirely surrounded by New South Wales, containing the city of Canberra and the surrounding Namadgi National Park uplands. Its modern extent is approximately **2,358 km²** — for scale, about the size of Luxembourg, and under 0.03% of Australia's total land area. This figure comes from published Australian Bureau of Statistics territory-area figures for the ACT, not from any geometry attached to this entry (none is attached yet); once a GADM ADM1 polygon is fetched and assigned, its measured area should be checked against this figure and any material discrepancy noted. The ACT's boundary with New South Wales has been essentially unchanged since the early 20th century, aside from the 1915 separation of the coastal Jervis Bay Territory (~73 km²) as a distinct Commonwealth territory, so a present-day boundary is a reasonable, if unstated, vintage approximation back to 1911 (flagged in the open questions).

## Predecessors and successors

This entry has no predecessor polity: the Australian Capital Territory was created directly by excision from the Colony (later State) of New South Wales in 1911, following the Seat of Government Acceptance Act 1909 and the Seat of Government (Administration) Act 1910, rather than by succession from an earlier distinct polity — the excised land is instead a subset of NSW's own continuous territory, and NSW's own row is not truncated by this creation since NSW remains a state of Australia throughout. There is no successor: the ACT continues to the present (end_year 2025 per the open-ended convention for still-current units), and is not scheduled to merge, split, or be abolished. The only structural neighbour worth noting is the Jervis Bay Territory, transferred from NSW to Commonwealth control in 1915 to give the ACT (a landlocked territory) a sea port; it has been administered as a legally separate Commonwealth territory ever since and is not treated as part of the ACT here (see open question oq-act-boundary-vintage) — if source data cites Jervis Bay Territory specifically, it should get its own row rather than being merged into this one.

## Sourced claims

- The Australian Capital Territory was created by excision from New South Wales under the Seat of Government Acceptance Act 1909 and formally transferred to Commonwealth administration on 1 January 1911.
- The Jervis Bay Territory, a coastal enclave giving the landlocked ACT access to the sea, was transferred separately from New South Wales to the Commonwealth in 1915 and has been administered as a distinct territory rather than as part of the ACT.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-adm1-australia-missing

**Local GADM 4.1 admin-1 file does not currently include Australia**

The registered source family gadm-4.1-adm1 is the correct one for a current Australian state/territory boundary (GADM does digitize the ACT as a distinct ADM1 feature with a stable GID_1 code), but the locally present GADM 4.1 file used by this pipeline is a subset covering 81 countries and Australia is not among them (0 features present for AUS). Fetching the full global GADM 4.1 ADM1 layer, or the Australia-specific extract, would resolve this and let polygon_status move from unassigned to assigned with a real polygon_feature_id. Until then this entry carries no geometry and polygon_area_km2 should not be populated from a placeholder.

### oq-act-boundary-vintage

**ACT boundaries were not fully stable from 1911 to the present**

The Federal Capital Territory as excised from New South Wales in 1911 covered roughly the modern ACT's ~2,358 km2, but the transfer included a later 1915 exchange of the Jervis Bay Territory (a small coastal enclave, ~73 km2, transferred separately for port access and administered as a distinct territory rather than as part of the ACT proper) and some minor early-20th-century boundary surveys before the border with NSW was finally fixed. A future GADM-based polygon reflects present-day boundaries and would be a reasonable but unstated vintage approximation for 1911-1930s; this should be flagged explicitly once a polygon is attached, and Jervis Bay Territory should be checked against whether it has (or needs) its own row rather than being silently folded into or excluded from this one.
