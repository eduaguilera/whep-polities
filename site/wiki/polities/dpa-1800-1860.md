# Duchy Parma

## Summary

The Duchy of Parma and Piacenza was a pre-unification Italian state in the Po Valley, ruled by the House of Bourbon-Parma after the Congress of Vienna (initially by Marie Louise of Austria until 1847). The database tracks it from 1800 until 1860, when it was absorbed into the emerging Kingdom of Italy. Like Modena and Tuscany, Parma was one of the small central Italian duchies that voted by plebiscite to join the Sardinian-led unification movement.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Cliopatria (Duchy of Parma and Piacenza 1815-1847). The duchy lay between the Duchy of Modena to the east, the Kingdom of Sardinia to the west, and the Papal States to the south.

## Sourced claims

- Italian unity was "only finally achieved in the 1860s, under the kingdom of Sardinia, by a combination of astute diplomacy and popular action, particularly Giuseppe Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand, which defeated the French Bourbon and papal forces. Plebiscites ratified the unification and established a monarchy." [biger-1995 §italy](../sources/biger-1995.md#italy)
- Italy's political fragmentation reflected the fact that "the particularistic city-states also laid the base for the political fragmentation of the Italian peninsula that was not to endure until the nineteenth century." The duchies were part of this fragmented system. [biger-1995 §italy](../sources/biger-1995.md#italy)

### oq-pre-1815-status

The duchy was dissolved during the Napoleonic period and reconstituted under Marie Louise of Austria at the Congress of Vienna. The database start year of 1800 spans both periods; the Cliopatria polygon covers 1815-1847 only. The next ingest should clarify whether the Napoleonic period needs separate treatment.
