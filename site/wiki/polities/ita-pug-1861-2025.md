# Puglia (region of Italy)

## Summary

Puglia (Apulia) is the NUTS2 statistical-administrative region occupying the heel of the Italian peninsula, comprising the provinces of Bari, Brindisi, Foggia, Lecce, Taranto, and (since 2004) Barletta-Andria-Trani. This entry captures Puglia as a statistical reporting territory tracked in Italian data panels from the Kingdom of Italy's unification in 1861 through the present. `type: subnational` is used deliberately, not `national`: Italy held sovereignty over this territory throughout, and Puglia was never an independent state. What this row exists to carry is the reporting territory, since Italian and international statistical series (population, agriculture, production) have long been tabulated at the compartimento/regione level distinct from the national Italian total. The 1861 start follows the country convention derived for Italy's regional panel: a region's start_year defaults to the start_year of the earliest national container row whose territory includes it, and Puglia was part of the original Kingdom of Italy at unification (unlike Trentino-Alto Adige/Friuli-Venezia Giulia, annexed in 1919, or Molise, split from Abruzzi e Molise only in 1963) -- so it takes the 1861 default rather than either listed departure.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the Puglia NUTS2 panel unit (unit_id ITA-ITF4) from an Italian regional data panel spanning 1861-2023 (per the country convention's evidence_used note: 'Panel admin_level NUTS, 15 units, coverage 1861-2023'). Before this entry, the only ITA candidates in the polity table were national totals (ITA-1861-1866, ITA-1866-1870, ITA-1870-1919, ITA-1919-2025); a NUTS2 region covering roughly 1/15th of Italy's territory and population is not equivalent to the country as a whole, so routing Puglia's data to a national row would misattribute region-scale figures to all of Italy. Puglia's distinctness as a reporting unit is confirmed by its continuous identity as one of Italy's historical compartimenti statistici (statistical divisions) established at unification and carried forward into the modern NUTS2 classification used by Istat and Eurostat -- the same administrative/statistical entity, under evolving degrees of self-government, for over 160 years.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is available in the GeoPackage for this period. The natural registered source is `gadm-4.1-adm1` (GADM 4.1 administrative level 1), which does publish Italian regions including Puglia, but the locally cached GADM 4.1 subset in this repository (`data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg`) covers only 81 countries and excludes Italy entirely (0 Italian features present). This is a fetch gap, not a source-coverage gap: the route is `registered_source_unfetched` and `polygon_source` is recorded as the registered slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` itself, with `polygon_status: unassigned` until Italy's rows are added to the local cache and a `GID_1` feature for Puglia can be identified.

**Territory description:** Puglia occupies the heel of the Italian boot in the southeast of the peninsula, bordered by the Adriatic Sea to the east, the Ionian Sea to the southeast, and the regions of Molise, Campania, and Basilicata to the west. Its modern extent is essentially stable across the whole 1861-2025 span -- unlike Trentino-Alto Adige or Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Puglia's boundaries were not affected by WWI annexations, and unlike Molise/Abruzzo it was not later split. Its present-day area is approximately **19,540 km2** (a figure from Istat/Eurostat regional statistics, not measured from any attached geometry, since none is attached here). Using present-day GADM adm1 geometry once fetched would be a reasonable proxy for the full span, since Puglia's boundary has been essentially unchanged since unification, with the caveat that GADM reflects the current six-province configuration (post-2004 creation of Barletta-Andria-Trani from parts of Bari and Foggia provinces) rather than any specific historical provincial subdivision -- a sub-regional change that does not affect the region's own outer boundary.

## Decisions

### d-follow-country-convention-1861-start

**Followed the Italy country convention's 1861 default start rather than reconciling against sibling 1970-start entries**

The routing decision's own `routing_concerns` field flags a real inconsistency: existing sibling entries in the table, `ITA-LIG-1970-2025` (Liguria) and `ITA-PIE-1970-2025` (Piemonte), both start in 1970 rather than 1861, even though the Italy country convention states a default unit_start_rule of 1861 for regions that were part of the original Kingdom of Italy (with only Trentino-Alto Adige/Friuli-Venezia Giulia at 1919 and Molise at 1963 as documented departures). Liguria and Piemonte are not among those documented departures either, so their 1970 start appears to reflect an earlier, different application of the convention -- possibly treating 1970 (when Italian regions became elected administrative bodies with councils) as the true system start, rather than 1861 (when the statistical/geographic divisions were first used). This page follows the country convention as currently written (1861 default) rather than the inconsistent precedent set by Liguria/Piemonte, because the convention document is the authoritative, reasoned artifact and the sibling entries look like they predate or diverge from it without documented justification. This is flagged rather than silently resolved because fixing Liguria/Piemonte is out of scope for authoring this single page, and I did not want to introduce a fourth inconsistent value without a decision to reconcile the whole regional set.

## Open questions

### oq-1861-vs-1970-start-inconsistency

**Does Puglia's start_year belong at 1861 (statistical division) or 1970 (administrative region with elected government), given sibling entries disagree?**

Liguria (`ITA-LIG-1970-2025`) and Piemonte (`ITA-PIE-1970-2025`) both start at 1970, not 1861, despite the Italy country convention's default unit_start_rule assigning 1861 to any region that was part of the original Kingdom of Italy. Puglia was such a region and this page uses 1861 per that convention, but if Liguria and Piemonte's 1970 start reflects a considered choice (e.g. treating 1970, when Italian regions gained elected councils and administrative budgets, as the true start of the region as a governance unit, versus 1861 as merely a pre-existing statistical/geographic grouping), then Puglia -- and every other original-Kingdom region entered under the current convention -- may need to be re-dated to 1970 for consistency, with the 1861-1970 data span re-routed to the national ITA rows instead. This needs to be resolved once, for all regions, rather than per-page.

### oq-gadm-italy-fetch-pending

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 polygon for Puglia has not been fetched into the local cache; when will Italy's rows be added?**

The registered source `gadm-4.1-adm1` is the correct polygon source for Puglia (GADM 4.1 does publish Italian regions), but the local cached file at `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg` only covers 81 countries and has zero Italian features. Until Italy's admin-1 rows are fetched into that cache (or the full global GADM 4.1 adm1 layer is obtained), this entry cannot move from `polygon_status: unassigned` to `assigned`, and no `GID_1` feature id can be recorded. This also blocks every other Italian NUTS2 region entry in the table for the same reason.

### oq-barletta-andria-trani-subdivision

**Does the 2004 creation of Barletta-Andria-Trani province (from parts of Bari and Foggia) affect anything beyond internal sub-regional boundaries?**

Puglia's outer regional boundary has not changed since unification, but its internal provincial subdivision changed in 2004 with the creation of a sixth province, Barletta-Andria-Trani, carved from Bari and Foggia provinces. Since this entry tracks the region as a whole, the outer polygon should be unaffected -- but if any data source in the pipeline reports at the province level rather than the region level for years after 2004, that data would need to be aggregated up to the regional boundary used here, and no check has been done for whether any input series does this.
