# Poland (1918-1919)

## Summary

The current CSV tracks this row as Poland from **1918** to
**1919** — a 2-year transitional row [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
Poland regained independence on 11 November 1918 (Armistice
Day) after 123 years of partition between Russia, Austria, and
Prussia (since 1795). The Second Polish Republic was
reconstituted from territory of all three partitioning empires,
making this one of the most complex multi-predecessor situations
in the database.

The CSV lists `predecessor = NA` for this row — this is
partially a **CSV bug**. Poland's territory came from the
Austrian partition (Galicia, from
[auh-1908-1918](auh-1908-1918.md)), the Russian partition (Congress
Poland, from the F228 chain), and the Prussian partition (from
the DEU chain). See
[oq-predecessor-triple-partition](#oq-predecessor-triple-partition).

Poland's borders were highly fluid during 1918-1921, with
territory expanding dramatically through treaties and the
Polish-Soviet War (1919-1921). See
[oq-fluid-borders-1918-1921](#oq-fluid-borders-1918-1921).

Predecessor per CSV: NA (**partial bug** — should reference
multiple predecessors). Successor: [pol-1919-1920](pol-1919-1920.md).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Full CShapes coverage from 1918
  onward [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope).
- **Rapidly expanding territory.** CShapes records Poland from
  1918-11-11 at ~17.03 sq deg, then growing rapidly:
  - 1919-06-28: ~23.36 sq deg (Treaty of Versailles confirmed
    western borders)
  - 1919-09-10: ~33.15 sq deg (Treaty of Saint-Germain settled
    the Galician border)
  - 1920-10-07: ~37.04 sq deg (post-Polish-Soviet War
    expansion)
  - 1921-03-18: ~50.71 sq deg (Treaty of Riga with Soviet
    Russia settled the eastern border)

  Poland's territory nearly tripled between 1918 and 1921.
- **COW entries.** POL, 290, from 1918-11-03 to 1939-09-27
  (first entry, ending with the German-Soviet invasion), then
  from 1945-06-28 onward (restored Poland, shifted westward)
  [cow-state-system-v2024](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md).

## Sourced claims

- **1918-11-11: Poland regains independence.** CShapes records
  Poland from this date at ~17.03 sq deg
  [cshapes-2.0](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md). COW records the start date
  as 1918-11-03 [cow-state-system-v2024](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md) — an
  8-day discrepancy between sources.
- **1919-06-28: Treaty of Versailles.** Confirmed Poland's
  western borders, including the "Polish Corridor" (West
  Prussia/Posen from Germany) and access to the Baltic Sea via
  the Free City of Danzig arrangement
  [cow-state-system-v2024](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md). CShapes records growth
  to ~23.36 sq deg on this date.
- **1921-03-18: Treaty of Riga.** Settled the Polish-Soviet
  border following the Polish-Soviet War (1919-1921). CShapes
  records Poland at ~50.71 sq deg — its maximum interwar extent
  [cshapes-2.0](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md). This event post-dates this
  row but is load-bearing for the Polish chain.

## Contradictions

- **COW vs CShapes start date.** COW records Poland from
  1918-11-03; CShapes from 1918-11-11. An 8-day discrepancy.
  Both dates are defensible — 11 November is Armistice Day
  (Pilsudski assumed command), while 3 November may reflect
  earlier de facto independence declarations. Not material for
  year-level WHEP analysis.

## Open questions

### oq-predecessor-triple-partition

Poland's territory came from three partitioning empires
(Austria-Hungary, Russia, Prussia/Germany). The CSV's
`predecessor = NA` is a bug for the Austrian and German portions
(which are tracked as WHEP polity chains), and arguably also for
the Russian portion (the F228 chain). This is the most complex
multi-predecessor situation in the database. Filing a CSV fix
requires deciding whether to list all three predecessors in the
CSV's single predecessor field (which may not support multiple
values) or to flag this as a structural limitation of the CSV
format.

### oq-fluid-borders-1918-1921

Poland's borders were highly fluid during 1918-1921. CShapes
records five distinct polygon changes in three years (17.03 →
23.36 → 33.15 → 37.04 → 50.71 sq deg). The WHEP CSV splits
this into multiple 1-2 year rows (POL-1918-1919, POL-1919-1920,
etc.), which is consistent with the rapid territorial changes.
However, the specific year-boundaries of the splits may not
correspond to specific territorial events — the Treaty of Riga
(March 1921) is the final border settlement but may not fall on
a row boundary. Needs verification against the full Polish chain.
