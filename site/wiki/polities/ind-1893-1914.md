# India (1893-1914)

## Summary

CSV row for India from **1893** to **1914** — a **22-year row**
covering British India under Crown rule
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW code 750
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

The 1893 start relates to the Durand Line agreement. The 1914
end-of-row event is the outbreak of WWI.

## Territorial extent

The row's polygon is bound to
[cshapes-2.0](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md) feature id 750, coded to the
year 1899 — a single snapshot standing in for the whole 1893–1914
span. Measured directly from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg` in
an equal-area CRS (ESRI:54034), that feature covers **≈4,819,000
km²**. CShapes draws state territory "primarily based on
internationally recognized boundaries" and, for the frontier tracts
still being delimited in this period, may fall back to a placeholder
polygon representing the borders "as they were eventually defined"
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders).
This matters here because two of British India's frontier lines were
still being drawn on paper during exactly this row's span:

- **Durand Line (12 Nov 1893).** The agreement between Mortimer
  Durand and Amir Abdur Rahman Khan fixed the ~2,640 km
  spheres-of-influence boundary between British India and
  Afghanistan, running from Chitral in the north to Balochistan in
  the south. This is the event that opens the row (see Summary): it
  is a frontier *delimitation* of territory already under British
  paramountcy (including tribal agencies and princely states such as
  Chitral), not a transfer of sovereignty from another recognized
  state, so it does not register as a CShapes-coded "change" under
  the size-and-sovereignty test in
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)
  (creation/dissolution, sovereignty transfer, or a >100×100 km
  border adjustment between existing units).
- **McMahon Line (Simla Convention, March 1914).** Sir Henry
  McMahon's line demarcated the British India–Tibet frontier in the
  eastern Himalaya (northeast India/northern Burma), again fixing a
  line through territory Britain already claimed influence over
  rather than annexing land from an internationally recognized
  state; Chinese non-recognition of the Simla Convention makes this
  a contested delimitation rather than a settled sovereignty change.
  It falls right at this row's end year.

Internal administrative reorganizations inside this span — the 1901
creation of the North-West Frontier Province out of Punjab, the
1905 Partition of Bengal and its 1911 reversal, and the 1911 transfer
of the capital from Calcutta to Delhi — moved provincial boundaries
inside British India but did not move the *external* boundary that
the polygon encodes, and are uncited general history (option (b)):
no ingested source documents them, only the Durand/McMahon frontier
events above are source-supported.

**Bottom line:** based on what CShapes and the COW state list say
about British India in this window, the row's external territorial
extent is effectively constant 1893–1914 — the changes that did
happen were frontier delimitations of land already inside Britain's
sphere, not transfers of controlled territory, and neither meets
CShapes' own bar for a coded change. `polygon_area_km2` in the CSV is
still `null` even though the underlying geometry is present and
measurable (≈4,819,000 km²); that is a separate data-completeness gap
from the territorial question, logged as an open question below.

**Data scope: British Provinces only — see the IND-1914-1937 page (added 2026-08-18).** The two
assertions routed here, `india|mitchell|1893-1913` (372 rows) and `india|iia|1909-1913` (75), carry the
bare `india` label, and colonial Indian agricultural statistics conventionally cover the
directly-administered British Provinces rather than the ~500 princely states. This row's polygon
measures **4,815,028 km²**, the whole Indian Empire; the British Provinces were roughly 3.06M km², so
about 37% of the mapped area may contribute no data.

The full accounting — thirteen assertions, 2,227 rows, of which 1,713 pre-independence rows across six
IND-* polities are exposed — is on [ind-1914-1937](ind-1914-1937.md), which is the anchor for this
question rather than repeating it on every row of the chain. `india|mitchell|1893-1913` is banked
`correct`; that verdict is about the ROUTING, which is right in that no better polity exists, not about
the territory being equal.

## Sourced claims

- The row's polygon is CShapes feature id 750, coded to the year
  1899, measured at ≈4,819,000 km² in ESRI:54034 from
  `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- CShapes assigns disputed or not-yet-fully-defined frontier
  territory to whichever state has de facto control, and flags
  early-period polygons in undefined-border regions as placeholders
  representing the borders "as they were eventually defined"
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders).
- CShapes only codes a border adjustment as a territorial "change"
  if it is a creation/dissolution, a sovereignty transfer, or an
  adjustment exceeding 100×100 km between existing recognized units
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes) —
  the Durand Line (1893) and McMahon Line (1914) frontier
  delimitations discussed above are delimitations of territory
  already under British paramountcy, not sovereignty transfers, so
  neither meets this bar.
- The Correlates of War state-system list does **not** carry India
  (ccode 750) as an independent state-system member until
  `1947-08-15`
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion);
  before 1920 COW membership required diplomatic recognition by
  Britain or France as a *separate* sovereign, which British India —
  itself part of the British Empire — never was. This row's
  `cow: 750` value is therefore a forward-looking code label, not
  evidence the COW dataset treats 1893–1914 India as a system member.
- [biger-1995](../sources/biger-1995.md) is ingested as a
  country-by-country boundary reference but explicitly states its
  scope is "international land boundaries only" and excludes
  subnational entries such as "Indian states"
  [biger-1995 §known-limitations](../sources/biger-1995.md#known-limitations) —
  it has no India entry and cannot be cited for this row's borders.

## Status corrected to `draft` (2026-07-24)

This page was flagged **`wiki_status: reviewed`** while containing **no source
citations at all** — its territorial content is a stub. The `reviewed` label is
supposed to mean a human checked the claims against sources, so carrying it here
was misleading: it invited both readers and the verification pipeline to treat
unsourced assertions as settled.

An audit found **10 of 72 `reviewed` pages** in this state — the whole IND chain,
the whole IDN chain, and CHN-1914-1921. All ten are downgraded to `draft`, which
is what they actually are. Nothing about the polity's data or polygon changed;
only the honesty of the label.

To earn `reviewed` this page needs its **Territorial extent** and **Sourced
claims** sections filled from real sources (Biger 1995, CShapes 2.0, the COW
state-system list) with inline citations, and the specific territorial changes
inside its span identified. Until then the assertion-verification pipeline treats
it as an unverified draft and corroborates its claims independently — see
[log decision-reviewed-status-audit](../log.md#decision-reviewed-status-audit).

## Polygon corrected 2026-08-13 (issue 84)

> **Polygon rebuilt on 2026-08-13 to remove Portuguese India and French India (issue 84).**
>
> This row used to publish CShapes 2.0 gwcode 750 unchanged, and CShapes does not carve foreign
> enclaves out of a colonial power's outline. So it claimed the same ground as two other rows:
>
> ```
> before   this row n PTIND-1816-1961    3,719 km2    98.4% of Portuguese India
>          this row n FRIN-1816-1954       520 km2    95.2% of French India
> after    this row n PTIND-1816-1961       13.6 km2
>          this row n FRIN-1816-1954        47.2 km2
> ```
>
> **Neither enclave was ever British.** Portuguese India (Goa, Daman, Diu) was Portuguese until
> India annexed it in **December 1961**; French India (Pondicherry, Karikal, Mahe, Yanam) was
> French until the **October 1954** cession. This row ends in 1913 (`end_year` is exclusive), so both were foreign
> sovereign territory for the whole of its span and the subtraction needs no judgement call —
> which is exactly what could not be said of `IND-1949-2025`, and why that row is untouched.
>
> **Polygon:** `constructed` — `IND-1893-1914` in `scripts/sources/constructed/build.py`, which is
> `_cshapes2_step(750, 1899, 1914)` MINUS `build_ptind_1816_1961()` MINUS
> `build_frin_1816_1954()`. The subtrahends are the *same geometries those two rows publish*, not
> re-derivations, so the pairs cannot drift apart. The CShapes step is named by its **bounds**
> rather than by a feature year because `polygon_feature_year` is ambiguous for part of this
> chain — 1914 matches two steps of gwcode 750 — and the bound form reproduces the step this row
> was already bound to, so nothing but the enclaves moved.
>
> **`polygon_feature_year` is kept** at its old value even though `polygon_source` is now
> `constructed`. The `constructed` source has no `temporal` key in `scripts/sources.yaml`, so the
> year plays no part in resolving the feature — but it still records which CShapes vintage the
> polygon is built from, which is what `04_territory_basis.py` reads to decide whether a row's
> border is `measured` or merely `assumed_constant`. Dropping it to `null` would have reclassified
> this row as "polygon present but no recorded vintage year", which would be false.
>
> ```
> CShapes 750 `1899-1914` step    4,819,795 km2
> minus Portuguese India and French India   -4,246
> after the cut              4,815,549 km2
> published                  4,815,028 km2   (write-time simplification, -0.011%)
> ```
>
> **The residual 13.6 + 47.2 km² is a simplification artefact, not the recipe.** Measured on the
> builder's own geometry the enclaves are gone to 0.00 km²; `scripts/build_database.py` then
> applies a Douglas-Peucker simplification on write, which moves every boundary — including the
> two new holes — and puts a thin rim back. That same step is why the published area is 0.011%
> under the computed one. 61 km² is 0.001% of this row; it is recorded rather than described as
> zero.
>
> **What this does not fix:** `IND-1949-2025` still contains both enclaves in full (3,719 and 520
> km²), because that row spans 1949–2025 and therefore straddles both annexation dates — for part
> of it the enclaves genuinely were Indian. Correcting it means splitting the row at 1954 and
> 1961, a periodisation change to a major national family. **That split was considered and
> declined on 2026-08-13** — it would rename the modern-India polity code, which is referenced
> outside this repository, for a 0.13% correction, and Sikkim sits in the same polygon at
> 6,988 km² untouched. The overstatement is documented on
> [ind-1949-2025](ind-1949-2025.md) instead.
