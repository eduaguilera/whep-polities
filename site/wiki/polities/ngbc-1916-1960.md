# Nigeria and British Cameroons (combined reporting unit, 1916-1960)

## Summary

Colonial Nigeria together with British Cameroons, the western part of German Kamerun that Britain
occupied in 1916 and held as a League of Nations mandate from 1922 and a UN trust territory from
1946. British Cameroons had no separate administration: it was governed from Lagos as part of
Nigeria, its northern strip with Nigeria's Northern Provinces and its southern strip with the
Eastern Provinces. In 1961 the Northern Cameroons joined Nigeria and the Southern Cameroons joined
the Federal Republic of Cameroon.
[wikipedia-french-africa-mandates-2026-08-13 §french-cameroon](../sources/wikipedia-french-africa-mandates-2026-08-13.md#french-cameroon)

Because it was administered as part of Nigeria, statistics often counted it in Nigeria's totals.
This row is that combined unit. Both parts have their own rows ([nga-1914-1960](nga-1914-1960.md),
[bcm-1916-1961](bcm-1916-1961.md)), so it is typed `aggregate`, as
[gct-1919-1956](gct-1919-1956.md) (Gold Coast plus British Togoland) is.

## Why this entry exists

Mitchell's *International Historical Statistics: Africa, Asia and Oceania* footnotes two of its
Nigeria columns as including British Cameroons (issue 688):

- C5 (output of cocoa beans), note 8: "Including British Cameroon from 1945 to 1959." Layer-B
  `nigeria` cacao, 1945-1959: **15 rows**.
- C11 (numbers of livestock), note 13: "Statistics to 1950 include British Cameroons." Layer-B
  `nigeria` asses, cattle, goats, horses, sheep and pigs, 1920-1950 (`page_8_table_1`): **133 rows**.

All 148 had been routed to NGA-1914-1960, whose CShapes polygon is Nigeria without the mandate, so
they sat on a territory about 9% smaller than the one they count. They are moved here by
item-scoped corrections in `data/final/source_label_item_corrections.csv`, because the same label
and years carry series that are Nigeria alone (cotton, groundnuts, rubber and the cacao before 1945).

**FAO 1952 reports the same unit.** Its labels `Nigeria and British cameroon(s)` and `Nigeria and Br
Cameroons` (rice, cassava and cacao 1950-1951, and the 1951 land-use table; 9 rows) had resolved to no
polity at all. They now route here by alias. FAO's own 1951 `use total` for the unit is 96,672
thousand ha, that is 966,720 km², 1.8% above this polygon: an independent statement of the combined
territory, and the best corroboration this row has.

The 1950 livestock cells read 13,000 in four items (cattle, goats, sheep, pigs), which is a broadcast value recorded separately as
`nga-1950-livestock-broadcast-13000`. It moves with its series and stays recorded.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status: `assigned`.** `constructed`: the union of CShapes 2.0 gwcode 475 (Nigeria,
1914-1960 step, 862,547 km²) and gwcode 472 (British Cameroons, 1922-1961 step, 87,444 km²), both
sampled at 1930. The union measures **949,991 km²** (ESRI:54034).
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)

- **Corroboration.** CShapes' Nigeria after the 1961 plebiscites (gwcode 475, 1961-2019 step) is
  908,494 km², that is 862,547 plus 45,947 km² of the Northern Cameroons. The Southern Cameroons,
  which went to Cameroon, make up the rest of the 87,444 km² mandate.
- **Why `assigned`.** Both parts are CShapes' own features for these years, and nothing is
  estimated.
- **1916-1922.** CShapes draws British Cameroons from 1919 (the provisional 1916 line and the 1919
  Milner-Simon line differ). The rows this polity receives are all 1920 or later, so the early
  years are not exercised.

## Decisions

- **Span 1916-1960**: from the British occupation of the Cameroons strip to Nigeria's independence,
  which is where NGA-1914-1960 ends.
- **Only the footnoted series move.** Mitchell's other Nigeria tables have no such note.
