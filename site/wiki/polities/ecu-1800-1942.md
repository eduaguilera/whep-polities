# Ecuador (to 1942)

## Summary

This row tracks Ecuador from the **database floor (1800)** to
**1942** — a **143-year row**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Ecuador was
part of Gran Colombia until 1830, then independent. The 1942 split
corresponds to the Protocol of Rio de Janeiro (29 January 1942),
in which Ecuador lost significant Amazonian territory to Peru.
COW: ECU, ccode=130.

**The 1942 Rio Protocol is not the only territorial change in this span, and it is the only one this
row models — added 2026-08-18.** Ecuador also ceded territory in the north-east (the Putumayo, Napo and
Caquetá basins) to Colombia under the Muñoz Vernaza-Suárez Treaty of 1916, ratified 1917, and the
Ecuador-Peru frontier remained an actively contested line throughout the period until the 1941 war. So
the reporting territory behind data routed here was not constant, and `ecuador|juan|1900-1941` is
`best_available` rather than `verified_equal` for that reason.

**But the size of that mid-span change is smaller here than the treaty figures suggest, and it is worth
being precise about why.** Measured from the committed geometries:

```
ECU-1800-1942   341,715 km²   CShapes vintage 1886
ECU-1942-2025   255,241 km²   CShapes vintage 1942
                 -86,474 km²   -25%, attributed here to the Rio Protocol
```

The 1916 cession is commonly given as roughly 180,000 km². That is larger than the entire 1886-to-1942
reduction this database records, and the 1886 extent of 341,715 km² is itself far below Ecuador's
maximal Amazonian claims. The reason is that the Putumayo and Caquetá basins were **claimed** rather
than administered: CShapes maps effective control, so most of what the 1916 treaty formally relinquished
was never attributed to Ecuador here in the first place. A page reading the treaty literally would
expect a change this database does not contain, and would be wrong about the polygon rather than about
the history.

**What that leaves unresolved** is the residue: some part of the 1916 cession was effective territory,
and the 1886 vintage predates it, so years after 1917 sit on a slightly pre-cession polygon. The
database has no finer Ecuador row to carry the distinction. Recorded as `oq-1916-colombia-cession`.

### oq-1916-colombia-cession

This row's polygon is CShapes at its **1886** vintage, which predates the 1916/1917 Muñoz
Vernaza-Suárez cession to Colombia. Most of what that treaty relinquished was claimed rather than
administered and so was never in the CShapes extent (see Territorial extent above), but the part that
was effective territory is inside this polygon and outside post-1917 Ecuador.

Is that residue large enough to justify splitting the row at 1917? Unquantified here — it would need a
1917-or-later CShapes step for ECU compared against the 1886 one, which is a measurement someone can
make from the same source the polygon already comes from. Until then the years after 1917 are carried
on a mildly over-large polygon, which is recorded rather than corrected.
