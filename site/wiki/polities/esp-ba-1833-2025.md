# Badajoz (province of Spain)

## Summary

Badajoz is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province -- the same reform that produced the existing sibling entries ESP-CA-1833-2025 (Cantabria), ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid). It is the largest province in Spain by area and, together with Cáceres to its north, makes up the autonomous community of Extremadura, formed in 1983. This entry is type: subnational because Badajoz is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated the province separately, at NUTS3 code ES431, since long before NUTS existed under its administrative predecessor. The routing decision for unit ES-ES431 found no existing polity row for this territory and proposed creating ESP-BA-1833-2025 following the country's default province-start convention (1833), noting that Badajoz is not one of the enumerated departures from that default (unlike the Canary Islands split) and remains a current administrative province.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES431 ("Badajoz"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling Spanish provincial units (ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-MD and others created from the same batch, e.g. ES112-ES241), following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, since Badajoz is not among the enumerated start-year departures like the Canary Islands split) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: no existing polity row in the database covers this exact territory -- only the national Spain polity (ESP-1800-2025) and unrelated provinces (A Coruña, Madrid) share the ESP iso3, none of which represents Badajoz's own reporting geography. Absent this entry, ES431's data would have had no province-level destination and would have needed to fall back to the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, roughly 23x larger by area (505,000 km2 vs Badajoz's ~21,766 km2) and incapable of representing province-level series without collapsing them into the national total. What confirms distinctness: Badajoz's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES431) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entries ESP-CA-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate. It is also, together with Cáceres, one of only two provinces making up Extremadura, so provincial-level series for Badajoz cannot be recovered from any autonomous-community aggregate either.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign (IGN provinces.gpkg, id_column=cpro), with polygon_feature_id proposed as "BA" pending confirmation of the exact cpro code (06) against the source file -- this repository already uses mapspain-ign for six other Spanish provinces created by the same 1833 reform (ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-OR, ESP-PO), so Badajoz fits the same convention, but as with those entries the feature has not yet been confirmed as fetched and attached. Territory description: Badajoz occupies the southwestern part of Spain, forming (with Cáceres to the north) the autonomous community of Extremadura. It borders Portugal's Alentejo region along its entire western edge -- the historic frontier city of Elvas sits just across the border from the provincial capital, Badajoz city, on the Guadiana river. To the east and southeast it borders the provinces of Ciudad Real and Córdoba; to the south, Huelva and Seville. The Guadiana river crosses the province west to east through the Vega Baja lowlands before turning south toward the Algarve; the Sierra Morena forms part of the province's southern edge. Badajoz is Spain's largest province by area, at approximately 21,766 km2 per INE -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low by the same reasoning already applied to the other ESP provincial entries, though this has not been individually verified for Badajoz's specific boundary (see open question).

## Predecessors and successors

Badajoz has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory now covered by the province was organized under the historic Kingdom of Castile's administrative and military divisions of Extremadura (including the Order of Santiago's territorial holdings and the Badajoz-Llerena partido structure), which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CA-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain, and since 1983 has formed one of the two provinces of the autonomous community of Extremadura, as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish provincial units.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-esp-ba

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern, not a bespoke name**

Badajoz is a standard modern Spanish province with no historical name of its own distinct from its administrative status (unlike ALK/HYD/RYU, which are historical territories with proper names predating or independent of their current administrative framing). Following the dominant pattern established across the majority of subnational rows and the existing ESP subnational precedents (ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-OR, ESP-PO), the code is ESP-BA-1833-2025, using BA as the subunit abbreviation (Badajoz's INE/ISO 3166-2 province code is BA, cpro 06). Start and end years in the code (1833, 2025) equal the frontmatter start_year/end_year exactly, satisfying the code/frontmatter equality gate.

### d-container-single-edge-esp-ba

ESP-1800-2025 is the national Spain row and spans 1800-2025, fully covering this polity's 1833-2025 span with no truncation on either end; a single edge with start_year=1833, end_year=2025 exactly matches the polity's own span, so it tiles the whole span with no gap and no edge falling outside either party's range, exactly as done for ESP-CA-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025.

### oq-badajoz-border-vintage

**Portuguese border stability across 1833-2025 unverified for this specific province**

Badajoz shares Spain's longest single-province land border with Portugal. While the general claim of Spanish provincial boundary stability since 1833 is asserted by convention for all ESP provinces, the Spain-Portugal international border along Badajoz's western edge has its own separate treaty history (e.g. 19th and 20th century boundary demarcation agreements, and disputed enclaves such as Olivenza/Olivença, which Portugal continues to claim despite de facto Spanish administration since 1801). Whether the mapspain-ign polygon, once fetched, represents the current de facto Spanish administrative boundary (including Olivenza) consistently across the full 1833-2025 span, or reflects any vintage drift, has not been checked and should be verified before treating the attached geometry as authoritative for the whole span.
