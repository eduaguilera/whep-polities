# Ashanti Empire

## Summary

The Ashanti Empire (also Asante) was a powerful Akan kingdom in the forest zone of modern Ghana, founded c. 1670 under Asantehene Osei Tutu I. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1896, when Britain defeated the empire in the Fourth Anglo-Ashanti War and established a protectorate. Biger notes that Ghana's colonial territory became the Gold Coast and that "Gold Coast became an independent state on 6 March 1957, adopting the name Ghana" — the name change honoring the pre-colonial polity tradition. The end year 1896 marks the British capture of Kumasi and deposition of Asantehene Agyeman Prempeh I. The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024) + Cliopatria, with 20 Cliopatria time-steps from 1797 to 1894 covering the empire's territorial evolution.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024) + Cliopatria (20 time-steps, 1797–1894). The Ashanti heartland was the Kumasi plateau in modern central Ghana, with approximately 154,508 km² at its maximum extent. The empire's reach fluctuated considerably: at peak it extended north toward Dagbon and south to the coast, with tributary states along the frontier. The Cliopatria multi-step polygon reflects this expansion and contraction across the 19th century. After 1896 the territory passed to the British Gold Coast colony, represented by [GHA-1898-1956](gha-1898-1956.md).

## Contradictions

None identified. The WHEP end year 1896 corresponds to the British military defeat and capture of Kumasi; the formal protectorate declaration and final annexation came in 1901, which would be an alternative end-date under a stricter sovereignty definition. The database follows 1896 (de facto British control); this is noted but not treated as a contradiction since WHEP end dates mark loss of meaningful independence.
