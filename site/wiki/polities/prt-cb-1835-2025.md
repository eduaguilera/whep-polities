# Castelo Branco (district of Portugal)

## Summary

Castelo Branco is one of Portugal's historic mainland districts (distritos), located in the eastern interior of the country (Beira Baixa), bordering Spain (Extremadura and Castile-La Mancha) to the east. Like Aveiro (PRT-AV-1835-2025), Castelo Branco was created as part of the 1835 administrative reform that replaced Portugal's older province structure with the district system, and it has persisted as the country's officially recognised sub-national reporting unit ever since, even as districts lost most administrative functions after the 1986 introduction of NUTS statistical regions. This entry is `type: subnational` because Castelo Branco is a component of Portugal, not a sovereign polity of its own; it exists to carry data reported at the district level rather than to compete with the national PRT-1800-2025 row for sovereignty-based matching. The district's capital is the city of Castelo Branco, and its territory covers the eastern Beira Baixa region including the municipalities of Castelo Branco, Covilhã (partially, shared historically with other divisions), Fundão, Idanha-a-Nova, Oleiros, Penamacor, Proença-a-Nova, Sertã, and Vila Velha de Ródão.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data reported against the label 'PT_CB' / Castelo Branco as a Portuguese sub-national reporting unit, per the routing decision for unit_id PRT-PTCB, which records 96.2% observed/blank-method coverage across 1880-2023. That data was previously routed toward the national row PRT-1800-2025 by default matching logic, which is wrong for the same reason it was wrong for Aveiro: a district is a fraction of the country, not the whole of it, so national-scale figures cannot stand in for district-scale figures without inflating implied population/area/production by roughly an order of magnitude. The routing decision treats Castelo Branco as a departure from the country convention's stated 1986 NUTS-default rule by direct analogy to Aveiro, the convention's own listed departure example, on the grounds that Castelo Branco is also a distrito created in the 1835 reform whose district-level identity long predates the NUTS framework. No historical trading-polity source (e.g. Federico-Tena) treats Castelo Branco separately, since districts are an internal Portuguese administrative unit rather than an independent customs or trading territory; the confirmation of distinctness rests instead on Portugal's own administrative law creating and continuously maintaining Castelo Branco as a named district since 1835, distinct from its neighbouring districts (Guarda, Coimbra, Santarém, Portalegre) and from the country as a whole. The routing decision itself flags that this analogy is not fully confirmed: unlike Aveiro, Castelo Branco is not explicitly named as a departure case in the evidence, so its 1835 start year rests on the general historical fact of the 1835 reform rather than a convention entry that names it individually.

**Territory description:** Castelo Branco district covers roughly 6,675 km2 of eastern interior Portugal (Beira Baixa), Portugal's third-largest district by area, stretching from the Serra da Estrela foothills in the north-west to the Spanish border (Extremadura) in the east and south-east, and bounded by Guarda district to the north, Coimbra to the west, Santarém to the south-west, and Portalegre to the south. On a modern map it corresponds approximately to the present-day Castelo Branco NUTS III sub-region, including the cities of Castelo Branco, Covilhã (partly), and Fundão, and the Serra da Gardunha and Tejo (Tagus) river valley. No polygon-derived km2 figure is attached to this entry, since none is assigned; the ~6,675 km2 figure above is a territory-description estimate from the modern district's administrative extent, not a geometry measurement.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity is listed. Castelo Branco district's 1835 creation was part of the same general national reorganisation that created Aveiro, replacing the earlier province system, so there is no single prior 'Castelo Branco' administrative unit to treat as a predecessor -- the pre-1835 territory was governed under overlapping province (Beira) and comarca structures that do not map cleanly onto the new district. No successor is listed because the district, though administratively hollowed out after 1986, has not been formally abolished and remains the reporting unit referenced by the routing decision through the container chain's open end_year of 2025.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-container-span

**Use PRT-CB code form and a single container edge spanning 1835-2025**

Followed the dominant 57/66 subnational pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, using PRT-CB-1835-2025 rather than a bespoke code, since Castelo Branco is an ordinary administrative district and not a historical territory with its own separate identity comparable to Alaska Territory or Hyderabad. This mirrors the precedent set by PRT-AV-1835-2025 (Aveiro), the country's first subnational entry, which used the same <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT> form with a two-letter district abbreviation rather than the NUTS code used for the 1986-cutover siblings (PT11, PT15, etc.). The container edge uses a single row (PRT-1800-2025, 1835-2025) because the polity table shows only one PRT national-era row spanning the country's whole 1800-2025 existence, so a single edge fully tiles the span with no gap, unlike cases where the container itself changed identity mid-span. I depart from the routing decision's flagged concern -- that Castelo Branco is not explicitly listed as a named departure case the way Aveiro is -- by following the routing decision's own reasoning (the 1835 reform applies uniformly to all mainland districts, and 96.2% data coverage from 1880 argues against truncating the span to 1986) while flagging this as unconfirmed in the open questions below, per the routing_concerns note asking for confirmation from data/final/nuts_code_names.csv or a Portuguese administrative history source.

## Open questions

### oq-castelobranco-polygon-source

**What source should eventually supply Castelo Branco's boundary polygon?**

No registered source currently carries Portuguese district-level boundaries. The routing decision's candidates are Eurostat's NUTS shapefiles or a full (non-subset) GADM adm1/adm2 release for Portugal; neither is registered in scripts/sources.yaml nor present in local geodata, and licence terms for either have not been checked. Whichever is adopted, using a present-day NUTS III/district boundary to represent the district back to 1835 would be a real anachronism -- district boundaries have likely shifted at the margins (municipality reassignments, e.g. parts of Covilhã shared with neighbouring districts) over 190 years -- and that vintage risk should be stated explicitly if/when a polygon is assigned, per the routing decision's own vintage_risk note.

### oq-castelobranco-1835-departure-unconfirmed

**Is the 1835 start year for Castelo Branco actually confirmed, or only assumed by analogy to Aveiro?**

The routing decision explicitly flags that Castelo Branco, unlike Aveiro, is not named as an explicit departure case in the country convention's evidence for the 1986 NUTS-default rule. The 1835 start year here rests on the general historical fact that all mainland Portuguese districts were created in that reform, not on a convention entry naming Castelo Branco specifically. This should be verified against data/final/nuts_code_names.csv or a Portuguese administrative history source before other similarly-unconfirmed sibling districts are created using the same reasoning, since if the analogy is wrong for Castelo Branco it may also be wrong for other 1880-1986 data currently unrouted for lack of a district-level polity.
