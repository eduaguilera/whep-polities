# Haute-Savoie

## Summary

Haute-Savoie is one of the two departments (with Savoie) created in 1860 when the Kingdom of Sardinia ceded Savoy to France under the Treaty of Turin, ratified by a plebiscite of the Savoyard population that same year. Before 1860 the territory was part of the Sardinian-ruled Duchy of Savoy, never part of Revolutionary France's 1790 departmental map, so it does not fit the default 1790 start rule used for most French departments — its start_year instead follows the annexation date, a piece of the department's own administrative history. `type: subnational` because this is a French administrative subdivision, not a sovereign polity in its own right: sovereignty over the territory runs through the FRA national chain (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), and this entry exists solely to carry the department-level reporting territory so that data keyed to "Haute-Savoie" rather than to all of France is not misattributed to the roughly 220x larger national row. The department has kept its 1860 boundaries essentially unchanged since creation, so a single row spanning 1860-2025 is appropriate rather than splitting on any later administrative reorganisation (there was none for this unit).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is created to carry any data source that reports statistics for "Haute-Savoie" specifically, as opposed to all of France — the same kind of reporting-unit mismatch documented in the Manchuria exemplar, where a regional label was previously routed to a national row roughly an order of magnitude too large. Before this entry existed, the routing decision noted that "no existing FRA polity IS this territory (only national-level FRA rows exist)" — so any Haute-Savoie-labelled row would have been mis-assigned to FRA-1800-1871/FRA-1871-1919/FRA-1919-2025, which cover all of France (~550,000 km² national vs. ~4,388 km² here, roughly 125x). What confirms Haute-Savoie is a distinct, nameable administrative entity rather than an arbitrary regional label is straightforward: it is one of France's ~96 metropolitan departments, created by a specific historical act (the 1860 Treaty of Turin annexation and plebiscite) rather than an informal geographic grouping, and it has held continuous, unchanged administrative status since — the same criterion (a real administrative or reporting unit that sources tabulate on its own terms) used to justify subnational rows elsewhere in this database (e.g. the Manchuria region entries, department-level rows for other countries such as ESP-AS, ESP-CA, DZA-CVD).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. None of the registered sources in this repository carry French department-level administrative boundaries: `gadm-4.1-adm1` (the natural candidate, since GADM does publish department polygons globally) only ships a subset of 81 countries in the locally fetched file, and France is not among them; `cshapes` only carries France at the whole-country level; no union or difference of existing registered features can produce a department boundary, because none of them subdivide France's territory at all. A correct polygon would come from IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product (the French national mapping agency's authoritative department layer) or from a full (not locally-truncated) fetch of GADM adm1 — both are named as an open question below rather than declared as `polygon_source`, since neither is registered and fetched in this repository today.

**Territory description (independent of any polygon):** Haute-Savoie is the French department occupying the Alpine territory between Lake Geneva and Mont Blanc, in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region, bordering Switzerland (Geneva canton and Valais) to the north and east and Italy's Aosta Valley to the southeast across the Mont Blanc massif. Its prefecture is Annecy; other principal towns include Thonon-les-Bains and Chamonix-Mont-Blanc. The department's area is approximately **4,388 km²**, a figure drawn from its administrative definition (INSEE/IGN), not measured from any geometry attached to this entry — no geometry is attached, per the polygon status above. This area figure is stated here as the territory-locating fact the page-requirements spec asks for, and must not be confused with a polygon-derived measurement, since there is none.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row: before 1860 this territory was the Duchy of Savoy under the Kingdom of Sardinia, which is not carried in this database as a distinct polity, so `predecessor` is empty rather than pointing at a Sardinian row that doesn't exist here. No successor: Haute-Savoie remains a French department today, so `end_year` is open at 2025 (exclusive) and `successor` is empty. The department's own administrative status has not changed since creation in 1860 — no split, merger, or renaming — so there is nothing for this entry to hand off to besides itself continuing.

## Sourced claims

- The Treaty of Turin (24 March 1860) ceded the Duchy of Savoy from the Kingdom of Sardinia to France, and the annexation was ratified by a plebiscite of the Savoyard population held in April 1860, after which the ceded territory was organized into the two departments of Savoie and Haute-Savoie.
- Haute-Savoie has an official administrative area of approximately 4,388 km2 as reported by INSEE/IGN department statistics, with Annecy as its prefecture.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-precedent

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern despite no existing FRA precedent**

France has zero existing subnational rows, so this entry sets its own precedent rather than following one. I chose to follow the dominant pattern observed across the other 57 subnational rows (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end, e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919) rather than inventing a bespoke code, because Haute-Savoie is a standard administrative subdivision (a department) rather than a historical territory with its own name independent of the modern country's administrative map — unlike the three bespoke cases (ALK, HYD, RYU), which are former sovereign or quasi-sovereign entities. The subunit abbreviation HSAV was chosen as a readable, unambiguous stand-in for Haute-Savoie; there is no ISO 3166-2 code cited in the routing decision to use instead, so this is a judgment call and a future entry for the sibling department Savoie should use a parallel convention (e.g. FRA-SAV).

### d-start-year-1860

**Used the 1860 annexation date rather than the extract's 1862 data start**

The routing decision's proposed start_year (1860) follows the department's own creation date rather than the data extract's earliest observed year (1862), consistent with the routing_reasoning that this territory wasn't French before 1860 so the generic 1790-default rule doesn't apply. I adopted 1860 as stated in the decision input rather than 1862, on the grounds that the territory (and hence the polity this row represents) existed for those two years even though the located data series happens to start in 1862; the small gap is inherent to when statistical collection began, not to when the department was created.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-department

**No department-level polygon source is registered for France**

This entry has polygon_status: unassigned because no registered source in this repository carries French department boundaries. Two remedies were identified but not executed here: (1) fetch a complete, unrestricted GADM 4.1 adm1 layer (the locally cached copy is truncated to 81 countries and excludes France) and register it as a source; (2) register IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product, which is the authoritative French national mapping agency's department-level layer and would avoid GADM's licensing and vintage uncertainties. Either would very likely need only a single modern-vintage fetch, since Haute-Savoie's boundaries have been stable since 1860 (low vintage risk, per the routing decision), but the licence terms for ADMIN-EXPRESS were explicitly flagged as unchecked and should be verified before use.

### oq-annexation-date-verification

**The 1860 annexation date is stated but not independently verified against a primary French administrative-history source in this repository**

The routing decision explicitly flagged: 'Confirm the exact annexation/creation date (1860) against an authoritative French administrative history source rather than general knowledge, since this was not independently verified via file lookup in this repo.' This page repeats the 1860 date (Treaty of Turin, ratified by plebiscite) from general historical knowledge, consistent with the routing decision's own framing, but no file lookup or citation check against an authoritative administrative-history source (e.g. INSEE's departmental creation records, or a French legal/historical archive) was performed as part of authoring this page. If a future check finds a different creation date for the department as opposed to the annexation treaty date, start_year may need to move.

### oq-siblingdepartment-savoie

**The sibling department Savoie (created by the same 1860 annexation) has no corresponding entry yet**

Savoy was split into the two departments of Savoie and Haute-Savoie by the same 1860 annexation and plebiscite. This page only creates the Haute-Savoie row per the routing decision it implements; whether a parallel FRA-SAV(...)-1860-2025 row is needed depends on whether any data source in this database's queue reports statistics keyed to 'Savoie' specifically, which was outside the scope of the decision this page implements. If such data exists or arrives later, that row should use a consistent code convention with this one (FRA-<SUBUNIT>-1860-2025).
