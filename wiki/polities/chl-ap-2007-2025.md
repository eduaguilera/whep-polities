# Arica y Parinacota

## Summary

Arica y Parinacota is one of Chile's sixteen first-order administrative regions, created in 2007 by splitting the northernmost communes off the former Tarapacá region. This page covers it as `type: subnational` from its 2007 creation to the present (open end_year 2025 per the country's convention for still-current units), matching the span the routing decision assigned it: data for this territory before 2007 is treated as belonging to the national Chile polity because no distinct regional administration existed yet to receive it, while genuinely regional statistics from 2007 onward belong here. The region sits at Chile's far north, bordering Peru and Bolivia, and includes the historically contested port city of Arica -- Chilean since the 1929 Treaty of Lima, following a joint-administration period after the 1883 Treaty of Ancón. The entry exists because the underlying evidence source ties a set of largely reconstructed (interpolated/scaled/luh2-calibrated) values to this specific present-day boundary, and the routing decision's own reasoning distinguishes the 2007-present genuinely-regional segment from the pre-2007 back-cast segment that has no dedicated Tarapacá-era polity to receive it.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: this polity is created to carry values for the Chilean administrative unit "Arica y Parinacota" (unit_id CHL-AP) in an evidence source whose row count and exact year range are given in the routing decision as covering roughly 1900-2025, split between an overwhelmingly reconstructed (back_cast) pre-2007 segment and a segment reported against the actual 2007-created regional boundary from 2007 onward. Previously matched to: the country convention's container chain names only national Chile polities, so absent this entry all of this unit's rows -- including the genuinely regional post-2007 ones -- would route to the national CHL-1902-2025 polity, which is roughly 45x this region's area and would mix a distinct sub-national reporting territory into national totals. What confirms distinctness: Chilean law itself (Law 20.175, 2007) created Arica y Parinacota as a formal first-order region with its own government, statistical reporting (INE regional accounts), and administrative boundary distinct from both the national total and the remaining Tarapacá region it was split from -- this is a legally and statistically distinct reporting unit, not merely a redrawn map line.

**Why this entry exists:** the region did not exist administratively before 11 October 2007, when Law 20.175 split it off from the former I Región (Tarapacá). Before that date, statistics for this territory were necessarily reported at the national or Tarapacá-region level; the routing decision found the evidence source's pre-2007 values for this unit are overwhelmingly reconstructed (interpolated/scaled/luh2-calibrated) rather than directly observed, consistent with back-projecting onto a boundary that did not yet exist as its own reporting unit. No candidate Tarapacá-era subnational polity exists in this project to receive that back-cast segment more precisely, so 1976-2006 was routed to the national Chile polity (CHL-1902-2025) instead, and this new polity was created to carry the genuinely regional 2007-2025 segment on its own boundary.

**Territory description:** Arica y Parinacota occupies Chile's extreme north, bordering Peru along the Ancón-line frontier and Bolivia along the Altiplano to the east, with the Pacific coast to the west. It comprises the Arica and Parinacota provinces and four communes (Arica, Camarones, Putre, General Lagos), running from the coastal city of Arica up through the Atacama foothills into the high Andean plateau near Lauca National Park. The region's officially cited area is approximately **16,873 km2** — a figure from Chilean government/INE regional statistics describing the modern administrative boundary, not a measurement of any polygon attached to this page, since none is yet assigned here.

## Sourced claims

- Arica y Parinacota was created as Chile's XV Región by Law 20.175, promulgated 11 October 2007, splitting off from the former I Región (Tarapacá), which itself lost its northern communes to the new region and was reconstituted around Iquique.
- The region's capital is the city of Arica, and it consists of two provinces, Arica and Parinacota, encompassing the communes of Arica, Camarones, Putre and General Lagos.
- Chile's title to the city and province of Arica was settled by the 1929 Treaty of Lima with Peru, following joint administration arrangements dating to the 1883 Treaty of Ancón that ended the War of the Pacific; the Tacna-Arica dispute over this territory ran for nearly five decades before the 1929 settlement.
