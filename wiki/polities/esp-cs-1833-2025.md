# Castellón/Castelló (province of Spain)

## Summary

Castellón (Castelló de la Plana in Valencian) is one of Spain's 50 provinces, located on the Mediterranean coast in the northern part of the Valencian Community, between Tarragona to the north and Valencia province to the south. This entry is `type: subnational` because Castellón is a first-order administrative division of the sovereign Spanish state, never an independent polity — it exists in this table to carry province-level statistical reporting that the national row ESP-1800-2025 cannot, since national totals do not equal Castellón's own figures. The province was created in the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform, which replaced Spain's older kingdom- and region-based divisions with 50 provinces roughly tracking the historical Kingdom of Valencia's northern territory, and it has persisted with essentially unchanged boundaries to the present. This page is one of a batch of Spanish NUTS/province-level entries created because sibling provinces in the same statistical series (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra) were consistently unmatched to any existing polity and required their own rows, and Castellón follows the identical pattern: no existing ESP subnational row covers its territory, and it is not the same unit as any neighboring province.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures province-level data reported for Castellón (ESP-ES522 in the NUTS unit list this batch works from) for Spain, keyed to the Spanish national iso3 ESP, spanning 1833-2025. The routing decision this page implements notes that no row count or specific source series is cited in the underlying unit record beyond the NUTS classification itself — the polity is being created ahead of, or alongside, statistical series that will route to it, following the same batch pattern already applied to six sibling provinces (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra). Before this entry existed, any data reported for Castellón specifically had no correct home: it could only be misrouted to the national ESP-1800-2025 total (which is roughly 60x too large in area and aggregates all 50 provinces) or to an unrelated province like ESP-CO (Córdoba, on the opposite side of the country) or ESP-MD (Madrid, the interior capital province) — both wrong because they are disjoint territories with their own independent statistical identities. What confirms Castellón is a distinct reporting unit rather than an artifact of NUTS classification: it is one of Spain's 50 constitutionally and statistically distinct provinces established by the 1833 territorial reform, each of which is independently tracked in Spanish national statistics (INE) and EU regional statistics (NUTS3 code ES522) precisely because provincial administration, not just autonomous-community administration, is the unit at which much historical and modern Spanish data is collected and published.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Assigned. `polygon_source: mapspain-ign`, feature id `12`, matching Castellón's INE province code. This is a present-day (current IGN) administrative boundary reused as a proxy across the full 1833-2025 span, on the grounds — already applied to six sibling provinces (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra) — that Spain's 1833 provincial boundaries have remained essentially stable, with only minor local municipal-level adjustments, so a present-day boundary is a low-risk stand-in for the historical extent rather than a materially different territory needing its own vintage-specific geometry.

**Territory description:** Castellón province covers the northernmost part of the Valencian Community on Spain's eastern Mediterranean coast, bordered by Tarragona (Catalonia) to the north, Teruel (Aragon) to the west, Valencia province to the south, and the Mediterranean Sea to the east. Its capital is the city of Castellón de la Plana. A reader can locate it on a modern map as the coastal strip and adjoining interior running roughly from the Ebro delta area south to just north of Valencia city, comprising the coastal plain (La Plana) and the interior mountainous comarcas (Els Ports, Alt Maestrat, Alcalatén). The province's modern area is approximately 6,632 km², which is not independently measured here from the attached geometry but is the standard published figure for the current administrative unit that the mapspain-ign feature represents.

**Why this entry exists:** No existing polity in the table matches or contains Castellón's own reporting territory — ESP-CO and ESP-MD are different, non-overlapping provinces, and ESP-1800-2025 is the national aggregate, which does not equal Castellón-specific data. As with the sibling provinces routed in the same batch, this confirms provinces of Spain systematically lack existing candidate rows and require creation rather than matching to a broader entity.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-cs

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, using the vehicle-plate abbreviation CS**

Following the pattern already used for six sibling Spanish provinces in this batch (ESP-AS Asturias, ESP-CA Cantabria, ESP-CO Córdoba, ESP-LU Lugo, ESP-OR Ourense, ESP-PO Pontevedra), the subunit token is the two-letter historical vehicle-registration abbreviation rather than the INE numeric code (12) or a full name. Castellón's plate code is CS, so the polity code is ESP-CS-1833-2025. This keeps the subunit token human-legible and consistent with the sibling rows already in the table, rather than mixing numeric INE codes into a scheme that is otherwise alphabetic.

## Open questions

### oq-1833-founding-date-unconfirmed

**No administrative-history source directly confirms Castellón's creation date**

The routing decision assumes Castellón follows the country default (created in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform) rather than being split later from another province, as flagged in the routing_concerns field of the decision this page implements. This is consistent with the sibling provinces already routed the same way, and Castellón is not one of the enumerated departures from the default (the Canary Islands split), but no primary source (e.g. a digitized copy of the 1833 royal decree or a Spanish administrative-history reference) has been checked against this specific province. If a source later shows Castellón was reorganized at a different date, start_year and the polity code would need to change together, and the containment edge into ESP-1800-2025 would need re-verification.

### oq-mapspain-ign-vintage

**mapspain-ign is a present-day boundary standing in for a 192-year span**

The polygon_feature_id 12 comes from mapspain-ign, a current IGN administrative boundary dataset, used as a proxy for the entire 1833-2025 span. Spanish provincial boundaries have been essentially stable since 1833 with only minor local municipal-level adjustments (e.g. occasional inter-municipal boundary corrections), so the risk is treated as low and consistent with the six sibling provinces already handled this way. However, no boundary-change catalogue has been checked specifically for Castellón over this 192-year window, so small-scale drift within the province (rather than to/from a neighbouring province) has not been ruled out.

### oq-present-locally-flag

The decision's polygon_reasoning notes that sources.yaml marks mapspain-ign as not present locally, which conflicts with the fact that seven other ESP provinces in this table already cite the same source at the same admin granularity.
