# Chaco (province of Argentina)

## Summary

Chaco is a province in northeastern Argentina, bordering Paraguay across the Pilcomayo and Paraguay rivers to the north and east, with Formosa province to its north, Santiago del Estero and Santa Fe to its south and west, and Corrientes to its east. This page covers Chaco specifically as a **province** of Argentina, from 1951 -- when Ley 14037 elevated the former Territorio Nacional del Chaco to full provincial status -- through the present day (2025, this table's standard open-ended terminus). The type is `subnational` because Chaco has never been a sovereign entity: it is, and was, a constituent unit of Argentina throughout, first as a directly-administered national territory (1884-1951, the sibling entry ARG-CHACO-1884-1951) and then, from 1951, as a self-governing province with its own constitution, legislature, and governor, entitled to national representation. The 1951 threshold is an administrative-status change, not a territorial one -- the province's boundary at creation is presumed to closely follow the territorio nacional's prior boundary, modulo any adjustments connected to Formosa's 1955 split from the same regional complex, which this page's open questions leave unverified. The entry exists as a separate row from the pre-1951 era specifically so that data collected under the territorio nacional's distinct administrative and statistical regime is not conflated with data collected under the province's own regime, following the one-polity-per-administrative-era convention documented in the routing decision this page implements.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry exists to hold the province-era (1951 onward) segment of the same underlying Chaco data that was split into two eras by the routing decision this page implements: the input data is Argentine subnational time series (the decision references unit_id ARG-CHACO with country Argentina, spanning multiple administrations) that had originally been treated as a single continuous unit. That was wrong because the administrative regime changed sharply in 1951 -- Ley 14037 converted the Territorio Nacional del Chaco (directly governed from Buenos Aires, no provincial legislature) into a full province with its own government -- and the pipeline's one-polity-per-administrative-era rule means pre-1951 and post-1951 Chaco data cannot share a single polity row. The pre-1951 segment is covered by the sibling page ARG-CHACO-1884-1951 (the national-territory era, itself dating to Ley 1532 of 1884); this page is the second of the two eras the routing decision explicitly calls for ('segment 2 of 2: 1951-2023'). What confirms Chaco is a distinct entity worth its own row, separate from a generic Argentina-wide bucket, is straightforward: Chaco is one of Argentina's 23 formally constituted provinces, each with its own INDEC statistical reporting, its own constitution, and its own legislature since 1951 -- it is not a contested or ambiguous unit, and the country convention already documented for Chaco (referenced directly in the routing decision's span_basis) treats the 1884/1951 boundary as the entry point for exactly this kind of era split, mirroring how ARG-FORMOSA and other national-territory-to-province transitions are handled elsewhere in this table.

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon has been fetched into this repository's source cache for this period. The routing decision identified `gadm-4.1-adm1` as the correct registered source for a province-level (adm1) Argentine boundary, but the locally curated GADM subset covers only 81 countries and does not currently include Argentina, even though GADM's global release does map ARG provinces (feature ARG.10_1 for Chaco). Per this pipeline's rule, `registered_source_unfetched` routes still name the real slug (`gadm-4.1-adm1`) rather than `none`, with `polygon_status: unassigned` reflecting that the geometry itself has not yet been pulled in. `polygon_feature_id` is left null until that fetch happens.

**Territory description:** Chaco province occupies roughly the southeastern portion of Argentina's Gran Chaco lowlands, in the country's northeast. It is bounded by the Bermejo river to the north (separating it from Formosa), the Paraguay and Paraná rivers to the east (separating it from Paraguay and Corrientes/Santa Fe), and shares land borders with Santiago del Estero and Salta to the west and south. Its capital is Resistencia. The modern province covers approximately 99,633 km2 (Argentina's official INDEC figure for Chaco province), which a reader can locate as the region directly across the Paraná from the city of Corrientes and directly south of Formosa province on any current map of Argentina. No area figure is measured from an attached polygon in this entry, since none is yet assigned; the 99,633 km2 figure above is drawn from Argentina's national statistics agency, not from this table's geometry, and should be treated as an approximate reference for locating the territory rather than a validated `polygon_area_km2` value.

## Decisions

### d-code-avoids-collision

**Code chosen as ARG-CHACO-1951-2025, not ARG-CHACO-1951-1951-adjacent forms**

The dominant subnational pattern in the table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the sibling era ARG-CHACO-1884-1951 already occupies the pre-1951 span, so this page's own start/end (1951-2025) keeps the code unique: ARG-CHACO-1951-2025. No bespoke code is warranted here because Chaco province has no separate historical name of its own distinct from the territorio nacional era already covered by the sibling row -- it is the same place under a later administrative classification, which is exactly the case the routing_reasoning describes as 'one polity per era, not a different territory.'

### d-no-predecessor-claim

**Dropped the predecessor/successor edge to ARG-CHACO-1884-1951 rather than asserting it unilaterally**

A prior draft of this page declared predecessor: [ARG-CHACO-1884-1951], but that page's own successor list is empty, and validate_chain_integrity requires both sides to assert an edge before it counts it. Since this page cannot edit the sibling page, predecessor and successor are both left empty here, and the missing edge is recorded as an open question naming exactly which field on which page would need to change to close it, rather than silently re-asserting a claim the other row does not reciprocate.

## Open questions

### oq-predecessor-edge-asymmetry

**ARG-CHACO-1884-1951 does not list this page as its successor**

This page (ARG-CHACO-1951-2025) is the direct continuation of the Territorio Nacional del Chaco era covered by ARG-CHACO-1884-1951 -- Ley 14037 (1951) converted the territorio nacional directly into the province this page describes, with no gap and no other intervening polity. But ARG-CHACO-1884-1951's own frontmatter declares successor: [], and a validate_chain_integrity check treats an edge as valid only when both endpoints assert it (an earlier version of this page asserted predecessor: [ARG-CHACO-1884-1951] unilaterally and was rejected for exactly this reason). Since this page's author cannot edit ARG-CHACO-1884-1951, the fix is: whoever next edits ARG-CHACO-1884-1951 should set its successor field to [ARG-CHACO-1951-2025], after which this page's predecessor field should be updated to match. Until then the chain is asymmetric and the 1884-1951 to 1951-2025 continuity is documented only in prose, not in the graph.

### oq-gadm-arg-not-fetched

**gadm-4.1-adm1 does not yet include Argentina's provinces locally**

The routing decision found no adm1-level polygon source currently holding Chaco province: gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered but the locally curated subset (81 countries) does not include ARG, even though the global GADM release does carry ARG.10_1 Chaco. Until that global layer (or an IGN Argentina alternative) is actually fetched into the local source cache, polygon_status here has to stay unassigned rather than assigned, even though the source slug itself is known and registered. Whoever next runs the source-fetch step for gadm-4.1-adm1 should extend the ARG coverage and then re-run assignment for this row and its sibling provinces (Buenos Aires, Catamarca, Cordoba, etc., all of which show the same gadm-4.1-adm1 gap).

### oq-boundary-stability-1951-2025

**Whether Chaco province's boundary has been stable across its full 1951-2025 span**

This entry spans 74 years (1951 to the table's present-day end year of 2025) under one polygon proxy, but Argentina's northeastern provinces saw at least one adjacent boundary event in this window: Formosa was split off as its own national territory in 1955, four years after Chaco became a province, and its territory was carved from what had been the Chaco/Formosa national-territory area rather than from Chaco province proper. It is not yet confirmed from a primary source (Ley 14037 or subsequent provincial boundary legislation) whether Chaco province's own borders were adjusted at any point between 1951 and 2025, so the modern GADM adm1 boundary for Chaco is assumed but not verified to apply across the full span once it is fetched.
