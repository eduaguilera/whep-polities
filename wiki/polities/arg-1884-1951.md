# Chaco (national territory of Argentina)

## Summary

This entry covers the Territorio Nacional del Chaco, a directly Buenos Aires-administered national territory of Argentina, from its creation by Ley 1532 in 1884 until Ley 14037 converted it into Chaco Province in 1951. Unlike Argentina's core provinces, national territories were not self-governing: the executive branch in Buenos Aires appointed a territorial governor, and residents lacked the provincial autonomy (own constitution, elected legislature, national Senate representation) that province status carries. This is why `type: subnational` rather than `national` is correct despite the territory sitting directly under the national government with no intermediate province layer: the entry exists to carry data reported at sub-country granularity, not to claim independent sovereignty, and keeping it subnational avoids it competing with the ARG national chain in the matcher's ranking. The 1884 start date is the territory's own legal creation, not an arbitrary data cutoff. The 1951 end date is likewise the territory's own legal transformation into a province — a genuine change in administrative status (self-government, provincial constitution, national legislative representation) rather than a mere renaming, which is why this gets a separate polity row from the province era instead of one continuous entry spanning both statuses.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ARG-CHACO) was raised against Argentina admin-1 data under the label "Chaco" for the country-convention pass on Argentine provinces; the specific row/year count is not restated in the decision payload here, but the decision's own reasoning establishes that data exists for Chaco going back into the pre-1951 territorio nacional period. Previously that data would have been routed to the national ARG chain (ARG-1902-2025 or its predecessor eras), which is wrong because those rows represent all of Argentina, not the roughly one-province-sized Chaco territory the data actually describes — a mismatch of well over an order of magnitude in territorial scope, the same category of error the routing decision explicitly names for the analogous Formosa case. What confirms Chaco was administratively distinct in this period: Ley 1532 (1884) is a primary legislative source establishing the Territorio Nacional del Chaco as a named, separately-governed unit distinct from the surrounding provinces and other national territories from that date, and Ley 14037 (1951) is the primary source confirming its administrative status changed at the far end of this span. The routing decision itself treats this as settled: it states the pre-1951 data "belongs to a distinct 'national territory' era polity" rather than being unroutable or foldable into the national total.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon has been fetched for this period. The routing decision identifies `gadm-4.1-adm1` as a registered source that in principle carries an Argentine Chaco province admin-1 feature, but the locally-held copy of gadm-4.1-adm1 is a curated 81-country subset that excludes Argentina entirely — so the source is registered but genuinely unfetched, not merely unassigned by oversight. `polygon_source` is therefore set to `gadm-4.1-adm1` (the registered slug that would supply the feature once the global GADM release is obtained) with `polygon_status: unassigned` and no `polygon_feature_id`, per the routing convention for this exact situation.

**Territory description:** The Territorio Nacional del Chaco occupied the eastern part of Argentina's Gran Chaco plain, bordering Paraguay across the Paraguay/Bermejo river system to the north and east, Santiago del Estero and Santa Fe to the south, and Salta/Formosa to the west and north. In present-day terms this corresponds closely to Chaco Province as constituted between 1951 and the 1955 split-off of Formosa — roughly the area between the Bermejo and Paraná/Paraguay river systems, centered on the modern provincial capital Resistencia. Modern Chaco Province (post-1955, smaller than this territory) covers about 99,633 km²; because this entry's span ends in 1951, four years before the Formosa split, the historical Territorio Nacional del Chaco was somewhat larger than that modern figure — plausibly closer to the combined Chaco+Formosa extent, i.e. on the order of 200,000 km², though this has not been confirmed against the original 1884/1951 legislative boundary text (see open question oq-territorio-nacional-internal-boundary-changes). No figure here is derived from an attached polygon, since none is assigned.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: 1884 marks the creation of the Territorio Nacional del Chaco by Ley 1532, carving it out of the frontier territory of the Gran Chaco region rather than continuing an existing administrative unit under a new name. No successor code exists yet: Ley 14037 (1951) converted the territory into Chaco Province, but that province-era polity has not been created in this database as of this page (see open question oq-province-successor-code-unknown); whoever creates it must set predecessor: [ARG-1884-1951] and this page's successor field should then be updated to match, closing the chain.

## Sourced claims

- Ley 1532 (1884) established the Territorio Nacional del Chaco (along with several other Argentine national territories: Misiones, Formosa, Pampa, Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego), replacing informal frontier administration of the Gran Chaco region with a governor appointed by the national executive.
- Ley 14037 (1951) elevated the Territorio Nacional del Chaco to the status of Provincia del Chaco (briefly named Provincia Presidente Perón until 1955), granting it a provincial constitution and representation in the national Congress that a territorio nacional did not have.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-country-convention

**Code follows <ISO3>-<start>-<end>, not <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>**

Of the 57 subnational rows using the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern, all represent units that persist unchanged in name/status across their span. Chaco does not: this entry captures only the 1884-1951 Territorio Nacional era, a *distinct administrative classification* from the post-1951 province, which will get its own polity row and its own SUBUNIT-style code once created (per the routing decision's own note that the 1951-2023 provincial segment should reuse whatever code is created for the sibling province verdict). Appending a subunit tag like ARG-CHACO to a code that only covers the pre-province era would misleadingly suggest the tag denotes the same thing as the eventual province code, when in fact they are two different polity rows for two different legal statuses of the same territory. Since ARG sets its own precedent (0 existing subnational rows), and since the six existing <ISO3>-<start>-<end> codes in the table (e.g. BDI-1922-1962) are precisely used for a bounded historical administrative era rather than an ISO3-stable modern subunit, ARG-1884-1951 follows that second pattern instead. This is a deliberate departure from the modal pattern, flagged per the instructions.

## Open questions

### oq-province-successor-code-unknown

**Successor province polity code for post-1951 Chaco does not exist yet**

Ley 14037 (1951) converted the Territorio Nacional del Chaco into Chaco Province, and the routing decision explicitly flags that the 1951-2023 provincial segment should reuse whatever polity code is created for the sibling province verdict already on record (referencing an ARG-FORMOSA-style entry), rather than being duplicated here. As of this page's creation, no ARG-CHACO or ARG-CHACO-1951-2025 style row exists in the polities database. `successor` is left empty here rather than pointing at a code that does not exist, which will make this entry's chain silently truncate at 1951 until the province-era page is authored. Whoever authors that page must set its `predecessor` back to this code (ARG-1884-1951) and this page should then be updated with the matching `successor`.

### oq-boundary-vintage-modern-chaco-vs-1884-1951

**Whether the modern Chaco province boundary (candidate gadm-4.1-adm1 feature) matches the 1884-1951 Territorio Nacional extent**

The routing decision itself flags this: Chaco province was reduced when Formosa was split off from it in 1955 (Ley 14315), which post-dates this entry's 1951 end year, so a modern GADM adm1 Chaco boundary is likely close but not certain to match the pre-1951 Territorio Nacional del Chaco extent. Ley 1532 (1884) and Ley 14037 (1951) have not been checked against modern boundary text in this pass. Until gadm-4.1-adm1 is actually fetched for Argentina (it is currently a curated 81-country subset that excludes ARG, per the routing decision's polygon_detail), this remains unverified and polygon_status is unassigned rather than proxy.

### oq-territorio-nacional-internal-boundary-changes

**Whether the Territorio Nacional del Chaco's own boundaries shifted between 1884 and 1951**

National territories in Argentina were occasionally adjusted by subsequent legislation (boundary transfers with neighbouring territories such as Formosa or Santiago del Estero) independent of the eventual 1951/1955 provincialization and Formosa split. This page assumes a single stable Chaco territorio nacional extent for the full 1884-1951 span but that has not been confirmed against the legislative record; if an internal boundary change occurred mid-span, this entry may need to be split.
