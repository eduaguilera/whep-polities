# Huesca (province of Spain)

## Summary

Huesca is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today also constitutes one of the three provinces of the Aragon autonomous community (with Zaragoza and Teruel), in Spain's northeast on the French border along the central Pyrenees. This entry is type: subnational because Huesca is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, distinguishable from it because INE and Eurostat have tabulated Huesca separately at NUTS3 code ES241 since the province's creation. Unlike Asturias or Madrid, Huesca is not coextensive with its autonomous community: Aragon comprises three provinces (Huesca, Zaragoza, Teruel), so the province boundary and the community boundary diverge, and Huesca-specific data cannot be treated as a proxy for all of Aragon or vice versa.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES241) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES241 ("Huesca"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units, following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Huesca is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states explicitly that no existing polity represents Huesca (only A Coruna, Madrid, and the national Spain row existed for ESP before this decision), so the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, collapsing a province-level series into a country-level one, which is rejected for the same reason it was rejected for the other Spanish provinces. What confirms distinctness: Huesca's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES241) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling ESP province rows -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 32x the area of Huesca.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the INE province numeric code), under which Huesca is conventionally cpro 22; the polygon_reasoning flags that sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign as present_locally=False even though several sibling province pages (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, and others) already cite it this session -- that discrepancy is unresolved and is recorded as an open question below rather than assumed away. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP entirely (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, and Huesca is one of three provinces within Aragon, so adm1 could not carry province-level attribution even where a community and province coincide, which is not the case here), so it was correctly ruled out. Territory description: Huesca province occupies the northeastern corner of Spain along the French border, spanning the central Pyrenees in the north (including peaks such as Aneto and the Ordesa y Monte Perdido national park) down to the flatter Somontano and Hoya de Huesca in the south, bordering Navarre and Zaragoza province to the west and south, Lleida province (Catalonia) to the east, and France across the Pyrenean watershed to the north. Its capital is the city of Huesca; other notable towns include Jaca, Barbastro and Fraga. On a modern map its outline matches the current INE/IGN provincial boundary. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 15,636 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is confirmed present and fetched, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low.

## Predecessors and successors

Huesca has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was organized under the Antiguo Regimen's corregimiento/partido structure of the Kingdom of Aragon, not as a bounded province, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, matching the treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: Huesca remains a current, active province and NUTS3 statistical unit of Spain (ES241) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), consistent with the ESP country convention for still-current units.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-hu-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation HU rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES241) or the INE province numeric code (22). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-HU-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. HU was chosen because it is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-HU) and the everyday abbreviation for the province (from the vehicle registration and postal code prefix), reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row.

## Open questions

### oq-mapspain-ign-present-locally-conflict

**sources.yaml marks mapspain-ign as not present locally, yet sibling pages already cite it as the route this session**

The routing decision's own polygon_reasoning flags that scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with present_locally=False, which conflicts with several sibling province pages (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, and others per the esp-as/esp-ca/esp-lu/esp-or/esp-po wiki stubs already on disk this session) already treating it as the route for their provinces. Either the manifest is stale and the provinces.gpkg file has in fact been fetched, or none of these routes are actually executable yet and all of them should carry polygon_status: unassigned until the file is confirmed present and the cpro=22 feature for Huesca is verified against the modern INE provincial boundary. This should be resolved once, at the country-convention level, rather than re-litigated per province page.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Kingdom of Aragon's corregimiento structure has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory now forming Huesca province was organized under the Antiguo Regimen's corregimiento/partido structure of the Kingdom of Aragon (itself part of the Crown of Aragon), which does not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province's boundary or administrative form. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CO-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Aragonese jurisdictions should eventually be modeled is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
