# León (province of Spain)

## Summary

This entry represents the Spanish province of León, one of the nine provinces of the present-day autonomous community of Castile and León, in north-western Spain. It is type: subnational because Spain has held uninterrupted national sovereignty over this territory throughout the entry's span — the row exists to carry a sub-national administrative reporting unit for province-level statistics, not to claim any independent sovereignty, exactly as with the six other Spanish provinces already routed through this same convention (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra). León as an administrative province dates to the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform, which replaced Spain's patchwork of historical kingdoms, lordships and ecclesiastical jurisdictions with 49 (later 50) provinces on a roughly uniform model; the historical Kingdom of León that gave the province its name had already been absorbed into the unified Spanish crown centuries earlier and does not correspond to the modern province's boundary, so 1833 rather than some earlier date is the correct start for this administrative entity. The province has remained essentially unchanged in extent since, through the 1978 Constitution's creation of autonomous communities (León became part of Castile and León in 1983) down to the present.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures province-level statistics reported for León (Spain) under whatever source and label the ingest pipeline routed here — following the same country-convention pathway already used to create the six sibling ESP province pages (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra), each of which followed identical reasoning: a genuine Spanish province matching no existing candidate polity (national ESP is a container, not this territory; and any homonym matches from other countries, such as Sierra Leone-related entries, are irrelevant). Before this entry existed, any León-labeled Spanish province data would have had nowhere correctly scoped to land except the national ESP-1800-2025 row, which is roughly 33x larger in area and would have mixed León's statistics into a nationwide total. What confirms León is a distinct, individually-tracked administrative and statistical unit rather than an artefact of a data label: it is one of Spain's 50 provinces under the Javier de Burgos reform, each with its own INE provincial code (León = 24) and its own entry in Spain's standard statistical geography, and it is precisely this provincial code that the mapspain-ign polygon source keys off in the cpro field, confirming the province has an independent identity in both administrative law and geodata, not just in whatever data source triggered this routing decision.

## Territorial extent

Polygon: assigned status, mapspain-ign source, feature id 24 (León's INE provincial code, cpro). This is a present-day IGN provincial boundary being used for the full 1833-2025 span, on the basis (following the same convention already applied to Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Ourense and Pontevedra) that Spain's provincial boundaries have been essentially stable since the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform created them. This is treated as assigned rather than proxy because mapspain-ign is a source purpose-built to represent exactly this administrative unit (province), not a period-mismatched stand-in for a differently-shaped territory — but see the open question on vintage risk below for the caveat this glosses over. Territory: León province occupies the north-western part of Castile and León, bordering Asturias to the north, Galicia (Lugo and Ourense) to the west, Zamora and Palencia to the south, and Cantabria and Palencia to the east. Its capital is the city of León. The province covers approximately 15,580 km², making it the largest province in Castile and León and one of the largest in Spain — this figure is drawn from standard modern references for the province's area, not measured from the attached polygon in this page (no geometry-derived figure is asserted here).

## Predecessors and successors

León has no predecessor or successor polity: it was created directly as one of the 50 original provinces of the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, replacing the ad-hoc kingdoms and lordships of the Ancien Régime administrative map, and it has continued in unbroken existence to the present day as a province within the modern autonomous community of Castile and León (created 1983). It has never been split, merged with a neighboring province, or had its name changed; the 1978 Constitution's creation of autonomous communities layered a new regional tier above the province but did not alter León's own boundary or status as a province.

## Open questions

### oq-vintage-risk

**mapspain-ign boundary is present-day, not 1833-vintage**

The polygon (mapspain-ign feature cpro=24) reflects León's current administrative boundary as maintained by Instituto Geográfico Nacional. Spanish provincial boundaries have been essentially stable since the 1833 reform, with only minor municipal-level adjustments between neighboring provinces over nearly two centuries, so vintage risk is treated as low. However, no systematic check has been done here to confirm zero net boundary change specifically for León against Zamora, Palencia, Valladolid, Ourense, Lugo, Asturias, or Cantabria, its neighbors on the reform-era map versus today. If any 19th- or 20th-century municipal boundary revision moved area between León and a neighboring province, this proxy would silently carry that later boundary back to 1833.
