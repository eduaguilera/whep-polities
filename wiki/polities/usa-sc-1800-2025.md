# South Carolina (state of the United States)

## Summary

South Carolina, one of the thirteen original states that ratified the U.S. Constitution (ratified 1788-05-23, the eighth state to do so). This entry captures South Carolina as a constituent state of the United States. `type: subnational` because the polity operates entirely inside U.S. sovereignty for its whole span; it exists as a row because USDA/Mitchell-style agricultural statistics report South Carolina separately from national US totals, and no existing polity in the table represents it. Its own administrative history includes: colonial founding (1663 as part of Carolina, split from North Carolina in 1712), statehood via constitutional ratification in 1788, secession from the Union in December 1860 and membership in the Confederate States of America (1861-1865), military Reconstruction and readmission to Congress in 1868, and continuous statehood since. The routing decision that produced this page proposed start_year 1788 (the ratification date) as the state's own historical start, per the country convention's original-13-states exception to the default admission-year rule. That proposal is NOT what this page uses: the polity table's own USA container chain (read from data/final/polities_database.csv) begins at USA-1800-1803, with no USA polity row covering 1788-1800. Starting this entry at 1788 would leave twelve years (1788-1800) uncontained, which the containment gate rejects. This page therefore starts at 1800, the earliest year for which a containing USA polity row exists, and documents the discrepancy with the convention as an open question rather than silently following the convention into a gap.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: USDA historical state-level agricultural series for South Carolina, extract beginning 1866, comparable in kind to the series that motivated ALK-1867-1959 (Territory of Alaska) as precedent for a US subnational row. Row count and exact source labels were not shown in the routing decision text handed to this page (only country/admin_name/reasoning were provided); the harness's own ingestion log is authoritative for the row count. Before this entry, any South Carolina-labeled rows in the source data had nowhere state-specific to route to and would have fallen back to the national row USA-1867-1959 or USA-1959-2025 (whichever covered the data year), which is roughly 47 times South Carolina's own area (82,933 km2 vs the U.S.'s ~3.9 million km2) and mixes South Carolina's agricultural output with that of every other state. Federico-Tena and comparable historical-statistics compilations, and the USDA itself, tabulate US states individually rather than only nationally, which is the same kind of evidence — a reporting unit that publishers tabulate on its own — that has justified other subnational rows in this table (e.g. Manchuria, Alaska). South Carolina's status as one of the original 13 states that founded the federal union by ratifying the Constitution on 1788-05-23 is well documented in standard U.S. constitutional history and is what distinguishes it from later-admitted states like Alaska, whose span instead starts at the year of U.S. acquisition (1867).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Assigned. `polygon_source: gadm-4.1-adm1`, `polygon_feature_id: USA.41_1` — the GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature for South Carolina, already present in this repository's GADM extract (the same source used for ALK-1867-1959's Alaska feature, USA.2_1). This is a direct registered-source feature, not a construction or a proxy: South Carolina's state boundary has been stable since statehood, so a present-day (c.2020s) GADM boundary is a low-risk stand-in for the entire 1800-2025 span.

**Area:** The attached GADM 4.1 geometry measures approximately 82,933 km2 (this is a measurement of the polygon feature itself, not an independent historical statement about the state's territory). This figure is consistent with the modern state's well-known land area (~82,933 km2 / 32,020 sq mi, the 40th-largest U.S. state), and matches published figures closely because South Carolina's external boundary has not moved during the period this row covers.

**Territory description:** South Carolina occupies the southeastern United States, bordered by North Carolina to the north, Georgia to the southwest (along the Savannah River), and the Atlantic Ocean to the east and southeast. It runs from the Blue Ridge Mountains in the northwestern tip, across the Piedmont, to the coastal plain and Sea Islands (Charleston, Hilton Head) on the coast. A reader can locate it on any modern map of the U.S. as the state directly south of North Carolina and east of Georgia.

No predecessor or successor polity rows: South Carolina has been a single continuous state entity since ratifying the Constitution in 1788, with no change of sovereign container type (it does not transition from a colonial or territorial row the way Alaska does from a pre-purchase predecessor). The 1861-1865 Confederate States of America period is not modeled as a predecessor/successor break in this entry — no CSA polity row exists in the table, and the container chain simply continues through USA-1848-1867 across that period, per the basis note on that container edge.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-clamped-to-1800

**start_year set to 1800, not the proposed 1788, to keep the containment gate satisfied**

The routing decision proposed start_year 1788, following the country convention's stated exception for original-13 states (ratification date, not admission date). But the polity table's USA container chain, read directly from data/final/polities_database.csv, begins at USA-1800-1803 with end_year exclusive; there is no USA polity row of any kind covering 1788-1800. The containment gate requires that every year of a member polity's span fall inside some container edge, and that a container edge fall entirely within its own container's span — an edge cannot be fabricated to cover years the container itself does not claim. Rather than write a container edge for 1788-1800 pointing at USA-1800-1803 (which the gate would reject, since that container's own span starts at 1800), this page starts South Carolina's own span at 1800, aligning it with the earliest containable year. This is a genuine departure from the country convention's original-13 exception, made because the convention and the polity table disagree about how far back USA containment reaches, and the table is what the gate actually checks against. The 1788 ratification date is preserved in the prose and sourced_claims as the historically correct founding date, but is not used as start_year.

## Open questions

### oq-usa-container-floor-1788-vs-1800

**Does the USA container chain need an earlier edge for 1788-1800, or should the original-13 convention be revised to 1800?**

The country convention for United States of America states the system floor is 1787-1788 (Constitution ratification) and explicitly carves out the original 13 states as starting then rather than at a later admission year. But the actual USA container chain in country_conventions.json, and the corresponding rows in data/final/polities_database.csv, both begin at USA-1800-1803 — there is no USA-1788-1800 or equivalent row. This means every one of the original 13 states, if given start_year 1788 per the convention, would create the same 1788-1800 containment gap this page ran into. Two fixes are possible: (1) add a USA-1788-1800 (or USA-1787-1800) container row to the polity table, if there is a data or administrative reason to split it from USA-1800-1803, after which this entry and any of the other 12 original-13-state entries could legitimately move their start_year back to 1788; or (2) revise the convention's original-13 exception to read 1800 instead of 1787/1788, matching what the table can actually support, and treat the ratification date as historical color rather than a span boundary. This entry took the second path locally (see decisions) but did not touch the convention file itself, so the next original-13 state routed through this harness will hit the identical question unless it is resolved centrally.

### oq-confederacy-not-modeled

**Should the 1861-1865 Confederate period break South Carolina's container continuity or get its own polity row?**

This entry's container chain treats 1848-1867 as a single continuous USA-1848-1867 edge, meaning South Carolina is modeled as continuously contained by the United States even during 1861-1865, when it was a founding member of the Confederate States of America and not under US governance in any practical sense. No CSA polity row exists anywhere in the current polity table, so there was no alternative container to point at for those four years. If a CSA polity is ever added to represent the Confederacy as its own national-type row, this entry's container chain would need a corresponding edge substituting CSA for USA across 1861-1865, and the same question applies to every other former-Confederate state that gets routed through this harness (South Carolina is likely to be first).
