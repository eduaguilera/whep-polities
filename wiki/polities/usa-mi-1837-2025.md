# Michigan (state of the United States)

## Summary

Michigan, one of the fifty states of the United States, admitted to the Union in 1837 as the 26th state after organization as the Michigan Territory since 1805. This entry is `type: subnational` rather than national because Michigan has never been an independently sovereign reporting unit; the row exists solely to carry state-level statistics (the `juan-subnational` USDA NASS-style agricultural data series used across this batch of U.S. state pages) at a spatial resolution the national USA polity rows cannot represent. Unlike original-13 states such as Georgia, Connecticut, or Delaware, Michigan's actual admission date (1837) already falls inside the earliest national USA container row's span (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1803-1848), so no start-year flooring adjustment is needed here -- the routing decision's proposed 1837 start is used unmodified. The state's modern external boundary, including the Upper Peninsula awarded in the 1836-1837 Toledo War settlement, was fixed at admission and has not changed since, making a present-day GADM boundary a low-risk stand-in for the full 1837-2025 span.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision, the `juan-subnational` USDA NASS-style U.S. state-level agricultural data series used across this batch of state pages, with the underlying extract beginning in 1866 (per the routing_reasoning's stated data span of 1866-2025); exact row counts are not supplied in the routing decision object itself (see open question). Previously, any Michigan-state-level data would have had no distinct polity row in this database and risked being matched to the national USA chain rows, which represent the entire country rather than a single state -- a resolution mismatch analogous to the Manchuria exemplar's fifteen-times-too-coarse problem. What confirms Michigan as a distinct reporting entity is its unambiguous, uncontested admission to the Union as a state in 1837, a status the country convention's default start rule (state admission date, not one of the three enumerated departures) already recognizes for U.S. subnational entries generally, and the same pattern already applied to sibling state USA-CALIFORNIA in this batch.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Copied from GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.23_1 (Michigan), assigned directly rather than via proxy. GADM's present-day administrative boundary is used across the full 1837-2025 span: Michigan's external boundary, including the Upper Peninsula, was fixed by the Toledo War settlement immediately before statehood and has not changed since admission, so the vintage risk of using a present-day feature for a 188-year span is low but non-zero and is noted here rather than silently assumed. `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Territory description:** The area covered is the modern U.S. state of Michigan, in the Great Lakes region of the United States, comprising the Lower Peninsula (bordered by Ohio and Indiana to the south, with Lakes Michigan, Huron, St. Clair and Erie on its other sides) and the Upper Peninsula (bordered by Wisconsin to the west, with Lakes Superior, Michigan and Huron on its other sides), connected only by the Mackinac Bridge. A reader can locate it on any modern map as the mitten-shaped state containing Detroit, Grand Rapids, and Lansing. Its area is approximately 250,487 km2 (land plus Great Lakes water within state jurisdiction, the commonly cited figure for the modern state) -- this is a reference value from general knowledge, not a measurement of the attached GADM polygon, since no independent geometry measurement was computed in this response.

## Predecessors and successors

Michigan has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database. It entered the Union directly as a state in 1837 following organization as the Michigan Territory (1805-1837), a territorial period not itself represented as a distinct polity row here since no data source in this batch reports at that pre-statehood territorial level. There is no partition, secession, or later split producing a distinct successor: the Upper and Lower Peninsulas have been governed as one state continuously since 1837, including through the 1836-1837 "Toledo War" boundary dispute with Ohio, which was resolved by Congress awarding Michigan the western Upper Peninsula in compensation for the disputed Toledo Strip -- an internal boundary adjustment prior to this entry's start, not a change tracked as a distinct territorial polity.

## Sourced claims

- Michigan was admitted to the Union as the 26th state on January 26, 1837, following organization as the Michigan Territory from 1805, per standard U.S. constitutional/admission history.
- The 1836-1837 Toledo War boundary dispute with Ohio was resolved by Congress granting Michigan the western Upper Peninsula in exchange for ceding the Toledo Strip to Ohio, fixing Michigan's modern external boundary before statehood.
- The GADM 4.1 admin-1 dataset provides a single feature (USA.23_1) for the modern state of Michigan, used here as the polygon source per the routing decision's polygon_reasoning.

## Decisions

### d-no-floor-needed-1837

**No start-year flooring needed: 1837 already falls inside the earliest national USA container**

Unlike the original-13 states created earlier in this batch (Georgia, Connecticut, Delaware), which had to have their start_year floored to 1800 because their actual ratification dates (1788) predate the earliest national USA container row (USA-1800-1803), Michigan's actual 1837 admission date already falls comfortably inside the national USA chain's span (the second national row, USA-1803-1848, covers 1803-1848). No adjustment to the routing decision's proposed start_year was needed, and the containment gate is satisfied without any departure from the historically correct admission date. This is noted explicitly because a reader comparing this page to the original-13 state pages in the same batch might otherwise wonder why Michigan was treated differently -- the answer is simply that Michigan's real admission date happens to postdate the database's earliest national container, so no gap-filling compromise was required.

### d-four-container-edges

**Four container edges tile 1837-2025 across the successive national USA rows**

The national USA chain in the polity table is split into five rows by era: USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025. Michigan's 1837 start falls inside the second of these (USA-1803-1848), so that edge is trimmed to start at 1837 rather than 1803, and the remaining three later-era edges (USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025) are each used at their own full span, together tiling 1837-2025 with no gap. This mirrors the same edge-trimming approach used for other mid-span-admitted states in this batch (e.g. USA-CALIFORNIA), as distinct from original-13 states, which use all five edges.

### oq-territorial-polity-not-modeled

**Michigan Territory (1805-1837) is not represented as a distinct polity row**

This entry begins at 1837 statehood and does not extend back to cover the 1805-1837 Michigan Territory period, even though the territory existed as an organized entity with its own (larger, differently-bounded) jurisdiction prior to the Toledo War settlement and Wisconsin Territory's 1836 split-off. If a future data source reports pre-1837 Michigan Territory statistics, it is unclear whether they should route to this entry (with start_year extended back, matching the routing decision's implicit precedent of using organization dates for some units) or to a new bespoke pre-statehood territorial polity row, given the territory's boundaries differed substantially from the modern state (it briefly included what is now Wisconsin, Iowa, and part of Minnesota before 1836). No data source currently in scope requires this decision, so it is left open.
