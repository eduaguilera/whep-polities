# La Rioja (province of Spain)

## Summary

La Rioja is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created as the province of Logrono by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833, renamed La Rioja in 1980, and since 1982 also coextensive with the La Rioja autonomous community in the upper Ebro valley of north-central Spain -- one of the handful of Spanish provinces (with Asturias, Cantabria, Murcia, Madrid, Navarre, and the Balearic and Canary archipelagos) where the 1833 province boundary and the post-Franco autonomous community boundary are identical, since La Rioja was never split among or merged with neighbouring provinces. This entry is type: subnational because La Rioja is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated La Rioja separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES230, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES230) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES230 ("La Rioja"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (A Coruna/ES111, Asturias/ES120, and onward through the ES2xx block), following the system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because La Rioja is not one of the two Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing dedicated -- the routing_reasoning states La Rioja "has no existing polity in the repository," so like Asturias (and unlike A Coruna, which found a pre-existing but undocumented row) this unit had no candidate at all before this decision; the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for the other Spanish province rows. What confirms distinctness: La Rioja's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES230) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is over 150x the area of La Rioja (~5,045 km2).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data is unit ESP-ES230 (La Rioja), country Spain, NUTS3-tabulated by INE/Eurostat since the 1833 reform, start_year 1833 under the country default rule and open end_year 2025. It was previously matched to nothing -- no existing polity row covered it, and the fallback would have wrongly collapsed it into the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, roughly 150x its area, exactly the failure mode already rejected for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and sibling Spanish province rows. Distinctness is confirmed by continuous separate provincial-level statistical reporting: La Rioja is tabulated as its own NUTS3 unit (ES230) by INE and Eurostat/GISCO, the same standard already accepted for the other Spanish province rows.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. Territory description: La Rioja occupies the middle Ebro valley in north-central Spain, bordered by the Basque Country (Alava) to the north, Navarre to the east, Castile and Leon (Soria and Burgos) to the south and west, and a short border with Aragon (Zaragoza) to the southeast. Its capital and largest city is Logrono; other notable towns include Haro and Calahorra. On a modern map it is the same outline as the La Rioja autonomous community, one of Spain's smallest and one of only a handful that are uniprovincial (single-province communities), since it was never subdivided among or merged with neighbouring provinces after 1833. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 5,045 km2 -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the other Spanish province rows.

## Predecessors and successors

La Rioja has no predecessor polity in this database: before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory now covered by the province was split between the intendancy of Soria (Old Castile) and adjoining Basque/Navarrese jurisdictions, and did not exist as a single administrative or historical unit of its own, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, matching the treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025 and other Spanish province entries. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1982, also the La Rioja autonomous community, one of Spain's uniprovincial communities like Asturias, Cantabria and Madrid) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-as-abbreviation

**Used the ISO 3166-2 two-letter abbreviation RI rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and siblings is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES230) or the INE province numeric code (26). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-RI-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. RI matches the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-RI) and the everyday abbreviation for the region (Rioja), reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row. No departure from the country default start rule applies here: La Rioja is not one of the Canary Islands units that take a different start year, so it begins in 1833 like the other 1833-reform provinces.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Rioja territory (split between Old Castile's Soria intendancy and Basque/Navarrese borderlands) has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory that became the province of Logrono (renamed La Rioja in 1980) was administratively split among neighbouring Old Castile jurisdictions rather than forming a single recognized historical unit of its own, unlike a kingdom or principality with continuous pre-1833 identity. This database does not model that fragmented pre-reform structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and sibling provinces. Whether any pre-1833 Rioja-area jurisdiction should eventually be modeled as its own polity is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.
