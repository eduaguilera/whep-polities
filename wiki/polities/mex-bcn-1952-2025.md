# Baja California

## Summary

Baja California is the Mexican state occupying the northern half of the Baja California peninsula, on the Pacific coast bordering the United States (California) to the north. It was created on 16 January 1952 when the Territory of Baja California Norte — a federal territory administered directly from Mexico City since 1931 — was admitted as Mexico's 29th state by constitutional amendment, gaining its own legislature, governor, and constitution. `type: subnational` because Mexico's sovereignty is unbroken across the 1952 transition; this row exists only to carry the state-level administrative unit and the statistics collected specifically against it, not to assert a change of sovereign. The span starts in 1952 because that is the year of statehood — the entity "Baja California the state" did not exist before that date, only "Baja California Norte the territory," a different administrative category (federally administered, no state congress, no vote in the Senate), which is treated as a separate open question rather than folded into this row's history.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is created from a routing decision (unit_id MEX-BAJACALIFORNIA) that identified Baja California as one of three explicit departures from Mexico's default federal-admission convention: unlike most states admitted at Mexican independence or shortly after, Baja California converted from a directly administered federal territory to a full state only in 1952. Before this entry, no polity row existed for Baja California specifically — only the national Mexico row MEX-1848-2025 — so any input data tagged to "Baja California" as a distinct reporting unit had nowhere to go except the national row, which is roughly 27x the state's own area (Mexico ~1,964,375 km2 vs Baja California ~71,450 km2) and would badly dilute any territorially specific measurement. The 1952 statehood date is confirmed by the constitutional amendment admitting Baja California as Mexico's 29th state (Diario Oficial de la Federación, 16 January 1952), which is the same event Mexican federal and state government sources cite as the origin of the state's own congress and constitution — the historical confirmation that this is a distinct administrative entity from both the pre-1952 territory and the national aggregate. No polygon has yet been fetched for this entry (see Territorial extent); the routing decision explicitly flagged 0 matching GADM adm1 features in the locally cached (81-country subset) GADM 4.1 file, which excludes Mexico.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The registered source `gadm-4.1-adm1` has the right schema for this unit (GID_1-keyed Mexican states would appear as e.g. `MEX.2_1`), but the file cached locally at `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg` is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Mexico (0 matching features). This is a `registered_source_unfetched` case, not a missing-source case: the fix is to fetch the global GADM 4.1 admin-1 layer (or the Mexico portion of it), not to construct a proxy or pick a different source. No other registered source (cshapes-2.0, cliopatria, constructed, reporting-areas) operates at admin-1 level for Mexico, so no interim proxy is available either — the state's borders have been stable since 1952 (no territorial changes since admission), so once fetched, GADM's present-day boundary should need no vintage adjustment.

**Territory description:** Baja California occupies the northern portion of the Baja California peninsula in northwestern Mexico, bordered by the U.S. state of California to the north (the international border runs from Tijuana/San Diego to Yuma/Mexicali), the Pacific Ocean to the west, the Gulf of California (Sea of Cortés) to the east, and the state of Baja California Sur to the south at the 28th parallel. Its capital is Mexicali; its largest city is Tijuana. The state's official area is approximately **71,450 km2** (INEGI figure), making it slightly larger than West Virginia or Panama — this is a stated administrative figure, not a measurement of any polygon attached to this entry, since no polygon is attached yet.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row is set. Data for Baja California before 1952 describes the Territory of Baja California Norte, a federally administered territory rather than a state; per the routing decision, that pre-1952 period is deliberately left folded into the national MEX-1848-2025 row rather than backfilled into this entry or given its own territory-period polity, pending a consistency check against how other pre-statehood Mexican territories (e.g. Baja California Sur before 1974, Quintana Roo before 1974) are or are not broken out elsewhere in the database. No successor is set: the state is extant and current, so `end_year` is the open default 2025 rather than a closure date.

## Sourced claims

- Baja California was admitted as Mexico's 29th state on 16 January 1952 by constitutional amendment published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación, converting the former Territorio Norte de Baja California into a state with its own congress and constitution.
- INEGI (Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía) states Baja California's territorial area as approximately 71,450 km2, making it the 12th-largest of Mexico's 32 federal entities by area.

## Decisions

### d-code-iso2-subunit

**Used the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (BCN) rather than a shorter ad hoc abbreviation**

Mexico has two 'Baja California' states — Baja California (north) and Baja California Sur — so a two-letter code like 'BC' would be ambiguous with a future Baja California Sur entry. ISO 3166-2:MX assigns 'BCN' to this state and 'BCS' to the other, so MEX-BCN-1952-2025 was chosen over the shorter MEX-BC-1952-2025 to avoid a future collision, following the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern (57 of 66 subnational rows) rather than the bespoke-name pattern, since Baja California is an ordinary federal state admission with no independent historical identity separate from Mexico.

### d-no-territory-period-row

**Declined to create a separate pre-1952 'Baja California Norte (Territory)' polity in this same pass**

The routing decision flagged this as an open concern: pre-1952 data for the territory has nowhere to go except the national MEX-1848-2025 row, which is much larger in area than the territory itself. Creating a second new polity in the same pass, without first checking whether Mexico's other pre-statehood territories (Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo and its predecessor territories) are handled consistently elsewhere in the database, risked introducing an inconsistent precedent. This is deferred to the open question below rather than decided unilaterally here.

## Open questions

### oq-territory-period-consistency

**Should pre-1952 Territorio de Baja California Norte get its own polity row, and is that pattern used elsewhere?**

Data years 1900-1951 for Baja California are currently routed to the national MEX-1848-2025 row rather than to this entry or to a dedicated territory-period polity, per the routing decision's reasoning. This should be checked against how the database treats Mexico's other pre-statehood federal territories — Baja California Sur (territory until 1974), Quintana Roo (territory until 1974) — to see whether any of them already have a dedicated territory-period row. If they do, consistency argues for creating MEX-BCN-1900-1952 (or similar) as a follow-on; if none do, the current national-row fallback is probably the established convention and this question can be closed with no action.

### oq-gadm-fetch-needed

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 layer for Mexico needs to be fetched before this entry can be assigned a polygon**

The locally cached gadm41_adm1.gpkg excludes Mexico entirely (0 features), so this entry currently has polygon_status: unassigned and no polygon_feature_id. Someone needs to fetch the full global GADM 4.1 admin-1 dataset (or at minimum the Mexico subset) and verify its licence terms (unconfirmed in the routing decision, licence_known: false) before this can move from unassigned to assigned. Until then, any downstream area/coverage check involving this polity will show zero polygon coverage, which is expected and not a data error.

### oq-mexicali-municipio-boundary

**Whether any pre-1952 Mexicali-area statistics were reported at a finer or coarser boundary than the eventual state line**

Federal territories in Mexico sometimes had internal partido/municipio boundaries that did not map cleanly onto the eventual state boundary at admission. This has not been checked for Baja California specifically — if any input series reports at a sub-territory unit (e.g. Distrito Norte vs Distrito Sur within the old territory) that doesn't align with the 1952 state line, that could affect how cleanly pre-1952 rows can later be re-routed if oq-territory-period-consistency is resolved in favor of a dedicated territory row.
