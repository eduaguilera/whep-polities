# Santarém (district of Portugal)

## Summary

Santarém is a NUTS III statistical sub-region of Portugal, in the middle Tagus (Ribatejo) valley northeast of Lisbon, named for its capital city Santarém. This entry captures it as a subnational reporting unit from 1989 -- the first year individual NUTS regions can be attributed under the country's post-1986 EU-accession statistical framework -- through the open end_year of 2025. `type: subnational` is deliberate: Santarém is a component of Portugal, not a sovereign polity, and this entry exists purely to carry data reported at the NUTS III scale without competing against the national PRT-1800-2025 row in family/sovereignty-based matching. Unlike the sibling entries Aveiro and Porto, which are explicit country-convention departures reported under historic district names since Portugal's 1835 administrative reform, no source consulted so far confirms Santarém was reported as a named unit before 1989, so this entry follows the plain NUTS-default rule rather than an earlier start -- a decision flagged as open below because it leaves the bulk of this unit's historical data (1880-1988) unrouted.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures data reported against the label 'PT_SA' / Santarém as a Portuguese NUTS III sub-national reporting unit (unit_id PRT-PTSA, country Portugal), drawn from an extract spanning 1880-2023 with 18,561 valued rows, of which only the 1989-2025 portion (35 data years, 24%) is captured here. That data was previously headed toward default routing under the national row PRT-1800-2025, or toward being marked unroutable for the pre-1989 span like the non-departure NUTS siblings (PT11, PT15, PTBJ, PTEV, PTFA, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG); the first is wrong because Santarém is a fraction of the country's territory, not the whole of it, so national-scale figures cannot stand in for district-scale figures, and the second is the convention's own designated default here since no distrito-level predecessor for Santarém has yet been confirmed distinct from the NUTS-era unit (contrast Aveiro and Porto, both confirmed 1835 distrito continuations). No historical trading-polity source (e.g. Federico-Tena) treats Santarém separately as its own trading territory; the confirmation of distinctness instead rests on Portugal's own post-1986 EU NUTS statistical framework, under which Santarém is one of mainland Portugal's officially delimited NUTS III regions, distinct from its neighbouring regions (Médio Tejo, Lezíria do Tejo's own successor arrangements, Oeste, Beira Baixa) and from the country as a whole.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-span-1989-start

**Use PRT-SA code form and start the span in 1989, not the extract's 1880**

Followed the dominant 57/66 subnational pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, giving PRT-SA-1835-2025, since Santarém is an ordinary NUTS III statistical district and not a bespoke historical territory like Alaska Territory or Hyderabad. Unlike the sibling entries Aveiro and Porto (both explicit country-convention departures reported under historic district names since the 1835 reform), no source found so far shows Santarém was reported as a named administrative unit before Portugal's 1986 EU accession and the resulting NUTS statistical framework; the routing decision's own default rule for this country is that individual NUTS units start in 1989, the first year NUTS-attributable data exists, and nothing in the record distinguishes Santarém's pedigree from the other non-departure siblings (PT11, PT15, PTBJ, PTEV, PTFA, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG) rather than from Aveiro/Porto. The 109 pre-1989 data years (1880-1988, 76% of this unit's 144 data years) are therefore left unrouted by this page rather than absorbed into it -- routing_concerns explicitly warns that leaving measured data with no polity is wrong if an earlier administration existed, but no such earlier Santarém-named administration has been confirmed here (see open question below); if one turns up, this entry's start_year and predecessor field should move rather than the pre-1989 data being force-fit into a 1989-start polity.

### d-prt-two-level-hierarchy

**Portugal is modelled as two levels — 18 districts, and from 1986 the five NUTS II regions that contain them**

The panel reports Portugal at two scales at once: five NUTS II regions and eighteen districts. Both sets tile mainland Portugal independently — measured on the committed geometry the regions sum to 88,798 km² and the districts to 88,801 km², a 0.003% difference — so treating them as one flat list of members would double-count every square kilometre. They are not a clean hierarchy either: the districts date from 1835 and NUTS II was delimited later across them, so five districts straddle a regional boundary (Lisboa is only 47% inside Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, Santarém 60%, Aveiro 63%, Viseu 65%, Setúbal 69%).

The resolution is a hierarchy **split in time**, which works because the regions postdate the districts. Until 1986 the districts are Portugal's direct members (17 of them, 18 once Setúbal exists in 1926) and no region competes with them. From 1986 the five regions become Portugal's only direct members and every district sits inside the region holding the largest share of its area, with that share recorded in the edge's own `basis`. A level-1 read is therefore exact in both periods; a level-2 read of a straddling district is approximate, and says so where it is.

For this row that means: inside Portugal (PRT-1800-2025) 1835-1986; inside Alentejo (PRT-ALE-1986-2025) 1986-2025. Its area splits Alentejo 60%, Centro 40%.

### oq-santarem-pre1989-administration

**Did a distrito or comparable Santarém-named unit exist and report data before 1989, and should this entry's start_year move earlier to capture it?**

The routing decision flags this as the central open problem: 18,561 valued rows span 1880-2023, but only the 1989-2025 span is captured by this entry, leaving 109 of 144 data years (76%) unrouted as unroutable under the current country convention default. Portugal's mainland district (distrito) system dates to 1835, and Santarém is also the name of one of those historic districts (distrito de Santarém), continuously maintained since 1835 like the sibling departures Aveiro and Porto (prt-av-1835-2025.md and prt-po-1835-2025.md). It has not been confirmed whether the pre-1989 data under the 'PT_SA' / Santarém label was in fact reported against that historic distrito (in which case this entry's start_year should move to 1835 and the district treated as a fourth departure alongside Aveiro and Porto, matching its territory almost exactly since NUTS III Santarém was itself delimited on the distrito's boundaries), or whether it reflects some other reporting basis. This should be checked against data/final/nuts_code_names.csv and Portugal's administrative law before the pre-1989 span is left permanently unrouted.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** Portugal's 1835 administrative reform created 17 mainland districts (distritos); Setúbal, the 18th, was carved out of Lisboa district in 1926. Ten of this table's Portuguese district rows already started in 1835, and the arithmetic closes exactly at 17 + 1 once the seven late-starting rows are corrected, which is the check that settled it. This row now spans 1835-2025 and carries all 144 data years (1880-2023), where before only the 35 years from 1989 were routed and the rest were left unroutable.

### oq-santarem-nuts-boundary-revisions

**Has the NUTS III Santarém boundary been stable since 1989, or did it lose territory in a later NUTS revision?**

The routing decision's own routing_concerns raise this directly: Portugal's NUTS III boundaries were revised multiple times (2003, 2013/2016), and at least one neighbouring Lisbon-area NUTS III unit changed composition when the Lisbon metropolitan area was carved out. It has not been verified whether Santarém's own boundary was affected by any of these revisions or has been stable throughout 1989-2025; if it changed, a single 1989-2025 span with one polygon vintage would misrepresent at least part of the period, and this entry might need to be split at the revision year the way overlapping-boundary sibling units elsewhere in this table have been.
