# Massachusetts (state of the United States)

## Summary

Massachusetts, one of the fifty states of the United States and one of the original 13 states that ratified the federal Constitution on February 6, 1788. This entry is `type: subnational` because Massachusetts has never been independently sovereign under the U.S. federal system covered by this database's national USA chain: the row exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory for statistics tabulated at the state rather than the national level, and must not compete with the national USA rows for sovereignty ranking in the matcher. Although Massachusetts's own administrative history as a distinct polity reaches back to its colonial charters (1629/1691) and its 1788 ratification, this entry's `start_year` is set to 1800 rather than 1788 because the earliest national USA container row in this database (USA-1800-1803) does not extend back to 1788 -- the containment gate cannot accept an edge for years a container row doesn't cover, so the gap could not simply be bridged (see decisions and open questions below). The span 1800-2025 then tracks continuous statehood, with one internal territorial reduction (the 1820 separation of Maine, which does not break this entry's chain edges but does affect its polygon's historical accuracy before 1820), through five successive eras of the national USA polity chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Massachusetts as a distinct subnational reporting unit within the United States, created by the agent-harness pipeline processing the administrative unit `USA-MASSACHUSETTS` ("MASSACHUSETTS", country United States of America). No prior polity row existed for Massachusetts at the state level -- the existing USA chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) covers the country as a whole, so any Massachusetts-specific statistics previously had no state-level home and would either be absent or folded into national USA totals, a mismatch whenever a source reports Massachusetts separately from all-U.S. figures. The routing decision's own `routing_reasoning` states this directly: no existing polity represents Massachusetts specifically, and the USA-* rows are national totals for different eras that contain but are not identical to this state. What confirms Massachusetts is a distinct reporting/administrative entity is uncontroversial: it is one of the fifty states, self-governing since its 17th-century colonial charters, one of the original 13 founding states (ratified the Constitution 1788), and long-standing use as a state-level reporting unit in economic and demographic series. The routing decision does not supply a specific source row count or year range for data driving this creation (its `routing_concerns` field instead focuses on the start-year/container mismatch), consistent with this being part of a systematic pass creating one row per U.S. state (following usa-al, usa-ar, ..., usa-ky, usa-la, usa-md, usa-me in this same harness run).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.22_1`, assigned directly (not a proxy) because this is a 1:1 match between the administrative unit and a GADM admin-1 feature already registered and locally present in this database's GeoPackage (`data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_USA.gpkg`, layer ADM_ADM_1). `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Area:** measured directly from the attached geometry in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034) at approximately 21,023 km2. This is a measurement of the attached polygon, not an independent territorial claim; it sits between the commonly cited land-only figure (~20,202 km2, U.S. Census Bureau) and the total land+water figure (~27,336 km2), consistent with GADM's admin-1 boundaries typically including inland and near-shore water but not the full extent of Massachusetts's outer coastal waters (Cape Cod Bay, Nantucket Sound, etc. beyond the immediate shoreline).

**Territory description:** Massachusetts occupies the core of New England along the Atlantic coast, bordered by New Hampshire and Vermont to the north, New York to the west, Connecticut and Rhode Island to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean (including Cape Cod, Martha's Vineyard, and Nantucket) to the east -- readily locatable on any modern map as the state containing Boston. Its external boundaries have been stable since 1820, when the District of Maine separated to become its own state under the Missouri Compromise; before 1820, "Massachusetts" as a reporting/administrative unit was substantially larger, including all of present-day Maine (~91,600 km2 additional). This entry's single GADM-derived polygon is therefore an accurate proxy for the state from 1820 onward but understates the pre-1820 (1800-1820) portion of its own span -- flagged as an open question below rather than silently assumed away.

## Predecessors and successors

Massachusetts has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database. It was a self-governing English colony (Massachusetts Bay, chartered 1629; Plymouth Colony merged in via the 1691 Province of Massachusetts Bay charter) well before U.S. independence, ratified the federal Constitution on February 6, 1788 as one of the original 13 states, and its span here runs open/current through 2025 with no interruption or successor. No colonial-era Massachusetts entry exists in this database, so there is no predecessor edge to record. One real internal split did occur -- the District of Maine separated in 1820 to become its own state -- but Maine already has (or will have) its own USA-ME row rather than a predecessor/successor edge into this one, since Massachusetts itself continued uninterrupted after the separation; see oq-boundary-stability-maine-separation for how that split affects this entry's polygon accuracy rather than its chain edges.

## Sourced claims

- Massachusetts ratified the U.S. Constitution on February 6, 1788, becoming the 6th state and one of the original 13 founding states of the Union.
- The District of Maine, part of Massachusetts since colonial times, separated and was admitted as a separate state in 1820 under the Missouri Compromise (3 Stat. 544), reducing Massachusetts to its present-day extent.
- GADM 4.1 administrative level 1 records Massachusetts's present-day external boundary as feature USA.22_1; measured in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034) this polygon covers approximately 21,023 km2, close to the commonly cited state land+water area of roughly 27,336 km2 total area / 20,202 km2 land area (U.S. Census Bureau), the difference reflecting how much of the state's extensive coastal and Cape Cod Bay waters GADM's admin-1 boundary includes.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

Massachusetts is a standard U.S. state with no distinct historical name of its own separate from statehood (unlike Alaska Territory, Hyderabad, or the Ryukyu Islands, which use bespoke codes). It follows the pattern used by 57 of 66 subnational rows: ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end. The subunit token is MA, the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US code for Massachusetts, matching the two-letter convention already used for the other U.S. states added in this harness run (usa-al, usa-ar, ..., usa-ct, usa-de, ..., usa-me). The full code is USA-MA-1800-2025.

### d-start-year-1800-not-1788

**Start year set to 1800, not Massachusetts's 1788 ratification, because no earlier national container exists**

The routing decision's proposed start_year is 1788, Massachusetts's ratification of the U.S. Constitution (February 6, 1788) as one of the original 13 states. That date is administratively correct, but the earliest national USA polity row in this database is USA-1800-1803 -- there is no container row covering 1788-1800, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's span, so a container edge cannot be manufactured to plug that gap. Rather than leave 1788-1800 uncontainered (a gate failure), this entry's start_year is set to 1800, the earliest year the national chain can actually contain it. This departs from the country convention's implied original-13 rule (ratification date) and is flagged as an open question below, exactly as it was for the sibling entry USA-CT-1800-2025: the correct fix is likely a new pre-1800 national USA row, not a permanent 1800 floor on every original-13 state.

### d-five-container-edges

**Five container edges tile 1800-2025 across the successive national USA rows**

The national USA chain in the polity table is split into five rows by era: USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025. Massachusetts's (adjusted) 1800 start coincides with the first of these, so all five edges are used, each trimmed to that national row's own span, tiling the full 1800-2025 range with no gap.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1800-gap-original-13

**Should Massachusetts's span (and every original-13 state's span) start in 1788, requiring a new pre-1800 national USA container row?**

Massachusetts ratified the U.S. Constitution on February 6, 1788, the sixth state and one of the original 13 founding states. This page starts at 1800 only because the polity table's national USA chain begins at USA-1800-1803, and the containment gate cannot accept an edge for 1788-1800 against a row that does not cover those years. The routing decision itself flagged this exact gap. If a pre-1800 national USA row is added later (e.g. USA-1788-1800, covering the Constitution's ratification through the first national row's current start), this page's start_year should be moved back to 1788 and a new container edge added -- the same fix already flagged on USA-CT-1800-2025 and the other original-13 state pages in this run. Until then, any 1788-1800 Massachusetts-level data has no home in this row.

### oq-what-data-populates-this-row

**What data, if any, currently routes to this new polity row?**

This page was authored from the harness's routing decision for the administrative unit USA-MASSACHUSETTS; the decision object supplied does not include row counts, source names, or year ranges of data actually matched to Massachusetts, only the routing_concerns note about the 1788 vs container-start mismatch. Whether Massachusetts-level series exist across the full 1800-2025 span, or cluster in a narrower modern window (e.g. only post-1866, matching the data extract's own coverage mentioned in the routing reasoning), is unresolved and should be checked against data/raw before the next ingest.

### oq-boundary-stability-maine-separation

**Does the GADM 4.1 boundary correctly exclude Maine for the entire 1800-2025 span, including the pre-1820 period when Maine was still part of Massachusetts?**

The polygon assigned (GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.22_1) captures Massachusetts's present-day external boundary, which excludes Maine. Maine was a district of Massachusetts until it separated and was admitted as its own state in 1820 (Missouri Compromise). That means for 1800-1820, the modern USA.22_1 polygon understates this entry's true historical territory by the full area of Maine (~91,600 km2) -- a boundary change larger than the entry's own measured area. This has not been corrected with an alternate pre-1820 polygon; the 21,023 km2 measured area and 'assigned' (not 'proxy') status apply cleanly only from 1820 onward, and the 1800-1820 sub-span is likely mis-stated by this single-polygon entry.
