# Florida (state of the United States)

## Summary

Florida, one of the fifty U.S. states, admitted to the Union on March 3, 1845, out of the former Florida Territory (organized 1822 from land Spain ceded to the United States under the 1819 Adams-Onis Treaty, formally transferred in 1821). This entry is `type: subnational` because Florida has never been an independently sovereign state within this database's period: it exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory for statistics tabulated at the state rather than the national level, so it must not compete with the national USA chain for sovereignty ranking in the matcher. The span 1845-2025 tracks continuous statehood, with no interruption or external boundary change, across four successive eras of the national USA polity chain (USA-1803-1848 through USA-1959-2025). Florida briefly left the Union during the Confederacy (1861-1865), but that interruption is not modeled as a separate polity row here, consistent with how continuous statehood is treated elsewhere in this database (e.g., Alabama's page does not carve out a Confederate-era row either).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Florida as a distinct subnational reporting unit within the United States, created by the agent-harness pipeline processing the administrative unit USA-FLORIDA ("Florida", country USA). No prior polity row existed for Florida at the state level: the existing USA chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) covers the country as a whole, so any Florida-specific statistics would previously have had no state-level home and would either be absent from the database or folded into national USA totals, a mismatch whenever a source reports Florida separately from all-U.S. figures. The routing decision does not name a specific source, row count, or year range of data driving this creation (unlike, e.g., the Manchuria exemplar, which cites 20 rows across two named sources) -- its routing_reasoning instead frames this as adding the state as a real, distinct administrative entity with no existing candidate polity, consistent with a systematic pass creating one row per U.S. state, mirroring the sibling Alabama, Arkansas, Arizona, Colorado, Connecticut and Delaware pages from this same run. What confirms Florida is a distinct reporting/administrative entity is uncontroversial: it is one of the fifty states, with its own government and long-standing use as a state-level reporting unit in economic and demographic series (e.g., USDA, Census Bureau, and Mitchell-style historical compilations). The open question below flags that the specific data motivating this row's creation was not supplied in the routing decision and should be traced once ingestion runs.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.10_1`, assigned directly (not a proxy) because this is a direct 1:1 match between the administrative unit "Florida" and a GADM admin-1 feature already registered and locally present in this database's GeoPackage. `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Area:** measured from the attached geometry at approximately 170,312 km2 (Florida's commonly cited total area, land plus inland/coastal water, is about 170,312 km2 / 65,758 sq mi -- one of the larger eastern states by area because of its extensive coastline and inland waters). This figure is a measurement of the attached polygon, consistent with Florida's real-world extent, not an independent territorial claim.

**Territory description:** Florida occupies the southeasternmost part of the continental United States: a peninsula extending south between the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic Ocean, bordered by Alabama and Georgia to the north, readily locatable on any modern map as the state forming the U.S. mainland's southern tip, terminating near the Florida Keys. Its external boundary with Alabama and Georgia was fixed by the 1845 admission act and has not changed since; the panhandle-to-peninsula shape is distinctive and stable. The routing decision's polygon_reasoning notes this is a direct GADM match requiring no union/difference construction, and its polygon_detail flags the vintage risk as low because "Florida's borders have been stable since admission," an assessment this page treats as plausible but not independently corroborated against a historical source (see open question below).

## Predecessors and successors

Florida has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database. It enters as a full U.S. state in 1845 and, per the routing decision, its span runs open/current through 2025 with no successor. Florida Territory (1822-1845), ceded by Spain under the 1819 Adams-Onis Treaty and organized as a U.S. territory in 1822, is not itself represented as a polity row here, so there is no predecessor edge to record. A prior Spanish colonial claim over the same land (East and West Florida) likewise has no row in this database's current state. If a territorial-period or Spanish-Florida entry is added later, this page's start_year and container edges would need revisiting.

## Sourced claims

- Congress admitted Florida as the 27th state on March 3, 1845, organized from the former Florida Territory (created 1822).
- Spain ceded East and West Florida to the United States under the 1819 Adams-Onis Treaty, with formal transfer completed in 1821, prior to territorial organization in 1822.
- GADM 4.1 administrative level 1 records Florida's present-day external boundary as feature USA.10_1, with no subdivision changes recorded relative to admission-era boundaries in the routing decision's polygon_reasoning.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

Florida is a standard U.S. state with no distinct historical name of its own separate from its statehood (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad, which are bespoke because they name a polity distinct from any modern state), so it follows the pattern used by 57 of 66 subnational rows: ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end. The subunit token chosen is FL, the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US code for Florida, matching the two-letter convention already used for the other USA state pages added in this harness run (usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co, usa-ct, usa-de). The full code is USA-FL-1845-2025.

### d-start-year-admission

**Start year is 1845 admission to the Union, per the country default rule**

The country convention's default rule for U.S. states is to start at formal admission to the Union unless the state is a departure case (Alaska, Hawaii, or an original-13 state departing to 1791, or a territory with a distinct pre-statehood identity already carrying its own polity row). Florida Territory (1822-1845) is not itself a polity row in this database, and the routing decision explicitly found no departure reason (Florida is neither Alaska/Hawaii nor an original colony), so 1845 is used directly rather than back-cast to the territorial period or to the earlier Spanish colonial period.

### d-four-container-edges

**Four container edges tile 1845-2025 across the successive national USA rows**

The polity table's national USA chain has five eras (USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025). Florida's 1845 start falls inside the third era (USA-1803-1848, spanning 1803-1848), so that edge is truncated to 1845-1848 rather than starting at 1803, and the remaining three later eras each get a full-span edge (1848-1867, 1867-1959, 1959-2025), together tiling the entire 1845-2025 span with no gap or overlap, mirroring the container structure used on the Alabama page for its own 1819 start inside the same third era.

## Open questions

### oq-vintage-risk-uncorroborated

**Boundary-stability claim since 1845 is not independently corroborated**

The routing decision's polygon_detail asserts Florida's borders have been stable since admission, framing the GADM proxy as low vintage risk, but this page does not independently verify that claim against a historical boundary source (e.g., a 19th-century state boundary survey or a Federico-Tena-style historical trading-polity reference). Florida's northern boundary with Georgia in particular was subject to 19th-century surveying disputes (e.g., the Ellicott's Mound line), and it should be checked whether any resulting adjustment is large enough to matter for the ~170,312 km2 figure before this proxy is treated as settled rather than assumed.

### oq-confederate-interruption-unmodeled

**1861-1865 Confederate secession is not modeled as a break in this row's span**

Florida seceded from the Union in January 1861 and was not readmitted until 1868, yet this entry treats 1845-2025 as one continuous span with no interruption, consistent with how the sibling Alabama page handles the same 1861-1865 period. It is unresolved whether the harness's broader convention should eventually split Confederate-era Southern states into a distinct subnational or even national-type polity row (parallel to how Manchuria's contested 1945-1949 period was treated as a separate entry from its 1950-1955 successor), or whether continuity is the deliberate, permanent choice for all ex-Confederate states in this database.

### oq-no-source-data-cited

**Routing decision does not name the specific data driving this creation**

As with the Alabama page, the routing_reasoning for USA-FLORIDA does not cite a source name, row count, or year range of data that motivated creating this row (contrast the Manchuria exemplar, which names 20 rows across mitchell and fao1952). It should be traced which specific dataset(s) route to USA-FL-1845-2025 once ingestion runs, to confirm the entry actually receives Florida-specific data rather than sitting empty, and to check whether that data's own year range is compatible with the 1845 start chosen here.
