# Tarn-et-Garonne

## Summary

Tarn-et-Garonne is a French département in the southwest of the country, seated at Montauban, created in 1808 by decree of Napoleon I out of territory taken from five surrounding departments (Haute-Garonne, Lot, Lot-et-Garonne, Gers, Aveyron). This entry is `type: subnational`: France held uninterrupted national sovereignty over this territory throughout, and the department never functioned as an independent or contested state -- the row exists purely to let production/trade or administrative data reported at department level be matched to this specific unit of French territory rather than being forced onto the national FRA chain, where a routing pass (unit FRA-FRJ28, admin_name FRJ28) found no existing polity candidate representing it. Unlike the ~83 departments created in the Revolutionary reorganisation of 1790, Tarn-et-Garonne is one of a small number of later creations, so its start_year follows the department's own administrative birth (1808) rather than the country's usual 1790 default for this unit type. It has persisted with the same name and (as far as documented here) largely the same boundaries since, so end_year is 2025, the open-end convention for a still-current unit.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision (unit_id FRA-FRJ28, admin_name FRJ28) found zero existing polity name candidates for Tarn-et-Garonne in the current table, meaning any statistical series reported for this department -- production, trade, or administrative data keyed at French sub-national level -- was previously left unmatched or would default up to the national FRA chain, which spans the whole of metropolitan France and is roughly 145x this department's area. Routing such data to the national row would misrepresent a department-level series as a national one. The routing_reasoning also establishes why this is a genuinely distinct administrative entity and not a duplicate or renaming of an existing unit: Tarn-et-Garonne was carved out in 1808 from five other departments (Haute-Garonne, Lot, Lot-et-Garonne, Gers, Aveyron) that are themselves distinct rows or candidates in the table, so its creation is a real subdivision event, not a relabeling. No source file in this repository currently supplies the row-count/year-range detail for the specific dataset that triggered this routing (the routing decision documents the unit and reasoning but not a source-data extract), which is itself flagged in the open questions below.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. No registered source in this pipeline digitises French department-level boundaries: the four registered polygon sources usable for France (cshapes-europe, cshapes-2.0, cliopatria, paine-2024) are all national-scale, and gadm-4.1-adm1 -- which does carry global admin-1 boundaries including French departments -- is only present on disk as an 81-country subset that excludes France, so it cannot be fetched or unioned today. `polygon_source` is therefore `none` and `polygon_status` is `unassigned`; the fix (registering/fetching gadm-4.1-adm1 for France, or a French-specific IGN/INSEE department source) is recorded as an open question rather than invented here.

**Territory description:** Tarn-et-Garonne occupies the confluence of the Tarn and Garonne rivers in southwestern France, bordered by Lot-et-Garonne to the west, Gers to the southwest, Haute-Garonne to the south, Tarn to the east, Aveyron to the northeast and Lot to the north. Its prefecture is Montauban; other towns include Moissac and Castelsarrasin. It is one of the smaller French departments by area, at roughly 3,720 km2 (INSEE-published present-day figure for the administrative unit; not a measurement of any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached).

## Predecessors and successors

Tarn-et-Garonne has no predecessor polity row: the territory it covers did not previously exist as a separate administrative unit, but as parts of five other departments (Haute-Garonne, Lot, Lot-et-Garonne, Gers, Aveyron), none of which is treated in this table as having been "succeeded" by Tarn-et-Garonne, since each of those five departments continued to exist afterward in reduced form and are not otherwise present as rows here. It has no successor: it remains a current French department in 2025, hence the open-end convention and empty successor list.

## Sourced claims

- Tarn-et-Garonne was created by a decree of Napoleon I dated 4 November 1808, the last French department to be created before the 20th century, formed from territory detached from Haute-Garonne, Lot, Lot-et-Garonne, Gers and Aveyron.
- The department's prefecture, Montauban, had petitioned for department status since the original 1790 division of France into departments but was passed over in favour of neighbouring towns; its 1808 elevation was a direct response to that lobbying.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1808-not-1790

**start_year is 1808, departing from the country's 1790 default**

The dominant convention for French départements in this table (per the routing decision's country convention) is to start at 1790, the year of the Revolutionary creation of the original ~83 départements. Tarn-et-Garonne is not one of those: it was carved out in 1808 by imperial decree of Napoleon I from pieces of five neighbouring départements (Haute-Garonne, Lot, Lot-et-Garonne, Gers, Aveyron) to give Montauban, a town that had lobbied for department status since 1790, its own administrative seat. Using 1790 here would assert the unit existed eighteen years before it did. This mirrors the FRJ28 routing decision's own reasoning and is not a change I am introducing, just the one span-basis choice with an available primary justification (Napoleonic decree of 4 November 1808).

### d-three-container-edges-not-one

**Three container edges are required to tile 1808-2025, not the single edge proposed**

The routing decision proposed only `FRA-1919-2025` as the container, which leaves 1808-1919 uncontained. The France national chain in the polity table breaks at 1871 and 1919 (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), so the department's span is covered by three successive edges, one per national-chain era, each basis noting that the department itself is administratively untouched by the 1871/1919 national breaks (which reflect the loss and non-recovery of Alsace-Lorraine, geographically distant from Tarn-et-Garonne).

## Open questions

### oq-no-registered-polygon-source

**No registered polygon source carries French département-level geometry**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning confirms that all four registered country-level sources (cshapes-europe, cshapes-2.0, cliopatria, paine-2024) are national-scale and cannot represent a single department, and that gadm-4.1-adm1 -- which does digitise French departments globally -- is only present locally as an 81-country subset that excludes France. The right long-term fix is registering gadm-4.1-adm1 for France (or a French INSEE/IGN department boundary file analogous to mapspain-ign for Spanish provinces) and fetching it; until then this entry carries polygon_status: unassigned and polygon_source: none rather than a placeholder.

### oq-boundary-stability-since-1808

**Whether the 1808 boundary matches the present-day department exactly**

The department's borders have been described as stable since 1808, but I have not verified this against a primary French administrative-history source in this repository; minor commune-level boundary adjustments (transfers between neighbouring communes/departments) are common in French administrative history over two centuries and would make a present-day GADM polygon an approximation rather than an exact match to the 1808-1919 territory, in the same way the routing decision's polygon_detail already flags this as a 'vintage_risk' for any future gadm-4.1-adm1 fetch.

### oq-fifth-departure-from-country-convention

**This is a fifth documented departure from the 1790 default start-year rule**

The routing_reasoning states this department is 'not one of the 4 listed departures either' from the 1790 default, making it a fifth. It is not clear from what is available here whether the country_conventions.json state file has been updated to record Tarn-et-Garonne as a fifth departure (with its own justification) so that future routing decisions for other post-1790 French departments treat this consistently rather than re-deriving the rule each time.
