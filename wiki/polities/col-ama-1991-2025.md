# Amazonas (department of Colombia)

## Summary

Amazonas is a department of Colombia occupying the country's far southeast, in the Amazon basin, bordering Peru and Brazil. This page covers it as a first-level administrative department from 1991, when the 1991 Constitution converted Colombia's remaining territorios nacionales (national territories, administered directly by Bogotá without their own departmental government) into full departments with elected governors and assemblies, through 2025, the open/current end used for still-active departments in this database. Before 1991 Amazonas had no department-level government of its own -- it appears in national statistics as part of Colombia proper, which is why type is subnational and the polity does not exist as a row before 1991: there is no distinct sub-national administration to give a row to in that earlier period, only the territory as counted inside the national totals. The department's capital is Leticia, on the Amazon River at the tripartite border with Peru and Brazil, and the department includes the Amacayacu and part of other protected areas along the river. Its 1991 creation is one of several explicit departures the country convention records from the default rule (used for departments already existing before this database's start), because Amazonas, Arauca, Casanare, Guainía, Guaviare, Putumayo, San Andrés, Vaupés and Vichada were all promoted from territorio nacional to department at that same constitutional moment rather than having existed as departments across the full database span.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the department-level portion of Colombian statistics tabulated for Amazonas from 1991 onward -- the exact row count and source labels are recorded in the harness's evidence packet for unit COL-AMAZONAS rather than reproduced here, since I did not re-derive them independently in this session (see routing_concerns below). Before this decision, all Amazonas-labelled data, including years after 1991, would have matched only the national row COL-1922-2025, which is roughly 116 times larger than the department (1,141,748 km2 for Colombia's mainland+islands vs. ~109,665 km2 for Amazonas) -- routing post-1991 department-level statistics there would silently attribute a small Amazon-basin territory's figures to the whole country. That was wrong because Colombia's 1991 Constitution gave Amazonas its own departmental government and its own reporting identity from that year, distinct from the pre-1991 territorio nacional era when it had no such separate administration. The historical source confirming this is the constitutional change itself (1991 Constitution, converting territorios nacionales into departments) plus the country convention's explicit departure-rule list, which already treats Amazonas, Arauca, and Casanare identically on this point -- this entry simply applies the same verdict already recorded for the sibling units to Amazonas.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The intended source is `gadm-4.1-adm1`, which is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and marked present_locally, but the actual local GADM 4.1 admin-1 extract only covers 81 countries and Colombia is not among them -- so there is zero fetched geometry for any Colombian department right now, not a boundary-selection problem. This is the `registered_source_unfetched` case: naming a specific `COL.x_1` GID_1 today would be an unverifiable guess, so `polygon_feature_id` is left null rather than filled with a plausible-looking code.

**Territory description:** Amazonas department occupies Colombia's southeastern tip, a roughly triangular wedge of lowland Amazon rainforest wedged between Peru to the south and Brazil to the east, with Putumayo and Caquetá departments to its north and west. Its capital and only significant town is Leticia, on the north bank of the Amazon River itself, at the point where Colombia, Peru, and Brazil meet (the "Tres Fronteras" area) -- a location any reader can find on a modern map by looking for Colombia's narrow southern finger of territory reaching the Amazon. The department's official area is commonly cited as approximately 109,665 km2 (Colombian national statistics, DANE), making it the country's second-largest department after Vichada, though it is one of the least populated. No figure in this paragraph is measured from an attached polygon -- there is none yet -- these are stated administrative-boundary figures pending the GADM fetch.

Because the 1991 boundary is the modern one (Amazonas has not been re-partitioned since), once GADM 4.1 Colombia data is fetched, the department's present-day GADM geometry can be assigned directly rather than treated as a retrojected proxy, since the polity's entire 1991-2025 span postdates the boundary the geometry represents.

## Predecessors and successors

Amazonas has no predecessor polity row of its own: before 1991 it was a territorio nacional administered directly by the national government of Colombia, not a self-governing unit, so its pre-1991 existence is folded into the national chain (COL-1903-1922, then COL-1922-2025) rather than represented by a separate department-level page. This page's predecessor field is therefore left empty rather than pointing at COL-1922-2025, because that national row's own successor field does not list this page (successor: []) and cannot be edited from here without creating an asymmetric, gate-failing edge -- see open question oq-col1922-successor-edge. No successor is declared either: end_year 2025 is the open/current end used for still-current Colombian departments, and Amazonas has not been superseded or re-partitioned since 1991.

## Sourced claims

- Amazonas was elevated from territorio nacional to full department (departamento) status under Colombia's 1991 Constitution (Constitución Política de Colombia, 1991), which converted the country's remaining national territories into departments.
- GADM 4.1's admin-1 layer digitises Colombia's 32 departments including Amazonas at present-day boundaries; as of this ingest that layer is registered in scripts/sources.yaml but the locally fetched subset (covering 81 of GADM's countries) does not include Colombia, so no feature can yet be assigned to this row.
- The routing decision for unit COL-AMAZONAS records that the same back-cast/new-polity split (pre-1991 to the national chain, 1991-onward to a new department row) was already applied to the neighbouring territorios nacionales Arauca and Casanare, which is the precedent this page follows rather than an independently derived rule.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-col-ama

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern**

Chose COL-AMA-1991-2025 over a bespoke code. Colombia has zero existing subnational rows, so there is no country-specific precedent to follow, but 57 of 66 subnational rows in the table use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and Amazonas is an ordinary first-level department with no distinct historical name of its own (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State) -- it is simply the modern department, so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. AMA is used as the subunit token since Amazonas has no standard 2-3 letter DANE/ISO code as clean as e.g. AS for Asturias; it is chosen to be readable and collision-free against the four existing COL rows.

### d-dropped-predecessor-claim

**Dropped the predecessor edge to COL-1922-2025 after the prior rejection**

A previous draft of this page declared predecessor: [COL-1922-2025], and validate_chain_integrity rejected it because COL-1922-2025 declares successor: [] and I cannot edit that page to add this row. Rather than re-assert a one-directional edge, I removed the predecessor claim entirely and instead expressed the pre-1991 relationship through the container edge (this row is contained by COL-1922-2025 for its whole span) plus prose in Summary and Predecessors/successors explaining that Amazonas was administered directly by the national government as a territorio nacional before 1991, with no separate polity row standing in as a 'predecessor' polity for that status. This is recorded as an open question below in case a future editor wants to add the reverse successor edge on COL-1922-2025 instead.

## Open questions

### oq-col1922-successor-edge

**COL-1922-2025 does not list this row (or any department) as a successor**

This page is contained by COL-1922-2025 for its entire 1991-2025 span, and the pre-1991 territorio nacional status of Amazonas was administered directly under that same national polity -- conceptually its immediate predecessor state, before departmental status existed. But COL-1922-2025's own successor field is [] and I cannot edit that page from here. If a future editor wants an explicit predecessor/successor edge between the national polity and this department (rather than only the container edge already declared), COL-1922-2025 needs successor: [COL-AMA-1991-2025, ...other departments...] added, and only then should this page's predecessor field be updated to match -- otherwise the asymmetric-edge gate will reject it again.

### oq-no-polygon-fetched

**GADM 4.1 adm1 is registered but Colombia's department layer is not present on disk**

polygon_status is 'unassigned' because, per the routing decision, scripts/sources.yaml lists gadm-4.1-adm1 as present_locally=True but the locally-fetched subset only covers 81 countries and Colombia is not one of them (0 features for COL in the current local file). This is a data-fetch gap, not a missing-source gap: once GADM 4.1 adm1 data for Colombia is actually pulled down, the correct feature (the GID_1 for Amazonas, something like COL.2_1 depending on GADM's internal Colombia department ordering) should be assigned directly, since the department boundary in that vintage postdates 1991 and needs no proxy reasoning.
