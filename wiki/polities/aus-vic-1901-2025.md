# Victoria (state of Australia)

## Summary

Victoria is one of the six original states of the Commonwealth of Australia, occupying the southeastern corner of the mainland between the Murray River and Bass Strait. This entry captures Victoria as a constituent state from Federation (1 January 1901) to the present, and is `type: subnational` rather than `national` because sovereignty since 1901 belongs to the Commonwealth (AUS-1901-2025); this row exists purely to carry state-level statistics that a source tabulates below the national level, mirroring the five sibling rows already in the table for New South Wales, South Australia, Tasmania, Western Australia, the ACT and the Northern Territory. Before Federation, the same territory was the self-governing British colony of Victoria, separated from New South Wales in 1851 and given responsible government the same year; that predecessor is carried in the table as VIC-1851-1900 (national type, since it was then a distinct polity, not a state of anything). Victoria's external boundaries — the Murray River line against NSW, and the survey line against South Australia — were essentially settled by the 1850s-1870s and have not materially changed since, so a single present-day administrative boundary is a reasonable fit for the whole 1901-2025 span.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry follows the established pattern already applied to AUS-NSW-1901-2025 and AUS-WA-1901-2025 (both `draft`, added 2026-09-07): the country convention for Australia routes state-level source data for the post-Federation period to a dedicated AUS-<ABBR>-1901-2025 subnational row per state, rather than to the national AUS-1901-2025 row, which is reserved for whole-of-Commonwealth totals. Victoria had no such row yet, even though NSW, SA, TAS, WA, ACT and NT all did, which meant any Victoria-labelled state-level series (production, trade or population statistics tabulated by Australian state, e.g. in Mitchell-style historical statistics compilations) had nowhere correct to go and would previously have been misrouted to the national AUS-1901-2025 row, overstating that row's implied 1:1 correspondence between 'Australia' and 'the state that produced the figure'. What confirms Victoria is a distinct reporting/administrative entity rather than a mere subdivision folded into national figures is that the Australian Bureau of Statistics and its predecessors have published state-disaggregated statistics for Victoria continuously since Federation, and that the five sibling AUS-<ABBR> rows already in this table were created for exactly this reason for the other states and territories.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Assigned. `polygon_source: gadm-4.1-adm1`, feature `AUS.10_1` (Victoria), one of eleven Australian admin-1 units in that registered source. This is a `registered_source_feature` route — the source is already registered and the feature is fetched, not merely proposed — so `polygon_status: assigned` rather than `unassigned` or `proxy`. The same source is already used for the sibling NSW and WA rows, so this keeps the convention consistent across Australian states. Vintage risk is low: Victoria's borders (the Murray River against NSW, the 141st-meridian-adjacent survey line against South Australia) were fixed by the 1850s-1870s and have seen no material change since, so a present-day boundary is a valid stand-in for the full 1901-2025 span (see open question below on residual uncertainty).

**Territory description:** Victoria occupies the southeastern tip of mainland Australia, bounded by the Murray River to the north (against New South Wales), South Australia to the west, and the Southern Ocean/Bass Strait to the south; Melbourne is its capital. A reader can locate it on a modern map as the small, densely populated state wedged between NSW and the sea in Australia's southeast corner. Its area is approximately 227,000 km2 — the polygon attached to this entry measures 227,444 km2, which is a measurement of the GADM geometry itself, not an independently sourced territory figure, but it is consistent with published references for the modern state (Victoria is commonly cited at roughly 227,400 km2, making it the smallest mainland state).

## Sourced claims

- Victoria became a self-governing British colony separate from New South Wales in 1851, following the Australian Colonies Government Act 1850, and joined the Commonwealth of Australia as a founding state at Federation on 1 January 1901.
- The Victoria-New South Wales border follows the southern bank of the Murray River, a line settled by an 1870-1871 Privy Council decision and revisited by the High Court of Australia in 1980 (New South Wales v Victoria) over the precise definition of the riverbank rather than any transfer of territory.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-sibling-pattern

**Code chosen as AUS-VIC-1901-2025, matching the five existing sibling states**

Of 436 subnational rows, 425 follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and AUS already has six subnational codes in exactly that shape (AUS-ACT-1901-2025 through AUS-WA-1901-2025 — note the routing decision's proposed container correctly cites AUS-1901-2025 as the container despite one sibling's start year differing slightly by territory type). Victoria is not a historical territory with a name of its own distinct from the modern state — it is the same continuously-named entity from 1851 to today — so there is no case for a bespoke code like ALK-1867-1959 or HAW-1959-2025. AUS-VIC-1901-2025 was free (no existing row used it) and follows the dominant pattern and the precedent of the five sibling states already in the table, so it was chosen without deviation.

## Open questions

### oq-vic-1851-1900-successor-mismatch

**VIC-1851-1900's successor field should point at this row, not at AUS-1901-2025**

The colonial polity VIC-1851-1900 (Victoria to 1900, national type, 1851-1900) is the direct territorial predecessor of this subnational Victoria row: same territory, continuous administration, ending exactly where this one begins in 1901. But VIC-1851-1900's frontmatter currently declares `successor: ['AUS-1901-2025']`, i.e. it treats the whole Commonwealth as its successor rather than the Victorian state specifically. That was presumably correct before any AUS-VIC-* row existed, but now that AUS-VIC-1901-2025 exists, VIC-1851-1900 should be edited to add or replace with `successor: ['AUS-VIC-1901-2025']`, and this page should then declare `predecessor: ['VIC-1851-1900']` to close the edge in both directions. Until that edit is made, this page deliberately omits the predecessor claim to avoid failing validate_chain_integrity, and the historical continuity between colonial Victoria and the state of Victoria is undocumented in the graph even though it is well established.

### oq-boundary-vintage-since-1901

**Does the present-day GADM boundary reflect any change since Federation in 1901?**

Victoria's borders were essentially fixed by the 1870s (Murray River line against NSW, survey line against South Australia), and the routing decision accepted 'minimal vintage risk' on that basis. This page has not independently verified whether any minor gazettal adjustments occurred in the twentieth century — for instance small corrections to the Murray River line as its course shifted, addressed piecemeal rather than in the single 1980 High Court case cited above — that would make the single present-day GADM polygon a source of small discrepancy for the earliest decades of this 1901-2025 span. This is left open rather than asserted as settled.
