# Vosges (département)

## Summary

Vosges is a French département in the Grand Est region, in the historic Duchy of Lorraine, straddling the Vosges mountain range on the border with Alsace. It is one of the roughly 83 original départements created by the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary administrative reform that replaced the ancien régime provinces with a uniform grid of units, and it is not one of the four recognized departures this pipeline tracks for French subnational units: it was not carved from Alsace-Lorraine and ceded to Germany in 1871 (that fate befell its eastern neighbour Moselle, and parts of Haut-Rhin/Bas-Rhin), it is not the Territoire de Belfort carve-out, it was not part of the 1975 Corse split, and it is not a post-1946 overseas département. It has therefore kept its present administrative form continuously since creation. This entry is `type: subnational` rather than `national` because Vosges is an ordinary administrative subdivision of the sovereign state of France, not a state in its own right and not a historical territory with an identity distinct from the modern département — the same reasoning already applied to the sibling entries for Aisne, Calvados, Cher, Côte-d'Or and Doubs created in this same pipeline run. It exists as a row in this table only because unit_id FRA-FRF34 (NUTS code FRF34, corresponding to Vosges) carries statistical data reported at a finer grain than any national FRA row, and that data had no département-level home before this entry.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: this entry is created to receive statistical rows keyed to unit_id FRA-FRF34 (NUTS code FRF34, i.e. Vosges), per the routing decision for this unit — one of a batch of French département/NUTS units being processed in this harness run alongside Aisne, Calvados, Cher, Côte-d'Or and Doubs. The routing decision's reasoning notes Vosges is one of the original 1790 départements and not among the documented departures, so no existing FRA polity represents it: the only candidates were the three national-level France rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), each of which would blend Vosges's département-scale figures into all of metropolitan France — roughly a 95-fold territorial overstatement, since metropolitan France comprises about 95-96 départements. What confirms Vosges is a distinct, real administrative entity rather than an artifact of the source's labelling: it is one of the original ~83 départements created by the Revolutionary reform of 1790, it carries its own INSEE code (88) and préfecture (Épinal), and GADM 4.1's global admin-1 layer digitises it as a discrete first-order administrative feature of France (though, as with the sibling entries, the GADM copy present in this repository is an 81-country subset that omits France).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** `none` — no geometry is attached. Not yet assigned, and not a proxy: no locally-present, registered source can supply a Vosges-specific boundary today. GADM 4.1 adm1 is the correct source for French départements at this administrative level and is registered in scripts/sources.yaml, but the copy present in this repository is an 81-country subset that does not include France (0 FRA rows), so the route cannot execute yet. cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe, which supply the national FRA rows, carry only whole-country geometry and cannot be subdivided into départements. IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS would be the natural France-specific alternative but is not registered either. See open question oq-no-polygon-source-registered.

**Territory description:** Vosges is a landlocked département in the Grand Est region, corresponding to the western part of the historic Duchy of Lorraine and named for the Vosges mountain range that runs along its eastern edge, forming the historic border with Alsace (the départements of Haut-Rhin and Bas-Rhin lie immediately east). It also borders Meurthe-et-Moselle to the north, Haute-Marne to the west, Haute-Saône to the south, and Territoire de Belfort at its southeastern tip. Its administrative seat (préfecture) is Épinal; other notable towns include Saint-Dié-des-Vosges and Remiremont. On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE département code 88, drained by the Moselle and Meuse rivers, which both rise within it. Its modern area is approximately **5,874 km²** (a figure quoted from published French administrative statistics for the present-day département, not measured from any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached — consistent with the general pattern that 1790-era départements were drawn to a broadly similar size, crossable on horseback in a day from any point to the préfecture seat).

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: Vosges has no earlier polity row in this table representing the same territory before 1800 (see open question on the 1790-1800 gap, oq-1790-1800-gap-uncontained). No successor: it remains a live, current French département in 2025, so both predecessor and successor lists are empty. Unlike Alsace-Lorraine départements, it never changed sovereignty; unlike Territoire de Belfort, it was never carved into a separate unit; it is not expected to merge, split, or be renamed going forward.

## Sourced claims

- Vosges is one of the original ~83 départements created by the French Revolutionary administrative reform of 4 March 1790 (Décret du 22 décembre 1789), per standard French administrative history; it is not one of the later exceptions (Territoire de Belfort split off 1871-1922, Alsace-Lorraine départements ceded 1871-1919, the 1975 Corse split, or the post-1946 overseas départements).
- GADM 4.1's global admin-1 layer digitises Vosges as a first-order administrative division of France (INSEE code 88), confirming a real, well-known modern boundary exists for this unit even though the locally-present GADM subset (81 countries) omits France entirely.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the département's 1790 creation date, per the container floor**

The routing decision proposed start_year 1790, citing the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform that created Vosges along with the ~83 original départements, and the country convention's system_start_year is likewise 1790. That administrative date is correct, but it is not usable here: the FRA container chain in the polity table begins at FRA-1800-1871 (cshapes-europe, first vintage 1816), and there is no France row covering 1790-1800 for this entry to container against. The containment gate rejects any edge that falls outside either party's span, so an 1790 start with no container for the first decade would fail immediately — this is the container-floor rule the harness enforces (commit 11e8ca1). Rather than leave a gap or invent a France row this pipeline wasn't asked to create, this entry starts at 1800, matching the earliest available container, exactly as done for the sibling entries FRA-02, FRA-14, FRA-18, FRA-21, FRA-25 (Aisne, Calvados, Cher, Côte-d'Or, Doubs). The 1790-1800 gap is left open below. No data is known to be lost by this choice, since French statistical series at département granularity essentially never predate the mid-19th century.

### d-subunit-code-is-insee-number

**Subunit code '88' follows INSEE numbering, matching the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern and the sibling département entries**

French départements are conventionally identified by their INSEE two-digit code rather than a postal or historical abbreviation. Vosges's INSEE code is 88. This keeps the code in the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> family (57 of 66 subnational rows, and now the established convention for every French département entry created in this pipeline run: 02, 14, 18, 21, 25, and this one) rather than treating Vosges as a bespoke historical territory. Vosges has no name-of-its-own distinct from the modern département and is not one of the four listed departures from the default département history (not Territoire de Belfort, not an Alsace-Lorraine département, not the Corsican split, not a post-1946 overseas département), so bespoke-code treatment (like ALK or HYD) would be unwarranted.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap-uncontained

**1790-1800 creation decade has no France container row to attach to**

Vosges existed as a département from March 1790, a full decade before this entry's start_year of 1800. The FRA container chain in the polity table begins at FRA-1800-1871, so there is currently no polity row representing France 1790-1800 for Vosges (or any other French département entered so far — Aisne, Calvados, Cher, Côte-d'Or, Doubs) to container against. If a pre-1800 France row is ever added to the table, this entry's start_year should be reconsidered and moved back to 1790, consistent with the département's actual creation date and the routing decision's original span_basis reasoning.

### oq-no-polygon-source-registered

**No registered, locally-present source can supply a Vosges boundary today**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning found that gadm-4.1-adm1, the only registered source with département-level granularity for France, is present locally only as an 81-country subset that excludes France entirely (0 rows for FRA). cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe, the sources used for the national FRA rows, only carry whole-country geometry, not sub-national units. No union/difference of currently registered, locally-present features can construct Vosges's boundary. A real boundary certainly exists (GADM 4.1's full/global adm1 layer, or IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS product specifically) but is not registered in scripts/sources.yaml today, so polygon_source is 'none' and polygon_status is 'unassigned' rather than a proxy. This should be revisited once gadm-4.1-adm1 is re-sourced as its full global layer, or an IGN product is registered.

### oq-wwi-front-line-status-unaddressed

**Vosges's WWI front-line and battle-of-the-Vosges history is not reflected in the territorial/data record**

Like Aisne, Vosges sat on or near the Western Front for much of the First World War — the 1914 Battle of the Vosges, the 1915 fighting around the Hartmannswillerkopf massif (just across the border in Haut-Rhin), and the département's role as a staging area throughout the war. This entry's container basis treats 1871-1919 as continuous, unaffected metropolitan French territory, which is true in the strict sovereignty/administrative sense (France never ceded Vosges, unlike neighbouring Alsace-Lorraine), but any production or population series reporting Vosges data for 1914-1918 may reflect wartime disruption, mobilisation, or non-reporting rather than ordinary peacetime figures. Whether the underlying series this entry actually receives cover those years, and if so whether they need a data-quality flag, has not been checked in this authoring pass.
