# Araba/Álava (province of Spain)

## Summary

Araba/Álava is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the three historical territories making up the Basque Autonomous Community in northern Spain, along with Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa. It takes its bilingual name from its capital, Vitoria-Gasteiz, and occupies the southern, interior portion of the Basque Country, bordered by Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa to the north, Navarre to the east, La Rioja to the south, and Burgos (Castile and León) to the west and south. This entry is type: subnational because Álava is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025 (notwithstanding a distinct foral/chartered governance regime discussed under open questions), and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because INE and Eurostat/GISCO have tabulated Álava separately, at NUTS3 code ES211, since the province's creation. It is the first Basque-region province entered into this database: unlike A Coruña, no existing polity row already carried this name and span, so this entry is a genuine create_new rather than the retroactive documentation of a pre-existing row.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES211 (Araba/Álava), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units, following the default system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing reasoning explicitly checked for an existing candidate matching this territory by name and found none -- unlike A Coruña, whose row already existed under a prior convention pass, no ESP-VI or ESP-AL row existed before this entry, and the only related sibling entered so far (ES111, A Coruña) is geographically and administratively unconnected to the Basque region, so there was no risk of this data being silently absorbed into an unrelated province row; absent this entry the ES211 rows would otherwise have had to fall back to the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which is roughly 166x larger by area (505,000 km2 vs Álava's ~3,037 km2) and incapable of representing province-level series without collapsing them into the national total. What confirms distinctness: Álava's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES211) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national aggregate.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, under which Álava is conventionally cpro 01 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False and no sibling ESP province row (ESP-CO, ESP-MD) has yet actually resolved a mapspain-ign feature either, so this entry follows the same unassigned pattern rather than a confirmed one; see open question oq-polygon-source-not-acquired. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and Álava does not coincide with the Basque Autonomous Community boundary (which also contains Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa), so an adm1 feature would be the wrong granularity. Territory description: Álava occupies the southern, interior portion of the Basque Country on the northern Iberian plateau, straddling the Ebro basin to the south and the Basque mountains to the north. Its capital and largest city is Vitoria-Gasteiz, seat of both the provincial Diputación and the Basque regional government (Eusko Jaurlaritza/Gobierno Vasco). On a modern map it is bounded by Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa to the north, Navarre to the east, La Rioja to the south, and Burgos province to the west and south. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 3,037 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025.

## Predecessors and successors

Araba/Álava has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was organized as the historic Lordship (Señorío) of Álava with its own foral institutions, which this database does not model at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, matching the treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain, and since 1936/1978 one of the three historical territories making up the Basque Autonomous Community, as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for other Spanish provincial units.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadística) and Eurostat both report Araba/Álava as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES211) with continuous province-level data collection dating from the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the same evidentiary basis already accepted for sibling entries ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025.
- The routing decision for unit ES211 records the official bilingual GISCO NUTS 2021 name 'Araba/Álava' and notes that sibling Basque/Cantabrian-region NUTS3 units have so far required create_new (no existing candidate matched by name), consistent with this being the first Basque province entered into the database.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-vi-abbreviation

**Used the historic vehicle-plate/postal abbreviation VI, not the Spanish-only name Álava, and not the NUTS digits**

The dominant subnational pattern for the seven existing ESP province rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> with a short letter code (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO), none of which are NUTS3 digits or full names. Álava has no established two-letter abbreviation in this repo yet, since it is the first Basque province entered. Spanish provinces conventionally carry a historic vehicle-registration/postal abbreviation still in everyday administrative use (e.g. plate prefixes: M=Madrid, B=Barcelona), and Álava's is VI, from its capital Vitoria-Gasteiz -- distinct from AL (which is reserved nationally for Almería) and from any Basque-language-only rendering. I chose ESP-VI-1833-2025 over spelling out AA or AL to avoid colliding with Almería's likely future code and to keep the two-letter slot free of ambiguity, since the repo's own siblings (CO for A Coruña, not a Coruña-derived NUTS digit) show the convention already favours a locally recognizable short code over a NUTS-digit transliteration. The official bilingual name 'Araba/Álava' (per GISCO NUTS 2021 nomenclature) is kept in polity_name rather than collapsed to the Spanish-only 'Álava', since the province's own statute uses the co-official form and the sibling ESP-MD entry already keeps a compound name where relevant ('Madrid (province/region of Spain)').

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**mapspain-ign is not yet fetched into the repo, so cpro=01 for Álava is unconfirmed**

The routing decision names mapspain-ign as the registered source keyed by cpro (INE province code) and flags Álava's cpro as 01, but also flags sources.yaml's present_locally as False for this source. The routing reasoning argues the repo's live use of mapspain-ign for ESP-CO and ESP-MD is stronger evidence the file exists than the stale flag, but neither of those two sibling entries actually carries a resolved polygon_feature_id either -- both are still polygon_status: unassigned with a null feature id, exactly as this entry now is. So there is no existing example in this repo of mapspain-ign actually resolving to a geometry; the claim that the source works is inferred, not verified. Before assigning a polygon, someone needs to confirm data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg exists, contains a cpro column, and that cpro=01 is in fact Álava (not, say, Álava's alphabetical INE ordinal being confused with a different numbering scheme used elsewhere).

### oq-basque-start-year-departure

**Does the 1833 default start rule actually apply cleanly to a historically foral (chartered) province?**

The country convention's default start rule is the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, which is what's applied here. But Álava, unlike Galician provinces such as A Coruña, retained a distinct fueros (charter) regime with its own provincial Diputación and tax/customs autonomy through much of the 19th century, only substantially curtailed after the Third Carlist War (1876) and further eroded in 1937-1939. It is unresolved whether the province as a statistical/administrative reporting geography is meaningfully continuous from 1833 given this parallel foral administration existed alongside the 1833 provincial boundary, or whether a future pass should record a distinct sub-period (e.g. a foral-era entry) the way MAN-1945-1950 and MAN-1950-1955 split Manchuria's administrative history. No departure was applied here because the boundary itself (not the tax/governance regime) is what this row tracks, but this should be revisited if foral-era statistics turn out to be reported on a different geography.
