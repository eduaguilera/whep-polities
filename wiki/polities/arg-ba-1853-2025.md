# Buenos Aires (province of Argentina)

## Summary

Buenos Aires is one of Argentina's original historic provinces, one of the fourteen that signed onto the 1853 Constitution as the basis of the federal republic (Buenos Aires itself acceded in 1860 after a period outside the Confederation, but the province as a territorial unit predates and survives that episode). type is subnational because this row is not a claim to sovereignty: Argentina held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1853-2025, and the ARG national chain (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025) already carries that. What this entry exists to carry instead is the reporting/administrative unit "Buenos Aires province," which statistical sources tabulate separately from national Argentina totals and separately from the federal capital (the City of Buenos Aires, itself an enclave carved out of the province in 1880). The province is not one of the four departure cases this pipeline's country convention enumerates for Argentina (the ex-territorios nacionales Chaco/Formosa/La Pampa/Misiones/Neuquén/Río Negro, the Patagonian pair Chubut/Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego, or Ciudad de Buenos Aires) — it is an original province, so the default start rule of 1853 applies directly, and because it remains a current first-order administrative division, end_year is the open convention date of 2025 rather than a data-driven cutoff.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the routing decision for unit_id ARG-BUENOSAIRES (admin_name "Buenos Aires", country Argentina) produced by the agent-harness routing pipeline. The pipeline found zero existing candidate polities for this admin1 unit: zero name candidates and zero boundary matches, meaning no wiki page previously represented Buenos Aires province as a distinct reporting unit. Before this decision, any data keyed to "Buenos Aires province" would have had nowhere specific to route except the national ARG rows, which are fifty-odd times the area and conflate the province's statistics with those of the other 22 provinces plus the federal capital. The historical source that confirms this is a distinct, persistently tabulated unit is administrative rather than a single trade-history compendium: Buenos Aires has been one of Argentina's constitutionally recognized provinces since the 1853 Constitution, with its own governor, legislature, and (from 1854) provincial statistical apparatus, and it remains today Argentina's most populous province and the unit under which INDEC and its provincial predecessors report population, production, and area figures. That is the same class of justification the exemplar (Manchuria) uses for a region tabulated separately by two publishers — here the "second publisher" is simply the continuous existence of Buenos Aires as a first-order administrative division of Argentina across 172 years, which is presumptively how any input data using the label "Buenos Aires" for this period is intending to be read.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is attached in the GeoPackage for this entry (`polygon_status: unassigned`). The routing decision names `gadm-4.1-adm1` — GADM 4.1's admin-level-1 layer — as the correct registered source for a province-level unit like this, but the locally cached copy of that GeoPackage is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Argentina at all (0 ARG features found). The source is real and correctly registered (present_locally=True in sources.yaml), it is simply missing this country's rows on this disk; someone will need to fetch the global `gadm41_adm1.gpkg` (or an ARG-specific slice of it) before a `GID_1` feature id can be named here. `cshapes-2.0`, the source already used for the ARG national rows, only carries country-level polygons and has no sub-national layer, so it cannot substitute.

**Territory description:** Buenos Aires province occupies the east-central part of Argentina, on the Atlantic coast and the Río de la Plata estuary, surrounding but administratively excluding the federal capital (Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires, split off as a separate federal district in 1880). It borders La Pampa, Córdoba, Santa Fe, Entre Ríos (across the Paraná delta) and Río Negro provinces. Its modern area is approximately 307,571 km2 (the second-largest Argentine province by area after Buenos Aires city's exclusion, and the most populous). This figure is the province's officially stated area from Argentine sources, not a measurement of a geometry attached to this entry — no geometry is attached yet. Between 1853 and 1880 the province's territory nominally included the city of Buenos Aires as its capital; a present-day GADM boundary would therefore modestly overstate the pre-1880 exclave-adjusted territory and should be flagged if ever used for that earlier span. From 1880 onward the boundary has been essentially stable, apart from a 19th-century southward extension as the "Conquest of the Desert" campaigns pushed the effective frontier into land later organized as La Pampa and Río Negro territories — so a present-day ADM1 feature is a reasonably low-risk proxy for roughly 1880-2025 but not a precise fit for 1853-1880.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polities are declared. Buenos Aires province is not treated here as continuing from, or transforming into, any other entity in this database: it is a single continuous administrative unit across its full 1853-2025 span, distinct from the national ARG chain it sits inside (ARG-1800-1899 / ARG-1899-1902 / ARG-1902-2025) and from the federal capital enclave carved out of it in 1880, which this pipeline's country convention treats as a separate departure case (Ciudad de Buenos Aires) rather than as this province's successor.

## Sourced claims

- Buenos Aires acceded to the Argentine Confederation in 1860, after having remained outside it as the separate State of Buenos Aires from 1852 to 1859-1860; this entry treats the province's territorial identity as continuous through that episode rather than starting the row in 1860.
- The City of Buenos Aires was federalized and separated from Buenos Aires province in 1880 (Ley de Federalización), which is the reason a present-day province boundary differs from the province's 1853-1880 extent that nominally included the capital.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years code pattern, not a bespoke code**

Buenos Aires province is an ordinary first-order administrative division of Argentina with no distinctive historical-territory identity of the kind that motivates the three bespoke codes in the table (Alaska Territory, Hyderabad, Ryukyu — units whose names denote a specific historical polity distinct from a generic subnational label). It is one of the routine 57-of-66 cases, so the code follows <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>: ARG-BA-1853-2025, using the two-letter postal-style abbreviation BA for Buenos Aires as the SUBUNIT token, consistent with how esp-* codes in this same wiki batch use short subunit abbreviations rather than full names.

### d-container-tiling

**Added two container edges beyond the one named in the routing decision to tile the full span**

The routing decision's `proposed.container_code` names only ARG-1902-2025, which only covers 1902-2025 of the required 1853-2025 span, leaving 1853-1902 uncontained. The polity table shows the ARG national chain actually has three consecutive eras across this window (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025), so this page adds the earlier two as additional container edges with their own start/end and basis, satisfying the containment gate's requirement that edges tile the whole span with no gap and that each edge fall inside both parties' own spans.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-fetch

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 data for Argentina is not present in the local GeoPackage**

The locally cached gadm-4.1-adm1 source is an 81-country curated subset that excludes Argentina entirely (0 ARG features on disk), even though GADM globally does digitise Argentine provinces. Until someone fetches the full gadm41_adm1.gpkg (or an ARG-specific extract) and identifies the correct GID_1 feature for Buenos Aires, this entry cannot move past polygon_status: unassigned. This blocks any area-based validation of the ~307,571 km2 figure cited in Territorial extent, which currently rests on an officially stated area rather than a measured geometry.

### oq-pre1880-boundary

**Whether the pre-1880 span (1853-1880) needs its own boundary treatment**

Before the 1880 federalization of the capital, Buenos Aires province's territory nominally included the city of Buenos Aires, and its effective southern frontier was also narrower than today's boundary before the Conquista del Desierto campaigns extended it southward through the 1870s-1880s. A single present-day ADM1 polygon, once fetched, would not accurately represent either the 1853-1880 sub-span (capital not yet excluded) or arguably parts of 1853-1870s (frontier not yet extended). It is left open whether this warrants a predecessor row split at 1880, or just a documented approximation note once a polygon is assigned.
