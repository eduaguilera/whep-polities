# Zaragoza (province of Spain)

## Summary

Zaragoza is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the three provinces (with Huesca and Teruel) making up the Autonomous Community of Aragon, of which Zaragoza city -- the historic capital of the medieval Kingdom of Aragon -- is also the regional capital. Unlike Asturias, Cantabria or Madrid, Aragon was not reduced to a single coextensive province in the post-Franco autonomous-community reorganization, so Zaragoza's boundary is a genuine sub-region of its autonomous community rather than identical to it. This entry is type: subnational because Zaragoza is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Zaragoza separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES243, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES243) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES243 ("Zaragoza"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (A Coruna/ES111, Asturias/ES120, Cantabria, Madrid/ES-MD, and onward through the ES112..ES241 range), all following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Zaragoza is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states explicitly that no existing polity matches or contains-as-identity Zaragoza province and that no name candidates or sibling matches exist; the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for every other ESP province sibling created in this same harness run. What confirms distinctness: Zaragoza's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES243) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 29x the area of Zaragoza province (approximately 17,274 km2 per INE).

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE province numeric code), under which Zaragoza is conventionally cpro 50 -- the polygon_detail asserts the source is "already registered and present locally" and already used for sibling ESP provinces created in this same run (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra), but the sample rows shown when routing those siblings did not themselves include a confirmed cpro=50 feature, so the specific Zaragoza polygon has not been directly verified to exist in the local file, only inferred from the source's general coverage of INE province codes. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and correctly ruled out, since it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level and Aragon's adm1 unit does not carry province-level attribution, so it cannot distinguish Zaragoza from Huesca or Teruel within the same community. Territory description: Zaragoza province occupies the central Ebro river valley of northeastern Spain, bordered by Huesca to the north, Teruel to the south, the province of Soria and La Rioja to the west, and Catalonia (Lleida province) to the east. Its capital and overwhelmingly dominant city is Zaragoza city, historic capital of the medieval Kingdom of Aragon and today Spain's fifth-largest city, sitting on the Ebro at its confluence with the Huerva and Gallego rivers. On a modern map the province spans roughly from the Pyrenees foothills in the north down through the arid Monegros steppe to the Sistema Iberico highlands in the south. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 17,274 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the mapspain-ign provinces layer's cpro=50 feature is confirmed, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings.

## Predecessors and successors

Zaragoza has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized within the historic Kingdom of Aragon under the Antiguo Regimen's own corregimiento/partido structure, with Zaragoza city as the kingdom's historic capital -- a distinct administrative form from the 1833 province even though the name and rough territory persisted through the transition. This database does not model that earlier Aragonese structure as a distinct polity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, matching the treatment given to ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain as of 2025 (also part of the Autonomous Community of Aragon, which -- unlike Asturias, Cantabria or Madrid -- was NOT reduced to a single province, since Aragon comprises three: Zaragoza, Huesca and Teruel), hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-as-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation ZA rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern already established for Spanish provinces by ESP-AS-1833-2025 (Asturias), ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, using a short letter abbreviation rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES243) or the INE province numeric code (50). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-ZA-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. ZA was chosen because it is the everyday province abbreviation used in Spanish administrative practice (vehicle plate prefix Z, and ZA as postal/statistical shorthand for Zaragoza) and is unambiguous against the other existing ESP subnational codes (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO, plus the six created alongside this one: GI, HU, NV, RI, TE, VI). No collision exists with any sibling code in the current table.

Whoever confirms the feature should also verify there is exactly one province-level polygon for Zaragoza, not a merged Aragon-region feature.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Kingdom of Aragon / Aragonese partido territory has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory now covered by Zaragoza province was part of the historic Kingdom of Aragon and its Antiguo Regimen partido/corregimiento structure, which does not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province's administrative form even though Zaragoza has been the historic capital of Aragon since the medieval period. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment recorded for ESP-AS-1833-2025 and its siblings. Whether a pre-1833 Aragon-level or Zaragoza-specific predecessor should eventually be modeled is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry, but it is more live here than for most siblings because Zaragoza carries a much longer and better-documented pre-1833 political identity (as a Kingdom-of-Aragon capital) than a plain 19th-century province name.
