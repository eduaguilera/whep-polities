# Idaho Territory

## Summary

Idaho Territory was organised by the Organic Act of 3 March 1863 from the eastern part of Washington Territory and the western parts of Dakota and Nebraska territories, and at first covered almost all of modern Idaho, Montana and Wyoming. Montana Territory was cut from its north-east on 26 May 1864, and the Wyoming Organic Act of 25 July 1868 took its remaining south-eastern portion east of the 111th meridian. From 1868 the territory had the outline of the present state. It was admitted as the 43rd state on 3 July 1890.

This row carries the territory from 1868 to 1890 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-id-1890-2025](usa-id-1890-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span starts in 1868, the year the territory reached the boundary this row carries. Idaho Territory existed from 1863, but its 1863-1864 and 1864-1868 extents included all of Montana and then most of Wyoming, so they are different territories; no data reports them and no row is created for them. The span ends in 1890 (exclusive), the year of admission, where USA-ID-1890-2025 begins.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-IDAHO`, label `IDAHO`),
reports 105 rows for 1879-1889 (11 year(s)) — wheat, oats, barley and potatoes (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as Idaho Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The panhandle state of the northern Rockies: bounded by British Columbia on the north, Montana and Wyoming on the east (the Bitterroot Range and Continental Divide, then the 111th meridian), Utah and Nevada on the south (42°N) and Washington and Oregon on the west (117°W and the Snake River): the modern state of Idaho.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.13_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 216,441 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1868
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  No boundary change is known between 1868 and admission, and the state's outline has not changed since, so the modern GADM polygon is recorded as `assigned`.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. Idaho Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1890 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1879-1889; the row spans 1868-1890, the years this boundary
existed as Idaho Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-IDT-1868-1890` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-territorial-series-provenance

**Were the 1879-1889 figures published for the territory at the time, or rebuilt later?**

USDA NASS Quick Stats serves these years under the state name, with no method marker distinguishing them from the post-1890 years (all `SURVEY`, lane `observational`). Whether they reproduce the contemporary USDA territorial estimates or a later revision is not established here; the boundary is the same either way.

### oq-pre-1868-territory

**No row exists for the larger 1863-1868 Idaho Territory**

Created only if a source reports it; the panel's Idaho series starts in 1879.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
