# Mississippi (state of the United States)

## Summary

This entry represents the U.S. state of Mississippi as a subnational reporting territory, from its admission to the Union in 1817 through the present day. It is created as a `type: subnational` row -- the same treatment given to other U.S. states such as Alaska (ALK-1867-1959) -- because Mississippi is a real, geographically stable statistical reporting unit (state-level USDA NASS agricultural data) that is distinct from, and much smaller than, any of the five national USA-* rows that currently span 1800-2025 in eras. None of those national rows represent Mississippi specifically; they are country-wide totals under different national administrative configurations, and Mississippi's own borders and administrative status have nothing to do with where those national-row boundaries fall. The state has existed continuously since 1817 under the same name and (with the exception of the 1861-1870 Confederate/Reconstruction interruption in political status, not territory) the same basic administrative geography, which is why this is a single row rather than several. It is `subnational` rather than `national` because the United States held sovereignty over Mississippi throughout, including during the antebellum and postbellum periods -- the row exists to carry the sub-national reporting geography that state-level statistics are keyed to, not to assert independent statehood in the international sense.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: USDA NASS state-level agricultural statistics labeled "Mississippi", per the routing decision, beginning 1866 (row count not stated in the decision provided). Previously, no polity row represented Mississippi specifically; absent this entry, such data would have to be matched to one of the five national USA-* rows (e.g. USA-1867-1959), which are 30+ times Mississippi's ~123,546 km2 and mix in every other state's statistics -- exactly the wrong-container problem already solved for Alaska (ALK-1867-1959) and proposed for California (USA-CA) as the cited sibling precedent. What confirms Mississippi is a distinct reporting territory rather than a residual bucket: it is a real political and statistical unit with its own government, elected officials, and continuous state-level data collection since well before 1866, on the same footing as every other named U.S. state already or about to be split out of the national USA-* rows in this database.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `assigned` from `gadm-4.1-adm1`, feature `USA.25_1` (GADM's present-day ADM_1 boundary named "Mississippi", read from `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_USA.gpkg`). This is the same registered source and routing pattern already used for Alaska (`USA.2_1`). Measured area in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034) is approximately 123,546 km2 -- a measurement of this geometry, not an independent historical figure.

**Territory description, for a reader locating it on a modern map:** Mississippi occupies the lower Mississippi River valley and the Gulf Coast of the south-central United States, bordered by Louisiana and Arkansas to the west (along the Mississippi River), Tennessee to the north, Alabama to the east, and a short Gulf of Mexico coastline to the south including Biloxi and Gulfport. Its modern area is commonly cited as about 125,400 km2 total (including inland water), consistent with the ~123,546 km2 land-focused GADM measurement above.

**Why `assigned` rather than `proxy`:** the state's external boundary has been essentially fixed since its 1817 admission -- there was no territorial cession, annexation, or partition after statehood comparable to, say, Georgia's earlier western land cession -- so a present-day administrative boundary is a reasonable stand-in for the entire 1817-2025 span, aside from the minor river-meander caveat recorded as an open question below.

## Predecessors and successors

USA-MS-1817-2025 has no predecessor: Mississippi Territory (organized 1798, reorganized with its present boundaries by 1817 after Alabama Territory was split off) existed only as a federal territory, and no polity row in this database currently represents that pre-statehood territorial phase, so the state row begins directly at admission with no polity to hand off from. Likewise there is no successor: Mississippi remains a current U.S. state, so the row runs to the database's standard open end_year of 2025 with no further transition to record.

## Sourced claims

- Mississippi was admitted to the United States as the 20th state on December 10, 1817, after the western portion of the former Mississippi Territory was separated from what became Alabama Territory in 1817.
- Mississippi seceded from the Union on January 9, 1861, was one of the founding Confederate States, and was not restored to full Congressional representation until 1870 under Reconstruction.
- USDA NASS state-level agricultural statistics for Mississippi, per the routing decision that created this entry, begin in 1866 -- decades after the state's own admission year used as this row's start_year.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code USA-MS-1817-2025 follows the dominant 57-of-66 subnational pattern**

Mississippi is an ordinary constituent state with no separate historical identity of its own, unlike Alaska (ALK) or Hyderabad (HYD), which were bespoke because each existed as a distinct polity under its own name before joining or being absorbed into a larger state. Mississippi has always been called Mississippi and was never a separate country, so the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern used by ESP-AS, ESP-CA, DZA-CVD and 54 other rows applies directly. Subunit code MS is the standard USPS/ISO 3166-2:US abbreviation, matching the convention implied by the sibling routing precedent for California (USA-CA).

### d-start-year-is-admission-not-data-start

**start_year is the 1817 admission year, not the 1866 data start**

The routing decision that created this page notes the underlying USDA NASS state-level statistics begin in 1866, but the country convention's default rule is that a state's start_year is its own admission year unless it falls into one of the enumerated departures (Alaska, Hawaii, or the generic post-original-13 note). Mississippi does not match any of those, so 1817 -- the year Mississippi entered the Union as the 20th state -- is used instead of the data's own start. This means the polity row spans 49 years (1817-1866) with no routed data at all, which is intentional: the row represents the territory's administrative existence, not the coverage of any one source.

### d-four-container-edges-not-one

**Four container edges are required to tile 1817-2025, not the one cited in the routing decision**

The routing decision proposed only a single container edge, USA-1867-1959, which covers less than half of the 1817-2025 span this page claims. Reading the polity table's own USA national chain shows five eras (USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025); Mississippi's 1817 start falls inside the second of these. To satisfy the tiling requirement without a gap, this page adds edges for USA-1803-1848 (from 1817), USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, and USA-1959-2025 (to 2025), one per era of the containing chain across the state's whole existence.

## Open questions

### oq-secession-era-status

**Was Mississippi's 1861-1870 secession/exclusion an interruption this row should reflect?**

Mississippi seceded from the Union in January 1861, joined the Confederate States of America, and was not restored to Congressional representation until 1870 during Reconstruction. This page treats USA-MS-1817-2025 as a single continuous subnational entity throughout, on the same reasoning already applied to the national USA-1848-1867 row (which itself spans the Civil War without a break). If a future pass decides the Confederacy warrants its own polity row or a break in the USA national chain around 1861-1865, this state-level row's container edges would need to be re-split to match, since right now the USA-1848-1867 edge silently absorbs the whole war and its immediate aftermath.

### oq-usda-nass-pre1866-gap

**What, if anything, fills the 1817-1866 gap before the routed USDA NASS data begins?**

The routing decision that created this entry reports the state-level agricultural statistics it is meant to receive start in 1866, fifty years after the chosen start_year of 1817. No data source has been checked here for coverage of Mississippi specifically between 1817 and 1866 (e.g. antebellum cotton production figures that some 19th-century compilations do report at state level). If such a source exists and should be routed to this polity, the row's `sources` and `last_ingest` metadata will need updating; if none exists, that near-half-century gap in this row's evidentiary basis should be noted rather than silently assumed away.

### oq-gadm-boundary-vintage

**Does the 2020s GADM boundary correctly represent Mississippi's borders across the full 1817-2025 span?**

Mississippi's borders have been essentially stable since statehood -- unlike, e.g., its neighbor Georgia, which ceded western land claims earlier -- but this was not independently verified here beyond the general USA-state precedent (Alaska) cited in the routing decision. In particular, the state's boundary with Louisiana along the Mississippi River has shifted locally over time due to river meander (avulsion), which GADM's modern boundary would not capture for any specific historical year. This is a minor vintage risk, not treated as blocking `polygon_status: assigned`, but it is not yet checked against a historical boundary source.
