# Karafuto (Japanese South Sakhalin)

## Summary

Karafuto, southern Sakhalin under Japan. "This territory became part of the Empire of Japan in 1905
after the Russo-Japanese War, when the portion of Sakhalin south of 50°N was ceded by the Russian
Empire under the Treaty of Portsmouth." "In August 1945, the Japanese administration ceased to
function following the Soviet invasion of South Sakhalin." It sits inside
[jpn-1895-1945](jpn-1895-1945.md).
[wikipedia-whep-normalize-2026-09-30 §karafuto](../sources/wikipedia-whep-normalize-2026-09-30.md#karafuto)

## Why this entry exists

The label `japanese karafuto` reports Karafuto for 1909 to 1939 (**30 (year, label) pairs**),
beside `japan`, `japanese korea` and `japanese taiwan`. No polity was Karafuto. The label list is described in [whep-normalize](../sources/whep-normalize.md).

**Layer B: 21 iia rows, by item-scoped relabel (issue 422).** Layer B has no Karafuto label: the
layer-B build filed raw IIA `japan: karafuto prefecture` under `russian federation`. Where the
Russian cell is Karafuto's figure ALONE -- the raw Russian labels carry no row for that product,

Every value equals the raw Karafuto row to the digit. 1918 is the whole Russian record that year
(IIA prints no Russian figure for 1918), and the eggs are a five-year run that had replaced
Russian production (388,244 t in 1930, then 286.7 t).

**Not routed here: 44 Russian cells with Karafuto summed inside.** In rye 1911-1917, hemp 1911-1917,
rapeseed 1915-1916, soybeans 1930-1933, flax 1930-1932 and eggs 1929-1930 the layer-B Russian cell
is a Russian sum PLUS Karafuto's value. A relabel would carry Russia with it, so these stay on the
Russian rows; Karafuto is at most 0.43% of any of them (rapeseed 1916). The fix belongs in the
layer-B build (`data_errors.csv` `rus-1918-is-karafuto-prefecture`).

**Double count with `japan`?** Not testable from the data: Karafuto's values are 0.03-0.10% of
IIA `japan`'s in the same cells, inside the source's rounding (issue 422). The data's `japan`
is metropolitan Japan (source_conventions `iia/japan`), and this row's polygon does not overlap
JPN-1895-1945's, so a sum of the two on the map counts no ground twice.

## Decisions

- **Ends 1945**, with the Soviet invasion; the island's post-war history is the USSR's.
- **The container edge is administrative, not spatial.** JPN-1895-1945's polygon (626,507 km²)
  does not include southern Sakhalin -- none of this row's 32,065 km² lies inside it -- although its
  page lists the 1905 cession. So the two rows do not overlap on the map, and this row is the only
  place Karafuto's ground is drawn; the part/whole pair is not registered in
  `polity_composition.csv`, whose containment check measures geometry.
