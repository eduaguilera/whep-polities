# Tarapacá (region of Chile)

## Summary

Tarapacá is one of Chile's first-order administrative regions, created in its present form by the 1974 regionalization law (Decreto Ley 573 and 575), which took effect in 1976 and replaced the country's older province/department system with a uniform set of numbered Regiones. This entry is `type: subnational` — Chile remained a unitary sovereign state the whole time; the polity exists to carry statistics reported at the regional level, not to claim any sovereignty distinct from CHL-1902-2025. Tarapacá is one of the original 1976 regions (then Región I), not one of the later administrative splits that created new regions out of an existing one (Arica y Parinacota split off Tarapacá itself in 2007; Los Ríos split from Los Lagos the same year; Ñuble split from Biobío in 2018). Because it is an original region rather than a later split-off, the country convention's default start rule applies and the entry begins in 1976 rather than at a later split date. It remains a current region, so `end_year` is the open convention year 2025. Under the modern boundary (post-2007), Tarapacá comprises the provinces of Iquique and Tamarugal in Chile's far north, bordering Peru and Bolivia — an area a reader can locate immediately northwest of the Atacama Desert's driest core, with the regional capital at Iquique on the Pacific coast.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Mitchell/regional-statistics rows reporting data under the label "Tarapacá" for years from 1976 onward, which had no polity to route to: the four existing CHL rows are all national totals (CHL-1810-1884, CHL-1884-1899, CHL-1899-1902, CHL-1902-2025), and per this repository's policy a national total cannot stand in for a subnational reporting unit — doing so would misattribute regional figures to the whole country. No prior CHL subnational polity existed to conflate this with (0 existing subnational codes for CHL), so there is no mis-routing history to correct here, only a gap to fill. That Tarapacá is a distinct, separately-administered reporting unit is confirmed by the region's own statutory basis: DL 573/575 (1974, effective 1976) created it as a first-order Región with its own regional government (intendencia, now gobierno regional), a status Chile's National Statistics Institute (INE) has used ever since as the basis for regional-level series such as population, GRP and production statistics. Data years 1900-1975, which predate the Regiones system, are deliberately left unrouted rather than forced into this entry or the national total — Tarapacá existed earlier as a province under a different administrative system (post-1884, following the War of the Pacific annexation) not covered by the current country convention, and that is recorded below as an open question rather than resolved here.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is attached in this entry. The correct registered source is GADM 4.1 admin-1 (`gadm-4.1-adm1`), which digitizes Chilean regions globally, but the locally cached `gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg` is an explicit 81-country subset that excludes Chile entirely (0 CHL rows on disk) — so this is `registered_source_unfetched`, not a missing-source problem: the fix is fetching the Chile portion of the existing global GADM dataset, not registering something new. `polygon_source` is therefore set to the slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` itself (the registered source), and `polygon_status` is `unassigned` rather than `proxy` or `assigned`, matching the routing decision's polygon_route of `registered_source_unfetched`.

**Territory description:** Modern Tarapacá covers the provinces of Iquique and Tamarugal, roughly 42,000 km² in Chile's Norte Grande, between the Peruvian/Bolivian borders to the north and Antofagasta region to the south, running from the Pacific coast (Iquique) inland across the Atacama's northern reaches to the Andean altiplano. **This modern boundary is a vintage risk for this entry's full 1976-2025 span**: GADM's current Tarapacá geometry reflects the post-2007 split that carved off Arica y Parinacota as its own region. Before 2007, "Tarapacá" (then Región I) also included what is now Arica y Parinacota — provinces of Arica and Parinacota — meaning the original 1976-2007 region was substantially larger than the area GADM would return today. Any future polygon fetch must either apply a vintage caveat for 1976-2007 or supplement GADM's current layer with a constructed union that adds back the Arica/Parinacota provinces for that earlier span; no km² figure is asserted here for the pre-2007 extent because no source for it has yet been checked.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity is recorded. This entry does not extend an earlier Tarapacá-labeled polity (none exists in the CHL subnational set) and does not itself get superseded — Tarapacá continues past 2025 under the same regional structure (the 2007 split only removed territory to form Arica y Parinacota, it did not end Tarapacá as a unit), so `successor` is left empty rather than pointing to a non-existent later entry.

## Sourced claims

- Decreto Ley 573 (1974) and DL 575 (1974) established Chile's current Región system, which took administrative effect in 1976, replacing the prior provincia/departamento structure nationwide.
- The 2007 law (Ley 20.175) split Arica y Parinacota off from Región I (Tarapacá) and Los Ríos off from Región X (Los Lagos), reducing Tarapacá's territory from its original 1976 extent to the current Iquique/Tamarugal provinces.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-chl-ta

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern despite CHL setting no prior precedent**

CHL has zero existing subnational codes, so there was no country-specific precedent to match. Chose `CHL-TA-1976-2025` following the 57/66 dominant pattern (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end) rather than a bespoke name, because Tarapacá is an ordinary first-order administrative region created by a generic regionalization law, not a historical territory with a name and identity of its own the way Alaska Territory or Hyderabad are. The two-letter subunit code TA is chosen to be short and distinctive within Chile's region set, paralleling how ESP-AS and ESP-CA abbreviate Spanish autonomous communities.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1976-tarapaca-province

**Should a pre-1976 Tarapacá province (1884-1975) be its own polity?**

Tarapacá existed as a Chilean province from the 1884 annexation (Treaty of Ancón, following the War of the Pacific) through 1975, under an administrative system distinct from the 1976 Regiones. Data years 1900-1975 labeled 'Tarapacá' are currently left unrouted rather than assigned to this entry or to the national CHL total, because routing province-level 20th-century statistics to the whole country would misattribute them, and this entry's span basis is specifically the 1976 regionalization. Whether the pre-1976 provincial system is in scope for this repository at all is an open policy question; if it is, a separate polity (e.g. CHL-TA-1884-1976 or similar) would need its own span basis and, likely, its own territorial extent, since the 19th/20th century province may not match the modern region's boundaries either.

### oq-pre2007-boundary-vintage

**What was Tarapacá's territory before the 2007 Arica y Parinacota split, and how should the 1976-2007 sub-span be handled once a polygon is fetched?**

The GADM 4.1 admin-1 layer, once fetched for Chile, will reflect the current (post-2007) boundary that excludes Arica y Parinacota. For 1976-2007 the entry's true territory was larger. This should be resolved by either splitting this entry at 2007 into two container-consistent periods with different polygons, or by constructing a union polygon (current Tarapacá + Arica y Parinacota provinces) for the 1976-2007 sub-span and flagging the resulting area as a proxy. No decision has been made yet, and no km² estimate for the larger pre-2007 extent has been sourced.
