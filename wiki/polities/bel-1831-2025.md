# Belgium

## Summary

This row tracks Belgium as a territorial-economic unit from **1831**
(Belgian independence) to **2025**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The **1831 start
is a real historical event** — the Belgian Revolution of 25 August 1830
created Belgium from the southern provinces of the United Kingdom of
the Netherlands. Belgium declared independence on 4 October 1830, and
the Treaty of London on 19 April 1839 formalized international
recognition. The CSV start year of 1831 aligns with the establishment
of the constitutional monarchy.

Under the WHEP polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition),
this row begins at a real territorial event (secession from the
Netherlands), not the database floor.

- **1831 start** is a real territorial event (Belgian independence).
- **2025 end** is the database ceiling; Belgium is still active.

Predecessor: [nld-1800-1830](nld-1800-1830.md) (United Kingdom of the
Netherlands). Note the three-way succession from NLD-1800-1830 to
Belgium + rump Netherlands + Luxembourg.
Successor: NA (still active at 2025).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). CShapes records
  Belgium (`cowcode=211`) as a static polygon with area ~3.89 sq degrees
  across 1886-2019 — no changes even during the WWII German occupation
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope).
- **Pre-1886 extent.** CShapes does not cover before 1886. The same
  polygon is likely back-projected to 1831.
- **Eupen-Malmedy.** Belgium gained Eupen-Malmedy from Germany after
  WWI via the Treaty of Versailles (1919). This is a small territorial
  change within the row not reflected as a CSV split. See
  [oq-eupen-malmedy](#oq-eupen-malmedy).

## Predecessors and successors

## Sourced claims

- **1830: Belgian Revolution.** The revolution of 25 August 1830
  created Belgium from the southern (predominantly Catholic,
  French-speaking) provinces of the United Kingdom of the Netherlands.
  Independence declared 4 October 1830.
- **1839: Treaty of London.** Signed 19 April 1839, this treaty
  formalized international recognition of Belgium and established
  its permanent neutrality. It also confirmed the separation of
  Luxembourg as a separate entity.
- **Static CShapes polygon.** CShapes records a single static polygon
  for Belgium (~3.89 sq degrees) across its entire 1886-2019 coverage
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope). No polygon
  changes even during the WWII occupation period.
- **COW state-system membership.** COW lists Belgium (`BEL`,
  `ccode=211`) from 1830-12-20 to 1940-05-28, then 1945-06-26 to
  2024-12-31 [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates).
  The gap (1940-05-28 to 1945-06-26) reflects the German occupation
  during WWII. COW starts Belgium at 1830-12-20, slightly earlier
  than the CSV's 1831.

## Contradictions

- **CSV predecessor = NA vs. historical record.** The CSV records
  `predecessor = NA` for BEL-1831-2025, but Belgium was clearly
  created from the United Kingdom of the Netherlands (NLD-1800-1830).
  This is a CSV oddity per
  [log decision-csv-is-evidence-not-authority](../log.md#decision-csv-is-evidence-not-authority).
  See [oq-predecessor-csv-gap](#oq-predecessor-csv-gap).
- **COW start vs CSV start.** COW starts Belgium at 1830-12-20;
  CSV starts at 1831. Minor discrepancy — COW likely uses the
  date of provisional government formation, while the CSV uses the
  year of constitutional monarchy establishment.

### oq-predecessor-csv-gap

The CSV has `predecessor = NA` for BEL-1831-2025, but Belgium
was created from the southern provinces of the United Kingdom
of the Netherlands (NLD-1800-1830). This is a CSV oddity — not
a source contradiction, but an incomplete CSV field. The correct
predecessor is NLD-1800-1830. Note also the three-way succession:
NLD-1800-1830 splits into Belgium, rump Netherlands, and
Luxembourg.

### oq-eupen-malmedy

Belgium gained Eupen-Malmedy from Germany after WWI via the
Treaty of Versailles (1919). This is a small territorial change
(~1,036 km²) within the row that is not reflected as a CSV split.
Under a strict reading of the WHEP territorial-change rule, this
could be a candidate mid-row split, though the area is small
relative to Belgium's total (~30,500 km²). The CShapes polygon
shows no change (static ~3.89 sq degrees), suggesting the polygon
may not reflect this acquisition.
