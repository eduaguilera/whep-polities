# British Lower Burma (1852-1885)

## Summary

British Lower Burma covers the territory seized by Britain during the **Second Anglo-Burmese War (1852)**: the **Pegu Division** (including the Irrawaddy delta and the Tenasserim coast previously acquired in 1826). After the war, the Pegu region was formally annexed as **British Burma** and administered as a Chief Commissionership, later absorbed into British Burma/British India after the Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885). The territory comprised roughly **116,000 km²**, dominated by the lower Irrawaddy valley and delta — the most productive rice-growing area in all of mainland Southeast Asia.

This polity exists to prevent a critical sub-territory-to-empire routing error: Mitchell's 'Burma' series for 1852–1885 records rice paddy production of approximately 4,015,500 tonnes — physically impossible for the independent Upper Burma rump (MMR-1852-1885, ~437,000 km² of the Irrawaddy uplands, Mandalay plateau, and Shan hills), which **excluded** the delta entirely. The 4M-tonne figure is consistent only with Lower Burma's delta/Pegu rice economy, which became the world's leading rice exporter under British administration during this period. Routing it to MMR-1852-1885 overstates Upper Burma's production by approximately 4.8× and conflates two separate political entities.

## Territorial extent

British Lower Burma comprised three modern administrative regions (modern Myanmar):

| Region | Historical name | Approx. area |
|--------|----------------|-------------|
| Ayeyarwady Region | Irrawaddy Division / Delta | ~35,000 km² |
| Bago (Pegu) Region | Pegu Division | ~39,000 km² |
| Yangon Region | Rangoon / part of Pegu | ~10,300 km² |
| Tenasserim Division (southern) | Tenasserim coast (British since 1826) | ~31,700 km² |
| **Total** | | **~116,000 km²** |

The total of ~116,000 km² is consistent with the area difference between the pre-1852 Burma polygon in Cliopatria (~552,000 km²) and the post-1852 Upper Burma polygon (~437,000 km²): 552,000 − 437,000 = **~115,000 km²**, confirming the implied extent.

**Note on Tenasserim**: The Tenasserim strip (coastal region south of the Salween) was already British since the First Anglo-Burmese War (1826). It was administered as part of British Burma from 1826 and was formally incorporated into the Lower Burma administration after 1852. Whether the Mitchell 'Burma' series includes or excludes Tenasserim output for 1852–1885 is unresolved (see Open Questions). The ~116,000 km² figure includes Tenasserim as part of the full British Lower Burma administrative unit.

### Polygon decision

**Polygon status: unassigned (honest gap)**

No period-specific historical GIS polygon for British Lower Burma 1852–1885 is available in WHEP's current sources:

- **Cliopatria v0.1.3**: Contains only the independent Konbaung Burma entries. The Cliopatria 1853–1858 and 1859–1884 time-steps (~437,000 km²) represent Upper Burma (the rump Konbaung kingdom) after the Pegu cession — explicitly *not* Lower Burma.
- **CShapes 2.0**: Coverage starts in 1886 (one year after Upper Burma's annexation); the 1852–1885 period is entirely outside CShapes scope.
- **GADM 4.1**: The modern administrative regions of Ayeyarwady, Bago, Yangon, and Tanintharyi (Tenasserim) Regions in Myanmar constitute a reasonable period proxy for British Lower Burma's territory, as the boundaries of these delta and coastal regions are geographically stable (delta topography constrains them). However, the GADM Myanmar file (gadm41_MMR.gpkg) is not currently in the WHEP geodata collection.

**Recommended polygon construction** (when GADM Myanmar is ingested):
- GADM level-1 features: Ayeyarwady Region (MMR.2_1), Bago Region (MMR.13_1 or equivalent), Yangon Region (MMR.15_1 or equivalent), and Tanintharyi Region (southern Tenasserim portion)
- Method: composed union of GADM adm1 features
- Expected area: ~116,000 km² (cross-check against Cliopatria delta: ~552,000 − ~437,000 = ~115,000 km²)
- Polygon confidence: **medium** — physical delta/coastal geography is stable; administrative boundary details may vary slightly from colonial divisions

Until GADM Myanmar is available, `polygon_status: unassigned` is the appropriate flag. A constructed estimate from the Cliopatria difference (Burma 1842–1852 minus Burma 1853–1858) could serve as an interim solution.

**Area cross-check**: Mitchell data (~4,015,500 tonnes rice paddy for 1852–1885) vs. MMR-1885-2025 full Myanmar (~6.2M tonnes). Ratio Lower Burma / full Myanmar ≈ 0.65; the delta/Lower Burma regions produce ~60–70% of Myanmar's total rice output (historically documented), consistent with this magnitude. This strongly confirms the data covers British Lower Burma, not Upper Burma.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor**: [MMR-1826-1852](mmr-1826-1852.md) — the undivided Konbaung Burma (1826–1852) encompassed both Upper and Lower Burma before British annexation of Pegu in 1852. Arakan and Tenasserim were already British from 1826.
- **Successor**: [MMR-1885-2025](mmr-1885-2025.md) — after the Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885), Britain annexed Upper Burma, and British Burma became a single unified province of India encompassing the entire territory. The Lower Burma administrative unit merged into British Burma proper.

**Relationship to MMR-1852-1885 (Upper Burma)**: These two polities are contemporary and spatially non-overlapping. Together they cover roughly the same territory as the predecessor MMR-1826-1852. They are not in a parent-child hierarchy; they are sibling partitions of historical Burma.

## Why this entry was created

The **WHEP sub-territory rule** prohibits routing data measuring one territorial unit to a polity whose polygon represents a different (larger or non-overlapping) territory. The Mitchell 'Burma' series for 1852–1885 records rice paddy statistics that are:

1. **Geographically impossible** for Upper Burma alone: ~4M tonnes from a landlocked upland region without the Irrawaddy delta is implausible; the delta produced ~80–90% of Burma's rice surplus.
2. **Consistent in magnitude** with the Lower Burma delta economy: ~4M tonnes rice from ~116,000 km² of delta land is a plausible colonial-era figure (Lower Burma was the world's leading rice exporter by the 1870s-80s).
3. **Consistent with British administrative statistics**: British colonial authorities collected and published agricultural data for their administered territories; the independent Konbaung kingdom of Upper Burma would not have reported to British statistical sources.

Without this entry, the Mitchell data would be incorrectly assigned to MMR-1852-1885 (Upper Burma), overstating that polity's production by approximately 4.8× and conflating the productive Irrawaddy delta with the Konbaung kingdom's upland territories.

## Sourced claims

- The Second Anglo-Burmese War (1852) resulted in British annexation of Pegu (Lower Burma), reducing the independent Konbaung kingdom to Upper Burma only (~437,000 km²). [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- Cliopatria v0.1.3 Burma time-steps: area drops from ~552,000 km² (1842–1852) to ~437,000 km² (1853–1858), implying ~115,000 km² ceded to Britain. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §scope](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#scope)
- British Lower Burma (Pegu) became the dominant rice-producing and exporting region of mainland Southeast Asia under British administration, particularly from the 1860s onward with the opening of the Suez Canal (1869) expanding export markets.
- Mitchell's 'Burma' series for 1852–1885 records rice paddy at ~4,015,500 tonnes — a 4.8× increase over the larger pre-1852 Burma (~834,000 tonnes), which is geographically impossible for the smaller Upper Burma rump and consistent only with delta-dominated Lower Burma production statistics. ## Contradictions

None identified. The territorial scope (Pegu/Irrawaddy delta ~116,000 km²) is confirmed by three independent cross-checks: (1) Cliopatria area difference, (2) modern GADM administrative region areas, and (3) data magnitude consistency with known Lower Burma rice export volumes.

## Decisions

- **dec-lwr-burma-polygon-unassigned**: Polygon status set to `unassigned` (honest gap). No period-specific historical GIS for British Lower Burma 1852–1885 exists in current WHEP sources. GADM Myanmar (Ayeyarwady + Bago + Yangon + Tanintharyi regions, composed union) is identified as the recommended proxy source; implementation deferred until gadm41_MMR.gpkg is ingested. Polygon area of ~116,000 km² is documented as an estimate derived from the Cliopatria Upper/pre-Upper Burma area difference.

- **dec-lwr-burma-mitchell-routing**: Mitchell 'Burma' data for 1852–1885 is routed to MMR-LWR-1852-1885 (this polity) via the alias `burma,mitchell,1852,1885`. The data measures British-administered Lower Burma agricultural production, not the independent Konbaung kingdom. Routing to MMR-1852-1885 (Upper Burma) is prohibited by the WHEP sub-territory rule.

## Open questions

### oq-mitchell-burma-scope

Does Mitchell's 'Burma' series for 1852–1885 cover:
(a) British Lower Burma only (~116,000 km²), or
(b) All British-controlled Burma including Tenasserim/Arakan (Tenasserim was British since 1826), or
(c) Some sub-set of the delta only?

The 4M-tonne figure strongly suggests a large rice-producing territory is captured. Confirmation from Mitchell's source notes or comparison with other colonial Burma agricultural series would clarify exact territorial scope.

### oq-tenasserim-arakan-separation

Tenasserim and Arakan were British territories from 1826 (First Anglo-Burmese War), predating the 1852 annexation of Pegu. If Mitchell's pre-1852 'Burma' series is compared carefully, it may be possible to isolate the Pegu contribution from the Tenasserim/Arakan contribution. This would clarify whether a separate 'British Tenasserim (1826–1885)' or 'British Arakan (1826–1885)' polity is warranted for the 1826–1852 period, or whether both regions' data is already captured in MMR-1826-1852.

### oq-gadm-polygon-extraction

GADM 4.1 Myanmar (gadm41_MMR.gpkg) is not in the current WHEP geodata collection. When it is ingested, the composed union of Ayeyarwady Region + Bago Region + Yangon Region + Tanintharyi Region (Tenasserim) should be used to construct the Lower Burma proxy polygon. Expected area ~116,000 km².
