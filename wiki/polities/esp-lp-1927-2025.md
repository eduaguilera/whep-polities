# Las Palmas (province of Spain)

## Summary

Las Palmas is one of Spain's 50 provinces, covering the eastern Canary Islands: Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, Lanzarote, and the small islets of Alegranza, La Graciosa, Montaña Clara, Lobos, and Alegranza. It did not exist as a distinct administrative unit before 1927. The Canary Islands were organized as a single province in the 1833 Javier de Burgos provincial reform, with its capital seat rotating and long-standing rivalry between the archipelago's two largest cities, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria and Santa Cruz de Tenerife. That rivalry was resolved in 1927 by royal decree splitting the single Canarias province into two: Las Palmas (Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, Lanzarote) and Santa Cruz de Tenerife (Tenerife, La Palma, La Gomera, El Hierro). This entry is `type: subnational` because Las Palmas is a province of the Spanish state, not a sovereign or quasi-sovereign polity — the same convention used for the other 50 ESP provincial entries in this table. Its start year (1927) marks the moment the province came into existence as a distinct reporting and administrative unit; its end year (2025, open) reflects that it remains current.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** the routed unit is raw admin code `ESP-ES701` ("Las Palmas"), a Spanish province-level (NUTS3-equivalent) reporting unit; the routing decision does not state a row count or source directly on this unit, only that it is being separated out of what would otherwise flow to national Spain or to the undivided Canary Islands.

**What it was previously matched to, and why that was wrong:** absent this entry, ES701 data would fall through to either `ESP-1800-2025` (national Spain — far too coarse, discarding the fact that Spain reports at province granularity) or, worse, to an undivided "Canary Islands" bucket for years after 1927, when no such single province existed anymore. The routing reasoning is explicit that pre-1927 Canary Islands data cannot be attributed to Las Palmas itself, precisely because Las Palmas was not yet a distinct unit — which is why this entry's `start_year` is 1927 and not 1833 or 1880 (the dates that would apply to the undivided predecessor, or to Spain's general provincial reform).

**What confirms it was distinct:** the 1927 royal decree splitting the single Canarias province into Las Palmas and Santa Cruz de Tenerife is standard, well-documented Spanish administrative history (paralleling how Spain's 49 other mainland/insular provinces already appear individually in this table from 1833). Las Palmas has functioned as an independent province — with its own Diputación Provincial, its own INE province code (35), and its own statistical reporting — for essentially a century since. The other 49 Spanish provincial entries already in the table (e.g. `ESP-A-1833-2025`, `ESP-B-1833-2025`) establish that Spain is tracked at province granularity throughout this repository; Las Palmas simply enters that granularity 94 years later than its siblings, because that is when it first existed as a separate reporting unit.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. The routing decision classifies this as `registered_source_unfetched`: `mapspain-ign` is the correct source — the repository already routes the other 50 Spanish provinces through it, keyed by INE `cpro` code, and Las Palmas's boundary (cpro 35) has been stable since 1927 — but `data/geodata/mapspain-ign/provinces.gpkg` is not present on disk in this environment, so no `polygon_feature_id` can be assigned yet. `polygon_status: unassigned` records that gap explicitly rather than leaving a fabricated feature id.

**Territory description:** Las Palmas province comprises the three easternmost Canary Islands — Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, and Lanzarote — plus the uninhabited Chinijo archipelago (Alegranza, La Graciosa, Montaña Clara) north of Lanzarote, and the Lobos islet. It sits roughly 100-150 km off the northwest African coast (Morocco/Western Sahara), at approximately 27.5-29.5°N, 13.4-15.8°W. Its combined land area is approximately 4,065 km2 (Gran Canaria ~1,560 km2, Fuerteventura ~1,660 km2, Lanzarote ~846 km2, plus minor islets), making it noticeably smaller than its sibling Santa Cruz de Tenerife province (~3,381 km2 across four larger-population but smaller-total-area islands, dominated by Tenerife itself at ~2,034 km2). This is a description of the territory from general geography, not a figure measured from an attached polygon — no polygon is attached to this entry yet.

## Predecessors and successors

Las Palmas has no predecessor entry in this table yet: before 1927 its territory was part of a single, undivided Canary Islands province (created 1833) that does not currently have its own polity row and would require a separate create_new proposal, as the routing decision itself notes. Las Palmas has no successor: it remains a current, active province of Spain through the present (end_year 2025 is open/ongoing, not a closure). It has one sibling born of the same 1927 administrative split, Santa Cruz de Tenerife, whose polity status is unconfirmed (see open questions).

## Sourced claims

- The Canary Islands were constituted as a single province in the 1833 territorial division of Spain drafted by Javier de Burgos, alongside the other 48 mainland/Balearic provinces created in the same reform.
- Royal decree of 21 September 1927 (Real Decreto de 21 de septiembre de 1927) divided the province of Canarias into two provinces, Las Palmas and Santa Cruz de Tenerife, each governed by its own Diputación Provincial, resolving a decades-long rivalry ('pleito insular') between Gran Canaria and Tenerife over administrative primacy.
