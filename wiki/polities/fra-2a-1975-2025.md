# Corse-du-Sud

## Summary

Corse-du-Sud (INSEE department code 2A) is the southern of the two departements that make up the island of Corsica, created on 1 January 1976 (effective from a 1975 law) when the single, pre-existing departement of Corse was split into Corse-du-Sud and Haute-Corse. It comprises the southern half of the island, including the city of Ajaccio (the departmental prefecture and Corsica's largest city) and the town of Bonifacio at the island's southern tip. `type: subnational` is used because this is a first-level administrative division (departement) of the French Republic, not a sovereign or formerly-sovereign state: France held uninterrupted sovereignty over the territory before, during, and after the 1975 split, and the entry exists solely to carry a reporting territory that a statistical series measures separately from both the national FRA total and from Haute-Corse.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision (unit_id FRA-FRM01, NUTS/admin code FRM01) found rows labeled at the Corse-du-Sud level with a data extract running back to 1862, but the administrative unit itself did not exist before 1975 — before that date the territory was reported, if at all, as part of the single undivided departement of Corse. Those rows were previously being matched to FRA-1919-2025, the national France row, which is roughly 165x the area of this one departement (France ~551,700 km2 vs. this departement's ~4,000 km2) and therefore cannot support a departement-level statistical series without deflating every per-area or per-capita figure computed against it. No existing FRA polity in the table represents Corse-du-Sud specifically or Corsica as a whole sub-unit; the national rows only ever cover all of France. The country convention's explicit departure list treats the 1975 Corse split as a genuine administrative event requiring two new rows (this one and Haute-Corse), rather than continuing to fold the post-1975 data into the national total or into a single undifferentiated "Corse" row. Federico-Tena-style departement-level reporting and modern INSEE/Eurostat NUTS statistics both tabulate Corse-du-Sud and Haute-Corse as distinct units from 1975 onward, which is the historical confirmation that this is a distinct reporting entity and not just a re-aggregation convenience.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. `polygon_status: unassigned` because the route selected by the pipeline is `registered_source_unfetched`: GADM 4.1's ADM1 layer (`gadm-4.1-adm1`) is the correct, registered source for a French departement boundary — GADM digitises departements as ADM1 units with GID_1 codes — but the local `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg` extract only covers a subset of 81 countries and was fetched without France, so it currently holds zero FRA features. The slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` is recorded as `polygon_source` per the routing decision's instruction that the registered slug itself is the value when a source is registered but not yet fetched; `polygon_feature_id` is left null until the FRA rows (or the global ADM1 layer) are pulled in and the GID_1 code for Corse-du-Sud (department 2A) can be identified.

**Territory description:** Corse-du-Sud is the southern departement of the island of Corsica in the Mediterranean, south of the Haute-Corse departement (the two together cover the whole island). Its prefecture is Ajaccio; it also contains Sartène, Porto-Vecchio, and Bonifacio at the island's southern tip. Modern published area for Corse-du-Sud is approximately 4,014 km2 (INSEE), roughly a little over half of Corsica's total ~8,680 km2. No polygon is attached to this entry yet, so no geometry-derived figure is reported here — the 4,014 km2 above is INSEE's own stated departmental area, not a measurement of an attached shape.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor is recorded: this entry's start_year of 1975 marks the departement's creation by administrative split, not a continuation of an existing polity's territory under a new code, so linking it as successor to a hypothetical "undivided Corse" row would misstate the event (the pre-1975 Corse departement is not itself in the table as a distinct row to link to). No successor is recorded: Corse-du-Sud remains the current departement today, with `end_year: 2025` reflecting the table's standard open-ended-present convention rather than any dissolution.

## Sourced claims

- INSEE records the creation of Corse-du-Sud and Haute-Corse effective 1 January 1976, implementing the 1975 law (no. 75-356) that split the single departement of Corse into two.
- INSEE's current departmental-area statistics give Corse-du-Sud an area of approximately 4,014 km2, with Ajaccio as its prefecture.

## Decisions

### d-code-uses-insee-2a

**Polity code uses INSEE department code 2A, not a numeric FRM01/NUTS code**

The routing decision's admin_name field (FRM01) is a NUTS-style identifier used by the data source, not the departement's own administrative code. Following the dominant subnational pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> observed across 82 existing FRA rows (e.g. FRA-02, FRA-08, FRA-14 using INSEE department numbers), Corse-du-Sud's actual INSEE department code is '2A' (Haute-Corse is '2B'), which is the identifier a reader or a sibling FRA page would recognize. I used FRA-2A-1975-2025 rather than a code built from the source's internal FRM01 label, since the source label is a filing artifact of the extract, not the departement's own name. This departs from copying the routing decision's admin_name verbatim, but stays within the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, just with the correct subunit token.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1975-corse-data-fate

**Where do pre-1975 rows labeled at the Corse-du-Sud/FRM01 level actually belong?**

The routing decision flagged that 97.6% of rows carry an 'observed/blank' method share, which is surprising given Corse-du-Sud only existed as an administrative unit from 1975 onward, yet the data extract runs back to 1862. It is unconfirmed whether those pre-1975 rows are genuinely mislabeled as this NUTS code, or whether they were retrospectively coded to FRM01/FRM02 by the source publisher using modern department boundaries applied backward onto historical Corse data (a common practice in long-run regional statistical series). If the latter, those pre-1975 rows should probably be routed to this entry too under a back-cast convention rather than left orphaned or sent to the undivided-Corse alternative, but that decision needs the actual pre-1975 row content inspected, which this page-authoring step did not do.

### oq-gadm-fra-fetch-needed

**GADM 4.1 ADM1 has not been fetched for France; this entry has no polygon until it is**

This polity currently ships with polygon_status: unassigned and no feature_id, because the local GADM 4.1 ADM1 GeoPackage excludes France entirely. Until someone fetches the FRA subset (or the global ADM1 layer) into data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg, this entry — and its sibling Haute-Corse entry, plus the other five FRA departement pages created alongside it in this same pipeline run (fra-04, fra-05, fra-13, fra-83, fra-84) — will remain without geometry. Whether GADM's present-day (c. 2020s) department boundary for Corse-du-Sud is a safe proxy for the full 1975-2025 span also needs confirming once fetched: no boundary change is known since 1975, but that has not been independently verified against a historical French administrative boundary source.
