# Salamanca (province of Spain)

## Summary

Salamanca is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the nine provinces making up the Castile and Leon autonomous community in the western interior of the country. Its capital and largest city is the city of Salamanca, seat of one of the world's oldest universities (founded 1218/1254) and a UNESCO World Heritage site. The province sits on Spain's western border with Portugal: bounded by Zamora to the north, Valladolid and Avila to the east, Caceres to the south, and the Portuguese districts of Braganca and Guarda to the west. This entry is type: subnational because Salamanca is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Salamanca separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES415, for over a century and a half. No pre-existing database row matched this unit by name (0 name candidates; only A Coruna, Madrid, and national Spain share iso3 ESP, none of which is Salamanca), so this is a genuine create_new following the same house convention already applied to the other Spanish provinces routed in this pass.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES415 ("Salamanca"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish province units, with data spanning 1865-2021 per the routing decision's own description of the data's extent, though a system start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 are used for the polity span rather than the data's own extent, since the province remains current. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing_reasoning states plainly that Salamanca has "0 candidates by name" among existing polities, and the only ESP-iso3 rows are A Coruna, Madrid, and national Spain, none of which is Salamanca -- so absent this entry, Salamanca's data would have had no province-specific row to route to and would either be dropped or misrouted to the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 against Salamanca's ~12,350 km2 is about 41x larger and incapable of representing province-level series without collapsing them into the national total. What confirms distinctness: this entry exactly mirrors the pattern of sibling units (ES112, ES113, and others) that all received create_new verdicts in the same convention pass, and INE/Eurostat's continuous separate tabulation of Salamanca as NUTS3 ES415 is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for ESP-CO-1833-2025, ESP-LU-1833-2025, ESP-OR-1833-2025 and the other newly staged ESP province rows in this branch.

Territory description: Salamanca province occupies the western part of Castile and Leon, on Spain's border with Portugal. Its capital is the city of Salamanca on the Tormes river. The province spans plains in the north and east (the Campo Charro and Tierra del Vino) and mountainous terrain in the south along the Sierra de Bejar and Sierra de Francia, part of the Sistema Central. On a modern map it is bounded by Zamora province to the north, Valladolid and Avila to the east, Caceres to the south, and Portugal (Braganca and Guarda districts) to the west. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 12,350 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. ### d-start-year-1833-convention

**Used the country convention start year 1833, not the data's own extent**

The routing decision explicitly states the span should be 1833-2025 per the country convention, not derived from the data's 1865-2021 extent. Salamanca was one of the provinces created simultaneously in the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform, so start_year is set to that administrative creation date rather than to the first year for which this pipeline happens to have data (1865). This mirrors the treatment of every other ESP province row created under the same convention pass, none of which tie start_year to their own earliest data row. The routing_reasoning also explicitly notes that no departure applies to Salamanca (unlike the 1927 Canary Islands split), ruling out any alternative start year.
