# Coimbra (district/NUTS III region of Portugal)

## Summary

Coimbra is one of the historic distritos created in Portugal's 1835 liberal administrative reorganization, which replaced the old ancien-régime comarcas with a uniform grid of districts under a civil governor. The district is centered on the city of Coimbra in west-central Portugal, on the Mondego river, and roughly corresponds to the modern NUTS III sub-region "Região de Coimbra." This entry is `type: subnational` because Coimbra was never sovereign — Portugal held sovereignty over it throughout — but it is the territorial unit that two of the sources routed to this pipeline actually report on: a district-level administrative division, and later, from 1986, the equivalent NUTS III statistical region used for EU regional reporting. Portugal's 1835 distritos survived largely intact as administrative and later statistical geographies (with municipality-level boundary tweaks) through the 20th century, which is why this entry treats the pre-1986 district and the post-1986 NUTS III region as the same continuously-administered territory rather than as two separate polities, following the same "departure from the NUTS-1986 default" pattern already used for the Aveiro entry in the Portugal country convention.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision (unit_id PRT-PTCO, admin_name PT_CO) reports data for Coimbra with a data span of 1880-2023. Before this entry, there was no Coimbra-specific polity: the only Portuguese row in the database was the national PRT-1800-2025 entry, which covers the whole of mainland Portugal, the Azores, and Madeira, and is roughly 15x the area of the Coimbra district. Routing Coimbra-specific statistics (e.g. district/NUTS III agricultural or demographic series) to PRT-1800-2025 would silently conflate one of Portugal's eighteen mainland districts with the entire country. The 1835 start_year, rather than 1986 (when NUTS III labeling began), follows the same convention already adopted for Aveiro in the Portugal country conventions file: Coimbra distrito is one of the units created by the 1835 division of the kingdom into distritos, so the administrative and statistical entity long predates its current EU statistical-region name. No prior Federico-Tena or other historical trading-polity source is needed to establish distinctness here — the district's creation in national law (the 1835 reform) is the confirming historical source that this was a distinct administrative unit, not a claim resting only on modern NUTS geography.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is attached in this entry. The routing decision correctly identifies `gadm-4.1-adm1` as the right kind of source (GADM's global admin-1 layer includes Portuguese distritos, including Coimbra) and it is already registered in `scripts/sources.yaml`, but the locally checked-out subset at `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg` is curated to 81 countries and Portugal is not among them (0 features for PRT). This is therefore a **fetch gap**, not an availability gap: `polygon_source: gadm-4.1-adm1` with `polygon_status: unassigned` records that the correct registered source is known but its Portugal rows have not yet been pulled into the local file. No construction from other registered sources is possible: the only other PRT-covering source in this repo, `cshapes-2.0`, supplies a single national polygon with no internal district subdivision, so it cannot be unioned or differenced to isolate Coimbra.

**Territory description:** Coimbra district sits in west-central mainland Portugal along the Mondego river valley, bordered by Aveiro to the north, Viseu and Guarda to the east, Castelo Branco and Leiria to the south, and the Atlantic to the west. Its seat is the city of Coimbra. As the modern NUTS III "Região de Coimbra," the area is approximately 3,900-4,300 km2 (the NUTS III boundary was redrawn in the 2013/2015 NUTS revisions and does not match the historic distrito exactly, which is one open question below); the older distrito, before 1986, is generally cited at approximately 3,950 km2. No area figure here is derived from an attached polygon, since none is attached in this entry.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: this is the first entry created for the Coimbra territory, and no other polity in the database currently claims to represent it (PRT-1800-2025 is the national container, not a predecessor). No successor: the span runs to 2025, the open end_year convention used across the database for currently-existing subnational units, so there is no future entity to hand off to.

## Sourced claims

- Portugal was reorganized into distritos (districts) as the primary sub-national administrative division in the liberal reforms following 1834, with the modern set of mainland distritos, including Coimbra, dating to the 1835 reform (per Portuguese administrative-history references such as statoids.com's Portugal entry).
- Eurostat's NUTS classification assigns Coimbra to NUTS III as part of the Região de Coimbra sub-region, with the current NUTS III boundaries dating to the 2013 (applied 2015) revision, distinct from the pre-1986 distrito boundary used administratively before EU statistical regions existed.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-subunit-pattern

**Used the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> code pattern, not a bespoke code**

Coimbra distrito is a standard administrative subdivision, not a historical territory with a name and identity of its own the way Alaska Territory or the Ryukyu Islands are. It fits the 57-row dominant pattern seen elsewhere for Portuguese-style district/region rows, so the code PRT-CO-1835-2025 follows <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using CO as a short, unambiguous subunit code for Coimbra (avoiding collision with any existing 'PRT-C*' codes). No bespoke code was justified here.

### d-start-year-1835-not-1986

**Set start_year to 1835 (district creation), not 1986 (NUTS III designation)**

The routing decision's proposed span_basis explicitly follows the precedent set for Aveiro in the Portugal country convention: distritos created in 1835 are treated as the same continuous territory later relabeled as NUTS III regions from 1986. Since the routed data (1880-2023) falls entirely inside 1835-2025, a single entry spanning the full administrative history avoids creating an artificial break at 1986 that the data does not actually reflect, and avoids orphaning any pre-1986 data that a NUTS-only span would leave unrouted.

## Open questions

### oq-coimbra-boundary-vintage

**Does the 1835 distrito boundary match the current NUTS III Região de Coimbra boundary?**

NUTS III Região de Coimbra was redrawn in the 2013/2015 EU statistical-region revision and may not be coterminous with the original 1835 distrito of Coimbra (municipality boundaries and even distrito assignments shifted over the 19th-20th centuries, e.g. some concelhos moved between adjacent districts). Once GADM's Portugal ADM1/ADM2 rows are fetched, this needs checking: does the polygon that would be attached represent the historic distrito, the pre-2013 NUTS III region, or the post-2013 NUTS III region, and how large is the discrepancy in km2? This determines whether a single polygon can honestly cover the full 1835-2025 span or whether the entry needs to be split at the 2013 boundary revision.

### oq-coimbra-gadm-fetch

**When will PRT rows be fetched into the local GADM 4.1 admin-1 subset, and what feature_id will Coimbra get?**

The polygon_source gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered but the local geodata file at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg excludes Portugal entirely (0 of 81 curated countries). This entry cannot move from polygon_status unassigned to assigned until someone fetches Portugal's ADM1 (or ADM2, if districts are modeled at ADM2 in GADM's Portugal hierarchy) rows and confirms which GADM feature corresponds to Coimbra district versus the NUTS III region of the same name.
