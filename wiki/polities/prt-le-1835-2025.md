# Leiria (district / NUTS region of Portugal)

## Summary

Leiria is one of mainland Portugal's historic distritos, one of eighteen created by the 1835 administrative reform that replaced the old ancien-regime comarca structure with a uniform district system reporting to the central government in Lisbon. That same distrito survived, with periodic municipality-level reshuffling, into the late twentieth century, when the 1986 NUTS classification adopted it (largely intact) as the NUTS III region of Leiria. This entry is `type: subnational` because Portugal was and remains a unitary sovereign state throughout 1835-2025 -- Leiria never held sovereignty of its own -- but two independent statistical traditions (feeding the routed data span of 1880-2023) report figures at district/region scale rather than folding them into the national total, so a row is needed to carry that reporting geography without competing with PRT-1800-2025 in the matcher's sovereignty ranking. The entry mirrors the already-accepted treatment of Aveiro, the other pre-1986 Portuguese distrito this country's routing convention departs from the NUTS-1986 default for, rather than inventing a new precedent from scratch.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** The routing decision (unit PRT-PTLE, admin_name PT_LE) reports data spanning 1880-2023 for the country Portugal, tabulated at district/region scale rather than nationally; the decision references this as the motivating dataset for creating a Portugal-specific subnational row, following the same reasoning already accepted for Aveiro under this country's convention. No exact row count is given in the decision payload beyond the span itself.

**Previous matching, and why it was wrong:** Absent this entry, PT_LE-labelled rows would fall back to PRT-1800-2025, the national Portugal row. That is wrong because PRT-1800-2025 is the whole country -- roughly 25-30 times the area of a single mainland district -- so routing district-scale statistics there would silently blend Leiria's figures into (or double-count them against) the national total, exactly the failure mode this pipeline's subnational rows exist to prevent.

**Confirmation the entity is distinct:** Distrito de Leiria is a documented, continuously-existing Portuguese administrative unit from the 1835 territorial reform onward, later codified as the NUTS III region of Leiria in the 1986 EU statistical classification -- it is not an ad hoc grouping invented for this dataset. The routing decision grounds this in precedent: Aveiro, one of Portugal's other pre-1986 distritos, is already recorded in this country's convention as a departure from the NUTS-1986 default for exactly this reason, and Leiria is argued to warrant the identical treatment as a sibling distrito from the same 1835 reform.

**Territory description:** Distrito de Leiria sits on Portugal's central-west Atlantic coast, roughly between Coimbra to the north and Lisbon/Santarem to the south, and includes the towns of Leiria (the seat), Marinha Grande, Alcobaca, Batalha, Pombal and Peniche. A reader can locate it on a modern map as the coastal-to-interior strip of central Portugal containing the Serra de Aires e Candeeiros and the Pinhal de Leiria forest. The modern NUTS III region of Leiria (the unit's present-day continuation) covers approximately **3,900-4,000 km2** -- a figure drawn from general knowledge of Portugal's district geography, not measured from any geometry attached to this entry, since no polygon has been fetched yet. Once PRT.9_1 is pulled and attached, its own measured area (in an equal-area projection, per repository convention) should be recorded and compared against this real-world figure to check for GADM-vintage boundary drift before the status is upgraded from `unassigned` to `assigned`.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-subunit-pattern

**Code PRT-LE-1835-2025 follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

57 of 66 existing subnational rows use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025), and the routing decision itself analogises Leiria to Aveiro, an existing departure recorded under this same country's convention. Leiria is an ordinary mainland distrito, not a historical territory with a name of its own comparable to Alaska Territory (ALK) or Hyderabad (HYD), so a bespoke code would be unjustified here. SUBUNIT is set to LE, the two-letter code used for Leiria in the modern Portuguese NUTS III / district abbreviation scheme, distinguishing it from the already-departed AV (Aveiro). No existing PRT subnational codes exist yet, so this entry sets the precedent other Portuguese districts should follow rather than conforming to one.

### d-single-span-since-1835-not-1986

**Span starts in 1835, not at the 1986 NUTS creation date**

The routing decision explicitly rejects treating this as a NUTS-1986-only unit because that would leave 1880-1985 data (mitchell-era district statistics) unroutable to a valid polity, repeating a previously-rejected gap. Instead the entry treats the modern NUTS III Leiria region as the administrative continuation of the 1835 Distrito de Leiria, mirroring the already-accepted Aveiro departure for the same country. This is a plausibility call, not a confirmed boundary equivalence -- flagged as an open question below, since Portuguese district boundaries could have been redrawn between 1835 and 1986 in ways that would break the equivalence.

## Open questions

### oq-district-boundary-stability-1835-1986

**Was the Distrito de Leiria boundary stable between 1835 and the 1986 NUTS reform?**

The routing decision only confirms that Aveiro, treated the same way, was accepted as a departure from the NUTS-1986 default -- it does not independently confirm that Leiria's own distrito boundary was unchanged across 150 years. Portuguese concelhos (municipalities) were reorganized several times in this period, and district boundaries can shift when concelhos are reassigned between neighbouring districts. If Leiria's district gained or lost concelhos at some point between 1835 and 1986, the single continuous 1835-2025 span implicitly claims a territorial constancy that may not hold, and the entry might need to split into two eras with a documented boundary change instead of one polygon proxy covering the whole run. This should be checked against a Portuguese administrative-history source (e.g. the reforms of 1836, 1878, or the Estado Novo-era rearrangements) before the polygon is fetched and formally assigned.

### oq-gadm-vintage-vs-1835-1986-extent

**GADM 4.1's present-day PRT.9_1 feature is a 2018-vintage boundary standing in for an 1835-2025 span**

Once fetched, PRT.9_1 will reflect present-day concelho/freguesia composition inside Leiria district, not the boundary as it stood in 1835 or as it may have stood before the last local-government reorganizations. This is the same vintage-risk noted in the routing decision's polygon_detail. Because polygon_status is currently unassigned (the source is registered locally but Portugal's rows are absent from data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg), no area figure or vintage caveat can yet be attached to this page; once fetched, the vintage gap should be stated explicitly rather than assumed negligible, since it directly bears on whether the same feature can be reused across the whole 1835-2025 span or whether an earlier boundary needs a separate proxy.

### oq-data-span-vs-polity-span

**The polity span (1835-2025) is wider than the data that motivates it (1880-2023)**

The routing reasoning gives the unit's actual data span as 1880-2023, but the polity is created with start_year 1835 (the administrative reform date) and end_year 2025 (aligned to the container's own end). This is intentional -- it avoids creating an artificial gap before the first observed data point and matches the container's full span per the tiling requirement -- but it means roughly 45 years at the start and 2 years at the end of this polity's nominal existence carry no routed data at all. Future ingest work should confirm this is expected (a full-lifetime administrative entity that simply lacks earlier/later statistical coverage) rather than evidence that the start or end year should be narrowed to match observed data.
