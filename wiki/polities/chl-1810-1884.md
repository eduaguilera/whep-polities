# Chile (to 1883)


> **`end_year` moved 1883 -> 1884 on 2026-08-06: the year 1883 belonged to no Chilean polity.**
>
> `end_year` is **exclusive**, so this row covered 1810-1882 while
> [chl-1884-1899](chl-1884-1899.md) began in 1884. **1883 fell between them** — an off-by-one in a
> pair of adjacent spans, not a historical question.
>
> **`validate_period_gaps` already knew.** The pair `("CHL-1810-1883", "CHL-1884-1899")` sat in its
> baseline as an accepted one-year gap, alongside genuine ones like `CZE-1804-1918 -> CZE-1993-2025`
> where the state really did not exist. So the gate was not wrong and this was not a discovery — the
> gap was recorded and nothing distinguished an arithmetic slip from a deliberate hole.
>
> What changed is that **whep_crops v1.0 puts 7 observed crop rows into 1883**, so an accepted hole
> became a routing failure with data falling through it. That is the whole value of the external
> panel here: not finding the gap, but making it matter.
>
> **1883 is assigned to the earlier row, not the later one, on the event date.** The War of the
> Pacific ended with the Treaty of Ancón on **20 October 1883**, so for roughly ten months of that
> year Chile did not yet hold Tarapacá. Extending this row is therefore the correct direction;
> starting `CHL-1884-1899` in 1883 would have credited Chile with the annexation for the whole of
> the year it was signed in. This is the convention question raised in issue 23 — do row boundaries
> follow the source's date or the historical event — answered here on the event.
>
> The row **now has a polygon**, and the reason it did not is worth keeping. Issue #158 declined
> Cliopatria's pre-1884 Chile (~313,000 km²) because it would have created a 2.4x step against
> this family's CShapes-sourced siblings (~745,000) that is mostly a difference between mapping
> conventions rather than territory — CShapes carries Patagonia as *claimed*, Cliopatria excludes
> it as not effectively controlled. **That reasoning is not overturned; it is the reason the fix
> stays inside CShapes.** See *Territorial extent* below: the annexation of 1883-1884 is a clip at
> a treaty parallel, so the family keeps one convention and the 1884 step becomes 1.24x — the
> annexation — instead of 2.4x. Both IIA and FAO independently state Chile at ~741,770 km², which
> is what the siblings carry.
## Summary

Pre-War-of-the-Pacific Chile. This polity covers Chile from independence
in **1810** to the end of the War of the Pacific in **1883** (Treaty of
Ancón, October 1883). During this period Chile's northern boundary ran
near 24°S (the Chilean–Bolivian border established in 1866), and the
Atacama Desert provinces of Antofagasta (Bolivia) and Tarapacá (Peru)
were not yet Chilean territory.

Predecessor: NA.
Successor: [chl-1884-1899](chl-1884-1899.md).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `constructed`, feature `CHL-1810-1884`, built by
  `scripts/sources/constructed/build.py`. The recipe is **CShapes 2.0
  gwcode 155's 1886-1899 step, clipped south of the 24th parallel**, with
  Easter Island dropped. `polygon_status: proxy`, for the eastern
  boundary rather than the northern one — see *Approximation direction*.
- **The 24°S line is a treaty, not an estimate.** The Treaty of Mutual
  Benefits (10 August 1866) fixed the Chile–Bolivia border at 24°S and the
  1874 Treaty of Sucre reaffirmed it. Every acre Chile acquired in
  1883–1884 — Tarapacá from Peru, the Bolivian Litoral, and the
  Tacna–Arica provinces it administered from 1883 — lies north of that
  parallel, so one number defines the whole northern edge of pre-war Chile.
- **Measured** (ESRI:54034, the projection the build uses):

  | step | km² |
  |---|---|
  | CShapes 155, 1886-1899 step (as attached to `CHL-1884-1899`) | 746,276 |
  | removed north of 24°S | 145,607 |
  | clip south of 24°S | 600,669 |
  | less Easter Island (annexed September 1888) | −179 |
  | **published for this row** | **600,490** |

- **The previous estimate on this page was wrong by ~85,000 km².** It said
  the 1886 polygon "already incorporates ~240,000 km² of territory annexed
  in 1883–1884" and put this row at ~516,000–520,000 km². Measured, the
  territory north of 24°S in that polygon is **145,607 km²**, not ~240,000;
  issue #158's ~180,000 is also too large. Independent arithmetic agrees
  with the measurement: modern Chile 756,096 km² − Tarapacá region 42,226 −
  Antofagasta region 126,049 **+ Taltal province 20,405** (Antofagasta's
  southernmost province, Chilean before the war) = 608,226, which scaled
  onto CShapes' slightly smaller base (746,276/756,096) is 600,325 —
  **0.03%** from the published 600,490. The old estimate's error was
  subtracting the whole modern Antofagasta region without adding Taltal
  back, and rounding upward on top of that.
- **Cliopatria check done, and it is not used.** Cliopatria v0.1.3 does
  carry "Republic of Chile" steps at 303,350 km² (1828-1865) and 313,424
  (1866-1879), and issue #158 declined both: attaching one would encode a
  2.4x step at 1884 that is mostly the CShapes-vs-Cliopatria treatment of
  Patagonia rather than territory, in a family whose other three rows are
  all CShapes. The clip above keeps the family in one convention and makes
  the 1884 step 746,276/600,490 = **1.24x**, which is the annexation itself.
  Cliopatria's neighbours cannot supply the annexed territory either: its
  Bolivia measures 1,576,633 km² at 1878 and 1,576,691 at 1890, i.e. it does
  **not** model the loss of the Litoral at all, so the
  before-minus-after pattern used for `FCC-1862-1887` has nothing to
  subtract here.
- **Approximation direction.** North: exact, on the treaty parallel. East:
  the polygon carries the **post-1881** Patagonian boundary, because the
  1886 CShapes step postdates the Boundary Treaty of 23 July 1881. Chile
  claimed considerably more of Patagonia before that treaty, so this
  polygon **understates** the claimed extent for 1810–1881 — 71 of this
  row's 74 years — by an amount no fetched source measures. That is the
  reason for `proxy`, and it is the opposite direction from the error the
  northern clip corrects.

## Sourced claims

- **~1818: Chilean independence.** Battle of Maipú (5 April 1818)
  secured independence from Spain.
- **1866: Atacama boundary treaty.** Chile and Bolivia fixed their
  border at 24°S (Parallel Treaty), with shared exploitation rights
  between 23°S and 25°S.
- **1879–1883: War of the Pacific.** Chile declared war on Bolivia
  (February 1879) and Peru (April 1879). The war ended with the
  Treaty of Ancón (October 1883) ceding Tarapacá to Chile, and a
  separate agreement (Truce of Valparaíso, April 1884) ceding
  Antofagasta to Chile. Territorial transfer completed 1883–1884.

## Polygon decision

`polygon_status=proxy`, `polygon_source=constructed`, feature
`CHL-1810-1884` = CShapes 155's 1886-1899 step clipped south of 24°S,
Easter Island dropped, **600,490 km²**.

CShapes 2.0 has a 1886 hard floor and its earliest Chilean polygon
(gwcode=155, vintage 1886) includes the territory annexed in 1883–1884, so
there is no pre-conquest CShapes *feature*. There is, however, a
pre-conquest **boundary**: the 1866/1874 Chile–Bolivia treaty line at 24°S,
north of which everything Chile gained in 1883–1884 lies. Clipping the 1886
step on that parallel is therefore a construction from the treaty rather
than a proxy for it, and it keeps this family in a single mapping
convention — which is exactly why issue #158 declined Cliopatria.

`proxy` rather than `assigned` because of the EASTERN boundary: the 1886
step postdates the 1881 Patagonian boundary treaty, so the polygon
understates Chile's claimed extent for 1810–1881. See
`oq-pre-1881-patagonian-claims`.

  acceptable as no territorial change occurred 1884–1886.
- **chl-pre-1884-clip-at-24s** (2026-08-17, issue 155) — Build this row's
  polygon as CShapes 155's 1886-1899 step clipped at 24°S rather than
  leaving it empty or attaching Cliopatria. Evidence: the 24°S line is the
  1866/1874 treaty border and bounds the whole 1883–1884 annexation;
  the clip measures 600,490 km² against 600,325 from independent
  modern-region arithmetic (0.03%); and it makes the 1884 step 1.24x
  instead of Cliopatria's 2.4x, so the step is territory rather than a
  change of source. Unblocks 168 layer-B rows that were matched but
  spatially unusable. What was NOT decided: the pre-1881 Patagonian
  claim, below.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1881-patagonian-claims

The attached polygon's eastern limit is the **post-1881** one. The Boundary
Treaty of 23 July 1881 settled Patagonia with Argentina; before it Chile
claimed considerably more, so for 1810–1881 (71 of this row's 74 years) the
polygon understates the claimed extent. No fetched source measures the
pre-1881 Chilean claim: CShapes has a 1886 floor, and Cliopatria's
pre-1880 Chile excludes Patagonia entirely rather than over-claiming it, so
it bounds the error from the wrong side. Splitting this row at 1881 would
need a source for the earlier half that does not exist here. Recorded, not
fixed; this is why `polygon_status` is `proxy`.

### oq-easter-island-1888

Easter Island (179 km², 109.4°W/27.1°S) is present in the CShapes 1886 step
and is dropped here because Chile annexed it in September 1888, after this
row ends. Dropping it here does not leave the island unclaimed, and that is
the residual defect: `CHL-1884-1899` runs 1884–1899 and its CShapes polygon
includes the island for the whole span, i.e. for the **four years before the
1888 annexation** as well. That is a 179 km²
overstatement on the successor, 0.02% of it, and it is not fixed here
because doing so would mean constructing a second clipped polygon to
correct a rounding-scale error.
