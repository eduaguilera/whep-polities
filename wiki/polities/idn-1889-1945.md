# Indonesia (1889-1945)

## Summary

CSV row for Indonesia from **1889** to **1945** -- a **57-year row**
covering the Dutch East Indies from the Anglo-Dutch boundary treaty
(1889) through Japanese occupation to the declaration of Indonesian
independence (17 August 1945).
COW code 750 [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
-- same cow=750 sharing issue as
[idn-1800-1889](idn-1800-1889.md#oq-cow-750-shared).

CShapes splits at 1945-08-17 (independence declaration) with no area
change (152.87 sq deg both before and after). The end year was
corrected from 1949 to 1945 to eliminate the overlap with successor
IDN-1945-1949 and reflect the sovereignty transition at independence.

## Territorial extent

The bound polygon is CShapes 2.0 feature 850 at the 1900 time-step
(`polygon_feature_year: 1900`), area 1,877,243 km² in the CSV;
re-measuring the committed geometry in an equal-area CRS (ESRI:54034)
gives 1,876,795 km², consistent within rounding. CShapes reports the
same area (152.87 sq deg) for the time-steps immediately before and
after the 1945-08-17 split, i.e. no border movement is coded across
that transition
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes).

That "no change" reading needs a caveat the source itself supplies.
CShapes only actively re-draws polygons at recorded change events;
"for countries with no changes in the period, we simply backdated
their borders to 1886"
[cshapes-2.0 §geocoding](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#geocoding). A
constant area for 1889–1945 is therefore consistent both with
genuinely static borders **and** with CShapes never having registered
a qualifying change to redraw around. The dataset's own change
threshold is 100×100 km for border adjustments
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes),
so any sub-threshold treaty-line adjustment in this period would be
invisible in the polygon regardless of whether it happened.

Known boundary business in this span, per the ingested sources:

- **Borneo (Dutch/British possessions).** The Anglo-Dutch boundary
  agreements on Borneo separated "the Dutch Indonesian regions of
  East and West Kalimantan from the Malaysian states of Sarawak and
  Sabah" [biger-1995 §brunei](../sources/biger-1995.md#brunei); the
  same line is described from the Malaysian side at
  [biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia). Biger
  does not give this a precise date; the 1889 start year of this row
  is taken from elsewhere (see the predecessor page) and is **not**
  independently confirmed against an ingested source here — flagged
  as an open question below rather than asserted as fact.
- **New Guinea and Timor** boundary refinements against, respectively,
  British/Australian New Guinea and Portuguese Timor are documented
  in general (uncited) colonial history but are not covered by any
  currently ingested source for this specific period, so they are not
  claimed here.

**Assessment:** borders are treated as constant for the whole
1889–1945 span, but the evidentiary basis is "CShapes recorded no
qualifying change," not "an ingested source affirmatively confirms
zero change." Given the 100×100 km threshold, that is a reasonable
but not airtight basis — see `territorial_changes_found` in this
report for what would need checking before fully trusting it.

## Sourced claims

- **This polity never appears in COW's state system.** The COW
  statelist codes Indonesia (INS, ccode=850) as entering the state
  system only on **1949-12-27** (the raw file is
  `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv`, row `INS,850,...`).
  This is expected, not an error: COW membership before 1920 requires
  "diplomatic recognition by Britain or France" and a population
  above 500,000, and from 1920 requires League of Nations/UN
  membership [cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion).
  A Dutch colonial possession never qualifies under either test, so
  its total absence from COW for 1889–1945 is exactly what the
  criteria predict, not evidence about this row's actual borders.
- **CShapes area is constant across the 1945-08-17 split** (152.87 sq
  deg immediately before and after), which is the basis for treating
  this as one WHEP polity through independence rather than splitting
  at the sovereignty change
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes).
  Per WHEP's own rule, a CShapes administrative split with unchanged
  area is not a WHEP split (`wiki/README.md`, "What a WHEP polity
  is"), so this row correctly spans 1889–1945 as a single unit.
- **Borneo boundary**: the Dutch/British Borneo line separating
  Dutch East and West Kalimantan from Sarawak and Sabah is attested
  in the ingested boundary literature, without a precise date
  [biger-1995 §brunei](../sources/biger-1995.md#brunei),
  [biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia). This
  supports the existence of a settled land boundary on Borneo during
  the period but does not, on its own, confirm the specific 1889
  start-year treaty date claimed in the Summary — that date is
  uncited general history and should be checked against a source that
  actually gives it (Biger's Indonesia entry covers only the
  post-1949 country and does not reach back to the 1889 Borneo
  convention) [biger-1995 §indonesia](../sources/biger-1995.md#indonesia).
- **Post-1945 area context**: Biger's modern-Indonesia entry (area
  1,947,999 km² as of the 1990s, after West New Guinea 1963 and East
  Timor 1975 were added) confirms this row's ~1.877M km² is smaller
  than the eventual national territory by roughly the area later
  added from West New Guinea and East Timor
  [biger-1995 §indonesia](../sources/biger-1995.md#indonesia) —
  consistent with this row representing the Dutch East Indies before
  those later annexations, not modern Indonesia's footprint.

## Open questions

### oq-cow-750-shared

COW code 750 is assigned to both the early Indonesia rows
(IDN-1800-1889, IDN-1889-1945) and all India rows. This is
almost certainly a CSV error. Independent Indonesia uses cow=850.

### oq-borneo-1889-date

The Summary and start_year assert an "Anglo-Dutch boundary treaty of
20 June 1889" finalizing the Borneo border. No ingested source dates
this specific instrument: `biger-1995` discusses the Dutch/British
Borneo line at [biger-1995 §brunei](../sources/biger-1995.md#brunei)
and [biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia) without
giving that date, and its Indonesia entry
([biger-1995 §indonesia](../sources/biger-1995.md#indonesia)) only
covers the country from independence onward. The historically
documented Anglo-Dutch Borneo boundary convention is commonly dated
1891, not 1889 — this is uncited general history, not sourced from
anything ingested, and is flagged here rather than silently
"corrected," since changing `start_year` is outside this pass's scope
(frontmatter must be preserved). Worth resolving with a dedicated
treaty-text or Wikipedia source before the next audit.
