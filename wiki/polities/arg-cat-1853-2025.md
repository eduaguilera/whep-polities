# Catamarca (province of Argentina)

## Summary

Catamarca (province of Argentina) is one of the fourteen historic provinces of the Argentine Republic, located in the country's northwest, bordered by Salta and Tucumán to the north/east, Córdoba and La Rioja to the south, San Juan to the southwest, and Chile across the Andes to the west. This entry is `type: subnational` because sovereignty throughout this span rests with Argentina — the entry exists to carry provincial-level statistics, not to claim any independent political status. Catamarca was one of the original signatories of the 1853 Constitution that organized the Argentine Confederation, and unlike several other current Argentine provinces (Chaco, Formosa, La Pampa, Misiones), it was never administered as a federal "national territory" before being granted provincehood — it has been a province in continuous existence since the mid-19th century. The routing decision that created this entry found no existing candidate polity representing Catamarca (zero name matches, no boundary features already covering it) among the entries in this table, and placed it inside the ARG national containment chain for its full span. The 1900-2023 production/trade data that motivated this entry's creation sits entirely within the proposed 1853-2025 span, so there is no coverage gap between the data and the entry's declared years.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Argentine province-level production/trade data keyed to the label "Catamarca," spanning approximately 1900-2023 per the routing decision (row count not separately itemized in the decision, but described there as data that "falls entirely within 1853-2025"). Before this entry was created, no polity row represented Catamarca at all: the routing search found zero name matches and no existing boundary features for it in the polities table, meaning any Catamarca-labeled rows in the source data would previously have had no correct home and, if matched at all, would have been misrouted to the national ARG rows (which are roughly 27x Catamarca's area and would blend its production figures into all-Argentina totals, hiding the provincial detail the source data actually reports). What confirms Catamarca is a distinct, real administrative entity worth its own row — as opposed to a label variant of the national total — is that it is one of Argentina's fourteen constitutionally organized provinces, continuously self-governing under its own provincial constitution and legislature since the 1853 national constitution, with its own dedicated statistical apparatus (provincial direcciones de estadística and census returns) that is exactly the kind of source that would report `Catamarca` as a distinct reporting unit rather than as shorthand for the nation.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is available in the GeoPackage for Catamarca at any period: the locally cached `gadm-4.1-adm1` dataset — otherwise the right registered source for a modern ADM1-level province boundary — is restricted to an 81-country subset that does not include Argentina, so zero features exist for any of Argentina's fourteen provinces, Catamarca included. `polygon_source` is set to the registered slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` (per the `registered_source_unfetched` route from the routing decision) with `polygon_feature_id` left null and `polygon_status: unassigned`, rather than fabricating a feature id or falling back to `new_source_needed`, since the correct source is known and registered — it simply has not been fetched globally yet.

**Territory description:** Catamarca province occupies Argentina's northwest, a strip running from the high Andean puna in the west (Puna de Atacama border region, over 4,000m elevation) down through the Sierras Pampeanas to lower valleys in the east near Santiago del Estero. Its capital is San Fernando del Valle de Catamarca. The modern province covers approximately **102,600 km²** (Argentina's own INDEC/IGN-published provincial area figure for Catamarca) — a figure a reader can check directly on any current map of Argentina's provinces; it is not a measurement of any geometry attached to this entry, since none is attached yet. Historical border adjustments with neighboring La Rioja over the Sierra de Ambato/Sierra de Velasco area were the main territorial dispute affecting Catamarca's shape across this span, settled by federal arbitration in the 20th century; the extent to which this affects a modern-boundary proxy is recorded as an open question below.

## Predecessors and successors

Catamarca has no predecessor or successor polity in this table: it does not split off from, merge into, or get renamed from any other subnational entry. It was one of the original signatory provinces of the Argentine Confederation formed under the 1853 Constitution and has remained a province of Argentina, under that same name and general territorial footprint, ever since — unlike national territories such as Chaco, Formosa, La Pampa, or Misiones, which were federal territories before being upgraded to provincehood decades later and would carry a predecessor/successor pair reflecting that status change. ## Open questions

### oq-global-gadm-fetch

**Global GADM 4.1 (or GADM 3.6) needs to be fetched to cover Argentina's provinces**

The repository's locally cached gadm-4.1-adm1 file is an 81-country subset that does not include Argentina, so no ADM1 feature exists yet for Catamarca or any of its thirteen sibling provinces. Fetching the global GADM 4.1 release (or falling back to the also-registered gadm-3.6) would supply an id, most likely GADM's own ISO_1 code AR.CT or the province name 'Catamarca', for all fourteen historic Argentine provinces at once. Until that fetch happens this entry stays polygon_status: unassigned and no boundary check can run against it.

### oq-boundary-vintage-1853

**How far does a modern-boundary proxy stretch back for Catamarca specifically**

Once a GADM feature is fetched, it will reflect Catamarca's present-day boundary. Argentine provincial boundaries were not fully static across 1853-2025: national territories were carved out of provinces at various points (e.g., parts of what became the Gran Chaco national territories were historically claimed or administered by neighboring provinces including Catamarca's northern neighbors), and there were 19th-century border adjustments and arbitrations between Catamarca and its neighbors (Salta, Tucumán, Santiago del Estero, Córdoba, La Rioja, San Juan, Catamarca-La Rioja disputes over the Sierra de Ambato area were only settled by federal law in the 20th century). Whether the modern GADM shape is a safe proxy for the full 1853-2025 span, or needs a documented caveat/split similar to the Ireland or Poland cases in the proxy rules, is unresolved and should be checked once a feature is available to compare against historical province maps.

### oq-data-coverage-gap

**Only 1900-2023 of production/trade data is confirmed; earlier span is asserted from constitutional history, not data**

The routing decision states the underlying data span for this unit is 1900-2023, entirely inside the proposed 1853-2025 span. The 1853 start year is not itself evidenced by any row in the data — it follows from Catamarca's status as one of the provinces organized under the 1853 Constitution — so this entry currently carries roughly 47 years (1853-1900) with no confirmed data of its own. Whether that pre-1900 stretch should instead be treated as uncovered by this entry (i.e., start_year raised to 1900) is worth revisiting if the historian-matching convention used elsewhere in this pipeline treats constitutional organization dates as sufficient grounds for start_year on their own.
