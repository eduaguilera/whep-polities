# Algerian civil departments (Alger, Oran, Constantine, 1902-1919)

## Summary

This row is the **statistical reporting unit** French colonial agricultural statistics for Algeria
were actually collected on between the 1902 reorganisation and 1919: the three **civil departments**
of Alger, Oran and Constantine — northern Algeria — and *not* the colony as a whole. The
departments were created in 1848 ("[Northern Algeria] was divided into the three French districts in
1848", [biger-1995 &sect;algeria](../sources/biger-1995.md#algeria)); the Sahara was "formally
annexed by France on 24 December 1902" (same source) and administered separately as the *Territoires
du Sud*, which is the event that made the reporting unit and the colony two different territories.

It exists because 418 layer-B rows from `mitchell` and `iia` describe the departments and had
nowhere to go. They sat on [dza-1902-1919](dza-1902-1919.md), whose polygon is **2,442,683 km²**
including the Sahara, against the **575,511 km²** the IIA yearbooks state for the territory those
figures cover — so every per-km² intensity computed from them understated by **4.24x**, with nothing
in the data to warn a consumer. The evidence was complete and recorded on that page and in
`data/final/source_stated_area_basis.csv` (`basis_flag = review`, issue 166); what was missing was a
row to put the answer in. Issue #400 consolidated that, and the answer is this page.

**This row has no polygon.** No source in `data/geodata` carries the civil departments, and inventing
one was refused. The declared 575,511 km² is IIA's own stated figure, carried as an area without a
geometry — the `CAN-1866-1870` / `PRY-1811-1870` / `SAC-1935-1947` pattern. See *Territorial extent*.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** none. `polygon_source: none`, `polygon_status: unassigned`.

**Declared area: 575,511 km², and it is the source's own figure, not ours.** IIA states an area for
each reporting unit it publishes, and for Algeria on the civil-departments basis it states, across
three editions (`data/final/source_stated_areas.csv`):

| edition | data year | raw label | stated km² |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1909 | 1911 | `ALGÉRIE` | 575,289 |
| 1925 | 1913 | `ALGÉRIE french` | **575,511** |
| 1929 | 1913 | `algérie` | 576,000 |

The three agree to **711 km², 0.12%** — this is not a case where the source disagrees with itself
(38% of IIA's repeated (label, data-year) pairs do; see `scripts/validate_stated_areas.py`). 575,511
is taken as the declared figure because it is the consensus value
`data/final/source_stated_area_basis.csv` already publishes for this basis, and the figure #400
names. Any of the three would move the ratio below by less than 0.2%.

**Why the figure is believable as a scope statement and not a bad number.** The same source, in the
same editions, states the *whole colony* for later data years — 2,195,097 (1929), 2,196,294
(1932, 1933), 2,204,864 (1937), against our 1919-1962 polygon's 2,318,386 km², which agrees to 5%.
So IIA states both territories, correctly, and the pair of statements is what identifies which
one each block of data was collected over. It is not an OCR digit loss: 575,511 is repeated in
three editions and 576,000 is the same figure rounded, which OCR does not do.

**What the departments are, in km².** The pre-1902 colony — CShapes feature 615 at its 1886 step,
bound to [dza-1886-1902](dza-1886-1902.md) — measures **655,282 km²** (ESRI:54034). The stated
575,511 is **1.139x** smaller: the difference, **79,771 km²**, is the part of the pre-1902 civil
territory that passed to the *Territoires du Sud* in 1902. That arithmetic is what lets a reader
size the territory: it is northern Algeria — the Tell, the high plateaux and the northern fringe of
the Saharan Atlas — and it is emphatically *not* the narrow coastal belt, which is far smaller than
575,511 km². Anyone approximating this row from a modern map should check the km² first.

**Why this entry exists (the measurement).** Two independent pieces of evidence, both already in the
repository before this page:

1. **The source states its own area.** 575,511 km² against a 2,442,683 km² polygon: 4.24x. Recorded
   on [dza-1902-1919](dza-1902-1919.md) and, **until this row existed**, published per (polity,
   source) in `data/final/source_stated_area_basis.csv` as `basis_flag = review` (issue 166). It no
   longer is — see the last paragraph of *Polygon decision* for why, which is the one loss this
   change causes.
2. **The series shows no step at the 1902 annexation.** Adding ~1.79M km² of Sahara to the colony
   added no measurable cropland or herd to the reporting basis:

   | series | pre-1902 | 1902-1918 |
   |---|---|---|
   | wheat (ha) | 1,295,000 (n=26) | 1,387,000 (n=17) |
   | barley (ha) | 1,423,000 (n=26) | 1,344,000 (n=17) |
   | camels (heads) | 203,000 (n=24) | 199,000 (n=13) |

   Barley area even falls. A basis that had genuinely gained the Southern Territories would have
   gained their date palms and their camels; this one gained neither.

**What the reroute changes, in per-km² terms.** Means over the 17 data years 1902-1918, on the rows
this row now receives:

| series | mean | per km² on 2,442,683 | per km² on 575,511 |
|---|---|---|---|
| wheat, area (ha) | 1,386,882 | 0.5677 | 2.4098 |
| wheat, production (t) | 903,882 | 0.3700 | 1.5706 |
| barley, area (ha) | 1,316,882 | 0.5391 | 2.2882 |
| barley, production (t) | 910,412 | 0.3727 | 1.5819 |
| oats, area (ha) | 176,824 | 0.0724 | 0.3072 |
| grapes, area (ha) | 150,563 | 0.0616 | 0.2616 |

Every ratio is the same 4.244, because the numerator did not move — only the denominator did. As a
cropped share: the mean of the annual sums of every `ha` series is 2,991,005 ha = 29,910 km², which
is **1.224%** of the old polygon and **5.197%** of the declared area. (Stated as arithmetic, not as
evidence: a cropped share cannot size a territory, and it is not what identified this one. The
stated area and the no-step measurement did.)

### Polygon decision

**`polygon_status: unassigned` — an honest gap, and every fetched source was queried for it.**
Tested against both forms in which a missing feature can still be constructible
(`scripts/validate_data_without_geometry.py`), and it fails both:

* **The operation form** ("X is defined as an operation on something a source has"). The departments
  *are* definable that way — pre-1902 colony minus the *Territoires du Sud* — but the operand is
  missing: no fetched source delineates the internal civil/military line, so the 79,771 km²
  to subtract cannot be located. The definition is known and the geometry to apply it to is not.
* **The difference form** ("X is the difference between two states a source records"). The only step
  any fetched source records for Algeria in this window is **1902**, and it runs the wrong way: it
  *adds* the Sahara (655,282 → 2,442,683 km²). No source records a 1902 step in which the civil
  territory shrinks.

Source-by-source, measured against the files in `data/geodata` rather than taken from prose:

| source | what it has for Algeria | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| CShapes 2.0 | cowcode 615 in 5 steps (1886-1901, 1901-1902, 1902-1919, 1919-1962, 1962-2019), every one the whole colony | no sub-national Algerian feature at any step |
| CShapes-Europe | Europe only | out of scope |
| Cliopatria (15,690 features) | no Algerian sub-polity; the 46 `alger|oran|constantine` name matches are the Principality of *Oran*ge and the Cimmerian Bosporus | nothing |
| GADM 4.1, as fetched | **0 features** where `GID_0='DZA'` at adm0, adm1 *and* adm2; no `gadm41_DZA.gpkg` in the fetched per-country set | Algeria is absent from this source entirely |
| Paine 2024 | precolonial Africa | wrong period |
| `subnational_polygons.gpkg` (91 features) | 0 Algerian units | nothing |
| legacy `polities_polygons.gpkg` | `ALG-1800-1902` at **655,581 km²** — the whole pre-1902 colony, 0.05% from CShapes' 655,282 | not the departments |

**The proxy that exists and is refused.** [dza-1886-1902](dza-1886-1902.md)'s polygon (CShapes 615 at
1886, 655,282 km²) is the closest geometry on disk: **1.139x** the declared area, against the parent
row's 4.244x. It is not bound here, for three reasons, and the refusal is the point:

1. It is a **different territory**, not a coarse version of this one. The pre-1902 colony still
   contained the 79,771 km² that became the *Territoires du Sud*; that is precisely the land the
   1902 reorganisation removed from the reporting basis.
2. A 1886 vintage on a 1902-1919 span is `back_projected` by `04_territory_basis.py`'s own rule, and
   would put the same feature on two rows that this database is asserting are *not* the same
   territory — the failure `validate_shared_polygons.py` exists to surface.
3. wiki/README.md and `new_polity.workflow.js` both say it outright: *"prefer
   `polygon_status=unassigned` with a documented reason over a guess."* A 14% overstatement quietly
   attached is worse than a declared area with no geometry, because only one of the two is visible
   to a consumer.

**Polygon confidence: n/a — there is no polygon.** The declared area's confidence is **high**: it is
the reporting authority's own published figure for its own unit, repeated across three editions
within 0.12%.

**The cost of having no polygon, stated rather than hidden.** `validate_stated_areas.analyse()`
compares a stated area only against a polity that *has* geometry (`if not code or code not in ours`),
so publishing this row **removes** the `(DZA-1902-1919, iia)` row from
`data/final/source_stated_area_basis.csv`: its three statements now resolve here, and here has no
polygon to compare them to. That is not information loss — the 4.24x divergence it warned about is
what this row resolves — but it does mean the *published* warning disappears at the same moment the
underlying problem does, and a reader looking for the old row should find this page. This row is
also baselined in `scripts/validate_data_without_geometry.py` as the largest data-bearing block with
no geometry in the database (418 rows), which is the honest accounting of that trade.

## Predecessors and successors

**None, deliberately.** This is a **reporting unit inside a continuing colony**, not a link in a
territorial chain: the departments did not succeed anything and were not succeeded by anything —
French Algeria ran continuously through 1902 and through 1919. Same shape as
[idn-jvm-1949-1951](idn-jvm-1949-1951.md), [idn-blb-1949-1951](idn-blb-1949-1951.md) and
[idn-oth-1949-1951](idn-oth-1949-1951.md), which also carry `predecessor: []` / `successor: []`.

The parent chain, which is where territorial succession lives:

- **Parent (1902-1919):** [dza-1902-1919](dza-1902-1919.md) — French Algeria including the
  *Territoires du Sud*, 2,442,683 km². Still the right answer to "what did France hold"; it is not
  the right answer to "what were these figures collected over".
- **Before:** [dza-1886-1902](dza-1886-1902.md) — the pre-1902 colony, 655,282 km², which is
  1.139x this row's declared area.
- **After:** [dza-1919-1962](dza-1919-1962.md) — 2,318,386 km². See
  `oq-dza-cvd-basis-after-1919`: IIA is still stating 575,511 for data year 1925.

## Sourced claims

- "[Northern Algeria] was divided into the three French districts in 1848" — the creation of the
  civil departments of Alger, Oran and Constantine.
  [biger-1995 &sect;algeria](../sources/biger-1995.md#algeria)
- "The Sahara region of Algeria became a military zone, formally annexed by France on 24 December
  1902" — the event that separates the reporting unit from the colony, and the reason this row starts
  in 1902. [biger-1995 &sect;algeria](../sources/biger-1995.md#algeria)
- "[Algeria was] integrated with France in 1881" — administrative, no territorial content; the
  departments were French departments in law, which is why French agricultural statistics were
  collected on them. [biger-1995 &sect;algeria](../sources/biger-1995.md#algeria)
- IIA states 575,289 / 575,511 / 576,000 km² for Algeria at data years 1911 and 1913 across its
  1909, 1925 and 1929 editions, and 2,195,097-2,204,864 km² for data years 1929-1937.
  [source_stated_areas](../../data/final/source_stated_areas.csv)
- The 4.24x basis divergence on the parent row was published per (polity, source) with
  `basis_flag = review` until this row existed; that table now carries 398 rows rather than 399,
  because the statements resolve here and here has no polygon to compare them against.
  [source_stated_area_basis](../../data/final/source_stated_area_basis.csv)
- `mitchell` shows no step in wheat area, barley area or camel numbers across the 1902 annexation;

- CShapes 2.0 carries Algeria as cowcode 615 in five whole-colony steps and no sub-national Algerian

## Contradictions

### The reporting basis outlives this row's span, and the row does not follow it

Recorded 2026-08-31. IIA's stated area is on the civil-departments basis for data years **1911,
1913, 1921 and 1925**, and on the whole-colony basis from data year **1929**. This row ends at 1919,
because that is where the parent chain steps
([dza-1902-1919](dza-1902-1919.md) → [dza-1919-1962](dza-1919-1962.md), the CShapes 1919-09-12
boundary) and because #400 scopes case 2 at 1902-1918. So IIA's 1919-1928 rows remain on
[dza-1919-1962](dza-1919-1962.md) at 2,318,386 km² while the source is still stating 575,511 for
1925 — a residual **~3.8x** on that window.

This is stated rather than fixed because fixing it is a second decision, not this one: it needs
either a second row for 1919-1929 or a re-span of this one across a chain boundary, and the
`(DZA-1919-1962, iia)` basis row currently reads `agrees` at 1.056x because the *median* of its six
statements is the whole-colony figure — the two civil-departments statements inside it are outvoted,
not absent. See `oq-dza-cvd-basis-after-1919`.

## Decisions

- **dec-dza-cvd-created (issue #400)**: created 2026-08-31 under the maintainer's decision on
  [issue #400](https://github.com/eduaguilera/whep-polities/issues/400), which asked whether a
  statistical reporting unit that was not a sovereign state qualifies for a polity row. The answer
  was yes, and Algeria was named as the case where it is clearest: *"the idea is the data is
  attached to a faithful polity. if the territory is clearly different, we need a polity for that
  territory, thats another basis of this project. if its not clear, of course small approximations
  make sense, but for the one you mentioned its clear we need a separate territory defined."* This
  row is that separate territory. It also resolves
  `oq-dza-civil-departments-as-a-reporting-unit` on [dza-1902-1919](dza-1902-1919.md), which had
  declined to answer it on the grounds that the precedent ran the other way; the maintainer's
  decision is the answer that page was waiting for.
- **dec-dza-cvd-code**: coded `DZA-CVD-1902-1919` on the `<parent iso3>-<unit>-<start>-<end>`
  precedent already used for sub-units of a larger polity — `IDN-JVM-1949-1951`,
  `IDN-BLB-1949-1951`, `IDN-OTH-1949-1951`, `MMR-LWR-1852-1885`, `AZE-SSR-1920-1991`. `CVD` is not
  an ISO 3166 code and is not an existing polity prefix, so it collides with nothing.
  `type: subnational`, as those five are. The documented vocabulary also offers `statistical`
  (wiki/README.md), which was considered and not used: it has zero members today, and #400 asks for
  the *closest precedent*, which is the Indonesian island-group set.
- **dec-dza-cvd-no-polygon**: `polygon_status: unassigned` with a declared area of 575,511 km². Every
  fetched source was queried and both constructibility forms tested; see *Polygon decision* for the
  per-source result. The available 1.139x proxy ([dza-1886-1902](dza-1886-1902.md)'s 1886 CShapes
  feature) is refused because it is a different territory, not a coarse version of this one.
- **dec-dza-cvd-aliases**: `algeria` is rerouted here for `mitchell` and `iia` over 1902-1918 by two
  source-scoped alias rules in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`. The
  unscoped `algeria | 1902-1918 -> DZA-1902-1919` rule is **left in place**: `matchlib.match_alias`
  ranks a source-scoped rule above an unscoped one at equal year specificity, so the two named
  sources come here and any future source labelled `algeria` in those years still lands on the
  colony rather than silently inheriting a claim about its reporting basis that has not been
  measured for it.

## Open questions

### oq-dza-cvd-polygon

**Build the departments when a source that has them is fetched.** Two candidates, both requiring an
acquisition rather than a rebinding:

- **GADM Algeria** (`gadm41_DZA.gpkg`, absent from the fetched set — Algeria has 0 features at adm0,
  adm1 and adm2 today). Its modern wilayas do not reproduce the 1902 department boundary, so this
  would be a `modern_proxy` at best and would have to be checked against 575,511 km² before use, not
  after. Note the trap this page's *Territorial extent* section states: a plausible-looking
  "northern Algeria" selection of coastal wilayas will fall well short of 575,511, because the
  departments reached the Saharan Atlas.
- **A French colonial-era departmental GIS.** The 24 December 1902 measure that created the
  *Territoires du Sud* also fixed the line between them and the departments, so it is a documented
  boundary; if any digitisation of it exists, it is the `exact_historical` answer and would also let
  the 79,771 km² residual be measured rather than inferred.

Until then the declared 575,511 km² stands without geometry, and this row stays in
`validate_data_without_geometry.py`'s baseline.

### oq-dza-cvd-basis-after-1919

**Where does the civil-departments basis actually end?** IIA states it for data year 1925 and the
whole colony from 1929, so the basis outlives this row by roughly a decade — see *Contradictions*.
Resolving it means deciding between a second row (1919-1929, on the same evidence as this one) and
re-spanning this row across the 1919 chain boundary, and it should be decided with the same
measurement this row rests on: check `mitchell` and `iia` for a step in the 1919-1929 window against
the 1929 basis switch. `mitchell`'s Algerian series runs to 1960, so the data to do it with is
present.

### oq-dza-cvd-pre-1902

**The same source, before 1902, sits on a 1.139x polygon and is not rerouted.** `mitchell`'s
Algerian series starts in 1860 and shows the same civil-departments basis throughout (that is what
the no-step measurement means), but for 1860-1902 the polity it lands on —
[dza-1831-1886](dza-1831-1886.md) / [dza-1886-1902](dza-1886-1902.md), 655,282 km² — is only 1.139x
the stated basis. That is inside the band the maintainer's #400 decision explicitly allows ("small
approximations make sense"), so it is recorded rather than acted on. If a departments polygon is
ever built, the pre-1902 rows are the next question, not a settled one.
