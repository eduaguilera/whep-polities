# Barcelona (province of Spain)

## Summary

Barcelona is one of the 49 Spanish provinces created by the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform, carved out of the former Principality of Catalonia's administrative territory along with the three other Catalan provinces (Girona, Lleida, Tarragona). It is entered here as `type: subnational` because it is a province within the sovereign Spanish state, not a sovereign entity in its own right -- the national polity ESP-1800-2025 already carries Spain's own sovereignty chain, and this row exists purely to give province-level statistical series (population, production, administrative data reported at INE province granularity) a matching territory distinct from either the national total or its Catalan neighbours. The span 1833-2025 tracks the province's full administrative lifetime: it was founded in 1833 and remains a live, current province of Spain, so the end year is left open at the harness's cutoff rather than marking any dissolution, mirroring the treatment already given to the seven other ESP subnational rows (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra) and the ~28 further provinces created alongside this one in the same harness run.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** this entry is created to receive province-level Spanish statistical rows keyed to the INE/administrative unit `ES511` (Barcelona), following the routing decision for unit_id ESP-ES511 -- the harness's standard workflow for the batch of Spanish NUTS/province units being processed in this session (siblings ES112 through ES241 were all routed `create_new` for the same reason). **Previous matching:** before this entry, any Barcelona-specific series would have had no correct home among existing ESP polities -- the three pre-existing candidates were A Coruña (a different, non-adjacent Galician province), Madrid (a different, non-adjacent central province), and the national Spain row (ESP itself, which is roughly 65x Barcelona province's area and would silently blend Barcelona's figures into every other Spanish region's). Routing Barcelona-labelled data to any of these would misattribute province-level statistics to the wrong territory. **Confirmation this is a distinct entity:** Barcelona province is a standing first-order administrative division of Spain, defined by the same 1833 Javier de Burgos decree that is documented and sourced for the seven pre-existing ESP subnational entries, carries its own INE province code (08) and ISO 3166-2 code (ES-B), and is the unit at which Spanish official statistics (INE censuses, agricultural production series, etc.) are conventionally published -- the same evidentiary basis already accepted for Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruña, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, and Pontevedra in this table.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Assigned. Feature '08' from the `mapspain-ign` source, keyed by INE province code (cpro), the same source and keying convention already used for the seven existing ESP subnational rows and for the ~28 sibling provinces created in this run. GADM's admin-1 layer for Spain sits at the autonomous-community level and carries no Spanish-province features, so it cannot serve this unit; mapspain-ign is the only available source with province-level Spanish geometry.

**Territory:** Barcelona province occupies the north-eastern coastal and pre-coastal strip of Catalonia, Spain, centred on the city of Barcelona. It borders Girona province to the north-east, Lleida to the west, Tarragona to the south-west, the Mediterranean Sea to the east, and Andorra/France along a short stretch of the Pyrenean interior. A reader can locate it on a modern map as the Catalan province containing Barcelona city, Badalona, Sabadell, and Terrassa. Its present-day area is approximately **7,728 km2** (the smallest of the four Catalan provinces by area but by far the most populous), a figure drawn from published INE/IGN reference statistics for the current province rather than measured from the attached geometry; the attached polygon should measure close to this independently. **`polygon_area_km2` in the database is populated by the pipeline from the geometry itself and is a measurement of the shape, not this independent figure** -- the two should agree closely since both describe the same modern boundary, and a large divergence would flag a geometry problem rather than a territory-definition one.

**Vintage caveat:** the polygon reflects the present-day IGN provincial boundary, applied back to 1833. Barcelona's external boundary with its neighbouring provinces has no recorded province-level revision since 1833, so drift risk is assessed as low, consistent with the treatment of the other six long-lived, stable ESP province rows already in the table.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: Barcelona province did not exist as an administrative unit before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, which carved it out of the former Principality of Catalonia's territorial administration alongside Girona, Lleida, and Tarragona. No successor: Barcelona remains a current, unbroken province of Spain as of 2025, with `end_year: 2025` left open per the country convention's default rule for still-current units rather than marking a terminal event that has not occurred.

## Sourced claims

- Barcelona was constituted as one of Spain's 49 provinces by the royal decree of 30 November 1833 promoted by Secretary of State Javier de Burgos, replacing the earlier, larger Catalan territorial divisions.
- Barcelona's INE province code (cpro) is 08, the key used by mapspain-ign to identify this polygon feature, distinct from the historic single-letter vehicle-registration code 'B' used in the polity code chosen here.

## Decisions

### d-code-single-letter-subunit

**Subunit code is the single letter B, not a two-letter abbreviation**

Every other ESP subnational code in the table uses a two-letter subunit (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO, and the newly created AB, AV, BA, BI, BU, CC, CR, CU, GI, GU, HU, LE, NV, PA, RI, SA, SG, SO, TE, TO, VA, VI, ZA, ZM). Barcelona province is the one Spanish province whose conventional short code is a single letter, 'B', inherited from its historic vehicle-registration and postal-sorting code, distinct from the INE numeric cpro '08' used to key the polygon. I kept the single-letter form (ESP-B-1833-2025) rather than inventing a two-letter mnemonic (e.g. 'BC' or 'BR') because no such two-letter abbreviation is in real administrative use for Barcelona, and fabricating one to match the visual pattern of sibling codes would be less faithful to precedent than accepting the one-character exception the country's own convention already makes.

## Open questions

### oq-vintage-risk-mapspain

**mapspain-ign boundary is present-day IGN, not an 1833 boundary**

The polygon assigned here (mapspain-ign, feature '08') reflects the current IGN provincial boundary of Barcelona, applied back to the province's 1833 founding. Barcelona's external boundary with its four neighbouring Catalan provinces (Girona, Lleida, Tarragona) has been administratively stable since 1833 with no province-level border changes recorded, so vintage drift is expected to be small, but this has not been checked against any 19th-century cartographic source (e.g. the original 1833 Javier de Burgos gazetteer boundary descriptions) the way it has been for provinces with documented boundary revisions. This should be flagged if a pre-1900 municipal-boundary source ever becomes available for cross-checking.

### oq-comarca-internal-changes

**Internal comarca/municipal reorganizations are invisible at province level**

Barcelona province underwent significant internal administrative reorganization over this span -- notably the creation and later suppression of the Mancomunitat de Catalunya (1914-1925), the Generalitat's comarcal division of 1936 (dissolved 1939), and the post-1987 restoration of Catalan comarques as a sub-provincial tier -- none of which changed the province's external boundary but which do mean 'Barcelona province' was not a stable single-tier administrative concept throughout 1833-2025. No data currently routed to this polity depends on distinguishing these internal tiers, but a future source reporting at comarca level (e.g. the Barcelonès or Vallès comarques specifically) would need routing below this entry, not to it.
