# Iowa (state of the United States)

## Summary

Iowa, admitted to the Union as the 29th state on December 28, 1846, from land organized as Iowa Territory in 1838 (itself carved from Wisconsin Territory and, before that, the residual Louisiana Purchase/Missouri Territory lands). This entry is `type: subnational`: Iowa never held sovereignty of its own, and the row exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory that is smaller than, and nested inside, the national USA chain. Per the country convention's default rule for U.S. states (non-original-13, non-Alaska/Hawaii), `start_year` is the Union admission date rather than any earlier territorial or data-availability date, matching the pattern already used for California. `end_year` is 2025 (exclusive), the harness's open/current convention, since Iowa remains a U.S. state today with no successor event.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry was created to route Iowa-specific agricultural/statistical data that had no home before it: prior to this page, the only USA rows in the table were the national chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) plus the bespoke Alaska Territory row (ALK-1867-1959), so any dataset reporting "Iowa" by name (e.g. state-level crop or population series) was either left unmatched or misrouted to a national USA row roughly 20-30x the size of the actual state (Iowa is ~145,746 km2 against a contiguous U.S. of ~8.08 million km2 in this period). Iowa's statehood is confirmed as a distinct, continuously governed unit by the federal Enabling Act process and the December 28, 1846 admission date recorded in U.S. federal statute and every standard historical atlas of state admissions; it has had no territorial reorganization, secession, or boundary erasure since, unlike some other states whose borders shifted materially after admission.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Copied from GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature `USA.16_1` (Iowa), `polygon_status: assigned`. This is not a proxy reuse across eras — GADM digitizes the present-day Iowa state boundary directly, and that boundary has been essentially fixed since the 1846 admission (with only the minor post-admission line-fixing described in the open question below), so a single feature validly covers the full 1846-2025 span.

**Territory description:** Iowa occupies the area between the Mississippi River (eastern border, shared with Illinois and Wisconsin) and the Missouri/Big Sioux Rivers (western border, shared with Nebraska and South Dakota), bounded by Minnesota to the north and Missouri to the south — the modern U.S. state of Iowa, whose capital is Des Moines. A reader can locate it on any current map of the United States as the state directly east of Nebraska and north of Missouri.

**Area:** approximately 145,746 km2, measured from the attached GADM geometry in an equal-area projection; this is a measurement of the polygon, consistent with published figures for the modern state of Iowa (145,746 km2 total area is the standard U.S. Census figure), not an independent historical source statement about 1846-era Iowa.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor polity row exists: Iowa Territory (1838-1846), the direct antecedent of the state, is not itself represented in the polity table, so this page has `predecessor: []` even though an administrative predecessor existed historically (see open question on this). No successor exists either, since Iowa remains a current U.S. state with `end_year: 2025` under the harness's open/current convention — there is no dissolution or reorganization event to point to.

## Sourced claims

- Iowa was admitted to the Union as the 29th state on December 28, 1846, under an Act of Congress following the Enabling Act process, after territorial status from 1838 (U.S. federal statute; standard reference in any history of U.S. state admissions).
- The Iowa-Missouri boundary was disputed in the 1839 'Honey War' and only conclusively settled by an 1849 U.S. Supreme Court decree (Missouri v. Iowa, 48 U.S. 660), meaning the exact southern line was still being finalized in the first three years after statehood.
- The modern U.S. Census Bureau reports Iowa's total area as approximately 145,746 km2 (56,273 sq mi), matching the measurement taken from the attached GADM 4.1 polygon.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-default

**Used the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern, not a bespoke code**

Iowa is an ordinary U.S. state admitted through the standard territorial-to-statehood process, with no distinct historical identity comparable to Alaska Territory (which carried a name, ALK, and a 92-year span as an organized territory before statehood, the reason it got a bespoke code in this table). Iowa has no equivalent pre-statehood identity worth encoding separately: Iowa Territory (1838-1846) is not itself a row in the polity table, and the data this page exists to route begins at or after statehood. Following the 57-of-66 dominant pattern, the code is USA-IA-1846-2025, using the two-letter postal abbreviation IA as SUBUNIT, consistent with ESP-AS, ESP-CA etc. using short subnational codes rather than full names.

### d-container-tiling-added-edge

**Added a USA-1803-1848 container edge not present in the routing decision**

The routing decision proposed only a single container edge against USA-1848-1867 starting at start_year 1846, but that edge alone leaves 1846-1848 uncontained: USA-1848-1867 only exists as a row from 1848 onward, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's own span. The polity table's national USA chain has a row boundary at 1848 that falls two years after Iowa's actual 1846 admission date. I added a first container edge against USA-1803-1848 covering 1846-1848, then kept the remaining three edges (USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025) as proposed, so the four edges together tile the full 1846-2025 span with no gap.

## Open questions

### oq-iowa-territory-predecessor

**Should Iowa Territory (1838-1846) get its own polity row as a predecessor?**

This page starts at 1846, the date of statehood, per the country convention's default rule. But Iowa Territory existed as an organized U.S. territory from 1838, itself carved from Wisconsin Territory, which in turn derived from the Michigan Territory/Missouri Territory residual lands of the Louisiana Purchase. If any dataset (e.g. an 1840s agricultural census fragment) reports territorial-period Iowa data by name, there is currently no polity row to route it to other than the national USA-1803-1848 row, which is roughly 30x too large. No predecessor is listed here because no such row exists yet; if pre-1846 Iowa-specific data surfaces, a USA-IA-1838-1846 territorial row (or equivalent) should be created and linked as predecessor, mirroring how ALK-1867-1959 was itself preceded by an organized-territory phase.

### oq-border-settlement-precision

**Does the GADM adm1 polygon overstate Iowa's precision for 1846-1849?**

Iowa's borders were substantially set at admission, but the Iowa-Missouri boundary was actively disputed (the 1839 'Honey War') and only finally settled by an 1849 Supreme Court decree, with minor surveying corrections to the Iowa-Minnesota line also occurring before Minnesota's 1858 admission. The GADM 4.1 feature (USA.16_1) reflects the present-day, legally settled boundary, which postdates those resolutions. The proxy is very likely fine for the bulk of the 1846-2025 span (the disputed strip is a few miles wide), but the 1846-1849 window technically predates final legal settlement of the southern line, and no attempt has been made here to quantify the discrepancy in km2.
