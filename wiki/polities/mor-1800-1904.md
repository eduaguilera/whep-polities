# Morocco (to 1904)

## Summary

The Alaouite Sultanate of Morocco from the 1800 database floor to the 1904 Anglo-French Entente Cordiale (which recognised French preponderance in Morocco and set the stage for the 1912 Treaty of Fez). Over this long window Morocco retained nominal independence under the Alaouite dynasty but progressively lost autonomy: the 1844 Franco-Moroccan War, the 1860 Hispano-Moroccan War, the 1880 Madrid Conference, and the 1904 Entente each narrowed sovereign space. Boundaries were often notional — per CSV notes: "Bled al-makhzen (governed territory) vs Bled es-siba (nominal allegiance only, Rif/Atlas). Southern border was non-existent."

## Territorial extent

Polygon from Cliopatria (Morocco 1769–1860, 388K km²). The polygon represents the *makhzen*'s notional extent; effective territorial control was substantially smaller in the Rif, Atlas, and pre-Saharan zones.

## Open questions

### oq-mor-siba

Whether to model Bled es-siba as a separate (possibly multi-row) polity rather than absorbing it into MOR-1800-1904 is an open question. Under the WHEP territorial-economic-unit rule, *effective* economic sovereignty mattered more than nominal authority; siba areas had different de facto governance and economic integration patterns.
