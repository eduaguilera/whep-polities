# Amapá

## Summary

Amapá is a territory in Brazil's far north, on the north bank of the Amazon river mouth, bordering French Guiana and Suriname. It was created as the Federal Territory of Amapá in 1943 (Decree-Law 5,812), carved out of the northern part of the state of Pará, primarily to settle a long-running border dispute with French Guiana (resolved in Brazil's favor by international arbitration in 1900) and to give the region direct federal administration for rubber extraction during WWII. It remained a federal territory, administered directly from Brasília rather than self-governing, until the 1988 Constitution elevated it to full statehood as the State of Amapá. This entry is `type: subnational` because Amapá has never been a sovereign polity -- it has always been a first-order administrative subdivision of Brazil, first as a territory then as a state -- and the entry exists to carry a reporting unit distinct from the national BRA totals, not to claim any independent sovereignty. The span starts in 1943, at the unit's administrative creation, rather than at 1988 statehood, because the routing decision found data reported under the Amapá label going back to 1900 that is understood as a back-cast reconstruction onto the territory's eventual (1943+) boundary rather than evidence of an earlier distinct unit; the entry's own span begins where the administrative entity begins, 1943, and the earlier back-cast rows route here on that basis rather than implying the polity itself is older.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Amapá-labeled rows from the LatAm Subnational data source spanning approximately 1900-2025 (per the routing decision, 71% of the pre-1943 portion of the series is flagged as reconstructed/scaled/interpolated/fallback rather than directly observed). Before this entry existed, all Amapá data had nowhere subnational to go and was matched to the national row BRA-1909-2025 (or its 1903-1909 predecessor for the earliest years), which aggregates the whole of Brazil -- at present-day scale about 8.5 million km2 versus Amapá's roughly 143,000 km2, a mismatch of roughly 60x that silently inflated the implied area of every Amapá-labeled statistic when read against the national polygon. No existing BRA polity represented specifically the territory carved from Pará in 1943. That the entity is a distinct, separately-administered unit rather than an artifact of the data source's labeling is confirmed by its own legal history: Decree-Law 5,812 of 1943 created it as one of several federal territories carved from existing states for direct federal administration (alongside Guaporé/Rondônia and Rio Branco/Roraima), and the 1988 Constitution (Article 14 of the Transitional Provisions) named it explicitly among the territories elevated to statehood -- both are independent confirmations, external to this data source, that Amapá was treated as a distinct administrative object throughout the period.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `polygon_source: none`, `polygon_status: unassigned`. The routing decision identified `geobr-ibge` (IBGE's official Brazilian state boundaries, via the `geobr` R package, id_column `abbrev_state`, feature `AP`) as the correct registered source, but the local checkout does not have `data/geodata/geobr-ibge/states.gpkg` present, so no feature could be fetched or verified in this run (`registered_source_unfetched` is a route, not a valid `polygon_source` value). `gadm-4.1-adm1` was checked as an alternative and rejected: the local GADM 4.1 admin-1 copy covers only an 81-country subset and Brazil has zero features in it. No composite construction applies because Amapá is an atomic administrative unit, not a union of smaller reported pieces. `polygon_area_km2` is left unset (no geometry attached), so no measured figure appears here.

**Territory description:** Amapá occupies Brazil's northeasternmost mainland corner, between the north bank of the Amazon river's mouth and the borders with French Guiana (east/northeast) and Suriname's tri-point area (northwest); its capital is Macapá, on the equator. The modern state covers approximately 142,800 km2 (IBGE's current published figure for the State of Amapá), comparable in size to Nepal or the U.S. state of North Dakota. The 1943 Federal Territory boundary is understood to be close to the modern state boundary -- the main relevant boundary adjustment, the 1900 arbitration with French Guiana, predates this entry's start and was already settled when the territory was created -- but this has not been verified here since no polygon of either vintage has been fetched.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity is recorded. Before 1943 there was no Amapá-specific administrative entity; the area was simply the northern part of the state of Pará, and Pará has no dedicated wiki row against which to declare a predecessor relationship at this time. No successor is recorded because Amapá's transition from federal territory to state in 1988 was a change of legal status within a continuous administrative unit (same boundary, same governing seat), not a territorial break, which is why this entry spans 1943-2025 as a single row rather than splitting at 1988.

## Sourced claims

- Decree-Law 5,812 of September 13, 1943 created the Federal Territory of Amapá by detaching it from the state of Pará, one of several wartime federal territories carved from existing states for direct federal administration.
- Article 14 of the Transitional Constitutional Provisions Act of the 1988 Brazilian Constitution elevated the Federal Territory of Amapá to the State of Amapá, effective with the promulgation of the constitution.
- IBGE reports the current State of Amapá's area as approximately 142,828 km2, making it one of Brazil's smaller states by area.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-with-name

**Used <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> despite BRA having no existing subnational precedent**

BRA has zero existing subnational rows, so there is no country-specific precedent to follow. The dominant pattern across the table overall (57 of 66 subnational rows, including ESP-AS-1833-2025 and DZA-CVD-1902-1919) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and Amapá is an ordinary first-order administrative subdivision with no independent historical identity of its own (unlike bespoke cases such as HYD-1724-1948 or RYU-1937-1945, which name historical territories that existed under different sovereignty or governance than their modern successor state). There is no reason to depart from the dominant pattern here, so the code is BRA-AMAPA-1943-2025, using the full name AMAPA as the subunit token since Amapá has no two-letter abbreviation as widely recognized as Spanish province codes; IBGE's own code AP remains available as a future geobr feature id rather than as the polity-code token.

### d-start-year-1943-not-1988

**Span starts at 1943 administrative creation, not 1988 statehood**

The routing decision's proposed span_basis argues for 1943 over both 1988 (statehood) and 1889 (the country-wide default) on the grounds that the federal-territory and state phases are the same continuous administrative unit. I followed this because the alternative -- splitting at 1988 into a federal-territory row and a state row -- would require deciding whether the pre-1943 back-cast rows (roughly 43 years of the ~125-year data series) belong to a territory-phase entry or should be dropped, and nothing in the routing decision suggests the territory's boundary or governance character changed materially at 1988 beyond its formal legal status. A single row covering 1943-2025 avoids inventing a boundary at 1988 that the data does not support.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-unfetched-geobr-ibge

**geobr-ibge boundary for Amapá (feature AP) needs to be fetched**

The correct polygon source, geobr-ibge (id_column abbrev_state, feature AP), is a registered source but its local geopackage (data/geodata/geobr-ibge/states.gpkg) is absent from this checkout, so polygon_source is left as none and polygon_status as unassigned rather than citing an unfetched source directly. Once geobr-ibge is fetched, this entry should be revisited to attach the AP feature and compute polygon_area_km2 from it, and to confirm whether IBGE's current AP boundary is stable back to 1943 or reflects any post-creation adjustment (e.g., minor tri-point resolution with Suriname/French Guiana).

### oq-pre1943-back-cast-methodology

**Pre-1943 back-cast rows (roughly 1900-1942) need methodology verification**

The routing decision flags 71% of rows in the back-cast segment as reconstructed via scaling, interpolation, or fallback methods, and assumes these represent the LatAm Subnational source's own back-projection of later Amapá boundaries onto earlier years rather than a genuinely distinct pre-1943 unit. This has not been confirmed against that source's own documented methodology. If the source instead intended these rows to represent the pre-1943 northern Pará area under a different definition (e.g., a municipality or comarca boundary that does not match the 1943+ territory), routing them to this entry would misattribute area and population figures to a boundary that did not yet exist in that form.

### oq-boundary-stability-1900-arbitration

**Whether the 1900 Franco-Brazilian arbitration line fully defines the entry's eastern/northern boundary throughout its span**

The 1900 Swiss arbitration (the 'Amapá Question') settled the Brazil-French Guiana border decades before this entry's 1943 start, but the exact modern tri-point with Suriname in the northwest was not necessarily fixed at the same time, and no check has been made here for any 20th-century boundary adjustment affecting the territory/state's northwestern corner. This matters for whether a future geobr-ibge polygon, digitized at present-day vintage, is a safe proxy for the boundary as it stood across the full 1943-2025 span or only for its later portion.
