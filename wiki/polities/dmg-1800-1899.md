# Sultanate of Damagaram

## Summary

The Sultanate of Damagaram was a centralised Muslim state centred on Zinder (Sinder) in the south-central zone of modern Niger. Founded c. 1736 as an offshoot of the Borno Empire's client states, Damagaram grew to become the dominant power of the Hausa-speaking borderlands between the Sokoto Caliphate and Borno. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1899, when French colonial forces defeated the sultanate at the Battle of Tirmini and occupied Zinder. Biger notes that "the people of Niger gained independence from the French in August 1960." The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). Damagaram's territory centred on Zinder and extended across the agricultural zone between the Air Massif to the north and the Nigerian border to the south. At its height the sultanate controlled trans-Saharan trade routes passing through Zinder.

## Open questions

### oq-chain-gap

The chain jumps from DMG-1800-1899 directly to NER-1960-2025. The French colonial period for the Zinder territory (1899–1960) — including the Territoire Militaire du Zinder (1900), later the Territoire du Niger (1922) — is not represented as a separate WHEP row. This is a coverage gap.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger covers Niger only at the post-independence level. A specialist source on Damagaram (e.g., Dunbar 1970 *History of Damagaram*; Séré de Rivières 1965 *Histoire du Niger*) is needed to source territorial extents and political claims.
