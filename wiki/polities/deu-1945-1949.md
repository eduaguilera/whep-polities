# Allied-occupied Germany (1945-1949)

## Summary

Allied-occupied Germany covers the period from the unconditional surrender of Nazi Germany on 8 May 1945 to the establishment of the Federal Republic of Germany and the German Democratic Republic in 1949. Germany was divided into four occupation zones administered by the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and the Soviet Union, with Berlin similarly partitioned. No sovereign German state existed in this window — a fact COW records directly, as set out below — but the territory was a real economic unit with tracked agricultural output, and this row exists to carry it.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `constructed` source, feature `DEU-1945-1949`. Built by `scripts/sources/constructed/build.py` as the **union of CShapes 2.0 gwcode 260 (Western Allied zones / Trizone) and gwcode 265 (Soviet zone)** for the 1945-1949 time-step. Measured in an equal-area CRS (ESRI:54034) from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`: **356,835 km²** — Germany within its post-war (Potsdam) borders, excluding the territories east of the Oder-Neisse line transferred to Poland and the USSR.

**The union decomposes exactly, and the components are measurable.** [F78-1949-1990](f78-1949-1990.md) (West Germany) measures **247,552 km²** and [F77-1949-1990](f77-1949-1990.md) (East Germany) measures **109,271 km²**; 247,552 + 109,271 = **356,823 km²**, within 12 km² (0.003%) of this row's union polygon. Using just the West-zone polygon — which earlier iterations of this page's binding did — coloured only 69% of Germany on the map and under-counted agricultural data by the whole Soviet zone.

**The eastern loss is the largest territorial fact of this row, and it is sourced.** [DEU-1938-1945](deu-1938-1945.md) measures **496,629 km²**, so the transition into this row drops **139,794 km² (28%)**. Biger's Germany entry documents the human side of the same event: after WWII "10,000,000 ethnic Germans — often centuries-old communities — were expelled from Central and Eastern Europe, throwing back the ethnic border between the Germans and the Slavs to the Oder-Neisse line: 7,000,000 from Posen, Silesia, Pomerania and East Prussia (now part of Poland)" ([biger-1995 §germany](../sources/biger-1995.md#germany)). Biger also gives modern Germany's area as 356,950 km² ([biger-1995 §germany](../sources/biger-1995.md#germany)) — **within 115 km² (0.03%) of this row's measured 356,835 km²**, which is a strong independent confirmation that the constructed polygon is Germany in its post-1945 borders and that reunification in 1990 restored exactly this extent.

**Territorial changes inside 1945-1949: yes, one, and it is administrative rather than a change of the row's outer extent.** The four zones were progressively merged on the western side — the US and British zones formed the Bizone on 1 January 1947 and the French zone joined to form the Trizone in 1948 — and the currency reform of 20 June 1948 and the Berlin Blockade from 24 June 1948 hardened the east-west line. **None of these changes this row's outer boundary**, which is why no split is warranted: the union of all four zones is constant from surrender to the two foundings. What they do change is the *reporting* unit: agricultural statistics for 1946-1948 may be compiled per zone, per Bizone, or for Germany as a whole, and a figure labelled "Germany" for 1948 might be Trizone-only. That is a data-scope risk this page cannot resolve from the sources on disk; see [oq-deu-zone-reporting-scope](#oq-deu-zone-reporting-scope).

**One real exception to the constant-extent claim, and it is in-span.** The **Saar** was detached from the occupation zones during this row, not before or after it: "On 16 February 1946, France disentangled the Saar from the Allied zones of occupation and established the separate Saar Protectorate, which was de facto no longer under the joint Allied jurisdiction by the Allied Control Council for Germany", and "By 18 December 1946 customs controls were established between the Saar and allied occupied Germany" ([wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13 §establishment](../sources/wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13.md#establishment)). The same source records the Saar's boundary churning repeatedly *inside* this row's span: 109 Prussian Rhine Province municipalities added with effect from 20 July 1946, then "61 municipalities returned to Germany, while 13 other municipalities were ceded to the Saar Protectorate between 8 June 1947 and 1949" ([wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13 §establishment](../sources/wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13.md#establishment)). The Saar rejoined West Germany only in 1957 ([wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13 §accession-1957](../sources/wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13.md#accession-1957)).

At roughly 2,570 km² the Saar is 0.7% of this row's area, so it does not justify splitting the row on magnitude grounds — but it does mean the constant-extent claim above is *approximately* rather than strictly true, and it raises a concrete overlap risk: if the constructed union polygon here includes the Saar while a separate Saar row also carries a polygon for 1946-1949, the two double-count. Not resolved on this page; see [oq-deu-saar-double-count](#oq-deu-saar-double-count).

**Why the polygon is `constructed` rather than a single CShapes feature.** CShapes codes occupied territory as one of its four dependency categories ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)) and gives the two German zones separate features; no single CShapes feature represents occupied Germany as a whole. Building the union is therefore the only way to get one polygon for a reporting unit that the data treats as one. CShapes' rule that wartime changes are dated to "the date of postwar agreements" ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)) is also why the Oder-Neisse loss appears at this row's start rather than spread across 1944-1945.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [DEU-1938-1945](deu-1938-1945.md) — Nazi Germany, measured **496,629 km²**; the 139,794 km² difference is the Oder-Neisse transfer plus the Austrian and Sudeten reversals.
- **Successors:** [F78-1949-1990](f78-1949-1990.md) — **West Germany**, the Federal Republic, measured 247,552 km²; and [F77-1949-1990](f77-1949-1990.md) — **East Germany**, the German Democratic Republic, measured 109,271 km². *An earlier version of this page had these two labels swapped*, attributing the FRG to F77 and the GDR to F78. The measured areas settle it beyond doubt: F77 is the 109,271 km² polygon and therefore the GDR, F78 the 247,552 km² polygon and therefore the FRG, and each page's own `polity_name` frontmatter agrees.

## Key dates

- 1945-05-08: unconditional surrender of Germany ([wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16 §articles-referenced](../sources/wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16.md#articles-referenced) lists the Allied-occupied Germany article this row was built from). Start-of-row event.
- 1945-07-17: Potsdam Conference begins; the Oder-Neisse line is settled in the resulting agreement.
- 1947-01-01: US and British zones merge into the Bizone. **Administrative**, not territorial.
- 1948-06-20: currency reform in the western zones.
- 1948-06-24: Berlin Blockade begins.
- 1949-05-23: Basic Law promulgated; FRG established. End-of-row event.
- 1949-10-07: GDR established. End-of-row event.

## Sourced claims

- This row was created from the *Allied-occupied Germany* article, one of the fourteen articles listed in [wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16 §articles-referenced](../sources/wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16.md#articles-referenced), and DEU-1945-1949 is named explicitly in that source's own polity table ([wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16 §polities-sourced](../sources/wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16.md#polities-sourced)).
- Post-war expulsions "thr[ew] back the ethnic border between the Germans and the Slavs to the Oder-Neisse line", with 7,000,000 expelled "from Posen, Silesia, Pomerania and East Prussia (now part of Poland)" ([biger-1995 §germany](../sources/biger-1995.md#germany)) — the demographic counterpart of this row's 139,794 km² territorial loss relative to its predecessor.
- Biger gives Germany's area as 137,818 sq miles (**356,950 km²**) ([biger-1995 §germany](../sources/biger-1995.md#germany)). Since Biger writes in 1994, after reunification, the agreement with this row's measured 356,835 km² to 0.03% confirms that occupied Germany 1945-1949 and reunified Germany occupy the same territory — the whole of the change is 1945, none of it 1990.
- **COW has no German member at all during this row.** `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv` gives `ccode=260` (German Federal Republic) `1955-05-05 → 1990-10-02` and `ccode=265` (German Democratic Republic) `1954-03-25 → 1990-10-02` ([cow-state-system-v2024 §schema](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#schema) for columns). Both start *years after* this row ends, so 1945-1949 — and indeed 1949-1954 — is a hole in COW's German coverage, consistent with COW's post-1920 criterion of League or UN membership ([cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion)). The frontmatter `cow: 260` is therefore a code assignment for the German family, **not** a claim that COW recognises a state here; treating it as one would be a mistake, and it is exactly the kind of case the WHEP polity definition exists to handle ([log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition)).
- Note also that CShapes' German features are cited here by **gwcode** 260/265 whereas COW's ccodes 260/265 map to FRG and GDR respectively; the numeric coincidence is a trap. The distribution on disk is the COW-coded one ([cshapes-2.0 §which-version-is-on-disk-2026-04-11](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#which-version-is-on-disk-2026-04-11)), so any claim of the form "CShapes 260 is West Germany" needs checking against that file rather than assuming.
- CShapes' four dependency categories include "occupied territories" ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)) — the category occupied Germany falls into, and the reason its zones are coded as separate features rather than as one state.
- CShapes dates wartime territorial change to "the date of postwar agreements ... as the date of the change" ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)) — why the eastern loss lands at 1945 rather than being spread across the Red Army's advance.
- Geometric measurement (this ingest, not an external source): this row **356,835 km²**; [F78-1949-1990](f78-1949-1990.md) **247,552 km²**; [F77-1949-1990](f77-1949-1990.md) **109,271 km²**; [DEU-1938-1945](deu-1938-1945.md) **496,629 km²**. All ESRI:54034 from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`.
- Data footprint (this ingest): **144 layer-B rows** route to this code per `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/territory_basis.csv`. An earlier version of this page said 113; the current figure is the measured one.

## Open questions

### oq-deu-zone-reporting-scope

For 1946-1948, is a figure labelled "Germany" the four-zone total, the Bizone, or the Trizone? The zones had separate administrations and separate statistical apparatus, and the western merger sequence (Bizone 1947, Trizone 1948) means the answer can change *within* this row's span even though its territory does not. This is the single largest interpretive risk to the row's 144 data rows and cannot be settled from the sources currently on disk; it needs the source files themselves inspected for a zone qualifier.

### oq-deu-saar-double-count

Does the constructed `DEU-1945-1949` union polygon include the Saar (~2,570 km²), detached from the Allied zones on 16 February 1946 ([wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13 §establishment](../sources/wikipedia-saar-protectorate-2026-08-13.md#establishment))? If it does and a separate Saar row also carries a polygon for the same years, the two overlap. Checkable with `scripts/validate_spatial_containment.py` against whatever Saar row exists.
