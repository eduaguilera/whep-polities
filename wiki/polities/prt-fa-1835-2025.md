# Faro (district of Portugal)

## Summary

Faro is the NUTS II region (coextensive with NUTS III "Algarve") of Portugal, corresponding to the historical Algarve province and modern Faro district in the country's far south, on the Atlantic coast bordering Spain (Huelva province, Andalusia) to the east. This entry is `type: subnational` — Portugal held uninterrupted sovereignty over the territory throughout; the row exists to carry a *reporting unit* that Portuguese and European statistical systems tabulate separately from the national total, not to represent a distinct state. The 1989 start follows the country convention agreed for Portuguese NUTS regions: Decree-Law 46/89 established the modern NUTS I/II/III nomenclature, and Faro is not among the units with a documented earlier or later creation date (unlike, e.g., Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, created later in 2013), so the default applies. Before 1989, statistics for this territory were reported as part of the historical Algarve district/province rather than a NUTS unit, and any pre-1989 data for the area is routed to the national polity PRT-1800-2025 rather than invented into this entry's span, per the country convention's explicit instruction that pre-NUTS data be attributed to the national total.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: rows tagged unit_id `PRT-PTFA` / admin_name `PT_FA` ("Faro") for Portugal, spanning 1880-2025 per the routing decision, though only the 1989-2025 portion is captured by this entry (the 1880-1988 portion is attributed to PRT-1800-2025, since the NUTS system and this reporting unit did not exist before 1989). Previously this data would have been matched to PRT-1800-2025 (the national total) or to FRO-1800-2025, an unrelated territory (the Faroe Islands, a Danish autonomous territory in the North Atlantic with no relationship to Portugal beyond a similar three-letter code). Both are wrong: the Faroes are a different country entirely, and the national total is roughly 18x the area of this one region, which would badly misrepresent any region-specific statistic (population density, land use, regional GDP) as if it applied to Portugal as a whole. What confirms this is a distinct reporting unit rather than an artefact of one dataset: Eurostat's NUTS classification treats Algarve (PT15) as one of Portugal's standard NUTS II regions with its own published regional accounts, population, and land-use statistics since 1989, independent of both the national Portuguese series and any other Portuguese NUTS region's series.

**Territory description:** Faro/Algarve occupies the southernmost strip of mainland Portugal, bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the south and west, the Guadiana river and Spain's Huelva province to the east, and the Alentejo region to the north. It corresponds closely to the pre-1976 Algarve province and the modern Faro district, running roughly from Vila Real de Santo António in the east to Sagres/Cape St. Vincent in the west, and including the city of Faro as its administrative seat. Its land area is approximately **4,997 km²** (the modern NUTS III Algarve figure published by INE/Eurostat) — this is a stated administrative figure from the territory's own statistical authority, not a measurement of any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached.

## Decisions

### d-follow-dominant-nuts-pattern

**Use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> rather than a bespoke code**

Faro/Algarve is a standard administrative NUTS region, not a historical territory with a name and identity independent of Portugal's modern administrative geography (unlike the bespoke-code cases such as Alaska Territory or Hyderabad). It therefore follows the dominant 57-of-66 pattern seen elsewhere in the table, e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CA-1833-2025 for Spanish regions. Chose the subunit token `FA` (from PT_FA / NUTS code PT15's common abbreviation Faro) rather than the ISO 3166-2 code PT-08, to keep it short and legible, consistent with how the two-letter Spanish examples abbreviate rather than reuse ISO 3166-2 codes directly.

### d-polygon-source-none-not-route-name

**Set polygon_source to none rather than repeating the route name**

The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed and its polygon_source field was left as an empty string with the actual candidate (GISCO NUTS) described only in polygon_detail. Per the harness's explicit instruction that new_source_needed and registered_source_unfetched must resolve to polygon_source: none (never a route name or an unregistered source name written as if registered), this entry sets polygon_source: none and polygon_feature_id: null, and records GISCO NUTS as a desired future source in the open questions instead of declaring it as a value that would fail validate_declared_sources.

### d-prt-two-level-hierarchy

**Portugal is modelled as two levels — 18 districts, and from 1986 the five NUTS II regions that contain them**

The panel reports Portugal at two scales at once: five NUTS II regions and eighteen districts. Both sets tile mainland Portugal independently — measured on the committed geometry the regions sum to 88,798 km² and the districts to 88,801 km², a 0.003% difference — so treating them as one flat list of members would double-count every square kilometre. They are not a clean hierarchy either: the districts date from 1835 and NUTS II was delimited later across them, so five districts straddle a regional boundary (Lisboa is only 47% inside Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, Santarém 60%, Aveiro 63%, Viseu 65%, Setúbal 69%).

The resolution is a hierarchy **split in time**, which works because the regions postdate the districts. Until 1986 the districts are Portugal's direct members (17 of them, 18 once Setúbal exists in 1926) and no region competes with them. From 1986 the five regions become Portugal's only direct members and every district sits inside the region holding the largest share of its area, with that share recorded in the edge's own `basis`. A level-1 read is therefore exact in both periods; a level-2 read of a straddling district is approximate, and says so where it is.

For this row that means: inside Portugal (PRT-1800-2025) 1835-1986; inside Algarve (PRT-PT15-1986-2025) 1986-2025. Its area splits Algarve 99%.

## Open questions

**SUPERSEDED 2026-09-11.** The span and polygon this decision chose have both changed; see `d-prt-two-level-hierarchy` below. Kept rather than deleted because the reasoning it records — that no pre-NUTS Portuguese administration had been confirmed — is exactly what the district-count check refuted.

### oq-gisco-nuts-vintage

**Which GISCO NUTS vintage should eventually back this polygon, and does Faro's boundary change across revisions?**

Eurostat's GISCO NUTS boundary files are revised on a multi-year cycle (2003, 2006, 2010, 2013, 2016, 2021 nomenclature versions), and while Algarve/Faro (PT15) has been stable as a single NUTS II/III region since 1989, the exact vector boundary and any minor municipal-boundary adjustments across these vintages have not been checked. Before fetching GISCO as a registered source and assigning polygon_status: assigned here, someone needs to (a) register GISCO NUTS as a source, (b) pick the vintage nearest 1989 for the earliest years and confirm whether later vintages diverge, and (c) confirm licence terms, which the routing decision flagged as unknown (licence_known: false).

### oq-pre1989-algarve-boundary-match

**Does the historical Algarve province (pre-1976) exactly match the modern NUTS Faro/Algarve boundary?**

This entry assumes 1880-1988 data for the same admin_name PT_FA can legitimately be folded into the national PRT-1800-2025 total, per the country convention. But if the pre-1976 Algarve province's boundary differs materially from the post-1989 NUTS II Algarve region (e.g. municipal boundary changes at the Alentejo border), a future historian pass may need to construct a distinct pre-1989 Algarve entry rather than relying on the national total, especially if region-specific series (not just national aggregates) exist for the 1880-1988 window. This was flagged as a concern in the original routing decision and remains unresolved here.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** Portugal's 1835 administrative reform created 17 mainland districts (distritos); Setúbal, the 18th, was carved out of Lisboa district in 1926. Ten of this table's Portuguese district rows already started in 1835, and the arithmetic closes exactly at 17 + 1 once the seven late-starting rows are corrected, which is the check that settled it. This row now spans 1835-2025 and carries all 144 data years (1880-2023), where before only the 35 years from 1989 were routed and the rest were left unroutable.
