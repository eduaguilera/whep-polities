# Upper Burma (1852-1885)

## Summary

Upper Burma (1852-1885) covers the rump Konbaung kingdom after the Second Anglo-Burmese War (1852), when Britain seized Lower Burma (the Pegu region). The kingdom survived as Upper Burma only — centred on Mandalay, approximately 437,000 km² — until the Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885), which led to Britain's annexation of the remaining territory and abolition of the kingdom on 1 January 1886. Upper Burma had no COW code during this period as it did not meet COW's independence criteria; CShapes does not cover this polity because CShapes starts in 1886, one year after Upper Burma ceased to exist as an independent entity.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Cliopatria (Burma 1853–1858 time-step). The Cliopatria polygon represents a single snapshot applied across the entire 1852–1885 period. Upper Burma at this stage comprised the Irrawaddy valley and Shan plateau but excluded the coastal regions (Arakan, Tenasserim, Pegu) lost in earlier wars. Approximately 437,000 km² per the WHEP CSV notes. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §scope](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#scope)

The single-time-step limitation applies: Cliopatria provides many time-steps for Burma, but WHEP extracts one snapshot. Territory lost in 1852 (Lower Burma) should not appear in this polygon. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §single-time-step](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#single-time-step)

## Open questions

### oq-lower-burma-coverage

Lower Burma (Pegu, Arakan, Tenasserim) from 1852 onwards is British-controlled territory. These regions are not tracked as a separate WHEP row in the current CSV — they are subsumed under British India. A future ingest should confirm whether a separate WHEP polity for British Lower Burma 1852–1885 is warranted, or whether the territory is accounted for in a broader British India row.

### oq-cliopatria-snapshot-accuracy

The Cliopatria Burma 1853–1858 time-step is used as the polygon for the entire 1852–1885 period. Internal Konbaung territorial shifts (Shan states consolidation, minor border adjustments) during this 33-year period are not captured. The next ingest should assess whether a later Cliopatria time-step (e.g. 1870s) would be more representative of the full period's territory.
