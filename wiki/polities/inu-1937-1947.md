# Future Dominion of India (territory within British India, 1937-1947)

## Summary

The part of British India, provinces and princely states together, that became
the Dominion of India at partition on 15 August 1947. It covers the decade
from the separation of Burma (1 April 1937) to independence.

This is a **reporting-territory row, not a state**. It mirrors
[pak-1937-1947](pak-1937-1947.md), the future-Pakistan territory within
British India, which already existed for the same reason. `type: subnational`
is deliberate: the row must not compete with the national
[ind-1937-1947](ind-1937-1947.md) in the matcher's family ranking, so every
other source's `india` label still reaches British India as a whole. Only
the `fao1952` `India` label is routed here, by alias.

**Why this entry exists.** FAO's 1952 yearbook (the 1951 volume) gives its
pre-war columns (1934-38 averages, 1937 population, 1939-40 livestock) **on
present boundaries**. For South Asia that means it splits British India into
the two successor states and reports each half under its post-1947 name:

| fao1952, 1937 `population total` | thousand |
|---|---:|
| `India` | 304,333 |
| `Pakistan` | 66,009 |
| sum | 370,342 |

370 million is undivided British India with the princely states (1931 census
338 million without Burma, 1941 census 389 million). So FAO's `India` is the
future Union, not British India. The 1940 horse counts split the same way:
India 1,186 and Pakistan 934 thousand.

Before this row existed, `Pakistan` went to PAK-1937-1947 but `India` went to
IND-1914-1937 (the 1934-38 averages) and IND-1937-1947 (1937, 1940). So British
India as a whole carried only the Union's figure, about 82% of its population.
This row gives each half of FAO's partition its own row, built the same way.

**Why a mirror row and not `back_cast` to IND-1947-1949.** The other
present-boundary labels in the same table (`Romania`, `Finland`,
`Czechoslovakia`, `Poland`, `Bulgaria`) are routed `back_cast` to their
post-war polity. They have no counterpart in the table, and there is no
pre-war row whose territory is theirs. `India` does have a counterpart.
Its complement `Pakistan` is already routed to a 1937-1947 row for the
future-Pakistan territory. Sending `India` to IND-1947-1949 instead would
treat the two halves of one partition differently: Pakistan's 1937 figure
would sit on a 1937 row and India's on a row that begins in 1947. A consumer
filtering out `back_cast` would then keep Pakistan and drop India. With the
mirror row, both halves use the same construction, and `polity_composition.csv`
can register both as parts of IND-1937-1947.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon (proxy).** The constructed `IND-1947-1949` feature, reused:
CShapes 2.0 `gwcode 750` at its 1947-1949 step minus the Pondicherry and Goa
enclaves, 3,041,829 km2 in ESRI:54034
([cshapes-2.0](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md)). This is the partition boundary of
15 August 1947, drawn onto the decade before it. `polygon_area_km2` is left
null because that figure is the reused feature's own measurement (issue 195).

It is a `proxy` rather than `assigned` because the boundary is a
reconstruction. The Radcliffe line through Punjab and Bengal did not exist
before August 1947. FAO's pre-war figures were redistributed across it
afterwards, district by district. So the polygon is the territory FAO counted,
but not a boundary that anyone administered in 1937. Within that caveat the
fit is close:

- **Area.** 3,041,829 km2 against IND-1937-1947's 4,223,063. The two halves
  together, this row plus PAK-1937-1947 (932,577), come to 3,974,406, which is
  0.94x British India. The remainder is 248,792 km2, and 248,660 km2 of it is
  one piece centred at 76.3°E 34.8°N: Jammu and Kashmir. Its accession was
  contested in 1947-49, and neither 1947 CShapes step assigns it to either
  dominion. Nothing here decides that question. The two reused polygons
  overlap by 21 km2 (border slivers).
- **Population.** FAO's own 1937 figures give India 82% of the two halves'
  total population, against 77% of their combined area.

Hyderabad (HYD-1724-1948), which acceded only in 1948, lies wholly inside the
reused polygon (measured: 100% of its area). This is consistent with FAO's
"present boundaries" of about 1950, when it had acceded.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Container:** [ind-1937-1947](ind-1937-1947.md), British India (1937-1947),
  for the whole span.
- **No predecessor or successor.** Like the other component rows
  ([tpap-1949-1975](tpap-1949-1975.md), [man-1945-1950](man-1945-1950.md)'s
  container edges), this row sits outside the national succession chain. The
  chain already runs IND-1937-1947 → [ind-1947-1949](ind-1947-1949.md), and
  adding a second predecessor to IND-1947-1949 would give the ISO3 successor
  map two parents for one Union. The territory this row reconstructs is the
  one IND-1947-1949 holds from 15 August 1947.
- **Sibling:** [pak-1937-1947](pak-1937-1947.md), the other half of FAO's
  partition.

## Contradictions

None recorded. FAO's convention is stated in the yearbook's own footing
("present boundaries") and is borne out by the India + Pakistan sum.

## Decisions

### d-created-2026-09-24

Created 2026-09-24 by Claude as the mirror of PAK-1937-1947. The alternative
was `back_cast` to IND-1947-1949, and the reasons for this choice are under
Summary. Routing:

- `India | fao1952 | 1937-1940`: plain (observed-span) alias.
- `India | fao1952 | 1934-1936`: `back_cast` slice. It only exists so that the
  1934-38 period averages reach this row: they start three years before it,
  as Pakistan's start three years before PAK-1937-1947. Without the slice the
  period router would score IND-1914-1937 as live for more of the window, and
  the average would go back to British India as a whole.

Rows moved: 44 (37 period averages from IND-1914-1937; 4 population rows from
1937 and 3 horse rows from 1940, all from IND-1937-1947).

## Open questions

### oq-kashmir-residual

About 249,000 km2 of IND-1937-1947 is in neither this polygon nor
PAK-1937-1947's, mostly Jammu and Kashmir. Whether FAO's 1934-38 India figures
include Kashmir, which FAO had no way of splitting in 1951, has not been
checked against the yearbook's footnotes.
