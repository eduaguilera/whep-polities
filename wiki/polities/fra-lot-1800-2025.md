# Lot (département, France)

## Summary

Lot is a French département in the southwest, part of the Occitanie region, with its préfecture at Cahors. It was created in March 1790 during the National Constituent Assembly's reorganization of France into roughly 83 départements, carved chiefly from the former province of Quercy (with slivers of Périgord and Rouergue). Unlike Alsace-Lorraine, Belfort, Corsica, or the overseas départements, Lot was never annexed, split, or reorganized as a distinct sub-unit event — it has retained essentially the same administrative footprint from creation to the present, aside from a minor loss of territory when Tarn-et-Garonne was carved out of several neighbouring départements (including Lot) in 1808. This entry is `type: subnational` because Lot has never been a sovereign or quasi-sovereign polity: it is, and always has been, an administrative subdivision of France, and sits below the national FRA chain in the container hierarchy throughout its existence.

**Why this entry exists.** Before this entry, any statistical series reported at the département level for Lot had no polity to route to except the national French rows (FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1919-2025), which cover roughly 80-95 other départements simultaneously and are ~100x the area of Lot alone — any Lot-specific agricultural, demographic, or production figure attributed to national France would badly overstate the reporting territory. No existing polity in the table represents Lot, or any French département, specifically; France currently has zero subnational rows, so this entry sets the country's first precedent for treating INSEE-level administrative units as distinct polities. That Lot is a genuinely distinct, continuously reported administrative and statistical unit is confirmed by INSEE, which has published département-level statistics (population censuses, agricultural surveys) for Lot under a stable code (46) since French statistical series began in the 19th century, and by the département's continuous existence as an electoral and administrative circumscription (with its own préfet, conseil départemental, and INSEE code) since 1790.

Until that fetch happens, polygon_source is `none` and polygon_status is `unassigned`; no geometry is attached to this entry, so no area is measured from it here.

**Territory description:** Lot occupies the Quercy region of southwestern France, bordered by Dordogne (north), Corrèze and Cantal (northeast), Aveyron (east), Tarn-et-Garonne (south), and Lot-et-Garonne (southwest). Its préfecture, Cahors, sits on the Lot river roughly 100 km north of Toulouse. On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE département code 46. Its present-day area is approximately **5,217 km2** (INSEE published figure), making it one of France's more sparsely populated rural départements. This figure describes the modern administrative boundary; whether the boundary matches the 1790-1808 extent exactly (before the 1808 Tarn-et-Garonne carve-out) is unconfirmed and noted as an open question.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Start year set to 1800, not the proposed 1790**

The routing decision proposed start_year 1790, following the general rule that a département not among the four departure cases (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, Corsica split, overseas départements) inherits France's founding-era start. Lot is indeed one of the original ~83 départements created by the 1790 Revolutionary reform and has kept essentially the same boundary since. But the container-tiling constraint is binding: the polity table's earliest FRA container rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919) both start in 1800, and no FRA national row exists for 1790-1800. Since the containment gate rejects an edge falling outside the container's own span, a 1790 start cannot be tiled today. Rather than leave a 10-year gap, this page starts the code and frontmatter at 1800, matching the earliest available container, and records the discrepancy as an open question rather than silently absorbing it.

### d-code-pattern-fra-lot

**Followed the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

France has no existing subnational rows, so there is no country-specific precedent to follow, but Lot is an ordinary, uninterrupted French département with no distinct historical name of its own comparable to Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State — it is not a bespoke case. The dominant pattern across the table (57 of 66 subnational rows) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, so this page uses FRA-LOT-1800-2025, with LOT as a short readable subunit code, consistent with examples like ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CA-1833-2025.

### d-container-chain-uses-1800-1919-row

**Used FRA-1800-1919 rather than the FRA-1800-1871/FRA-1871-1919 pair**

The polity table carries two overlapping national chains for this period: FRA-1800-1871 and FRA-1871-1919 (split at the Alsace-Lorraine cession), and a separate FRA-1800-1919 row spanning the whole interval. Using the split pair for Lot's container would introduce an internal 1871 boundary that has no bearing on Lot, a southwestern département untouched by Alsace-Lorraine's loss or return. Using the single FRA-1800-1919 row instead gives one clean edge for 1800-1919 that documents nothing spurious about Lot, then hands off to FRA-1919-2025 for the remainder.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap-vs-true-origin

**Ten-year gap between Lot's real 1790 origin and this page's 1800 start**

Lot was created as a département in March 1790, but this page starts at 1800 only because the polity table's earliest FRA container row begins there, and the containment gate requires every edge to fall inside the container's own span. If a future ingest adds an earlier FRA national row (e.g. FRA-1790-1800, or an extension of FRA-1800-1919 backward to 1790), this entry's start_year should move to 1790 to match Lot's actual administrative founding, and the polity code should be renamed to FRA-LOT-1790-2025. Until then, any Lot-labelled data dated 1790-1799 has nowhere to route under this entry.

### oq-no-polygon-source-registered

**No registered, fetched source currently supplies French département boundaries**

gadm-4.1-adm1 is the correct source family for département-level polygons per the routing decision, but the locally fetched GADM 4.1 file is a curated 81-country subset excluding France (0 features for FRA). The remedy is to fetch the missing GADM adm1 data for France (or a full gadm-3.6 pull), not to invent a new source — polygon_source is left as `none` and polygon_status as `unassigned` until that fetch happens. The 5,217 km2 figure in the territorial extent is INSEE's published modern area, not a measurement of any attached geometry, since none is attached.

### oq-lot-et-garonne-boundary-adjustment

**Whether Lot's boundary changed materially between 1790 and the present needs checking**

The 1808 creation of Tarn-et-Garonne drew territory from several surrounding départements including Lot, meaning pre-1808 Lot was likely somewhat larger than its present 5,217 km2. It is not yet confirmed how much territory Lot lost in that reorganization, nor whether any further 19th-century canton-level adjustments with Corrèze, Cantal, Aveyron, Dordogne, Tarn-et-Garonne, or Lot-et-Garonne changed the boundary further. This matters if a polygon is ever constructed for the full 1800-2025 span rather than just the modern (post-1808, or even post-19th-century) boundary, since a single modern-boundary polygon used as a proxy for the entire span would slightly overstate Lot's pre-1808 territory's absence and understate its actual 1800-1808 extent.
