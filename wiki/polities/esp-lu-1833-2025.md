# Lugo (province of Spain)

## Summary

Lugo is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and today one of the four provinces making up the Galicia autonomous community in the northwestern corner of the Iberian Peninsula. It takes its name from its capital, the inland city of Lugo (notable for its intact Roman walls), and occupies Galicia's interior and northeastern reaches, bordered by A Coruna province to the west, Pontevedra and Ourense to the south, Asturias to the east, and the Bay of Biscay along a short northern coastline near Ribadeo. This entry is type: subnational because Lugo is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Lugo separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES112, for over a century and a half. The routing decision for unit ES112 found no existing candidate matching Lugo by name (the only ESP subnational rows on file, A Coruna and Madrid, are different provinces), so this is a genuine create_new case rather than documentation of a pre-existing but unwritten row, unlike the sibling A Coruna page.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision captures valued statistical rows for unit ES112 ("Lugo"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (ES111 A Coruna, ES113 Ourense, and onward through ES243), all following a system_start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to / why wrong: the routing reasoning explicitly checked the three ESP candidates on file (A Coruna, Madrid, and national Spain) and found none IS Lugo -- Lugo is a distinct province from both named subnational candidates, and folding its data into the national ESP-1800-2025 row would collapse a province-level reporting geography into an aggregate roughly 51x larger (505,000 km2 national vs Lugo's ~9,856 km2), losing the province-level distinction the source data actually carries. What confirms distinctness: Lugo's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES112) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling entries ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national aggregate, and sibling units in the same NUTS3 batch (ES112 onward) are being uniformly created as new subnational polities under the country's default 1833-2025 span rule.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish province numeric code), under which Lugo is conventionally cpro 27 -- but that source is flagged present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository), so the feature cannot be confirmed and this entry carries no geometry yet, exactly as ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 do for the same reason. gadm-4.1-adm1 was not used because it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, not the 50-province level, and Lugo province does not coincide with the Galicia autonomous community boundary (Galicia contains three other provinces: A Coruna, Ourense, Pontevedra), so an adm1 feature would be the wrong granularity entirely. Territory description: Lugo province occupies the northeastern and interior part of Galicia, the only Galician province with a coastline on the Cantabrian Sea (Bay of Biscay) rather than the Atlantic proper, along a stretch near Ribadeo and Foz. Its capital and largest city is Lugo, an inland city on the Mino river known for its UNESCO-listed Roman walls; the province is otherwise largely rural and mountainous, bordering Asturias to the east and Leon (Castile and Leon) to the southeast. On a modern map it is bounded by A Coruna province to the west, Pontevedra and Ourense to the south, Asturias to the east, and the Bay of Biscay to the north. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 9,856 km2, making it the largest of Galicia's four provinces -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform (Lugo was not among the units affected by the 1927 Canary Islands split or any other known province-level boundary revision), so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025.

## Predecessors and successors

Lugo has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized under the Kingdom of Galicia's historic partido and pre-1833 provincial system, which this database does not model at sub-kingdom granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1978, one of the four provinces making up the Galicia autonomous community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for its Spanish NUTS3 sibling rows.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Lugo as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES112) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ES112 records that sibling Spanish NUTS3 units (ES111 A Coruna, ES113 Ourense, and others through ES243) were all routed create_new under the same 1833-start/2025-open-end country convention, which is the evidentiary basis for treating Lugo as a distinct, continuously-tabulated reporting geography rather than folding it into the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate.
- mapspain-ign is the source already used to register the two other Spanish province rows created in this convention pass (ESP-CO-1833-2025 for cpro=15, ESP-MD-1833-2025 for Madrid's cpro), and Lugo's own INE province code (cpro) is 27, the routing decision's basis for expecting a matching feature once the source is fetched.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-lu-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation LU, matching the already-decided ESP subnational house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern in this table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name, and the two existing ESP subnational rows follow it exactly: ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid). Lugo is not a historical territory with a name and identity of its own independent of its status as a Spanish province (unlike the bespoke cases ALK-1867-1959, HYD-1724-1948, RYU-1937-1945), so the bespoke-code pattern does not apply here. LU is chosen as the abbreviation because it is the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for Lugo (ES-LU) and reads unambiguously against the sibling CO/MD codes, giving ESP-LU-1833-2025. This also keeps the code independent of the INE province numeric code (27) and the NUTS3 code (ES112), neither of which appears in the sibling codes either.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-source-not-acquired

**The mapspain-ign polygon source is registered but not fetched, and Lugo's cpro=27 feature is unconfirmed**

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, the Spanish province numeric code, under which Lugo is conventionally cpro 27 (its INE province code) -- but the routing decision for ES112 could not confirm this because the source file is marked present_locally=False (the shapefile/geopackage has not been fetched into this repository) and its licence for redistribution has not been checked. Until that fetch happens and the cpro=27 feature is confirmed to be Lugo (and confirmed to match the 1833-2025 province boundary rather than some other vintage), this entry must carry polygon_status: unassigned and no geometry, exactly as ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025 do for the same reason. Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Lugo, distinct from Galicia's other three provinces (A Coruna, Ourense, Pontevedra), before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 territory that became Lugo province has no distinct predecessor polity**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the area now covered by Lugo province was part of the Kingdom of Galicia's older territorial divisions (partidos and the historic Reino de Galicia jurisdiction, plus parts of the ancien-regime Reino de Galicia's seven provincias that predate 1833), which do not correspond cleanly to the modern province boundary and are not modeled as a distinct polity row in this database. Lugo's predecessor list is therefore left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already recorded for ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. Whether pre-1833 Galician administrative divisions should eventually be modeled at all remains an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry.

### oq-canary-islands-departure-check

**Confirm Lugo genuinely has no 1927 Canary Islands-style span departure**

The routing reasoning asserts Lugo 'is not a Canary Islands unit' and therefore uses the default 1833 start with no departure, but this was stated rather than independently checked against a list of which Spanish provinces were split or reorganized in the 1927 Canary Islands provincial division (which created Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas from the single earlier Canarias province). Lugo, as a mainland Galician province untouched by that or any other known 19th/20th-century provincial boundary revision, is very likely a clean 1833-2025 case, but this has not been cross-checked against a canonical list of all Spanish provincial reorganizations since 1833 (there were also boundary adjustments involving Santander/Cantabria and Logrono/La Rioja renamings, though not splits). If such a list surfaces a Lugo-specific departure, start_year or end_year here would need to change alongside the code.
