# Denmark (with Schleswig-Holstein)

## Summary

This row tracks Denmark as a territorial-economic unit from **1800**
(the WHEP database floor) to **1864** (Second Schleswig War)
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The CSV notes:
"Split from DNK-1800-1920. Pre-1864 Denmark including Schleswig-Holstein
(~57086 km2). CShapes-Europe has pre-1864 polygon."

At the 1800 database floor, Denmark was a substantial composite
monarchy including Schleswig-Holstein (ethnically mixed German-Danish
duchies), Iceland, the Faroe Islands, Greenland, and Norway (until the
1814 Treaty of Kiel). The 1864 end-of-row event is the Second Schleswig
War, in which Prussia and Austria stripped Schleswig-Holstein from
Denmark -- a ~33% territorial loss (~57K km² to ~38K km²).

Under the WHEP polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition),
the 1864 split is justified by a substantial metropolitan territorial
change.

- **1800 start** is the database floor. The CSV has `predecessor: NA`.
- **1864 end** is a real territorial event (loss of Schleswig-Holstein).

Predecessor: NA (database floor).
Successor: [dnk-1864-1920](dnk-1864-1920.md) --
post-Schleswig War Denmark, reduced to ~38K km².

## Sourced claims

- **1814: Treaty of Kiel.** Denmark ceded Norway to Sweden (14 January
  1814). Norway entered a personal union with Sweden that lasted until
  1905. This is a massive territorial change within the row -- Norway
  had been part of Denmark since 1380 -- but it is NOT a CSV split.
  See [oq-1814-norway-loss](#oq-1814-norway-loss).
  [biger-1995 §denmark](../sources/biger-1995.md#denmark)
- **1864: Second Schleswig War.** Prussia and Austria defeated Denmark
  in the Second Schleswig War. Denmark lost all of Schleswig-Holstein
  to Prussia. Biger states: "In 1864 Denmark lost the whole of
  Schleswig-Holstein (representing 40% of its territory) to Prussia"
  [biger-1995 §denmark](../sources/biger-1995.md#denmark).
  This is the end-of-row event.
- **Post-WWI partial recovery.** After World War I, "only the
  Danish-populated zones of northern Schleswig reverted to Denmark
  after a referendum; Southern Schleswig joined Germany"
  [biger-1995 §denmark](../sources/biger-1995.md#denmark). This event
  falls in the successor row [dnk-1864-1920](dnk-1864-1920.md).
- **COW state-system membership.** COW lists Denmark (`DNK`,
  `ccode=390`) from 1816-01-01 to 2024-12-31 as a single continuous
  tenure
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates).
  COW makes no split at 1864 or 1920 -- it treats Denmark as
  continuous throughout.

### oq-1814-norway-loss

Denmark lost Norway to Sweden via the Treaty of Kiel (14 January
1814). This is a massive territorial change within the row -- Norway
had been part of Denmark-Norway since 1380. Under a strict reading of
the WHEP territorial-change rule, the 1814 loss of Norway could be a
candidate mid-row split. However, the CSV does not split at 1814, and
the ~57K km² area figure appears to exclude Norway (suggesting the
polygon tracks European metropolitan Denmark + Schleswig-Holstein
only). The relationship between Norway's loss and Denmark's WHEP row
structure needs clarification.
