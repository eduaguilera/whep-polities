# Russian Empire (1856-1905)

## Summary

The current CSV tracks this row as the Russian Empire from
**1856** (Treaty of Paris ending the Crimean War) to **1905**
(Treaty of Portsmouth ending the Russo-Japanese War) [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
Both endpoints correspond to real territorial events, which
makes this the **least rule-vs-CSV conflict** polity page in the
current wiki — the 1856/1905 splits both satisfy the WHEP
polity-definition rule [log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition).

This row is notable for a very different reason: it is the
**last correctly labeled** F228 row before a block of five
subsequent rows mislabeled "USSR" despite predating the USSR's
existence by up to 17 years. This page is the first F228 polity
page in the wiki and serves as a worked example of the Russian
Empire label *before* the anachronism kicks in. See
[log proposal-f228-ussr-anachronism](../log.md#proposal-f228-ussr-anachronism) for the audit of the
downstream mislabeled rows.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `Cliopatria (Russian Empire 1885-1894)`
  per the CSV — a single Cliopatria time-step from the decade
  1885–1894 applied across all 50 years of the row (1856–1905)
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Same single-time-step issue as the Ottoman
  1800–1886 row [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §single-time-step](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#single-time-step). The
  1885–1894 polygon represents the configuration **after** the
  Central Asian conquests (Tashkent 1865, Bukhara 1868, Khiva
  1873, Kokand 1876, Merv 1884) but **before** the Russo-Japanese
  War losses, so it matches the territorial maximum of the row's
  window. For the pre-1884 years of the row (1856–1884), the
  polygon overstates the actual territory by roughly the Central
  Asian conquered territory; the amount is not quantified in the
  current sources.
- **docs/06 confirms** the temporal-coverage problem:
  "Russian Empire: CShapes polygon from 1886 applied across 19th
  century" — at 14–18% coverage the row's polygon is
  under-representing the 19th-century territorial dynamics. This
  is not corrected in the current pipeline and is flagged as
  [oq-polygon-time-step-scaling](#oq-polygon-time-step-scaling).
- **CSV notes field is rich** and worth quoting directly:
  "Russian Empire post-Crimean War. Massive Central Asian
  expansion: Tashkent (1865), Bukhara protectorate (1868), Khiva
  (1873), Kokand (1876), Merv (1884). Far East: Amur/Ussuri
  (1858-60), Sakhalin (1875). ~22M km² by 1900. Russo-Japanese
  War (1904-05) triggers next period." [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Every
  date in this notes field is corroborated by
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11].
- **COW code NA** in the CSV [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv), but **should be 365**.
  COW `statelist2024.csv` records Russia (`RUS`, `ccode=365`)
  with a single continuous tenure from 1816-01-01 to 2024-12-31
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates). Adjacent F228 rows
  (1905+) correctly use `cow_code = 365`. The NA on this row
  and F228-1800-1856 is an omission — likely because COW codes
  were populated from CShapes 2.0 (starts 1886) and not
  backfilled to the Cliopatria-based pre-1886 rows. See
  [log autonomous-3-cow-russia-dates](../log.md#autonomous-3-cow-russia-dates).
- **iso3 RUS** [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The iso3 switches to NA starting at
  F228-1905-1914 (see audit Finding 3 in
  [log proposal-f228-ussr-anachronism](../log.md#proposal-f228-ussr-anachronism)).

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [f228-1800-1856](f228-1800-1856.md) <!-- TODO: page not yet created --> per the CSV. The split is
  the **30 March 1856 Treaty of Paris** ending the Crimean War
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1856](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1856). Territorial
  change was modest (Southern Bessarabia ceded to Moldova,
  ~10,200 km²) plus the demilitarization of the Black Sea (not
  a territorial loss per se but a major regime-of-sovereignty
  change over the Black Sea region). Under the strict WHEP
  territorial-change rule, the split is defensible but marginal
  given the Central Asian conquests in the years immediately
  following dwarf the Bessarabia loss by two orders of magnitude.
  The 1856 split captures the "Crimean War defeat → Alexander II
  reform era" transition, which is a historical watershed even
  if the territorial change is small.
- **Successor:** [f228-1905-1914](f228-1905-1914.md) <!-- TODO: page not yet created; also mislabeled USSR per proposal --> per the CSV. The split is the
  **5 September 1905 Treaty of Portsmouth** ending the
  Russo-Japanese War [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1905](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1905).
  Russia ceded South Sakhalin (~36,000 km²) and the Kwantung
  leased territory (~3,500 km²) to Japan. Real territorial event.
  **Warning**: the successor row `F228-1905-1914` is currently
  mislabeled "USSR (1905-1914)" in the CSV despite the USSR not
  existing until 1922. See audit Finding 1 in
  [log proposal-f228-ussr-anachronism](../log.md#proposal-f228-ussr-anachronism).

## Sourced claims

- **1856-03-30: Treaty of Paris ends Crimean War.** "The treaty
  mandated 'demilitarization of the Black Sea' and forced
  Russia to cede southern Bessarabia to Moldova"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1856](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1856). Start-of-row
  event.
- **1858: Treaty of Aigun.** "Russia acquired the Amur region
  from China through this treaty"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1858](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1858). Major Far East
  territorial gain at the left bank of the Amur River.
- **1860: Treaty of Peking.** "China ceded the Ussuri region to
  Russia, establishing Russian presence on the Pacific coast"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1860](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1860). Extends the 1858
  gains to Vladivostok and the Sea of Japan. Combined 1858+1860
  added roughly 1 million km² to the Russian Empire.
- **1861: Emancipation Reform.** Alexander II freed
  "approximately 23 million serfs through imperial decree"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1861](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1861). Not a territorial
  event but the defining domestic reform of the period.
- **1865: Conquest of Tashkent** — "Russian forces captured the
  Central Asian city of Tashkent, marking the beginning of
  systematic conquest of the khanates"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1865](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1865). Start of ~20
  years of Central Asian expansion.
- **1867: Alaska sale.** "Russia sold Alaska to the United
  States for $7.2 million, relinquishing North American
  colonial holdings"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1867](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1867). ~1.5 million km²
  transferred. Major *reduction* in Russian territorial
  extent — under the WHEP rule a substantial-change event.
  Candidate for a mid-row split under a strict reading, but
  not a current split point in the CSV.
- **1868: Conquest of Bukhara.** "Russian military forces
  subjugated the Khanate of Bukhara"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1868](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1868). Became a Russian
  protectorate, not directly annexed.
- **1873: Conquest of Khiva.** Russian conquest; became a
  protectorate [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1873](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1873).
- **1875: Treaty of Saint Petersburg.** "Russia exchanged the
  Kuril Islands for control of Sakhalin Island"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1875](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1875). Minor territorial
  rearrangement with Japan.
- **1876: Conquest of Kokand.** Annexed directly (unlike
  Bukhara and Khiva) [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1876](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1876).
- **1878: Congress of Berlin.** "Russia gained southern
  Bessarabia recovery but faced constraints on further expansion"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1878](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1878). Russia recovered
  the Bessarabia lost in 1856 (a territorial gain) but was
  forced to accept a reduced version of the San Stefano treaty
  gains from the Russo-Turkish War of 1877-78. Same event as
  referenced on [ott-1886-1908](ott-1886-1908.md) for the Ottoman side. Worth
  noting: the 1856 → 1878 → 1905 sequence of Russian Empire
  territorial events does not map onto a clean set of WHEP
  splits; 1878 is not a split point in the CSV despite being a
  real gain-and-loss event.
- **1884: Conquest of Merv.** End of the Central Asian conquest
  campaign [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1884](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1884).
- **1897: Imperial Census.** Population 125,640,021
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1897](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1897). Useful for trade
  denominators.
- **1904-1905: Russo-Japanese War.**
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1904-1905](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1904-1905). Military
  defeat leading to the end-of-row Portsmouth treaty.
- **1905-09-05: Treaty of Portsmouth.** "Russia ceded southern
  Sakhalin Island and the Kwantung leased territory to Japan"
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1905](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1905). ~40,000 km²
  total transferred. End-of-row event.
- **1905: Revolution and October Manifesto.** Not a territorial
  event but the defining political moment of 1905 — the State
  Duma was authorized, converting the Russian Empire to a
  constitutional monarchy. Under the WHEP rule this is a
  regime change, not a split trigger
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11 §1905-revolution](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md#1905-revolution). The
  CSV's 1905 split is therefore justified by the Portsmouth
  territorial event, not by the Revolution per se.

## Open questions

### oq-biger-russia

Biger 1995's RUSSIA main entry was not read in this ingest
because the page range needed would have cost 15–20 more PDF
pages of context. A future iteration should read Biger
RUSSIA and add a `§russia` section to
`wiki/sources/biger-1995.md`, then update this polity page
with Biger citations for the 1856, 1867, 1878, and 1905
events. Biger's Russia entry is likely in the PDF pages
450–470 range (alphabetical; R between Q and S).

### oq-polygon-time-step-scaling

The CShapes/Cliopatria single-time-step polygon issue is well
known (see [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §single-time-step](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#single-time-step) and
docs/06_KNOWN_ISSUES_AND_DECISIONS.md §Russian Empire). For
this row the polygon is from 1885-1894, which overstates the
territory by approximately the Central Asian conquests for the
row's first ~25 years. Quantitative correction would need
per-event km² estimates and a Cliopatria multi-time-step read.
Not load-bearing if the row's polygon is used for post-1884
analyses only; load-bearing for 1856–1884 analyses.

### oq-alaska-sale-split

The 1867 Alaska sale transferred ~1.5 million km² out of the
Russian Empire. Under a strict reading of the WHEP
polity-definition rule, this is a substantial territorial
change that should arguably trigger a mid-row split within
F228-1856-1905. The current CSV does not split at 1867.
Under-policy question: does the WHEP rule require a split for
*any* substantial territorial change, or only for changes that
reshape the identity of the polity? This is a variant of the
general split-rule question also flagged on the Ottoman
[oq-1886-split-is-polygon-not-territory](ott-1800-1886.md#oq-1886-split-is-polygon-not-territory) and the Austria-Hungary
audit.

### oq-cow-russia-early-empire

**Resolved 2026-04-12** by [log autonomous-3-cow-russia-dates](../log.md#autonomous-3-cow-russia-dates).
COW `statelist2024.csv` has a single row for Russia (`RUS`,
`ccode=365`): **1816-01-01 to 2024-12-31** — continuous, no
breaks. The `cow_code = NA` on F228-1800-1856 and
F228-1856-1905 is an omission: later F228 rows (1905+)
correctly use `cow_code = 365`. The NA likely arose because
COW codes were populated from CShapes 2.0 (which starts 1886)
and not backfilled to the Cliopatria-based pre-1886 rows.
The correct COW code for this row is 365.

### oq-1878-berlin-split-candidate

The 1878 Congress of Berlin was a significant territorial event
for Russia (Bessarabia recovery + reduced San Stefano gains).
It is not a split point in the current CSV. Under a strict
reading of the WHEP rule, it might be. Related to the general
split-rule question.
