# Los Ríos (region of Chile)

## Summary

This entry represents the Región de Los Ríos, one of Chile's sixteen first-order administrative regions, from its creation in 2007 to the end of the table's current horizon in 2025 (exclusive). Los Ríos was split off from the Región de Los Lagos by Laws 20174 and 20175 of 2007, becoming Chile's fifteenth region at the time (the country now has sixteen), with its capital at Valdivia. The entry is typed `subnational` because it is a first-order administrative division of the sovereign Chilean state, not an independent polity: Chile held continuous sovereignty over this territory throughout, and the row exists purely to carry a reporting/administrative unit that a subnational statistical source tabulates separately from the national CHL total. The 2007 start year is not an arbitrary data-driven choice — it is the region's actual date of legal creation, and the country convention for CHL already flags this as one of only two documented departures from the otherwise-uniform 1976 start applied to Chile's other regions, which were created in the Pinochet-era regionalization reform. This entry only covers the post-2007 portion of a longer 1900-2023 data extract; the pre-2007 portion, which predates the region's existence, is deliberately left unrouted (see open questions) rather than being absorbed into this entry or the national total.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** A LatAm Subnational-type source reports a series labelled "Los Rios" for Chile spanning 1900-2023, presumably a run of production/economic statistics tabulated at the modern regional level. This entry only claims the 2007-2025 portion of that span.

**Prior mismatch:** Before this decision, the natural fallback was to route all "Los Rios" rows to the national CHL polity (CHL-1902-2025), which is wrong for two independent reasons: first, it would conflate a subnational administrative reporting unit with a national aggregate, discarding the regional signal the source deliberately captured; second, even setting that aside, for 1900-2006 there simply is no "Los Ríos" in any administrative sense to route to, since the region did not exist — the source's own historical rows for that period necessarily use modern regional definitions applied retroactively, a labelling practice common in LatAm subnational statistical series but not one that creates the underlying territory earlier than its legal creation.

**Confirmation of distinctness:** The Chilean state's own enabling legislation, Laws 20174 and 20175 (2007), is the authoritative confirmation that Los Ríos is a legally distinct administrative region, not a re-labelling of Los Lagos — it was carved out as new territory with its own governor, regional government, and capital (Valdivia), formally the fifteenth of what were then thirteen (now sixteen) Chilean regions. The country convention document already treats this 2007 creation as one of only two explicit exceptions to the uniform 1976 start date used for Chile's other regions, which independently corroborates that Los Ríos is administratively distinct from the older regionalization scheme rather than a cosmetic subdivision.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. `polygon_source: none`, `polygon_status: unassigned`. The routing decision's polygon_route was `new_source_needed`: no registered source carries Chile's first-order regions. `cshapes-2.0` only reaches country level; the locally-held `gadm-4.1-adm1` file is a curated 81-country subset that omits Chile entirely (0 matching features), even though a global GADM release almost certainly digitises this exact boundary; `cliopatria`, `paine`, and `histogis` do not cover modern Chilean subdivisions; and `constructed` holds only a single national CHL union, not its regional breakdown. A correct fix is to register a global GADM adm1 release, geoBoundaries ADM1, or official Chilean BCN/IDE cartography as a new source — see the open question on this.

**Territory description:** Los Ríos region occupies south-central Chile, between Región de Los Lagos to its south and Región de la Araucanía to its north, fronting the Pacific to the west and the Argentine border (Neuquén/Río Negro provinces) to the east. Its capital and largest city is Valdivia; other notable towns include La Unión and Río Bueno. The region comprises two provinces, Valdivia and Ranco, formed from twelve communes carved out of what had been the northern part of the old Región de Los Lagos (specifically the former Valdivia province). Modern published figures put the region's area at approximately 18,400 km², making it one of Chile's smaller regions — for scale, roughly the size of Slovenia or Kuwait. No area figure is attached to this entry's geometry (there is none), so this km² comes from external published regional statistics, not from a measured polygon.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-span

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern; span starts at creation, not at the data**

Used CHL-LR-2007-2025, matching the 57-row dominant pattern (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end) rather than a bespoke code, because Los Ríos is an ordinary administrative region with no name or identity independent of its status as a Chilean region — it is not a historical territory like Alaska or Ryukyu. The start year is 2007, not 1900 where the source data begins, because Los Ríos did not exist as a unit before Laws 20174/20175 split it from Región de Los Lagos. The country convention for CHL already documents 2007 as an explicit departure from the default 1976 regionalization start, so this entry implements a decision the convention had already made, not a new judgment call. The 1900-2006 portion of the source data is left unrouted per the routing decision; no polity is created here to cover it, and that gap is recorded as an open question rather than silently absorbed into this entry or the national CHL row.

### d-polygon-none-not-unfetched

**polygon_source is none, not a route name, because no registered source carries this feature**

The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed, which per the authoring instructions must resolve to polygon_source: none with polygon_status: unassigned, and the desired source recorded as an open question rather than written into polygon_source or polygon_feature_id. Here, gadm-4.1-adm1 (the natural home for a modern first-order Chilean region) is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Chile at all, so there is nothing registered to point at, fetched or not — a genuinely new source (global GADM release, geoBoundaries, or Chilean BCN/IDE cartography) would need to be registered first.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-2007-data-unrouted

**What happens to the 1900-2006 'Los Rios' data that predates the region's creation?**

The source extract runs 1900-2023 under the label 'Los Rios', but the region was only created in 2007 (Laws 20174/20175), splitting it off from Región de Los Lagos, which itself descends from older Valdivia-province structures. No existing CHL polity represents exactly this territory at the right subnational granularity for 1900-2006, so that segment remains unrouted rather than being folded into either a fabricated pre-2007 Los Ríos polity or the national CHL total (which would conflate a subnational reporting unit with a national aggregate, a call the not_a_territory policy already forbids). Someone should check whether the LatAm Subnational source is retrofitting modern regional boundaries onto historical statistics before deciding whether a distinct pre-2007 polity (e.g., a Valdivia-province entry) is warranted, or whether the pre-2007 rows should instead route to whatever polity already represents Los Lagos/Valdivia for those years.

### oq-polygon-source-to-register

**Which polygon source should be registered to cover Chile's 16 current regions?**

No registered source in sources.yaml carries Chilean first-order administrative boundaries: cshapes-2.0 is country-level only, gadm-4.1-adm1's local file is curated to 81 countries and excludes Chile entirely (0 matching features), cliopatria/paine/histogis don't cover this granularity, and constructed only has a single CHL national union. A correct fix likely means registering either a global GADM adm1 release, geoBoundaries ADM1, or official Chilean cartography (BCN/IDE) as a new source, then assigning CHL-LR-2007-2025 (and presumably its 15 sibling regions) from it. Until that happens this entry's polygon_status stays unassigned and no area figure exists to compare against any stated-area evidence.

### oq-boundary-stability-since-2007

**Has the Los Ríos regional boundary itself changed since 2007, or only its administrative status?**

The routing decision asserts 'low vintage risk' because the region's boundary has been stable since 2007, but this has not been independently verified against a gazetteer or the enabling legislation's actual province/commune list. If any commune has moved in or out of the region since 2007 (redistricting is not unheard of in Chile's newer regions), a present-day boundary source would not be an accurate vintage match for the full 2007-2025 span and the container edge's basis would need qualification.
