## Summary

The French colony of Middle Congo (Moyen-Congo) in its reduced configuration from 30 March 1912 to 27 June 1919, during the reorganisation of French Equatorial Africa. "The Congolese gained their independence from the French in 1960" [biger-1995 §congo](../sources/biger-1995.md#congo). CShapes records the area as 21.35 sq deg for this period, substantially smaller than both the preceding phase (COG-1906-1912 at 27.97 sq deg) and the succeeding phase (COG-1919-1960 at 27.97 sq deg). The reduction likely reflects the temporary merger of Middle Congo with parts of French Equatorial Africa during the reorganisation period, or the cession of territories following the Agadir Crisis (1911) when France ceded part of the French Congo to Germany in exchange for German recognition of the French protectorate in Morocco. The restoration of territory in 1919 follows the end of World War I and the redistribution of German Kamerun. This is a WHEP-distinct row because both endpoints represent genuine area changes.

## Open questions

### oq-cog-1912-agadir-cession

The area reduction at 1912-03-30 likely relates to the Agadir Crisis resolution: France ceded approximately 275,000 km² of French Congo to Germany in the Franco-German Convention of 4 November 1911 (Treaty of Fez and the adjacent convention). CShapes dates the effective boundary change as 30 March 1912, possibly reflecting the treaty ratification or implementation date. A primary or secondary source should confirm.

### oq-cog-1919-restoration-mechanism

The exact mechanism by which the Congo territory was restored to 27.97 sq deg on 28 June 1919 should be documented. This likely corresponds to the Versailles Treaty redistribution of former German Kamerun, but the specific articles or boundary documents should be cited.
