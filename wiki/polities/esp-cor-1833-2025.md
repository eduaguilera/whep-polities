# Córdoba (province of Spain)

## Summary

Córdoba is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created in the single administrative stroke of the Javier de Burgos territorial reform of 30 November 1833, and since 1833 has occupied the same swath of the middle Guadalquivir valley in inland Andalusia. This entry is type: subnational, not national, because sovereignty over this territory belongs to Spain throughout 1833-2025; the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography below the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, on the same basis already established for the sibling NUTS3 rows ESP-AS-1833-2025 (Asturias), ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) -- Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Córdoba separately from the national total, as NUTS3 code ES613, for as long as such series have existed. Since 1978 the province has also been the constituent unit of the Andalusia autonomous community together with seven other Andalusian provinces, but that is a later administrative layer above the 1833 province, not a redefinition of it: the province boundary set in 1833 is the one this entry carries end to end.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision for unit_id ESP-ES613 captures valued statistical rows for the NUTS3 unit ES613 ("Córdoba"), country Spain, under the same country convention already fixed for the sibling Spanish NUTS3 units from ES111 (A Coruña) through ES243, all defaulting to the 1833 Javier de Burgos start year because Córdoba is not one of the two Canary Islands units that depart from it (Las Palmas and Santa Cruz de Tenerife, which start 1927), and to an open end_year of 2025 because the unit is still current. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states plainly that no existing candidate polity represents Córdoba specifically, since the only iso3=ESP rows before this decision were A Coruña, Madrid, and the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, none of which is Córdoba; absent this entry, Córdoba's province-level series would have had to collapse into the roughly 505,000 km2 national total, discarding the sub-national resolution the source data actually carries. What confirms distinctness: Córdoba's continuous existence as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit and an INE province-level statistical division since 1833 is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the sibling rows ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruña) and ESP-MD-1833-2025 -- separately and continuously tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national row it would otherwise have been folded into.

Its capital and largest city is Córdoba, on the Guadalquivir, with Lucena, Puente Genil and Cabra as its next largest municipalities. On a modern map its outline is the same as the province used both for the 1833 administrative division and, since 1978, as one of the eight constituent provinces of the Andalusia autonomous community. ## Predecessors and successors

Córdoba has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the same broad territory was administered under Antiguo Régimen jurisdictions (including the old Kingdom of Córdoba, one of the historic Reinos of Andalusia) whose administrative form and internal boundaries do not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province, and this database does not model that pre-reform structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment already given to ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CO-1833-2025 and ESP-MD-1833-2025. It has no successor either: Córdoba remains a current, active INE province and Eurostat NUTS3 unit as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), capped at the containing national row's own end_year rather than at any other date.

## Decisions

### d-code-cor-not-co

**Minted ESP-COR-1833-2025 rather than the more obvious ESP-CO-1833-2025, because CO is already taken**

The dominant ESP subnational pattern abbreviates the province name to two letters (ESP-AS for Asturias, ESP-MD for Madrid), and Córdoba's most natural two-letter abbreviation under that rule would also be CO. But ESP-CO-1833-2025 already exists in this database as A Coruña, so using CO for Córdoba would silently collide two distinct provinces onto one code. This is exactly the situation that already produced ESP-CDZ-1833-2025 for Cádiz, whose natural two-letter form CA was unavailable because ESP-CA-1833-2025 already denotes Cantabria: when the short form collides with an existing row, the house practice is to lengthen the abbreviation rather than overload the code. Following that precedent, this entry uses the three-letter COR (the first three letters of Córdoba) rather than CO, keeping the sibling set collision-free without introducing a numeric or otherwise inconsistent code for this one province. Note this creates a naming asymmetry worth flagging in open questions: the routing decision's own polygon_detail independently uses the string "CO" as Córdoba's mapspain-ign feature_id, which is not this entry's polity_code and should not be confused with it.

### oq-pre-1833-kingdom-of-cordoba-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Kingdom of Córdoba and its Antiguo Régimen boundary are not modeled as a predecessor**

Before the 1833 reform, the same broad area was organized as the historic Kingdom of Córdoba, one of the four historic Reinos into which Antiguo Régimen Andalusia was divided, with a jurisdictional boundary that does not necessarily match the 1833 province's limits even though the name and rough territory persisted across the transition. This database leaves predecessor empty for this entry rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment already given to the sibling ESP rows. Whether any pre-1833 Andalusian kingdom-level jurisdiction should eventually be modeled as its own polity (as the bespoke ALK/HYD/RYU codes model other historically-named territories) is a live question for the ESP country convention generally, and Córdoba -- like Asturias -- carries a well-documented historic name of its own that a future author might mistake for grounds to add a predecessor row without first checking whether that bespoke-code precedent should apply instead of a blank predecessor list.
