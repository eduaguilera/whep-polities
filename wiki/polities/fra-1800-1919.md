# France (to 1919)

## Summary

This row tracks France as a territorial-economic unit from **1800**
(the WHEP database floor) to **1919** (Treaty of Versailles)
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The 120-year window spans six regime changes — First
Empire, Bourbon Restoration, July Monarchy, Second Republic,
Second Empire, Third Republic — none of which trigger a WHEP split
because the WHEP polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition) splits on substantial
territorial change, not regime change.

- **1800 start** is the database floor, not a territorial event.
  At 1800 France was the French Republic (Consulate period under
  Napoleon Bonaparte).
- **1919 end** is a real territorial event: the Treaty of
  Versailles (signed 28 June 1919) returned Alsace-Lorraine to
  France [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1919-versailles](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1919-versailles). CShapes
  records the polygon change at exactly 1919-06-28, with an area
  increase of ~1.92 sq degrees [cshapes-2.0 §scope](cshapes-2.0.md#scope).

The two largest mid-row territorial changes — the 1860 Nice/Savoy
gain (8,261 km²) and the 1871 Alsace-Lorraine loss — are not
CSV splits. Under a strict reading of the WHEP rule, both are
candidate split points; see open questions below.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0 + CShapes-Europe` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). CShapes records France (`cowcode=220`) from
  1886-01-01 to 1919-06-27 with area ~62.43 sq degrees (post-1871,
  without Alsace-Lorraine). At 1919-06-28 the area increases to
  ~64.35 sq degrees (Alsace-Lorraine returned) [cshapes-2.0 §scope](cshapes-2.0.md#scope).
- **Pre-1886 extent.** CShapes does not cover before 1886. The
  CSV notes "CShapes-Europe" as a supplementary polygon source
  for the pre-1886 window, but the specific polygon is not
  documented [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- **1860 boundary change.** Nice and Savoy ceded by Piedmont to
  France in March 1860, adding 8,261 km²
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1860-nice-savoy](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1860-nice-savoy). The CShapes
  polygon for this row starts in 1886, after the 1860 gain, so
  it already includes Nice/Savoy.
- **1871 boundary change.** The Treaty of Frankfurt (10 May 1871)
  ceded Alsace and parts of Lorraine to Germany
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1871-alsace-lorraine](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1871-alsace-lorraine). The CShapes
  polygon for 1886–1919 reflects this loss (62.43 sq degrees, vs
  64.35 after the 1919 return).

## Sourced claims

- **1815: Congress of Vienna.** France was "restored to its 1792
  boundaries" after the Napoleonic Wars
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1815](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1815). All Napoleonic European
  conquests were lost.
- **1830: French invasion of Algeria.** "In 1830, France invaded
  Algeria, and in 1848 this north African country was fully
  integrated into France as a department"
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1830-algeria](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1830-algeria). Algeria was "fully
  conquered by 1903." WHEP tracks Algeria as a separate row
  (`ALG-1830-1902` and successors) [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- **1860 March: Nice and Savoy annexed.** "Piedmont ceded its
  provinces of Nice and Savoy to France in March 1860"
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1860-nice-savoy](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1860-nice-savoy). 8,261 km²
  gained. This is a substantial territorial change within the
  row; see [oq-1860-nice-savoy-split-candidate](#oq-1860-nice-savoy-split-candidate).
- **1871-05-10: Treaty of Frankfurt.** France lost Alsace and
  portions of Lorraine to Germany after the Franco-Prussian War
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1871-alsace-lorraine](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1871-alsace-lorraine). This is
  the largest territorial loss in the row and a candidate
  mid-row split; see [oq-1871-alsace-lorraine-split-candidate](#oq-1871-alsace-lorraine-split-candidate).
- **1881: Tunisia protectorate.** French protectorate of Tunisia
  from 1881; troops deployed 24 April 1881; Treaty of Bardo
  signed 12 May 1881 [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1881-tunisia](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1881-tunisia).
  Cross-referenced by [biger-1995 §tunisia](biger-1995.md#tunisia). WHEP tracks Tunisia
  separately.
- **1919-06-28: Treaty of Versailles.** Alsace-Lorraine returned
  to France. CShapes polygon change at this exact date
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §1919-versailles](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#1919-versailles). This is the
  CSV's end-of-row event.
- **Regime transitions.** Six regimes across 120 years: First
  Empire (1804–1814), Bourbon Restoration (1814–1830), July
  Monarchy (1830–1848), Second Republic (1848–1852), Second
  Empire (1852–1870), Third Republic (1870–)
  [wikipedia-france-2026-04-12 §regimes](wikipedia-france-2026-04-12.md#regimes). None triggers a WHEP
  split under the territorial-change rule.
- **COW state-system membership.** COW lists France (`FRN`,
  `ccode=220`) from 1816-01-01 to 1942-11-11, then 1944-08-25
  onward [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates). The COW break at
  1942–1944 (Vichy occupation) falls outside this WHEP row. COW
  makes no split at 1860, 1871, or 1919.

## Open questions

### oq-biger-france

Biger 1995's FRANCE main entry (estimated pages ~200–220 by
alphabetical position) was not read in this ingest. A future
iteration should add a `§france` section to
`wiki/sources/biger-1995.md` covering FRANCE main entry plus
FRANCE–GERMANY (for Alsace-Lorraine), FRANCE–ITALY (for
Nice/Savoy), and FRANCE–SPAIN. Biger will likely give exact
Treaty of Frankfurt text and km² figures for Alsace-Lorraine.

### oq-1871-alsace-lorraine-split-candidate

The 1871 loss of Alsace-Lorraine via the Treaty of Frankfurt is
a substantial territorial change. Under a strict reading of the
WHEP polity-definition rule, it is a candidate mid-row split:
- Real territorial event (Treaty of Frankfurt, 10 May 1871)
- Significant area (~14,500 km² estimated, ~2.7% of
  metropolitan France)
- The CShapes polygon reflects the change (62.43 sq degrees
  post-1871 vs 64.35 post-1919)

The CSV does not split at 1871. The 1919 split (where
Alsace-Lorraine returns) is a split, but the 1871 loss is not.
This asymmetry is unexplained — no docs/ entry or log entry
provides a rationale. This is a Tier X question requiring a
human decision on split policy.

### oq-1860-nice-savoy-split-candidate

The March 1860 cession of Nice and Savoy from Piedmont to France
(8,261 km² per docs/03) is another candidate mid-row split under
the WHEP territorial-change rule. The gain is smaller than the
1871 Alsace-Lorraine loss but larger than some existing split
triggers in the database (e.g. the 1908 Bosnia annexation, which
was a de jure formalization of a de facto situation). Not split
in the CSV. No documented rationale found.

### oq-notes-na

Both FRA-1800-1919 and FRA-1919-2025 have `notes = NA` in the
CSV despite France being one of the most important and
best-documented European states. The Russian Empire rows have
substantive notes; the Ottoman rows have substantive notes.
France has none. `verification_status = REGION` on both rows
(not VERIFIED). Low priority but worth flagging.

### oq-pre-1886-polygon-source

The CSV notes "CShapes-Europe" as a supplementary polygon source
for the pre-1886 window, but the specific polygon and its
provenance are not documented. The CShapes paper covers only
1886 onward. What is "CShapes-Europe" and how does it provide
pre-1886 France polygons? A future ingest should examine the
CShapes-Europe dataset or documentation.
