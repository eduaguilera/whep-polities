# Queensland (to 1900)

## Summary

The Colony of Queensland from its separation from New South Wales on 6 June 1859 to the eve of Australian federation. Queensland was formally established when Queen Victoria signed Letters Patent on 6 June 1859 creating the colony from the northern portion of NSW. The WHEP row ends on 31 December 1900 before the 1 January 1901 federation.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from CShapes 2.0 (dependency-level). Queensland included what is now the state of Queensland; territorial adjustments in the 1860s–1880s included the Torres Strait Islands annexation (1872, 1879) and the Queensland-administered British New Guinea protectorate (1883–1902) — the latter is not part of the modern state but fell under Queensland's administrative remit for part of this window. The CSV polygon likely reflects the core Queensland territory, not the dependent protectorates.

### d-end-year-1901-not-1900

**The colony existed through all of 1900, so its exclusive `end_year` is 1901**

The Commonwealth of Australia was proclaimed on 1 January 1901, so this colony existed for every day of 1900 and ceased on the last of it. With `end_year` exclusive that is 1901, and the row previously said 1900 -- covering only to the end of 1899 and leaving 1900 with no colony at all.

The error was visible inside this table before it was visible in the data: the aggregate row [AUS-1800-1901](aus-1800-1901.md) already ends in 1901 and the successor states all begin in 1901, so the six colonies were the only rows dating federation to 1900. What surfaced it was the panel -- six Australian units report 1900 and those rows resolved to nothing, because the colony alias stopped at 1899 and the state alias began at 1901. Corrected 2026-09-17.

## Open questions

### oq-que-png-protectorate

Queensland's administrative responsibility for British New Guinea (1883–1902) is not captured in the polygon. Whether WHEP should record BNG as a separate subnational row is an open modelling question — currently AUS-1901-2025's 1906 PNG transfer (noted in the AUS page) handles it on the successor side.
