# Bemba Kingdom

## Summary

The Bemba Kingdom was a pre-colonial centralised chieftaincy in the northeastern plateau of modern Zambia, led by the Chitimukulu paramount chief. Founded c. 17th century, the Bemba polity was one of the most powerful kingdoms in the Central African interior. The database tracks it from 1800 (the WHEP window floor) until 1899, when the British South Africa Company (BSAC) established administrative control. Biger notes that "the central part of Africa remained free of European occupation until the 1880s" — the Bemba were among the last to succumb, holding independence through the 1890s. The polygon source is Paine et al. (2024).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Paine et al. (2024). The Bemba heartland was the high plateau of northeastern Zambia (Bembaland), centred on the upper Chambeshi River basin. The kingdom exercised suzerainty over a wide surrounding area through tribute-taking and slave-raiding networks extending into what is now the DRC and Tanzania.

## Open questions

### oq-bsac-takeover-date

The end year 1899 is given in the CSV as the BSAC takeover, but the Bemba did not fully submit until after several armed confrontations in 1898–1899. The exact date of effective BSAC control needs sourcing from a specialist study of BSAC administration in Northern Rhodesia.

### oq-deeper-sourcing

Biger covers Zambia only at the independence-era level and does not discuss pre-colonial kingdoms. A specialist source (e.g., Roberts 1973 *A History of the Bemba*) is needed to source boundary and political claims.

### oq-chain-gap

The WHEP chain jumps from BMB-1800-1899 directly to ZMB-1964-2025 (Zambia). The BSAC administration (1899–1924) and Northern Rhodesia colonial period (1924–1964) are not represented as separate WHEP rows, creating a 65-year chain gap. This may be a coverage gap to flag for a future ingest.
