# China (1913-1914)

## Summary

A **2-year transitional row** covering the early Republic of China
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW code 710
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The Republic of
China was proclaimed 1 January 1912; Yuan Shikai became president
in March 1912. This short row likely corresponds to a CShapes
polygon change rather than a major territorial event.

Predecessor per CSV: [chn-1895-1913](chn-1895-1913.md) (the
frontmatter's `CHN-1895-1913` code does not exist -- see
[oq-predecessor-code-mismatch](#oq-predecessor-code-mismatch)).
Successor per CSV: [chn-1914-1921](chn-1914-1921.md).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0`, feature id 710 (COW ccode), pinned
  to CShapes time-step `1913` [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
  CShapes traces borders daily from 1886 onward and this row's
  time-step is one of "249 political units ... represented by 476
  polygons over time"
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope).
- **Measured area.** Directly measured from the committed
  `data/final/polities_database.gpkg` (reprojected to the equal-area
  CRS `ESRI:54034`): this row's polygon is **9,224,461 km²**. The
  immediate predecessor row `CHN-1895-1913` (CShapes time-step 1899)
  measures **11,110,680 km²**, and the immediate successor row
  `CHN-1914-1921` (CShapes time-step 1914) measures **9,059,518 km²**.
  Both neighboring differences are far above CShapes' own **100×100 km
  (10,000 km²) size threshold** for what counts as a coded border
  adjustment
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes),
  so per the wiki's own rule ("different area = territorial change =
  new row") both the 1913 start-split and the 1914 end-split are
  polygon-backed, not arbitrary.
- **The 1899→1913 predecessor-side drop (~1,886,219 km²) predates
  this row's span** (it is already baked into the CShapes 1913
  snapshot used here) and in magnitude is consistent with Outer
  Mongolia's secession -- the Bogd Khan's December 1911 declaration
  of independence covered roughly 1.5 million km² of former Qing
  territory. This is uncited general history: no source ingested
  into `wiki/sources/` documents the Mongolian declaration or its
  area; it is stated here plainly as unsupported by an ingested
  citation, not dressed up as sourced.
- **The 1913→1914 drop inside this row's own span (~164,943 km²) is
  the change that explains the successor split.** In magnitude this
  closely matches Tannu Uriankhai (Tuva), a roughly 170,000 km²
  region administered by China as part of Outer Mongolia since 1758,
  which passed to a Russian protectorate in April 1914 following the
  collapse of Qing/Republican authority there. Again, this is
  uncited general history -- no ingested source in `wiki/sources/`
  covers Tuva -- but the date (April 1914) and area both line up with
  the measured CShapes polygon change far better than the previous
  guess on this page (Japan's occupation of the German Kiaochow Bay
  leasehold in Shandong, November 1914, which was only ~552 km² --
  two orders of magnitude too small to account for the measured
  drop).
- CShapes also has an explicit **wartime-change rule**: changes made
  during a war are excluded unless "made permanent in treaties signed
  after the war," in which case the date of the postwar agreement is
  used as the coded change date
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes).
  Tuva's 1914 protectorate was not a wartime cession and was not
  reversed by a later treaty in this window, so it does not run into
  that exclusion -- but this is inference from the general coding
  rule, not a China-specific statement in the source.
- `cow-state-system-v2024` is listed as a source for this row but its
  ingested `§key-dates` content only covers Luxembourg, Canada, Cuba,
  and Haiti; it has no China-specific membership dates to cite here.

## Open questions

### oq-1914-split-event

Measured polygon areas (see *Territorial extent*) rule out the
originally-guessed Shandong/Kiaochow explanation (too small by two
orders of magnitude) and point instead to Tannu Uriankhai/Tuva's
April 1914 transfer to Russian-protectorate status (~164,943 km²
measured drop, matching Tuva's ~170,000 km² in the right direction
and roughly the right month). This is still uncited general history
-- no source in `wiki/sources/` documents Tuva or the CShapes 1914
coding-change record for this specific transfer. Needs verification
against the CShapes changelog data itself (not just the paper) or a
Central Asia/Mongolia-focused historical source before it can be
promoted from "uncited general history" to a sourced claim.

### oq-predecessor-code-mismatch

This page's frontmatter lists `predecessor: [CHN-1895-1913]`, but no

page -- the actual predecessor row in the CSV is `CHN-1895-1913`
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv), which does have a
wiki page at [chn-1895-1913](chn-1895-1913.md). The prose sections
above have been corrected to link to the real page, but the
frontmatter itself was left untouched per this ingest's instructions.
This looks like a transcription bug (off-by-one on the year) rather
than a deliberate choice -- no `log.md` decision entry justifies a
`CHN-1895-1913` code. Flagging for a human/lint pass to fix the
frontmatter and re-run `scripts/build_database.py`.
