# Ávila (province of Spain)

## Summary

Ávila is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833, occupying a landlocked stretch of the northern sub-plateau (submeseta norte) on the southern edge of Old Castile. Since 1983 it has also been one of the nine provinces of the Castile and León autonomous community, but unlike Asturias or Madrid its 1833 province boundary is NOT coextensive with any single-province autonomous community -- Castile and León groups Ávila together with Burgos, León, Palencia, Salamanca, Segovia, Soria, Valladolid and Zamora, so the province remains the finer-grained, statistically distinct reporting unit within a much larger regional aggregate. This entry is type: subnational because Ávila is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Ávila separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES411, since the province system's creation.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES411) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES411 ("Ávila"), country Spain, following the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (Asturias/ES120, A Coruña/ES111, Madrid/ES-MD, and onward through the ES1xx-ES4xx series), all defaulting to start_year 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform) because Ávila is not one of the enumerated departure units (only the Canary Islands split differently, in 1927) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing specific -- the routing_reasoning states explicitly that neither ESP-CO (A Coruña, a different province entirely) nor ESP-MD (Madrid, likewise a different province) represents Ávila, and the national row ESP-1800-2025 is a country-level total, not this province; per the rule that match_existing requires the candidate to BE the territory rather than merely contain it, none of the existing rows qualified, so Ávila had no home before this decision beyond the collapsing fallback of the national aggregate. What confirms distinctness: Ávila's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES411) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the seven Spanish provinces routed before it (ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-OR, ESP-PO, plus ESP-BI, ESP-GI, ESP-HU, ESP-NV, ESP-RI, ESP-TE, ESP-VI, ESP-ZA already staged) -- continuous, separately tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the ~505,000 km2 national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, of which Ávila's roughly 8,050 km2 is under 2%.

Territory description: Ávila province occupies the southern rim of Castile and León, on the northern side of the Sistema Central mountain range (Sierra de Gredos, Sierra de Ávila), bordering Salamanca to the west, Valladolid and Segovia to the north and east, Madrid to the east, and Toledo and Cáceres (Extremadura) to the south across the Gredos massif. Its capital and namesake city, Ávila, is a UNESCO World Heritage walled city on the Adaja river. On a modern map the province is the same outline used by INE/Eurostat for NUTS3 code ES411, one of the nine constituent provinces of Castile and León. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 8,048 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform aside from minor municipal-limit adjustments, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired and confirmed, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the other Spanish provinces.

## Predecessors and successors

Ávila has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was organized under the Antiguo Régimen's own partido/corregimiento structure (the historic Comunidad de Villa y Tierra de Ávila and surrounding jurisdictions), a different administrative form than the 1833 province even though the name and rough territorial core persisted; this database does not model that pre-reform structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, matching the treatment given to every other Spanish province entry (ESP-AS, ESP-CO, ESP-MD, etc.). It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and one of nine provinces of the Castile and León autonomous community since 1983) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-av-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation AV rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by the fifteen sibling entries (ESP-AS, ESP-BI, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-GI, ESP-HU, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-NV, ESP-OR, ESP-PO, ESP-RI, ESP-TE, ESP-VI, ESP-ZA) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES411) or the INE province numeric code (05). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-AV-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. AV was chosen because it is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-AV) and the everyday abbreviation for the province, reducing ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row -- and because no other already-routed Spanish province shares the AV prefix.

### d-polygon-left-unassigned

**Left polygon_status as unassigned rather than trusting the routing decision's stale-flag theory**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning argues that sources.yaml's present_locally=False flag for mapspain-ign is 'very likely a stale manifest flag' since seven provinces were already routed through it, and suggests the feature_id ESP-05-1833-2025 could likely be assigned directly. This page does not take that shortcut: inspecting all fifteen already-created Spanish province pages shows every single one shipped with polygon_status: unassigned and polygon_feature_id: null, meaning none of them actually confirmed the file's presence on disk before writing -- the 'likely stale' judgment was never verified in practice, only asserted in the routing note. Following feedback that conventions must be corroborated rather than assumed, this entry mirrors the actual, observed behavior of its precedents (unassigned) rather than the routing decision's unverified optimism, until someone actually fetches mapspain-ign and confirms a cpro=05 feature.

## Open questions

scripts/sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro, under which Ávila is conventionally cpro 05, but no Spanish province entry created so far -- including this one -- has actually confirmed the provinces.gpkg file is present and contains a feature for that code. The routing decision speculates the present_locally=False flag is stale given precedent, but the precedent itself (fifteen unassigned sibling pages) is equally consistent with the flag being accurate and nobody having fetched the file yet. Until someone acquires mapspain-ign, opens it, and confirms there is exactly one clean feature for cpro=05 matching the modern Ávila province outline, this entry must stay polygon_status: unassigned. Whoever does that fetch should also verify all fifteen-plus queued Spanish province features in one pass rather than one at a time.

### oq-pre-1833-territory-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Ávila jurisdiction (Comunidad de Villa y Tierra de Ávila) has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the territory that became Ávila province was organized under Old Castile's own historic jurisdictions, principally the Comunidad de Villa y Tierra de Ávila, an Antiguo Régimen structure whose boundaries do not correspond exactly to the 1833 province even though the city and general area persisted across the transition. This database does not model that earlier structure as a distinct polity row, so predecessor is left empty, mirroring the treatment already recorded for every other Spanish province. Whether any pre-1833 Castilian comunidad/corregimiento should eventually be modeled as its own polity is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, not specific to this entry, but it recurs with every new province page and has not yet been resolved one way or the other.
