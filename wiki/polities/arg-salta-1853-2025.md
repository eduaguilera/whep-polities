# Salta (province of Argentina)

## Summary

Salta is a province in northwestern Argentina, on the eastern slope of the Andes bordering Chile and Bolivia, one of the fourteen historic provinces that ratified the 1853 Constitution and constituted the Argentine Confederation/Republic. It is not a former territorio nacional promoted to province status in the 20th century (unlike Chaco, Formosa, La Pampa, or Chubut), nor a bespoke historical territory with a name of its own distinct from the present-day province (unlike Alaska Territory or the Kingdom of Hawaii). It has held continuous province-level status since 1853, which is why this entry's start_year follows the country's default founding-province convention rather than a later provincialization date; the routing decision explicitly confirmed no evidence of Salta having been administered as a territorio nacional at any point. `type: subnational` is used because sovereignty over Salta sits with the successive national ARG rows this entry is containered under across the full span, not with the province itself; the entry exists purely as a reporting-territory row because statistical sources tabulate Salta separately from national ARG totals, with a routed data span of 1900-2023. Salta's economy and geography are historically distinguished by its Andean puna highlands in the west (home to the historic Los Andes territorio nacional carved from part of its area between 1900 and 1943) and its lower, more temperate valleys and Chaco-adjacent lowlands in the east, a distinguishing feature of the province's statistical footprint relative to the pampas provinces. End_year is fixed at the open-end convention value of 2025 because Salta remains a current, unchanged province of Argentina.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures production/statistical data reported for the Argentine province of Salta (per the routing decision for unit_id ARG-SALTA, admin_name "Salta", country Argentina, data span 1900-2023) under the country convention's default provincial span rule (start 1853, open end 2025). Previously, no subnational polity existed for Salta in this table beyond the national ARG chain (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025); any Salta-labelled data would have been left unmatched or folded into the national ARG total, which is wrong because it conflates one northwestern province's output with the whole country's, discarding the sub-national resolution the source actually provides. That Salta is a distinct, long-standing administrative and statistical reporting unit is confirmed by its status as one of the signatory provinces of the 1853 Constitution and its continuous, unbroken existence as a first-order administrative division of Argentina ever since (setting aside the partial, temporary Los Andes carve-out), unlike the ex-territorios nacionales whose provincial status and spans required separate deliberation. The routing_reasoning explicitly found zero existing candidate polities matching Salta by name and confirmed no departure entry for it in the country convention, so create_new with the default span rule was applied rather than match_existing.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. `polygon_source: none` and `polygon_status: unassigned`, because the routing decision's polygon_route is `new_source_needed`: Salta is an admin1-level unit, and the only registered sources with admin1 granularity are gadm-4.1-adm1 and gadm-3.6. gadm-4.1-adm1's local file present in this repo is explicitly a curated 81-country subset that excludes Argentina entirely (0 features for ARG), and gadm-3.6 is not present locally at all. cshapes-2.0, the source this repo already uses for national ARG rows, only encodes country-level polygons, not provincial subdivisions, and cannot supply Salta's boundary. No registered, locally-present source can produce this feature, so no polygon is attached; the desired future source (a global GADM 4.1 admin-1 fetch, or an Argentina-specific IGN/INDEC source) is recorded as an open question rather than declared here, per the rule that `new_source_needed` cannot itself be written as `polygon_source`.

**Territory description:** Salta province occupies the northwestern corner of Argentina, bordered by Bolivia and Paraguay to the north, Chile to the west across the Andes, Jujuy to the northwest, and the provinces of Formosa, Chaco, Santiago del Estero, Tucumán, and Catamarca along its eastern and southern edges. Its modern area is approximately **155,500 km²** (a figure from general reference geography, not measured from any attached polygon in this repository, since none is attached). The provincial capital is the city of Salta (Salta de la Nueva Orán / Salta as commonly known). The province spans a strong east-west geographic gradient, from the high Andean puna in the west (over 4,000m elevation, including the area once carved out as the Territorio Nacional de Los Andes between 1900 and 1943) to subtropical valleys (the Calchaquí and Lerma valleys) and lowland Chaco-transition plains in the east. Because of the Los Andes episode, Salta's western boundary and territorial extent were not fully stable across the 1853-2025 span in the way most other founding provinces were -- this is flagged as an open question (oq-boundary-vintage-risk) rather than assumed settled, since any future modern polygon proxy would need to account for whether it reflects the pre-1900, 1900-1943 reduced, or post-1943 restored extent.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Used the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> code pattern**

Salta is an ordinary, unbroken Argentine province with no bespoke historical name of its own distinct from the present-day province (unlike Alaska Territory or the Kingdom of Hawaii), so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. Following the dominant pattern used by 338 of 351 subnational rows, and consistent with the precedent already set for other Argentine provinces in this table (ARG-BA-1853-2025, ARG-CORDOBA-1853-2025, ARG-CORRIENTES-1853-2025, ARG-MENDOZA-1853-2025), the code chosen is ARG-SALTA-1853-2025: ISO3, then an uppercase ASCII subunit token (SALTA, no diacritics), then the start and end years, matching the frontmatter years exactly as the code/containment gate requires.

### d-three-container-edges

**Split the container into three edges to tile 1853-2025 with no gap**

The country's national ARG chain (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025) means a 1853-2025 span crosses three national-era container polities. A single container edge referencing only ARG-1902-2025, as the routing decision's 'proposed.container_code' field suggests in isolation, would leave 1853-1902 uncontained and fail the containment gate, since an edge cannot fall outside either party's own span. Instead three edges were declared, matching the pattern already used for ARG-MENDOZA-1853-2025 and ARG-CORDOBA-1853-2025: ARG-1800-1899 for 1853-1899, ARG-1899-1902 for 1899-1902, and ARG-1902-2025 for 1902-2025, each basis explaining that Salta's provincial status and territory did not change across these national-era transitions -- only the identity of the sovereign national row containing it changed.

### d-polygon-source-none-not-new-source-needed

**polygon_source set to none, not the rejected placeholder new_source_needed**

The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed, and its polygon_source field was left blank in the decision input. Per the harness instructions, new_source_needed is a route category, not a slug that validate_declared_sources accepts, so polygon_source is set to the literal none and polygon_status to unassigned. The GADM 4.1 adm1 global dataset that would supply Salta (the repo's local gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg is an 81-country subset that excludes Argentina) is recorded as the desired future source in the open questions below rather than declared as polygon_source now.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-global-fetch-needed

**No registered, locally-present source can supply Salta's boundary yet**

The routing decision found that GADM 4.1's admin-1 layer does digitize Argentine provinces globally, but the copy registered and present in this repo (data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg) is a curated 81-country subset that excludes Argentina entirely (0 features for ARG). Neither cshapes-2.0 (country-level only, no subdivisions) nor gadm-3.6 (not present locally) can substitute. Fetching the full/global GADM 4.1 admin-1 release, or an Argentina-specific source from IGN/INDEC, would be needed before polygon_status can move from unassigned toward assigned; licence status for either has not been checked in this session.

### oq-boundary-vintage-risk

**A modern provincial boundary would need to stand in for the full 1853-2025 span**

Once a GADM or IGN polygon for Salta is fetched, it would be a present-day boundary asked to represent the entire 1853-2025 period. Salta's borders are believed to have been broadly stable since the 19th century as one of the original 1853 provinces, but this has not been verified against a period-accurate historical source -- in particular whether any 19th- or early-20th-century adjustments occurred with neighbouring Jujuy (which separated from Salta as its own province only in 1834, just before this span begins) or with the territorio-nacional-era boundaries of Los Andes (a national territory carved partly from Salta's puna zone between 1900 and 1943, later reincorporated into Salta and Jujuy). That Los Andes history should be checked before treating any future modern polygon as a safe proxy for the earliest decades of this span.

### oq-data-coverage-start-date

**Actual start year of Salta-specific data is unconfirmed against the 1853 structural start**

The routing decision gives no explicit first data year for unit_id ARG-SALTA beyond noting the data span runs 1900-2023 within the 1853-2025 structural window; it does not confirm whether any Salta-labelled statistics exist between 1853 and 1900, or whether that gap is purely a structural/administrative-history convention choice with no corresponding routed data, as was found to be the case for Córdoba (1853-1900 gap) and should be checked here too.

### oq-los-andes-overlap

**Whether the former Territorio Nacional de Los Andes creates any overlap with this row**

Los Andes was split off as a separate national territory covering part of Salta's (and Jujuy's) puna highlands from 1900 to 1943, then dissolved and returned to the two provinces. If any data in this table's sources is labelled specifically to Los Andes rather than to Salta or Jujuy during that 1900-1943 window, it should not be silently absorbed into this ARG-SALTA-1853-2025 row without checking whether a separate Los Andes polity row already exists or is needed, to avoid double-counting or mis-attributing territory between neighbouring provincial rows.
