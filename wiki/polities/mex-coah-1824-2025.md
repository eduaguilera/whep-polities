# Coahuila

## Summary

Coahuila is a state of the Mexican federation, located in northeastern Mexico bordering Texas (USA) along the Rio Grande. It was one of the original states admitted under the 1824 Constitution, initially joined with Texas as the single state "Coahuila y Tejas." This entry spans 1824-2025 (end exclusive, meaning through 2025) because Coahuila has existed continuously as a first-order Mexican administrative division since the federation's founding, even though its exact composition and borders changed twice: Texas separated in 1836 (formalized in Mexican law only after the 1848 war), and Coahuila was merged with Nuevo León from 1856 to 1864 before splitting back into separate states. `type: subnational` is used because Coahuila is a constituent unit of the sovereign Mexican state, not an independent polity, and the national MEX chain already carries Mexico's sovereignty and international relations. This entry exists to carry state-level statistical reporting for Coahuila where the source data is tabulated below the national level, distinct from the aggregate MEX rows which run at country scale.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Mexican agricultural/economic data reported at the Coahuila state level starting around 1900 in the source extract, per the routing decision for unit_id MEX-COAHUILA. Previously, no Coahuila-specific polity existed in the database — only the two national MEX rows (MEX-1800-1848 and MEX-1848-2025) were available, so any Coahuila-labeled data would have had to route to the national row, conflating one state's figures with all-Mexico totals roughly 32 times its size (Coahuila is about 151,595 km2 against Mexico's ~1.96 million km2). Coahuila's status as a distinct first-order administrative unit is confirmed by uninterrupted inclusion as a named state in Mexican federal constitutions since 1824 (with the sole exception of the 1836-1846 Texan secession dispute and the 1856-1864 merger with Nuevo León), and it is one of INEGI's 32 present-day federal entities, each separately enumerated in Mexican national censuses and agricultural statistics back to the late 19th century.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct registered source is `gadm-4.1-adm1` (GADM 4.1 admin-level-1 boundaries), which is the standard route used elsewhere in this repository for state/province-level units, but the locally fetched `gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg` subset covers only 81 countries and does not currently include Mexico (0 features for MEX in the local file). GADM does publish a Mexico ADM1 layer upstream; it has not yet been fetched into the local subset, so no `polygon_feature_id` can be assigned yet — this is the `registered_source_unfetched` case, not a missing-source case.

**Territory description:** Coahuila (officially Coahuila de Zaragoza) occupies the north-central part of northeastern Mexico, bordering the U.S. state of Texas across the Rio Grande to the north, and the Mexican states of Nuevo León, Zacatecas, Durango, and Chihuahua. Its present-day area is approximately 151,595 km2, making it Mexico's third-largest state. A reader can locate it on a modern map as the large, mostly arid state directly south of Big Bend/Eagle Pass, Texas, with its capital at Saltillo. No km2 figure here is derived from an attached polygon, since none is attached yet; the 151,595 km2 figure is the present-day INEGI-published state area, cited as a locating reference only. **Caution:** the pre-1848 portion of this span (1824-1836, and arguably 1824-1848 pending Mexican recognition of the 1836 secession) covers "Coahuila y Tejas," a combined state whose territory included all of present-day Texas plus Coahuila — several times larger than modern Coahuila alone. Any future polygon assignment must not silently apply the modern Coahuila boundary back across the pre-1836/1848 years without flagging this territorial change, exactly as the polygon-proxy rules require for dramatic territory shifts.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity rows exist: this is the first Coahuila-specific entry in the database, and it remains current (end_year 2025, exclusive, per the open-end country convention for MEX). Internally, the territory passed through "Coahuila y Tejas" (1824-1836ish, contested through 1848), plain "Coahuila" after Texan separation, "Coahuila y Nuevo León" during the 1856-1864 merger, and back to standalone "Coahuila" from 1864 onward — these are administrative-name changes within one continuous federal-state lineage, not treated as separate polity rows here, consistent with the proposed decision's single continuous span.

## Sourced claims

- Coahuila was one of the original states admitted to the Mexican federation under the 1824 Constitution, initially united with Texas as "Coahuila y Tejas" until Texas's 1836 secession.
- Coahuila and Nuevo León were merged into a single state, "Coahuila y Nuevo León," from 1856 to 1864 before separating again into the two present-day states.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Used <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> code pattern, not a bespoke code**

Coahuila is a standard first-order Mexican federal state, not a historical territory with its own proper name distinct from its administrative role (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State, which carry bespoke codes because they denote a specific historical regime rather than a generic administrative slot). It therefore follows the dominant 57-of-66 pattern: MEX-COAH-1824-2025, using a 4-letter subunit abbreviation (COAH) to avoid collision with any other Mexican state starting with 'C' (e.g., a future Campeche or Chiapas entry) since no 2-letter ISO 3166-2 code was verified against sibling MEX entries (there are none yet to check against).

### d-start-year-1824-not-data-start

**start_year set to 1824 per country convention, not the data's 1900 start**

The routing decision explicitly invokes a MEX country convention that state spans follow administrative history (federation admission in 1824) rather than the extract's own data range (beginning around 1900). This entry follows that convention, accepting a 76-year span with no underlying data, consistent with how the exemplar MAN-1950-1955 page treats administrative history as authoritative over data availability for span boundaries.

### d-single-span-not-era-split

**Kept a single 1824-2025 span rather than splitting eras for the Texas and Nuevo León mergers**

The routing_concerns field flagged the Coahuila y Tejas (to 1836) and Coahuila y Nuevo León (1856-1864) episodes as possible triggers for era-splitting. This entry does not split, because in both episodes Coahuila's own institutional continuity (as a legislature and administrative seat) persisted through the merger rather than being dissolved and re-created, unlike the case of e.g. Jehol being absorbed into and later carved back out of Manchukuo. If a future audit finds the merged periods were administered with no distinct Coahuila apparatus, this span should be split at 1836 and 1856/1864 instead.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-mexico-adm1-not-fetched

**GADM Mexico ADM1 layer needs to be fetched before any polygon can be assigned**

The local gadm-4.1-adm1.gpkg subset excludes Mexico entirely (0 features), even though GADM publishes Mexico's ADM1 layer upstream. Until that layer is fetched and added to the local source file, this entry cannot receive a polygon_feature_id, and polygon_status must remain unassigned. This blocks not just this entry but any other Mexican state-level polity this pipeline creates.

### oq-pre1848-territory-mismatch

**Whether the pre-1848 span needs its own polygon distinct from modern Coahuila**

From 1824 until Texas's secession was consummated (contested between 1836 and the 1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo), the state was "Coahuila y Tejas," territorially several times larger than modern Coahuila. Once a GADM Mexico ADM1 polygon is fetched, it will reflect only present-day Coahuila's boundary, and applying it back across 1824-1848 (or even 1824-1836) would overstate how much territory equals how little reported area — this needs an explicit proxy-rejection note or a bespoke pre-1836 geometry once boundary data is available.

### oq-nuevo-leon-merger-territory

**Whether the 1856-1864 Coahuila y Nuevo León merger years should carry a different territory or be split into a separate row**

During this eight-year window, Coahuila's statistical/administrative territory may have been reported jointly with Nuevo León rather than separately. If source data from this period exists and is routed here, it should be checked against whether it actually reports combined Coahuila-Nuevo León figures, which would make this entry's territory claim for those years incorrect as stated.

### oq-subunit-code-collision

**No sibling MEX subnational entries exist yet to confirm the COAH abbreviation convention**

This is the first MEX subnational row, so there is no existing convention to check the 4-letter 'COAH' subunit code against. If a future Mexican state entry (e.g., a different state also starting with C) is created with a conflicting or differently-styled code, this entry's code may need reconciliation for consistency across the MEX subnational set.
