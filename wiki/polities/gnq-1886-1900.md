# Equatorial Guinea (1886-1900)

## Summary

Spanish Guinea in its initial CShapes-covered configuration from 1 January 1886 to 26 June 1900, before the boundary was definitively fixed by the Franco-Spanish Convention of 27 June 1900. "The Franco-Spanish Convention of 27 June 1900 ... Spanish Guinea became the independent republic of Equatorial Guinea on 12 October 1968" [biger-1995 §equatorial-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#equatorial-guinea). "The boundary was formulated in the Franco-German agreements of 1885 and 1894 that outlined the European powers' spheres of influence in Africa" [biger-1995 §equatorial-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#equatorial-guinea). CShapes records area 2.19 sq deg throughout 1886–1968 with no change at the 1900 step (both steps have identical area), indicating the 1900 event is an administrative/legal change (treaty ratification) without a measurable territorial change at the CShapes resolution. This is nonetheless a WHEP-distinct row from the CSV because the polity ends at 1900 — the question of whether GNQ-1886-1900 and GNQ-1900-1968 should be merged is addressed in Open questions.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from CShapes 2.0 (dependencies=TRUE). CShapes records area 2.19 sq deg for both steps (1886-01-01 to 1900-06-26, and 1900-06-27 to 1968-10-11), status=colony, owner=Spain (COW 230). Equatorial Guinea is bounded by Cameroon (118 mi; 189 km) and Gabon [biger-1995 §equatorial-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#equatorial-guinea). The territory comprises the mainland enclave of Río Muni (between Cameroon and Gabon) and the islands of Bioko (formerly Fernando Poo), Annobón, and the Corisco group. "The French established themselves on the coast of central Africa in the late nineteenth century and Cameroon was established as a German protectorate (Kamerun) on 12 July 1884. France and Germany first established a boundary between their spheres of influence in central Africa on 24 December 1885" [biger-1995 §equatorial-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#equatorial-guinea).

## Contradictions

CShapes records no area change between GNQ-1886-1900 and GNQ-1900-1968 (both 2.19 sq deg). Under strict WHEP methodology (same area = same polity row), these two rows should be merged into a single GNQ-1886-1968 row. The existing CSV split at 1900 is plausible as a legal-boundary-codification event but is not supported by a territorial change. This should be reviewed — see [oq-gnq-merge-candidate](#oq-gnq-merge-candidate).

## Open questions

### oq-gnq-merge-candidate

The CShapes area is identical for GNQ-1886-1900 and GNQ-1900-1968 (both 2.19 sq deg). WHEP policy is to merge same-area continuous steps into a single row. The 1900 Franco-Spanish Convention is a significant legal event but does not alter the polygon. A proposal to merge these two rows into GNQ-1886-1968 should be evaluated. Counter-argument: the Franco-Spanish Convention may have materially altered the boundary definition even if CShapes does not resolve the change.

### oq-gnq-pre-1886-predecessor

The CSV records predecessor=NA for GNQ-1886-1900, but Spanish presence in Equatorial Guinea (Fernando Poo) predates 1886. A pre-1886 row for Spanish Guinea may be needed to close the chain. The WHEP coverage goal requires territory to be accounted for before 1886.
