# Czechoslovakia (1918-1938)


> **`iso3` set to `CSK` on 2026-08-05 (issue 55).** This row previously carried none,
> so it belonged to no ISO family at all and could not be reached by
> `matchlib.pick_by_year`'s ISO route -- only by an explicit alias.
>
> `CSK` is the **ISO 3166-3** code for Czechoslovakia, and using it follows the rule the
> database already applies: a dissolved entity inherits its successor's code when it has
> exactly ONE successor, and takes its own 3166-3 code when it split into several.
> `ANT-1961-2010` and `SCG-1992-2006` already do the latter. Czechoslovakia became two
> states and Yugoslavia several, so no successor's code is the right one.
>
> Verified before applying: this creates **no family tie**. Every row in the family is a
> consecutive period, so no pair shares the code over overlapping years -- which matters
> because a wrong `iso3` is exactly what put a Belgium-Luxembourg row in Belgium's family
> (issue 40) and made "Malaysia" resolve to British North Borneo (issue 44).
## Summary

The current CSV tracks this row as Czechoslovakia from **1918**
to **1938** — a 21-year row covering the First Czechoslovak
Republic [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). This is a successor state of
[auh-1908-1918](auh-1908-1918.md), formed from Bohemia, Moravia, and
Silesia (Austrian crown lands) plus Slovakia and Ruthenia
(Hungarian territory)
[wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11 §successor-states](../sources/wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11.md#successor-states).

The CSV lists `predecessor = NA` for this row — this is a
**CSV bug**. Czechoslovakia was formed entirely from
Austria-Hungary territory and should reference
[auh-1908-1918](auh-1908-1918.md) as predecessor. See
[oq-predecessor-csv-bug](#oq-predecessor-csv-bug).

The row ends at 1938, corresponding to the Munich Agreement
(30 September 1938) which forced Czechoslovakia to cede the
Sudetenland to Nazi Germany. The chain continues with
[f51-1938-1945](f51-1938-1945.md) (German protectorate/occupation period).

Predecessor per CSV: NA (**bug** — should be
[auh-1908-1918](auh-1908-1918.md)). Successor per CSV: NA for this
row; the chain continues with [f51-1938-1945](f51-1938-1945.md).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Full CShapes coverage from 1918
  onward [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope).
- **Stable territory, then rapid loss.** CShapes records
  Czechoslovakia from 1918-11-11 at ~17.35 sq deg. The
  territory remained largely stable for 20 years until the
  Munich Agreement (1938-09-30) reduced it to ~14.36 sq deg
  (Sudetenland ceded to Germany), then the First Vienna Award
  (1938-11-02) further reduced it to ~12.45 sq deg (southern
  Slovakia ceded to Hungary). German occupation followed on
  1939-03-15.
- **COW entries.** CZE, 315, from 1918-10-28 to 1939-03-15
  (first entry), then 1945-05-10 to 1992-12-31 (second entry,
  restored Czechoslovakia through to the Velvet Divorce)
  [cow-state-system-v2024](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md).

  as ceasing to exist on this date
  [cow-state-system-v2024](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md). Germany occupied the
  remainder of Bohemia and Moravia (creating the Protectorate),
  while Slovakia became a nominally independent client state.

## Open questions

### oq-predecessor-csv-bug

The CSV lists `predecessor = NA` for this row. This is a bug.
Czechoslovakia was formed entirely from Austria-Hungary
territory (Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia from the Austrian half;
Slovakia and Ruthenia from the Hungarian half). The predecessor
should be [auh-1908-1918](auh-1908-1918.md). This means any WHEP
analysis that walks the predecessor graph backward from modern
Czechia or Slovakia will hit a `predecessor: NA` wall at 1918
and never reach the Austro-Hungarian parent. Filed as part of
[log proposal-auh-chain-audit](../log.md#proposal-auh-chain-audit) Finding 1.

### oq-munich-agreement-1938

The end of this row at 1938 corresponds to the Munich Agreement
(30 September 1938), in which Czechoslovakia was forced to cede
the Sudetenland (~28,000 km²) to Nazi Germany. The CShapes
polygon reduction from ~17.35 to ~14.36 sq deg on this date
confirms this as a real territorial event. However, the row end
aligns with the year 1938 rather than the specific Munich
date — and the First Vienna Award (2 November 1938) is a second
territorial loss within the same year. The successor row
F51-1938-1945 presumably starts at or after the Munich date.
