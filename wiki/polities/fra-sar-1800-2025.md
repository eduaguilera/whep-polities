# Sarthe (département of France)

## Summary

Sarthe is one of the original French Revolutionary départements, created by the territorial reform of 4 March 1790 that replaced the Ancien Régime provinces with a uniform grid of administrative units; it occupies roughly the western half of the historic province of Maine, in what is now the Pays de la Loire region, with Le Mans as its capital. This entry is type: subnational, not national, because sovereignty over this territory belongs to France throughout its span -- the row exists purely to carry a statistical reporting geography below the successive national containers (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), the same purpose served by the ESP province rows and the MAN Manchuria rows in this database. Unlike Sarthe's true 1790 origin, this entry's start_year is set to 1800 because the earliest FRA national container row present in the database begins in 1800 and the containment gate rejects any edge falling outside a container's own span; the resulting 1790-1800 gap is recorded as an open question rather than silently absorbed. The entry runs to the present (end_year 2025, exclusive) because Sarthe remains a current, active French département with no successor.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision for unit_id FRA-FRG04 (admin_name FRG04, country France) captures a département-level statistical series for Sarthe spanning 1862-2023, 97.6% observed / 1.4% interpolated-residual -- real measurement at département resolution across all three national administrative eras, not an artefact of aggregation. Before this decision there was no FRA row below the three national containers at all (0 of 66 subnational rows in the table belong to FRA, per the precedent note), so this data had nowhere non-national to attach; folding it into the roughly 550,000 km2 national aggregate (FRA-1800-1871 / FRA-1871-1919 / FRA-1919-2025) would have discarded the sub-national resolution the source actually carries and mixed Sarthe's series into 95 other départements' totals. What confirms Sarthe is a distinct, addressable reporting unit rather than an artefact of the source's own labelling: it is one of France's historic 83 (now ~96 metropolitan) départements, a first-order administrative and statistical division with INSEE code 72, continuously in place since the 1790 reform and still the base unit of INSEE and Eurostat NUTS3 reporting today (NUTS3 FRG04) -- the same evidentiary basis already used for the Spanish NUTS3 province rows in this database (ESP-AS, ESP-COR, etc.).

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period, and none of this project's currently registered sources cover French département-level boundaries. The routing decision's polygon_reasoning checked every registered source: gadm-4.1-adm1 is cached only as an 81-country subset that omits France entirely (even though GADM's global release does digitise French départements at admin-2); cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe carry only the three national FRA rows, not subdivisions; mapspain-ign and geobr-ibge cover Spain and Brazil respectively and are irrelevant. The one identified candidate -- a full global GADM 4.1 download including the ADM2 level -- is not yet present locally and its licence terms are unverified, so no polygon is attached rather than guessing at one. Territory description: Sarthe sits in north-western France, in the Pays de la Loire region, bordered by Orne and Eure-et-Loir to the north and east, Loir-et-Cher and Indre-et-Loire to the south-east, Maine-et-Loire to the south, and Mayenne to the west; landlocked, drained by the Sarthe and Loir rivers. Its capital and largest city is Le Mans (famous for the 24 Hours endurance race circuit on its outskirts). Its official INSEE-reported area is approximately 6,206 km2 -- this is the département's own stated area, not a measurement of any attached geometry, since no geometry is attached to this entry yet; when a polygon is eventually assigned, any km2 figure derived from it must be labelled explicitly as a measurement of that geometry rather than presented as independent territorial evidence.

## Predecessors and successors

Sarthe has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1790 the same broad territory (roughly the historic province of Maine, with slivers of Anjou and Perche) was organized under Ancien Régime provincial jurisdictions that do not correspond cleanly to the 1790 département boundary, and this database does not model that pre-Revolutionary structure as a distinct row -- predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent entry, and the true 1790 origin is instead flagged as the open question oq-1790-1800-gap-and-territoire-de-belfort-analogy given this entry's start_year is set to 1800. Sarthe has no successor either: it remains a current, active French département (INSEE code 72) as of 2025, part of the Pays de la Loire region since the 1982 regionalisation, hence the open end_year of 2025 (exclusive), capped at the containing national row FRA-1919-2025's own end_year.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Entry starts 1800, not the routing decision's proposed 1790**

The routing decision proposed start_year=1790, reasoning that Sarthe is one of the original ~83 Revolutionary départements created 4 March 1790 and none of the four documented country-convention departures (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, Corse split, 1946 overseas départements) apply. That reasoning about the département's own history is correct, but it cannot be honoured in this schema: the containment gate requires every container edge to fall inside both parties' own spans with no gap, and the earliest national FRA container row in this database, FRA-1800-1871, itself only starts in 1800. There is no FRA-national row of any kind covering 1790-1800 (checked against the full polities table: the earliest is FRA-1800-1871, alongside the superseded FRA-1800-1919). Rather than leave 1790-1800 uncontained, which the gate would reject outright, this entry's start_year is set to 1800, matching the earliest containing row actually present. The ten years 1790-1800 are recorded below as an open question rather than silently dropped, since a future FRA-1790-1800 national row (or a widened FRA-1800-1871) would let this entry's true 1790 origin be restored.

### d-code-fra-sar-three-letter

**Minted FRA-SAR-1800-2025 rather than a two-letter abbreviation**

This is the first FRA subnational row in the database, so there is no existing FRA sibling-code collision to avoid, but a plain two-letter code (e.g. FRA-SA) risks ambiguity against France's own official département numbering (Sarthe is INSEE department 72, not associated with a two-letter code in any French administrative convention, unlike Spain's provinces). SAR is used instead as an unambiguous three-letter mnemonic. Note this does not collide with the existing bespoke code SAR-1800-1860 (Sardinia): that code has no ISO3 prefix and denotes a different sovereign entity entirely; the dominant 57/66 <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern is followed here, with FRA- as the prefix distinguishing it. Future French département entries should use INSEE department codes or unambiguous letter mnemonics, checked against this entry's SAR before reuse.

## Open questions

### oq-no-polygon-source-registered

**No registered source in this project digitises French département boundaries**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning is explicit: gadm-4.1-adm1 is present locally only as an 81-country subset that excludes France even though GADM does publish French département (admin-2) boundaries globally; cshapes-2.0, cshapes-europe carry only country-level France rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), not subdivisions; mapspain-ign and geobr-ibge are Spain/Brazil-only and irrelevant. The one candidate identified -- a full global GADM 4.1 download including the ADM2 level -- is not currently cached locally, and its licence terms (possible non-commercial redistribution restriction) have not been checked. Until that download is added as a registered source (or another French department-boundary source is found), this entry stays polygon_status: unassigned with polygon_source: none. Whoever adds French département geometry next should populate this entry's polygon fields and re-derive polygon_area_km2 from the actual attached feature -- Sarthe's official INSEE-reported area is approximately 6,206 km2, which is territory evidence, not a geometry measurement, and should not be written into polygon_area_km2 until a real polygon is attached.

### oq-1790-1800-gap-and-territoire-de-belfort-analogy

**The 1790-1800 gap between Sarthe's true founding and this entry's start_year is unresolved**

Sarthe's département boundary was fixed in 1790, but this entry starts in 1800 solely because no FRA-national container exists earlier (see decision d-start-year-1800-not-1790). This is analogous to -- but distinct from -- the Territoire de Belfort case that the country convention already treats as a documented departure (Belfort split off from Haut-Rhin only in 1871-1922, so it legitimately starts later than 1790); Sarthe's true start is 1790, and the gap here is an artefact of container-table coverage, not of Sarthe's own administrative history. If any pre-1800 valued data rows exist for Sarthe (none are known to this author at authoring time -- no source/row count for 1790-1800 was surfaced in the routing decision), they would currently have nowhere to attach. Resolving this requires either adding an earlier FRA national container row or confirming, by checking the source data directly, that no rows predate 1800 for this unit, in which case the gap is real but harmless.
