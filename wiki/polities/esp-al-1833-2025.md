# Almería (province of Spain)

## Summary

Almería is one of the 50 provinces of Spain, located in the eastern part of the autonomous community of Andalusia on the Mediterranean coast, bordering Granada to the west, Murcia to the northeast, and Jaén to the north. This entry is `type: subnational` because a Spanish province is not itself a sovereign entity but a stable second-tier administrative division of the Kingdom of Spain (later the Second Republic, the Franco state, and the current constitutional monarchy) that has, unlike sovereignty itself, been the unit at which a great deal of historical statistical reporting was actually collected. The province was created in November 1833 by the territorial reform of Javier de Burgos, which divided Spain into 49 provinces replacing the old kingdoms, principalities and lordships; this reform is the reason the entry's start_year is 1833 rather than some earlier or later date tied to Almería's own history as a place (which as a Moorish and then Christian territory long predates 1833). The province has remained a stable administrative unit since its creation, through the constitutional monarchy, the First and Second Republics, the Franco dictatorship, and the current 1978 constitutional order, which is why a single container edge to national Spain (ESP-1800-2025) covers the whole 1833-2025 span with no intervening sovereignty change. This entry was created alongside several other Spanish provinces in the same harness session, following the default start-rule established for this country's convention (all mainland provinces begin in 1833; only the Canary Islands provinces have a documented later departure), because Almería had no existing polity row despite having its own current provincial statistics.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: this entry is created directly from a routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES611, admin_name ES611, country Spain) processed by the harness's country-convention pipeline for Spain, rather than from a specific dataset row count visible here — the decision record does not enumerate the underlying rows, only that Almería (NUTS/admin code ES611) had no existing polity match. What it was previously matched to: nothing existed for Almería specifically; any statistics reported at the Almería-province level would previously have had no distinct polity row to attach to other than the national Spain row (ESP-1800-2025), which is roughly 58x the province's own area and would silently misattribute province-level detail to the whole country. What confirms this entity is distinct: the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform created Almería as one of Spain's original provinces, a division that has persisted through every subsequent regime change (monarchy, republics, dictatorship, current constitution) to the present day, and the repository already treats six sibling provinces (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra) as separate polities under the identical reasoning, establishing the precedent this entry follows rather than departs from.

Why this entry exists: it captures province-level Almería data under Spain's national statistical apparatus. Before this entry, any data series reporting at the Almería-province level would have had nowhere to route except the national ESP-1800-2025 row (roughly 505,000 km2 as Spain, versus Almería's own ~8,775 km2), a mismatch of about 58x that this entry corrects, mirroring the same problem the routing decision's sibling entries (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra) were created to solve. The 1833 Javier de Burgos reform is the historical source confirming Almería is a distinct, persistent administrative unit distinguishable from both the national total and its neighbouring provinces (Granada, Murcia, Jaén), and it has continued to be treated as a distinct statistical and administrative unit by the INE ever since.

Territory description: the province of Almería covers the easternmost part of Andalusia, on Spain's southeastern Mediterranean coast, including the city of Almería and towns such as Roquetas de Mar, El Ejido, and Níjar, and extending inland to the Sierra de los Filabres and the semi-arid Tabernas desert. Its current area is approximately **8,775 km2**. This figure is drawn from published INE/geographic reference data describing the modern province, not measured from any geometry attached to this entry, since no polygon is yet attached.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: Almería did not exist as an administrative unit before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform created the modern Spanish provincial map from scratch, replacing the old kingdoms and historical regions (this area was previously part of the Kingdom of Granada). No successor: the province remains current as of 2025, with end_year left open per the routing decision's note that the unit is still active. It is a sibling, not predecessor/successor, of the other six ESP subnational entries already in the table (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra) and of the still-to-be-created remaining Spanish provinces from this same 1833 reform.

## Sourced claims

- The province of Almería was created in the November 1833 territorial division of Spain drafted by Javier de Burgos, Secretary of State for Development, which replaced the historical kingdoms with 49 (later 50, with the 1927 split of the Canaries) provinces still in use today.
- Almería province's INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadística) code is 04 and its postal/vehicle abbreviation is AL, the convention this repository's sibling ESP entries (e.g. ESP-AS for Asturias code 33, ESP-PO for Pontevedra code 36) already use for subnational province codes.
- The mapspain package (R) and its underlying IGN (Instituto Geográfico Nacional) province boundary layer, registered in this repository as source mapspain-ign, publishes current administrative boundaries keyed by cpro (INE province code), which this routing decision identifies as the correct polygon source for Almería but which is not yet present on disk in this checkout.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, using the INE province code**

The routing decision proposed the polity_name 'Almería (province of Spain)' but did not fix a code. 57 of 66 subnational rows in the table follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the six existing ESP subnational siblings (ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-OR, ESP-PO) all use this pattern keyed on either the INE/postal province abbreviation or a short code, not a numeric admin id. Almería's standard INE two-letter abbreviation is 'AL', giving ESP-AL-1833-2025. This is not a historical territory with a name of its own (unlike ALK or HYD), so the bespoke-code exception does not apply, and there is no reason to depart from the sibling convention.

### d-polygon-unassigned-not-proxy

**Left polygon_status as unassigned rather than proxy or assigned**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning identifies mapspain-ign, keyed by INE province code (cpro), as the correct source for this and the other Spanish province entries created this session, and notes explicitly that mapspain-ign is registered in sources.yaml but present_locally=False in this checkout, so the actual provinces.gpkg cannot be read here even though it is the intended route. Rather than fabricate a feature_id or borrow a neighbouring province's geometry as a proxy (which the proxy rules reserve for territories whose shape is genuinely a stand-in for another period of the same polity, not for a missing file), this page records polygon_status: unassigned with polygon_source left as the intended mapspain-ign target, so a future ingest that has the file present can fill in polygon_feature_id without re-deciding the source.

## Open questions

### oq-mapspain-not-fetched

**mapspain-ign is registered but its geodata file is not present in this checkout**

sources.yaml lists mapspain-ign with present_locally: False, so the provinces.gpkg backing this source cannot be read to assign polygon_feature_id even though the routing decision identifies the INE cpro-keyed feature for Almería (province code 04) as the correct match. Until the file is fetched and placed locally, this entry stays polygon_status: unassigned. When it is fetched, the feature keyed cpro='04' should be assigned directly, and polygon_status should move to assigned rather than proxy, since IGN's current province boundary is being used for essentially its whole historical span, not as a stand-in for a different period.

### oq-1833-boundary-stability

**Whether Almería's provincial boundary has been literally unchanged since 1833**

The routing decision's span_basis asserts 'no major provincial boundary changes for Almería' since the 1833 reform, which licenses treating a present-day IGN polygon as a low-risk proxy across the whole 1833-2025 span once mapspain-ign is available. This has not been independently verified against a documented boundary history for Almería specifically (e.g. minor municipal boundary adjustments with neighbouring Granada or Murcia provinces are common in Spain and would not necessarily be visible without a dedicated check). If such an adjustment exists, the vintage risk noted in polygon_detail should be revisited rather than assumed low by default.
