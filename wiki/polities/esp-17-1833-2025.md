# Girona (province of Spain)

## Summary

Girona is one of the four provinces of Catalonia, in the north-east of Spain, bordering France and Andorra to the north and the Mediterranean to the east. It was created, along with the rest of Spain's current provinces, by Javier de Burgos's 1833 territorial reorganisation, which replaced the earlier kingdoms and historical territories with 49 (later 50) provinces intended for uniform administration and statistical reporting. Girona's boundaries have been essentially unchanged since 1833: it corresponds closely to the historical comarques of the Empordà, Gironès, La Selva, Garrotxa, Ripollès, Pla de l'Estany and Cerdanya (Spanish portion). The entry is `type: subnational` because Girona is not and has never been a sovereign polity; it is a first-order administrative division of Spain, reporting through provincial and later Catalan autonomous-community statistics that this row exists to hold separately from the national ESP total.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Spanish province-level statistical rows for Girona (NUTS3 code ES512, INE/cpro provincial code 17) drawn from the agent-harness routing run over Spanish NUTS/province units. Before this pass, no polity row represented Girona specifically: the only Spanish polities in the database were the national total (ESP-1800-2025) and a handful of other individually created provinces (Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra), none of which is Girona and none of which is coextensive with it. Routing Girona-labelled rows to ESP-1800-2025 would have overstated Girona's territory roughly fifty-fold and merged a distinct sub-national reporting series into the national one. Girona's separateness is confirmed by its continuous administrative identity since the 1833 reform (Real Decreto of 30 November 1833) as one of Spain's historical provinces, still in active use today as a NUTS3 statistical unit and as the second-tier administrative division under the Generalitat de Catalunya.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `assigned`, from source `mapspain-ign` (feature id `17`, the official INE/IGN provincial code `cpro=17` for Girona), the same source already used for the sibling provinces Asturias, Cantabria, Córdoba, Lugo, Ourense and Pontevedra in this repository. `mapspain-ign` digitises present-day Spanish provincial boundaries; because Girona's extent as an 1833 Javier de Burgos province has remained essentially unchanged to the present, a present-day source is an appropriate fit for the full 1833-2025 span, so this is treated as a direct assignment rather than a cross-period proxy.

**Territory description:** Girona province covers the north-easternmost corner of Catalonia and of Spain, bounded by France and Andorra to the north, the Mediterranean Sea to the east, the province of Barcelona to the south, and the province of Lleida to the west. It includes the Costa Brava coastline, the city of Girona, and towns such as Figueres, Blanes and Puigcerdà. Its present-day area is approximately 5,910 km², making it the smallest of Catalonia's four provinces.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: prior to the 1833 reform this territory was part of the historical Principality of Catalonia rather than a discrete administrative unit of the kind the database tracks, so no predecessor row is asserted. No successor: the province remains active as of 2025, both as a Spanish provincial administrative unit and as NUTS3 unit ES512 within the autonomous community of Catalonia, so end_year 2025 is open-ended rather than terminal.

## Sourced claims

- The Real Decreto of 30 November 1833, drafted by Javier de Burgos, divided Spain into 49 provinces including Girona, replacing the prior kingdom- and historical-territory-based divisions.
- Eurostat's NUTS classification assigns Girona province the code ES512 within the NUTS2 region ES51 (Cataluña).

## Decisions

### d-code-choice-espgi-taken

**Chose ESP-17-1833-2025 after ESP-GI-1833-2025 was already assigned**

The natural code for Girona under the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern would use the two-letter abbreviation GI (Girona's standard provincial abbreviation, e.g. on vehicle plates and in INE tables). That code was already assigned in this run to a different unit, ESP-ES212 (Gipuzkoa, in the Basque Country), which also abbreviates to GI. To avoid the collision without inventing an unrecognisable bespoke code, this entry uses the numeric INE/IGN provincial code (cpro) 17 as the subunit token instead of the letter abbreviation, giving ESP-17-1833-2025. This keeps the code within the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> family (the subunit is still a standard, source-verifiable identifier for the province, just numeric rather than alphabetic) and keeps it distinguishable from the Gipuzkoa row.

## Open questions

### oq-gi-abbrev-collision

**Two-letter GI abbreviation is ambiguous between Girona and Gipuzkoa**

Both Girona (Catalonia) and Gipuzkoa (Basque Country) are commonly abbreviated 'GI' in Spanish administrative shorthand (vehicle plates, some INE tables), and this run already assigned ESP-GI-1833-2025 to the Gipuzkoa unit ESP-ES212. This entry sidesteps the collision by using the numeric cpro code 17, but any future source-matching or alias work for Spanish provinces should check both codes against cpro/NUTS identifiers rather than the two-letter shorthand alone, since a naive alias table keyed only on 'GI' would misroute one of the two provinces. Should the numeric-code convention be applied retroactively to all other single-letter-ambiguous Spanish provinces for consistency, or is this an isolated exception?
