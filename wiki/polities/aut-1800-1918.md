# Austria (Cisleithania)

## Summary

Tracks the Austrian lands from **1800** through the end of **1918**. Initially the core of the Austrian Empire, then from 1867 the Cisleithanian half of Austria-Hungary under the Ausgleich (Compromise). The polity dissolved with the collapse of Austria-Hungary at the end of World War I [wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16](../sources/wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16.md).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** Assigned from **HistoGIS 1860 (ACDH-CH, Austrian Academy of Sciences)**. Built by dissolving 14 Cisleithanian crownlands from the [Austrian Empire Crownlands 1860 shapefile](https://histogis.acdh.oeaw.ac.at/shp-samples): Lower Austria, Upper Austria, Styria, Carinthia, Carniola, Küstenland (Trieste/Gorizia/Istria), Tyrol & Vorarlberg, Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia, Galicia, Bukovina, Dalmatia, Salzburg. Venetia excluded (lost to Italy 1866). Measured area ≈ **300,560 km²** (ETRS89 LAEA), matching Wikipedia's 1910 figure of 300,005 km² to within 0.2%. The 1867 Ausgleich was constitutional not territorial, so 1860 crownland boundaries hold for the whole 1800–1918 period. Source shapefile under open academic license. Assembly script: `scripts/sources/histogis-1860-habsburg/build.py`. Output GeoPackage: `data/geodata/histogis-1860-habsburg/habsburg_cisleithania_transleithania.gpkg` (gitignored).

**Why the post-1918 polygon was not used:** post-1918 Austria ([AUT-1918-1919](aut-1918-1919.md), ~84,000 km²) lost ~72% of Cisleithanian territory at the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919) — Bohemia, Moravia, Galicia, Bukovina, Slovenia, Dalmatia, South Tyrol all went to successor states. Cisleithania covered ~300,000 km² (modern Austria, Czechia, southern Poland, western Ukraine, Slovenia, parts of Italy and Romania).

**Data territory confirmed from BOTH sources:** Mitchell's *International Historical Statistics* explicitly defines "Austria up to 1918" as **the Cisleithanian part of the Habsburg monarchy**. Juan's team (WHEP project) data, despite using FAOSTAT area codes, also covers Cisleithanian territory — verified by wheat area values: ~970k-1,037k ha in the 1870s, consistent with Cisleithania (1,213,000 ha in 1913 per *1914-1918-Online Encyclopedia*), NOT modern Austria (~270,000 ha). This confirms the polygon rejection was correct: the post-1918 Austria polygon represents only ~28% of the data's territory.

**Why this entry exists:** The pre-1961 input data reports "Austria" and "Hungary" as separate countries throughout 1851-1917, reflecting the real administrative split within the Habsburg Monarchy (especially post-Ausgleich 1867). Mitchell confirms this follows Cisleithania/Transleithania boundaries. Previously, the pipeline routed AUT pre-1918 → AUH (Austria-Hungary), merging Austrian data into the combined dual-monarchy polygon. This lost the distinction the data compilers preserved.

## Contradictions

### the 1918 dissolution IS visible, confirming Cisleithanian scope

Recorded 2026-08-18. Independent corroboration from IIA for the Cisleithania
scope already established from Mitchell and FAOSTAT.

IIA's `austria` label, median values either side of the dissolution:

| item | 1909-1918 | 1919 onward | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| sugar, raw centrifugal (t) | **850,802** (n=10) | 123,450 (n=22) | **0.15** |
| wine (t) | **278,677** (n=10) | 83,431 (n=21) | 0.30 |

Rump and modern Austria cannot produce 850,802 t of sugar — the beet-sugar
industry was concentrated in Bohemia and Moravia, which were Cisleithanian
crownlands and are not in the 1919+ territory. The wine drop points the same way,
to the crownland vineyards.

**Unlike most territorial tests here, this one relies on a step that SHOULD exist
and does.** That is the mirror of the Germany/Prussia case on `deu-1800-1866`,
where a step that should have appeared at 1866 and 1871 does not, proving the
opposite. Both are stronger than a magnitude-plausibility judgement.

This corroborates the registered `iia` / `austria` convention in
`state/source_conventions.csv`, which measures the same dissolution across six
items at a median post/pre ratio of 0.14.

## Border eras inside this row, and why it is not split (issue 686, 2026-10-07)

Issue 686 listed this row for the loss of Lombardy (1859, Zurich) and Venetia (1866, Vienna)
[wikipedia-border-eras-2026-10-07 §austria](../sources/wikipedia-border-eras-2026-10-07.md#austria), and for the Napoleonic losses of 1805-1809 and the 1815 restoration. **The row is not
split, on purpose.** Its territory is the crown lands that became Cisleithania, and that territory never
included Lombardy-Venetia: the polygon leaves Venetia out (see Territorial extent) and never had
Lombardy. The Habsburg state's Italian provinces are on the empire rows, which do split at both dates:
[auh-1800-1859](auh-1800-1859.md), [auh-1859-1866](auh-1859-1866.md) and
[auh-1866-1908](auh-1866-1908.md).

**The data says the same.** Everything routed here before 1866 is on the crown-lands basis. Juan's
`austria` maize, the crop Lombardy-Venetia grew most of, is 230,370-341,926 t in 1851-1859 and
289,111-341,333 t in 1868-1871, with no step at either 1859 or 1866; wheat is 740,769 t in 1851 and
875,154-987,308 t in 1869-1871. A series that included Lombardy-Venetia would fall sharply at 1859 and
again at 1866. Splitting the row would have moved 1851-1865 data onto polygons 45,500 and 25,800 km²
larger than the territory it measures. A split was built and measured before this was found: the
crown lands with Venetia would be 326,141 km² and with Lombardy too 345,841 km² (Mollweide), against
300,330 km² for this polygon.

**Still open:** the Napoleonic years (Tyrol, Salzburg, Illyria and West Galicia lost in 1805-1809,
regained in 1814-1815) and Krakow's annexation to Galicia in 1846 (about 1,200 km²). No fetched source
draws the crown lands for those years, and no data reaches them.
