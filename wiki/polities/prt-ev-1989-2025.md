# Évora (NUTS III region of Portugal)

## Summary

Évora is a NUTS III statistical sub-region of Portugal, one of the four sub-regions making up the NUTS II Alentejo region, corresponding to the historic Évora district in south-central Portugal. `type: subnational` is used deliberately, following the pattern already set for ten-plus other Portuguese NUTS rows (PRT-ALE, PRT-PT11, PRT-AV, PRT-BG, etc.): Portugal held unbroken sovereignty throughout this span, and the row exists purely to carry a reporting territory smaller than the national total, not to assert independent statehood. It cannot be matched to the national polity PRT-1800-2025 because doing so would mean the national total is simultaneously claimed by Évora and by each of its eleven other NUTS sibling rows — the rejected double-claim pattern documented in the routing decision. The routing decision that created this page reports 144 data years of measured values for the unit labelled PT_EV, with the NUTS statistical framework itself only dating to Portugal's 1986 EU accession and 1989 practical implementation, so this entry's start_year of 1989 reflects when the current administrative classification began, not when the underlying territory or its statistical reporting began (see predecessors_and_successors and the open questions on the resulting pre-1989 gap).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the unit labelled PRT-PTEV (admin_name PT_EV) from the source underlying the agent-harness routing pipeline, with 144 data years of measured values spanning 1880-2023. That data was previously implicitly absorbed into the national polity PRT-1800-2025 by default routing — wrong because Évora is one of twelve NUTS-level Portuguese sub-regions each independently tabulated by Eurostat, so matching every one of them to the national total produces the rejected double-claim pattern (twelve regions each separately claiming to be the whole of Portugal's reported value for the same year). That Évora is treated as a genuinely distinct reporting/statistical entity is confirmed by Eurostat's own NUTS nomenclature, which assigns it a dedicated NUTS III code (PT187) within Alentejo (PT18/PT2) and publishes region-specific statistics for it independently of Portugal's other eleven NUTS regions — the same evidentiary standard already applied to sibling PRT-ALE-1989-2025 and PRT-PT11-1986-2025. Following the country convention already recorded across eleven sibling PRT NUTS verdicts, the routing decision set this entry's span to start_year 1989 (the practical NUTS implementation date, one sibling year later than the 1986 accession date used by some other NUTS II rows) through an open end_year of 2025, leaving the 109 pre-1989 data years (76% of the total) unrouted for now rather than force-matched to the national row — see open questions for whether that gap is fillable with a pre-1989 predecessor polity.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. Per the routing decision's own polygon_reasoning: gadm-4.1-adm1 is present locally but excludes Portugal from its curated subset entirely, and even if it were fetched, GADM's admin-1 (distrito) boundaries are administrative districts rather than NUTS statistical regions, which follow distinct Eurostat/GISCO aggregation rules that can diverge from district lines. No other registered source (cshapes, cliopatria, histogis, paine, reporting-areas, constructed) carries Portuguese sub-national geometry at any vintage. This is therefore a `new_source_needed` case: `polygon_source` is set to `none`, and the desired source — Eurostat/GISCO's NUTS region boundary dataset — is recorded as an open question (oq-nuts-boundary-source) rather than declared here, since it is not currently registered in sources.yaml.

**Territory description:** The Évora NUTS III sub-region occupies south-central Portugal, corresponding closely to the historic district of Évora — the area centred on the city of Évora (a UNESCO World Heritage site) and extending across the rolling cereal- and cork-oak (montado) plains of central Alentejo, bordered by the Beja sub-region to the south, Alto Alentejo to the north and east toward the Spanish border, and Lezíria do Tejo/Alentejo Litoral to the west. Eurostat cites the NUTS III Évora region (code PT187) at approximately 7,400 km², making it one of the larger but most sparsely populated NUTS III units in mainland Portugal. This figure is drawn from published Eurostat/NUTS regional statistics, not measured from any polygon attached to this page, since none is attached (polygon_feature_id is null and no area is measured here).

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor is assigned: this entry's routing decision found no distinct pre-1989 sub-national polity to route Évora's 109 pre-1989 data years to, so those years were marked unroutable under the country's NUTS-region convention rather than retroactively backdating this code or inventing an unattested earlier polity — see oq-pre1989-evora-district for why that gap may still be fillable. No successor is assigned since the NUTS classification remains current administrative practice in Portugal today; end_year is left open at 2025 (exclusive) per the country convention already set by the other ten-plus Portuguese NUTS sibling rows, matching the routing decision's proposed span.

## Sourced claims

- Eurostat's NUTS nomenclature assigns Évora its own NUTS III code (PT187, within the Alentejo NUTS II region PT18/PT2), distinct from Portugal's other Alentejo sub-regions (Alentejo Litoral, Baixo Alentejo, Alto Alentejo, Lezíria do Tejo), confirming it is tabulated by Eurostat as a separate reporting unit rather than folded into a wider Alentejo total.
- The routing decision's source data for unit PRT-PTEV (admin_name PT_EV) reports measured values across 144 data years spanning 1880-2023, of which 109 years (76%) predate the 1989 introduction of the NUTS framework that currently names and delimits the unit, meaning the reporting territory existed and was measured long before its current administrative classification existed.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Used <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> per the 57-row dominant pattern, giving PRT-EV-1989-2025**

Évora is a standard EU NUTS III statistical sub-region, not a historical territory with an identity independent of its administrative classification (unlike the three bespoke-coded rows ALK, HYD, RYU, which name genuinely distinct former polities such as the Territory of Alaska or Hyderabad State). It therefore follows the dominant 57-row pattern ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end already used for Portugal's other NUTS siblings (PRT-ALE-1989-2025, PRT-PT11-1986-2025, etc.), giving PRT-EV-1989-2025. The subunit abbreviation EV mirrors the routing decision's own admin_name (PT_EV) and Eurostat's own NUTS3 code prefix, keeping it consistent with the ALE/AV/BG/LIS abbreviation style already set as precedent for this country. The routing decision's start_year of 1989 (rather than 1986, the year of accession itself) follows the pattern already recorded for 11 sibling PRT NUTS verdicts, per the routing_reasoning; this page does not re-derive that year independently.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1989-evora-district

**Did the historic Évora district report this territory's data separately before 1989?**

The routing decision states that 76% of this unit's 144 data years (109 of 144, spanning 1880-2023) fall before the 1989 NUTS start date and were left unrouted under the sibling convention. Pre-1989, the area now called the Évora NUTS III region corresponds closely to the historic district (distrito) of Évora, one of Portugal's pre-1976 administrative divisions, which very likely reported agricultural or demographic statistics under its own name well before 1989. If the underlying source in fact reports an 'Évora district' or comparable pre-NUTS aggregate for those years, a predecessor polity spanning roughly 1880-1989 should be created and those years re-routed, rather than left permanently unroutable — mirroring how PRT-AV-1835-2025 (Aveiro) already extends its own span back past the NUTS start rather than leaving pre-1989 years stranded. This should be checked before assuming the 1880-1989 gap is correctly unfilled here.

### oq-nuts-boundary-source

**Should Eurostat/GISCO NUTS boundaries be registered so this and sibling PRT rows can get a polygon?**

No registered source in sources.yaml carries Portuguese sub-national boundaries at the NUTS III level: gadm-4.1-adm1 is a curated subset that excludes Portugal entirely, and no other registered source (cshapes, cliopatria, histogis, paine, reporting-areas, constructed) has Portuguese sub-national geometry of any kind. Eurostat/GISCO publishes versioned NUTS boundary files (e.g. NUTS 2016, NUTS 2021) that would resolve polygon_status for this row and for all other Portuguese NUTS rows at once, but its licence terms have not been verified and no vintage has been checked against the 1989-2025 span this entry covers (NUTS III boundaries have been revised at least once, in 2015). This is recorded here as the open question rather than declaring a source, per the routing decision's own polygon_detail, and should be resolved as a batch registration decision covering all ten-plus Portuguese NUTS rows rather than per-entry.

### oq-nuts3-vs-district-mismatch

**Does the NUTS III Évora region's boundary actually match the historic Évora district it is named after?**

Portugal's NUTS III sub-regions were drawn as EU statistical aggregations and do not necessarily follow the older distrito boundaries exactly, even where they share a name. Before any predecessor polity is created under oq-pre1989-evora-district, or before any GADM/GISCO polygon is assigned, someone needs to confirm whether the modern Évora NUTS III boundary is congruent with the historic Évora district or merely overlaps it, since a mismatch would mean neither the pre-1989 predecessor nor a future polygon assignment can simply reuse the district's shape.
