# Greece (1913-1919)

## Summary

This row tracks Greece for **7 years**, from the Balkan Wars to the
post-WWI settlements
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Greece gained Macedonia, Epirus, Crete, and Aegean islands in the
Balkan Wars (1912-1913). The 1913 start date aligns with the Treaty
of Bucharest (10 August 1913), which concluded the Second Balkan War.
During WWI, Greece was deeply divided between pro-Allied Prime Minister
Venizelos and pro-neutrality King Constantine I (the "National
Schism"), eventually joining the Allies in 1917. The 1919 end date
aligns with the Treaty of Neuilly (Bulgaria, 27 November 1919) and
the lead-up to the Treaty of Sevres (Ottoman Empire, 1920).

Predecessor: [grc-1881-1913](grc-1881-1913.md) -- Balkan Wars
territorial gains.

Successor: [grc-1919-1947](grc-1919-1947.md) -- post-WWI
Greece (pre-Dodecanese).

## Dodecanese removed from the polygon (2026-09-25)

**Polygon status: `assigned`, rebuilt.** `constructed` (`build_grc_1913_1919()`): CShapes 350's `1913-1919` step
MINUS the Dodecanese ([itaeg-1912-1947](itaeg-1912-1947.md)) widened by 3 km, **119,369 km²** (ESRI:54034)
at source against CShapes' 121,697 km². CShapes draws Rhodes, Kos, Karpathos, Kalymnos and Astypalaia
inside Greece for this period (the five removed pieces are 1,430.5, 280.2, 310.6, 152.1 and
154.9 km², all inside 26.26-28.24 E, 35.40-37.07 N), but the islands were Italian "from 1912" until
"ceded to the Kingdom of Greece in 1947 under the Treaty of Paris", and they have their own row. The
3 km margin removes CShapes' own coarser outline of each island, which subtracting the GADM outline
alone would leave behind as rings of coast; the nearest Greek land outside the group is over 15 km
away, so nothing else moves. The change is 1.9% of the row. No row of this polity moves: the rows
routed here are Greece's own (FAO 1952 prints the Dodecanese separately in 1937 and `Greece` without
them).

## Open questions

### oq-national-schism

Greece was deeply divided during WWI between pro-Allied Venizelos and
pro-neutrality King Constantine I -- the "National Schism" (Ethnikos
Dikhasmos). This is not a territorial issue but is contextually
important: Greece effectively had two rival governments in 1916-1917
(the official Athens government and Venizelos's provisional government
in Thessaloniki). The schism does not affect WHEP territorial tracking
but should be noted for political context.
