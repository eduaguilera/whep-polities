# Greece (1913-1919)

## Summary

This row tracks Greece for **7 years**, from the Balkan Wars to the
post-WWI settlements
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Greece gained Macedonia, Epirus, Crete, and Aegean islands in the
Balkan Wars (1912-1913). The 1913 start date aligns with the Treaty
of Bucharest (10 August 1913), which concluded the Second Balkan War.
During WWI, Greece was deeply divided between pro-Allied Prime Minister
Venizelos and pro-neutrality King Constantine I (the "National
Schism"), eventually joining the Allies in 1917. The 1919 end date
aligns with the Treaty of Neuilly (Bulgaria, 27 November 1919) and
the lead-up to the Treaty of Sevres (Ottoman Empire, 1920).

Predecessor: [grc-1830-1913](grc-1830-1913.md) -- Balkan Wars
territorial gains.

Successor: [grc-1919-2025](grc-1919-2025.md) -- post-WWI
Greece.

## Sourced claims

- **1913: Balkan Wars conclusion.** Treaty of Bucharest (10 August
  1913) concluded the Second Balkan War. Greece gained Macedonia,
  Epirus, and Crete -- the largest single territorial expansion in
  Greek history. This is the start-of-row event.
- **1914-1917: National Schism.** Greece was divided between
  pro-Allied Venizelos and pro-neutrality King Constantine I. Greece
  eventually joined the Allies in June 1917.
- **1919: Post-WWI settlements.** Treaty of Neuilly (Bulgaria,
  27 November 1919) transferred Western Thrace to Greece. This is
  the end-of-row event.

## Open questions

### oq-national-schism

Greece was deeply divided during WWI between pro-Allied Venizelos and
pro-neutrality King Constantine I -- the "National Schism" (Ethnikos
Dikhasmos). This is not a territorial issue but is contextually
important: Greece effectively had two rival governments in 1916-1917
(the official Athens government and Venizelos's provisional government
in Thessaloniki). The schism does not affect WHEP territorial tracking
but should be noted for political context.
