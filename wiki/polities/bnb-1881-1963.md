## Summary

British North Borneo covers the northeast portion of Borneo island (now the Malaysian state of Sabah), from its establishment as a Chartered Company territory in 1881 through its incorporation into Malaysia in 1963. Three administrative phases: Chartered Company (1881-1888), British Protectorate (1888-1946), Crown Colony (1946-1963). CShapes 2.0 cowcode=823. Area ~72,620 km².

The FAO 1952 dataset reported "British Borneo North Borneo" as a distinct sub-entry. This polity must NOT be routed to British Malaya (MYS-1946-1957, Malay Peninsula ~132,000 km²) or Brunei (BRN-1888-2025).

### fao1952 tobacco AREA is impossible, and the yield identity proves it

Recorded 2026-08-18. A queued note flagged the tobacco acreage as too high; this
confirms it and replaces the external-knowledge argument with two internal ones.

| | 1934-1938 | 1949 | 1950 | 1951 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tobacco area (1000 ha) | 40.0 | 48.0 | **148.0** | 55.0 |
| tobacco production (1000 t) | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.5 | — |

**First, the yield.** 0.2-0.5 thousand tonnes on 40-148 thousand hectares is
**0.005 t/ha, about 5 kg per hectare**. Tobacco yields on the order of 1 t/ha, so
the two columns cannot both be right and the area is the implausible one.

**Second, the land budget.** The same source gives this territory **206** thousand
ha of arable land. Tobacco at 148 thousand ha would be **72% of all arable land**,
against rice paddy's 33-36 — impossible for a minor export crop.

Neither argument needs a figure from outside the source, which is what makes this
a confirmation rather than an opinion. The production column is plausible
throughout and should be kept.

**Two further defects in the same table**, not in the queued note:

* `cattle` reads **1,011** thousand heads for 1949 against **11** for 1951 — a 92x
  step with no possible cause, and 1,011,000 cattle is implausible here. The 1951
  figure is the credible one.
* `poultry` 1951 carries **two rows** (31 and 369 thousand heads), and the
  population series carries two for 1951 as well (222 and 335 thousand people).
  Nothing in the panel distinguishes either pair — the class measured in
  `state/item_axis_aggregates.csv` and issue #367, here on a territory small enough
  that the ambiguity affects every per-capita figure.

The queued note also observed that **Labuan joined North Borneo in 1946**,
mid-span, which remains a scope caveat on the 1949-1951 rows.
