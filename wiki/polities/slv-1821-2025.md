# El Salvador

## Summary

El Salvador is the smallest Central American country, bounded by Guatemala to the west and Honduras to the north and east, with the Pacific Ocean to the south. Biger 1995 shows El Salvador on the regional boundary map (p.264) and notes that the Central American colonies separated from Europe in 1821 [biger-1995 &sect;el-salvador](../sources/biger-1995.md#el-salvador). WHEP tracks El Salvador from 1821 (independence from Spain) as a continuous row because its territory has remained essentially constant since independence.

## Territorial extent

El Salvador is bounded by Guatemala to the west and Honduras (213 miles; 342 km.) to the north and east, with the Pacific Ocean to the south [biger-1995 &sect;honduras](../sources/biger-1995.md#honduras). The polygon source is CShapes 2.0 gwcode=92, vintage year 1886, area ~21,041 km² [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

### Polygon vintage and approximation by sub-period

The single CShapes 2.0 polygon (vintage 1886, ~21,041 km²) is used for the entire 1821–2025 span. Its accuracy varies across three sub-periods:

**1821–1884 (pre-Jutiapa Agreement)**
The El Salvador–Honduras border was disputed and de facto unsurveyed after independence in 1821. The 1886 polygon almost certainly reflects a boundary agreed by the 1884 Jutiapa Agreement (signed 11 April 1884 between El Salvador and Honduras) rather than any pre-1884 colonial line. For the 1821–1884 sub-period the polygon therefore approximates a boundary that was not formally fixed. No WHEP data rows fall in this sub-period, so the approximation does not affect any matched observations. Direction and magnitude of the approximation are unknown but likely small in absolute km² terms given the similarity of colonial and post-colonial boundaries in Central America.

**1884–1992 (post-Jutiapa Agreement, pre-ICJ)**
The 1884 Jutiapa Agreement fixed most of the El Salvador–Honduras land border. The 1886 CShapes polygon accurately reflects this boundary. All current WHEP data rows (1894–1960) fall within this sub-period. The polygon is the correct representation for these observations. polygon_vintage_drift between the polygon vintage (1886) and the data years (1894–1960) is negligible: the boundary did not change during this interval.

**1992–2025 (post-ICJ ruling)**
On 11 September 1992 the International Court of Justice issued its final judgment in the *Land, Island and Maritime Frontier Dispute* (El Salvador v. Honduras; Nicaragua intervening). The ruling transferred approximately 330 km² in three disputed sectors — Tepangüisir, Monteca/Orillas del Río Negro, and El Torola — from El Salvador to Honduras. As a result, El Salvador's territory decreased from ~21,041 km² to ~20,711 km². The 1886 CShapes polygon therefore overstates El Salvador's post-1992 territory by ~330 km² (~1.5%). No current WHEP data rows fall in this sub-period, so the overstatement does not affect any matched observations.
