# Mato Grosso do Sul

## Summary

Mato Grosso do Sul is a state of the Federative Republic of Brazil, in the country's Centro-Oeste (Center-West) region, bordering Paraguay and Bolivia and the states of Mato Grosso, Goiás, Minas Gerais, São Paulo, Paraná, and Rio Grande do Sul. It was created by federal Complementary Law 31 of 11 October 1977, which split the pre-existing state of Mato Grosso into two: a northern Mato Grosso (retaining the name and its capital Cuiabá) and a southern Mato Grosso do Sul, with its own capital at Campo Grande. The split responded to the vast size and administrative unmanageability of the undivided state and to decades of regional demands from the more populated, more economically developed southern half (cattle ranching, and by the late 20th century soy and export agriculture) for its own administration separate from the sparsely populated Amazonian north. `type: subnational` because the state has never been sovereign — it has always been a constituent unit of Brazil, first under the 1967/1969 Constitution during the military government period, and continuously since the 1988 Constitution. The entry's span begins at the 1977 creation date, not at any earlier data year, because before that date there was no "Mato Grosso do Sul" as an administrative or statistical unit — only the undivided Mato Grosso.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Brazilian state-level statistical data (production/agricultural series in the pipeline's extract, keyed to unit_id BRA-MATOGROSSODOSUL) reported for the modern state of Mato Grosso do Sul, spanning years from as early as 1900 through the present in the source table, though the state itself was only created in 1977. Rows dated 1900-1976 report on this territory before the state existed as a separate administrative unit; the routing decision found that the shares attributed to that pre-1977 slice of the series carry high fallback_interpolated/scaled markers (47.5%+22.4%+10.5%), indicating those pre-1977 figures are reconstructions or back-cast allocations of undivided-Mato-Grosso totals rather than direct observations at the modern MS boundary. Those pre-1977 rows were therefore NOT routed to this entry — they were routed instead to the sibling polity BRA-MATOGROSSO, which already covers the undivided territory as a proposed polity for roughly 1900-2023. This entry exists to hold only the post-1977 data that genuinely describes the post-split state, rather than continuing to fold it into a national Brazil aggregate or into the undivided Mato Grosso row, which after 1977 measures a territory roughly 60% smaller than before the split (Mato Grosso do Sul's ~357,000 km2 came out of an undivided state of roughly 900,000 km2). The historical source confirming this is a distinct administrative and statistical entity is Complementary Law 31/1977 itself (the instrument of creation), together with IBGE, which has published Mato Grosso do Sul as a separate federative unit in every state-level statistical series (agricultural censuses, population counts, GDP) since 1977.

**Territory description:** Mato Grosso do Sul occupies the southern portion of what was, before 1977, the single state of Mato Grosso, in west-central Brazil. It is bounded by Paraguay to the west and south-west, Bolivia to the north-west (a short border), and by the Brazilian states of Mato Grosso (north), Goiás and Minas Gerais (east), São Paulo (south-east), and Paraná (south). Its capital is Campo Grande. The state's terrain includes the northern portion of the Pantanal wetlands along its western edge and a cerrado/agricultural plateau to the east, the latter now a major soy, corn, and cattle-ranching region. On a modern map it corresponds to Brazil's IBGE state code 50. Its area is approximately 357,000 km2 (IBGE's official published state area), noticeably smaller than the roughly 900,000 km2 of the undivided pre-1977 Mato Grosso from which it was carved — this figure is drawn from IBGE's own published state area, not measured from any polygon attached to this page, since no polygon has yet been assigned here.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Used the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern despite BRA having no existing subnational precedent**

BRA has zero existing subnational rows in the polity table, so this entry sets the country's own precedent. Rather than inventing a bespoke code, I followed the dominant pattern seen across 57 of 66 subnational rows (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end, e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025), producing BRA-MS-1977-2025, using MS as the standard IBGE two-letter abbreviation for Mato Grosso do Sul (abbrev_state in geobr-ibge). This is not a historical territory with a bespoke name of its own (unlike ALK or HYD) - it is an ordinary federative subdivision - so the bespoke-code exception does not apply, and using ISO3-SUBUNIT-years keeps later Brazilian state entries (which will follow) consistent with this first one.

### d-span-starts-1977-not-data-start

**Span starts at 1977 legal creation, not at the extract's 1900 data start**

The routing decision's proposed span (1977-2025) was adopted as-is rather than extending back to the extract's earliest data year (1900), because the high fallback_interpolated/scaled shares on the pre-1977 rows indicate those figures are reconstructions of the undivided Mato Grosso territory, not direct observations of the modern MS boundary. Those rows are routed to BRA-MATOGROSSO instead, per the routing_reasoning. This keeps the two entries territorially honest: BRA-MS-1977-2025 only ever describes the post-split boundary, and BRA-MATOGROSSO carries the pre-split back-cast.

Not verified here since it is out of scope for this entry, but worth flagging since other Brazilian subnational pages in this same batch will make the same assumption.
