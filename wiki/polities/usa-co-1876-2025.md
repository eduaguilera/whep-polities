# Colorado (state of the United States)

## Summary

Colorado is one of the fifty United States, admitted to the Union on August 1, 1876 (hence its nickname, the "Centennial State," a century after independence). Before statehood the same territory was organized as Colorado Territory (created 1861, itself carved from portions of Kansas, Nebraska, Utah, and New Mexico Territories), and this database does not model that pre-statehood territorial phase as a distinct row -- data from 1869-1875 that falls inside the routed unit is back-cast onto this polity rather than pointing at a separate territorial-era entity, matching the treatment given to sibling states with pre-admission data (e.g. California). This entry is type: subnational because Colorado is a constituent state of the sovereign United States, not a sovereign polity itself: the USA held sovereignty over this territory throughout, and the row exists purely to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate. No existing polity represented Colorado specifically before this decision -- the fallback in its absence would have been the national USA row, which would collapse state-level data into a country-level total for a state whose modern boundary (finalized substantially at admission, with only minor early surveying corrections) has been stable for a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id USA-COLORADO) captures valued statistical rows for the administrative unit "Colorado", country United States of America, under the default state-start rule of the USA country convention (Colorado is not one of the enumerated departures -- Alaska, Hawaii, or the original thirteen colonies -- so its start_year is its actual admission date, August 1, 1876). The routing_reasoning records that data years 1869-1875, predating statehood by up to seven years, are handled as back_cast onto this polity rather than a separate territorial-era row, consistent with how sibling states with pre-admission data (e.g. California) are treated. Previously matched to: nothing dedicated -- no existing polity represented Colorado specifically; absent this entry, Colorado's rows would fall back to whichever USA national-era row spans the data year (USA-1867-1959 for 1869-1959 data, USA-1959-2025 thereafter), which conflates a single state with the entire country, roughly 35x Colorado's area for the 1867-1959 national row alone (contiguous USA is on the order of 8 million km2 against Colorado's ~270,000 km2). What confirms distinctness: Colorado has been continuously tabulated as a distinct first-order administrative unit by the U.S. Census Bureau and federal agricultural/economic statistical series since territorial days, and it is exactly the same order of statistical unit as other US states already present in this database (e.g. Alaska, ALK-1867-1959) -- a state is not its country, and federal series report at state granularity as a matter of course.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Copied from the registered gadm-4.1-adm1 source, feature USA.6_1 (this is a direct source-feature assignment, not a proxy copy from a sibling polity period). gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered in scripts/sources.yaml, and unlike the situation recorded on ALK-1867-1959 in 2026-06 -- where the GADM extract on disk was a curated subset that excluded the United States entirely -- the local extract now includes data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_USA.gpkg, whose ADM_ADM_1 layer contains a single feature with GID_1 "USA.6_1", NAME_1 "Colorado", VARNAME_1 "CO|Colo.": an unambiguous match. polygon_status is therefore assigned, not proxy, since this is the state's own modern GADM boundary, not a boundary borrowed from a different period or a different unit.

Vintage risk across the 1876-2025 span is low: Colorado's borders were essentially fixed at admission in 1876, with only minor early surveying corrections (e.g. the 1879-1925 disputes over the Colorado-Utah and Colorado-New Mexico boundary surveys, none of which materially changed the state's area), so the present-day (~2020s) GADM boundary is a good proxy for the state's boundary throughout its 149-year existence as a state, and for the 1869-1875 back-cast territorial-era data, which fell within the same eventual state boundary.

Territory description: Colorado occupies a rectangular block of the south-central Rocky Mountain region of the United States, bordered by Wyoming and Nebraska to the north, Kansas to the east, Oklahoma and New Mexico to the south, and Utah to the west, with its southeast corner touching the Oklahoma panhandle. It spans the Southern Rocky Mountains (including the state's 58 "fourteener" peaks) in its western half and the High Plains in its eastern half; its capital and largest city is Denver. Its area is approximately 269,743 km2 as measured from the attached gadm-4.1-adm1 geometry in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034) -- this figure is a measurement of the attached polygon, not an independently sourced official area, though it is consistent with commonly cited state-area figures for Colorado (~269,600-270,000 km2 depending on land-vs-total-area convention), which corroborates rather than substitutes for the geometric measurement.

## Predecessors and successors

Colorado has no predecessor polity in this database: before the 1876 admission, the same territory was organized first as unorganized land split among Kansas, Nebraska, Utah, and New Mexico Territories, then from 1861 as the single Colorado Territory, but this database does not model that pre-statehood territorial phase as a distinct row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent entity -- and the 1869-1875 data that falls inside the territorial period is instead back-cast onto this polity per the routing decision. It has no successor either: Colorado remains a current, active US state as of 2025, hence the open end_year of 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention used for other still-active USA subnational rows.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-state-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter postal abbreviation CO rather than a numeric FIPS code, matching the dominant subnational pattern**

The dominant subnational pattern measured from the polity table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, covering 57 of 66 subnational rows, using short letter abbreviations (as already established for the Spanish province rows ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-LU, ESP-OR, ESP-PO). This page follows that convention, minting USA-CO-1876-2025 with CO as the standard USPS/ISO 3166-2 postal abbreviation for Colorado, rather than a FIPS numeric code (08) or a bespoke historical name. A bespoke code (the ALK/HYD/RYU pattern) was not used because Colorado, unlike the Territory of Alaska, is not a historical territory with its own distinct name and identity separate from its role as a present-day US state -- it is simply one of the fifty states, so the standard <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT> pattern applies directly, keeping the code predictable for any future US-state entry without a lookup table.

### d-two-container-edges-for-usa-national-eras

**Split the container into two edges to tile Colorado's full 1876-2025 span across two USA national-era rows**

Colorado's span (1876-2025) crosses the boundary between two USA national-era rows in the polity table: USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025. A single container edge pointing at only one of these would leave part of Colorado's span uncontained and would be rejected by the containment gate, which requires an edge to fall entirely within both parties' spans. This page therefore uses two container edges -- USA-1867-1959 for 1876-1959 and USA-1959-2025 for 1959-2025 -- together tiling the full 1876-2025 span with no gap, following the same one-edge-per-containing-era practice used elsewhere in the table (e.g. MAN-1950-1955's single edge onto CHN-1950-2025 for its shorter span).

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1876-territorial-data-backcast

**1869-1875 pre-statehood data is back-cast onto the 1876-2025 state boundary without a distinct territorial-era row**

The routing decision states that data years 1869-1875 (predating the August 1876 admission) are handled as back_cast onto this polity, consistent with treatment of other states with pre-admission data such as California. But Colorado Territory (1861-1876) was not identical in boundary to the eventual state -- it was carved from, and its boundary finalized within, portions of four different pre-existing territories, and its own territorial boundary was already essentially the modern state boundary by the time of the 1869-1875 data. This entry does not verify that the 1869-1875 rows in fact refer to the same geographic extent as the modern GADM feature USA.6_1, only that the routing decision asserts back-casting is appropriate. Whoever next touches sourced data for this polity should confirm the 1869-1875 rows describe the Colorado Territory boundary as it stood in that period, not an earlier or larger unorganized-territory extent, before treating the back-cast as settled.

### oq-boundary-vintage-minor-surveys

**GADM's present-day boundary may not reflect early 20th-century boundary survey corrections**

Colorado's boundary was subject to several early boundary-survey disputes and corrections after admission -- notably resurveys along the Colorado-Utah line (early 1900s) and points along the New Mexico border -- that shifted the legal boundary by small amounts relative to the original 1876 admission boundary. gadm-4.1-adm1's USA.6_1 feature reflects the present-day (~2020s) legally settled boundary, which postdates all of these corrections. This entry treats the vintage risk as low because the corrections were minor relative to the state's ~270,000 km2 area, but that has not been quantified here (no km2 figure for the pre-correction boundary was checked against the post-correction one), so a future author with access to a historical-vintage Colorado boundary source should verify the assumption rather than take it as confirmed.
