# Lisboa (district/NUTS region of Portugal)

## Summary

This entry captures Distrito de Lisboa, one of Portugal's mainland administrative districts established in the 1835 reform that carved continental Portugal into districts (the same reform basis already used for this country's Aveiro entry). It is coded as a subnational unit (type: subnational) because Portugal itself held continuous sovereignty over this territory throughout the entry's span -- the district is a sub-national reporting and administrative tier, not an independent or quasi-independent state, and must not compete with the national PRT-1800-2025 row in any family/sovereignty-based matching. The entry exists because a set of production/trade data rows labelled at the 'Lisboa' (PT_LI) district level, running 1880-2023 with a 96.2% observed/blank method share, were previously being folded into the national PRT-1800-2025 total, which overstates the reporting scope of those specific rows by covering the whole of mainland and island Portugal rather than the single district that was actually measured. The district persists today, restyled under the NUTS classification system, but the administrative entity is the same continuous unit; the NUTS label is a modern relabelling, not a new territory. Its span is set from the district's own 1835 origin (not from 1880, where the extracted data happens to start) through the open end_year 2025, since no evidence indicates the district's dissolution.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: source rows labelled 'Lisboa' (unit PT_LI) spanning 1880-2023, with a 96.2% observed/blank method share, drawn from the country's production/trade extraction pipeline (row count and exact source table not independently re-tallied in this authoring pass; taken from the routing decision's stated method-share figure). Previous matching: these rows were being routed to PRT-1800-2025, the whole-country national total, which is roughly the entire ~89,000+ km2 of mainland and island Portugal -- many times the area of a single district -- so any district-level figure (production volumes, trade flows, etc.) reported under that national code would be misread as national-scale when it is in fact one district's contribution. Confirmation of distinctness: Distrito de Lisboa is a standing administrative unit of the Portuguese state, created in the 1835 district reform and still in active use today (restyled under NUTS), independently tabulated in national statistics separately from whole-country totals -- the same basis (a genuine, persistently-measured sub-national administrative unit) that justified creating the sibling Aveiro district entry in this country's convention.

Territory description: Distrito de Lisboa is the historic district centred on the city of Lisbon, on Portugal's Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Tagus (Tejo) estuary, roughly bounded by the districts of Leiria to the north, Santarém to the northeast, and Setúbal to the south and east across the Tagus. On a modern map this corresponds to the Grande Lisboa and Oeste sub-regions together with the city of Lisbon itself -- an area on the order of a few thousand km2 (Portugal's 18 mainland districts average roughly 4,700 km2 each across the country's ~89,000 km2 mainland area; Lisboa district, being coastal and containing the capital, is expected to be on the smaller side of that average, but no figure is confirmed here pending polygon assignment).

## Predecessors and successors

This entry has no predecessor: it begins at the district's own administrative creation in 1835 under the Kingdom of Portugal, not as a continuation of an earlier reporting unit. It has no successor either, running with an open end_year of 2025 as the current-day district/NUTS III-aligned region -- the same NUTS classification is simply the present-day label for a territory that predates it by roughly 150 years. It should not be confused with, or treated as continuous with, 'Área Metropolitana de Lisboa', a distinct and more recent metropolitan unit with its own 2015 departure recorded separately in this country's convention.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Code follows the ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke name**

Chose PRT-LI-1835-2025 over a bespoke code because Lisboa distrito is a generic administrative subdivision created by the same 1835 reform that produced every other Portuguese district (Aveiro included, per the country convention already in use), not a historical territory with an identity independent of the national administrative grid. This matches 57 of 66 precedent rows (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025) and is the same reasoning that would apply to any other Portuguese district entry, so no bespoke code is warranted here. 'LI' is used as the subunit code for Lisboa to avoid collision with any national-level 'PT' abbreviation and to stay legible against the NUTS/ISO short form.

### d-span-1835-not-1880

**Span starts at the district's 1835 creation, not at the 1880 data start**

The extract that motivated this entry only has observations from 1880, but the reporting unit -- Distrito de Lisboa -- existed as an administrative entity from 1835, sixteen years before Portugal's earliest returns for this district would plausibly have been tabulated at all. Anchoring start_year to the data's own first observed year would conflate 'when we have data' with 'when the territory existed', and would produce an artificial predecessor gap between 1835 and 1880 that no other polity fills. The same logic already governs the Aveiro district entry in this country's convention, so 1835 keeps this entry internally consistent with a sibling row rather than idiosyncratic to this dataset.

### d-polygon-unassigned-not-copied

**Polygon left unassigned rather than copied from a national or metropolitan proxy**

PRT-1800-2025 covers the whole of Portugal and PT_LI (Área Metropolitana de Lisboa) is a distinct, much smaller and more recent unit with its own 2015 departure; neither is a valid proxy for the 1835 district boundary, so no polygon is copied. gadm-4.1-adm1 is the correct registered source and PRT.11_1 the correct feature (Portugal's district-level GADM admin-1 layer), but the locally-fetched GADM subset covers only 81 countries and Portugal is not among them, so the feature cannot be verified today. polygon_source is therefore set to the registered slug 'gadm-4.1-adm1' (not the route name 'registered_source_unfetched', which is not a valid slug), with polygon_status 'unassigned' until the fetch happens.

## Open questions

### oq-1835-creation-date

**1835 creation date for Distrito de Lisboa is inferred by analogy, not independently confirmed**

The 1835 start_year rests entirely on analogy to the Aveiro district's departure already recorded in this country's convention; no primary source (e.g. the actual 1835 administrative-reform decree, or a Portuguese public-administration history) has been independently checked for Lisboa specifically. Portuguese district boundaries and even district creation dates varied somewhat by region during the 19th-century reforms, and it is possible Lisboa's district was formalized in a slightly different year than Aveiro's. This should be confirmed against a primary or secondary administrative-history source before the 1835 start_year is treated as settled rather than inherited.

### oq-metropolitan-lisboa-conflation

**Risk of conflation between Distrito de Lisboa and Área Metropolitana de Lisboa**

The source unit PT_LI ('Lisboa') must be kept distinct from 'Área Metropolitana de Lisboa', a different and more recent NUTS-aligned unit with its own 2015 departure noted elsewhere in the country convention. If any of the 1880-2023 rows routed to this entry actually describe the metropolitan area rather than the historic district (they cover different territory and the metropolitan area is roughly half the district's size), those rows would be mis-territorialized under this code. This has not been checked row-by-row and should be verified against the raw source labels before treating the full 1880-2023 span as belonging cleanly to the district.
