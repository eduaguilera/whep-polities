# Quintana Roo

## Summary

Quintana Roo is the Mexican federal entity occupying the eastern third of the Yucatan Peninsula, bordering the Caribbean Sea, Belize, and Guatemala. It was carved out of the state of Yucatan in 1902 as a Federal Territory -- a category of Mexican subdivision administered directly by the federal government rather than self-governing as a state -- and remained one for over seven decades, a period covering the low-population, low-infrastructure era before mass tourism development at Cancun (begun in the 1970s). In 1974 it was converted into the 31st state of the federation, gaining full statehood (its own constitution, legislature, and governor) without any change to its external borders or name. This entry spans both eras as a single continuous polity (1902-2025, open end per the project's country convention for still-current units) because the 1974 event was a change in constitutional status, not a redrawing of territory or a re-founding of the reporting unit: the same named administrative area was measured continuously by statistical sources across the transition. `type: subnational` reflects that Quintana Roo was never a sovereign polity in either era -- it was always a first-order administrative division of Mexico, first as a directly-administered federal territory and later as a self-governing state within the federation -- distinguishing it from the small set of bespoke-coded entries in this table (Alaska, Hyderabad, Ryukyu) that carry independent historical identities of their own.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Mexican statistical data reported under a "Quintana Roo" label, spanning the territory's creation in 1902 through the present. It exists because no polity row previously represented this specific reporting territory at any point in the table: the only existing MEX row is the national total (MEX-1848-2025), and data at Quintana Roo's sub-national scale was previously either unmatched or incorrectly routed to that national total, which is roughly 43 times larger in area and aggregates all 32 of Mexico's federal entities. Routing state-level series to the national row silently misattributes territorially-specific figures to the whole country. What confirms Quintana Roo is a distinct, continuously-measured reporting unit rather than an artifact of the national series is that it has had its own formal administrative identity -- first as a Federal Territory decreed in 1902, then as a constitutionally distinct state from 1974 -- under which Mexican federal statistical agencies (and their historical predecessors) have consistently published disaggregated figures, exactly the kind of independently-tabulated administrative unit this table's subnational rows are meant to capture. Two data years (1900-1901), predating the territory's 1902 creation, could not be attributed to this identity and are called out as unresolved rather than silently dropped or force-fit.

## Territorial extent

The routing decision identified GADM 4.1's admin-1 layer as the correct registered source (Quintana Roo appears there as feature MEX.23_1), but the project's local extract of that dataset (data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg) only covers 81 countries and Mexico is not among them -- it returns zero features for any MEX admin-1 unit. This is a fetch gap in an already-registered source, not a missing source, so polygon_source is set to the registered slug gadm-4.1-adm1 with polygon_status: unassigned rather than none, per the routing rule that a registered-but-unfetched source keeps its slug. No area figure is recorded here since no geometry is attached; see the open question on re-fetching the source.

Territory description: Quintana Roo occupies the eastern portion of the Yucatan Peninsula in southeastern Mexico, fronting the Caribbean Sea along its entire eastern edge, with Belize on its southern border and a short border with Guatemala at its southwestern tip; to the west it borders the Mexican states of Yucatan and Campeche. It contains, among other places, Cancun, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, and Chetumal (the state capital). The modern state's area is approximately 44,700 km2, comparable in size to Denmark or the U.S. state of Ohio, and this figure has been effectively stable since the territory's 1902 creation as external borders were not altered by the 1974 statehood conversion, though this has not been independently confirmed against a period-specific historical source (see open question below on boundary vintage).

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor is declared: the 1900-1901 data years that arguably belong to this reporting unit predate its 1902 creation and are left as an open question rather than folded into a predecessor edge to Yucatan, since it is unconfirmed whether Yucatan's own polity row already accounts for that territory in those years. No successor is declared either -- the entry runs open-ended to 2025 per the country convention, since Quintana Roo continues to exist as a Mexican state with unchanged borders and name after its 1974 conversion from federal territory to state, which this entry treats as a status change internal to one continuous polity rather than a succession event.

## Sourced claims

- The Federal Territory of Quintana Roo was created in 1902, carved out of the state of Yucatan, as part of the Porfiriato-era reorganization of Mexico's southeastern territories (alongside the earlier 1893 creation of the Federal Territory of Tepic).
- Quintana Roo was converted from a federal territory into the 31st state of the Mexican federation in 1974, under President Luis Echeverria, without a change to its external administrative boundaries.
- GADM 4.1's admin-1 layer digitizes Quintana Roo under feature code MEX.23_1, reflecting its modern (post-1974) state boundary, but the local project extract of that dataset currently omits Mexico entirely (0 of the country's 32 admin-1 units are present).

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

Quintana Roo is a standard first-order Mexican administrative division (Federal Territory 1902, State from 1974) with no historical name or identity independent of that administrative role, unlike the three bespoke-coded cases (Alaska, Hyderabad, Ryukyu), which are historical territories with names of their own predating or independent of their administrative status. The 57-of-66 dominant pattern applies directly: MEX-QROO-1902-2025, using QROO as a short, unambiguous subunit abbreviation (Mexico has no existing subnational rows to set a country-specific abbreviation convention, so this is the first precedent for MEX).

### d-single-span-not-split-at-1974

**Kept one continuous polity across the 1902 territory-to-state transition**

The routing decision explicitly considered and rejected splitting into a Territory (1902-1974) and State (1974-2025) polity. I followed that call: the territory's borders and name did not change in 1974, only its constitutional status within the federation (from federal territory to full state), which is a status change, not a re-founding or boundary event. This mirrors how the table treats similar status-only transitions elsewhere without introducing a new polity row, and avoids fragmenting a single continuous reporting unit into two rows that would need to be stitched back together by any consumer wanting a 1902-2025 series.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-mex-not-fetched

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 source needs to be re-fetched to include Mexico**

polygon_source is set to the registered slug gadm-4.1-adm1 with polygon_status: unassigned because the local extract at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg only covers 81 countries and Mexico is not among them (0 features returned for MEX). GADM 4.1 does publish Mexican admin-1 units, including Quintana Roo under GID_1 pattern MEX.23_1, so no new source needs to be registered -- the existing local copy just needs to be extended or re-fetched to include MEX. Until that happens this entry carries no geometry and no area figure.

### oq-territorial-vs-state-boundary-vintage

**Modern GADM boundary is a vintage proxy for the pre-1974 Federal Territory era**

Once fetched, the GADM 4.1 adm1 feature for Quintana Roo reflects the state's current, post-1974 extent. Whether the 1902-1974 Federal Territory of Quintana Roo had exactly the same borders is unconfirmed here -- Mexican federal territories occasionally saw minor boundary adjustments before statehood. This should be checked against a historical source (e.g., INEGI's territorial-division archives or a Mexican historical atlas) before treating the modern polygon as a valid proxy for the full 1902-2025 span; if the pre-1974 borders differed, the proxy should be documented with the same km2-based reasoning used elsewhere in this table rather than assumed identical.

### oq-1900-1901-predecessor-data

**Two data years (1900-1901) predate this polity's creation and are unassigned**

The routing decision notes 1900-1901 data rows exist for what appears to be this same reporting unit, but Quintana Roo was not created until 1902 (carved from Yucatan), so this entry's start_year excludes them. Whether those two years should be attributed to Yucatan as the predecessor territory, treated as a data artifact (e.g., a mislabeled early edition of the territory before its formal 1902 decree), or handled some other way is unresolved and was not addressed by creating this polity; no predecessor edge is declared here pending that determination.
