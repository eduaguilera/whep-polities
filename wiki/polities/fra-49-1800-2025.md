# Maine-et-Loire (department of France)

## Summary

Maine-et-Loire is one of France's original ~83 departments, created by the Revolutionary administrative reform of 4 March 1790 out of parts of the former provinces of Anjou, Poitou, Touraine and Maine, centered on the Loire river town of Angers. Unlike Belfort (carved out of the Territoire de Belfort after 1871), Alsace-Lorraine (annexed by Germany 1871-1919), the departments created from the 1975 Corsica split, or the overseas departments created in 1946 -- the four departures from the default 1790 start rule already established for French subnational entries in this database -- Maine-et-Loire is an ordinary original department whose boundaries have been stable since creation and required no exceptional treatment. This entry is type: subnational because Maine-et-Loire is a sub-unit of the sovereign French state, not a state in its own right: France held sovereignty over this territory throughout its existence, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate (the FRA national chain: FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) and distinguishable from it because it has 97.6% observed/blank data coverage in the routing decision's source series, indicating continuous department-level statistical reporting rather than a residual or national-total bucket.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id FRA-FRG02, admin_name FRG02) captures valued statistical rows for Maine-et-Loire, country France, with 97.6% observed/blank data coverage across its source series -- a continuous department-level reporting geography, not a residual bucket or national total. Previously matched to: nothing existing in this database, since France has zero subnational polities prior to this entry (0 name candidates, 0 GADM adm1 matches found by the routing pipeline); the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national FRA aggregate (FRA-1800-1871/FRA-1871-1919/FRA-1919-2025 chain), which would collapse a department-level series into a country-level one covering roughly 75x Maine-et-Loire's own area. What confirms distinctness: Maine-et-Loire is one of the original ~83 departments fixed by the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform, and INSEE has tabulated it as a continuous department-level statistical and administrative unit (code 49) since that reform -- the same kind of durable, separately-reported administrative geography already accepted as grounds for creating subnational rows for Spanish provinces in this database.

## Predecessors and successors

Maine-et-Loire has no predecessor polity in this database: before its creation on 4 March 1790, the territory was divided among parts of the pre-Revolutionary provinces of Anjou, Poitou, Touraine and Maine, none of which this database models as a distinct polity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row. There is no successor either: the department remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of France as of 2025 (part of the Pays de la Loire administrative region since 1972, alongside Loire-Atlantique, Mayenne, Sarthe and Vendee), hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive). Its start_year in this entry is 1800 rather than the department's true 1790 creation date, for the container-tiling reason recorded in decision d-start-year-1800-not-1790 and open question oq-pre1800-gap-uncontained.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Started this entry at 1800, not the proposed 1790 creation date of the department**

The routing decision's proposed start_year was 1790, the year the Revolutionary reform created Maine-et-Loire and the ~83 original departments. That date cannot be used here: the FRA container chain in this database begins at FRA-1800-1871, with nothing covering 1790-1800, and the container tiling rule requires every year of a subnational row's span to be covered by a container edge with no gap. Starting this entry at 1790 would leave 1790-1800 uncontained and fail the containment gate. Rather than inventing a container row for a decade this database does not otherwise model for France, this entry starts at 1800, aligned with the earliest FRA container actually present. The 1790-1799 gap in coverage of Maine-et-Loire as a department is recorded as an open question below; the department's own administrative existence is unaffected, since it was still Maine-et-Loire under the Revolutionary and Consulate governments in that decade, but this database simply does not carry a French national polity for it to sit inside yet.

### d-code-numeric-insee

**Used the numeric INSEE department code 49, not a letter abbreviation, as the subunit code**

France has no subnational precedent in this database, so the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern from other countries (e.g. ESP uses single- or double-letter vehicle-plate/INE codes) does not transfer directly, because French departments are not conventionally abbreviated by letters -- they are identified by the two-digit INSEE/postal department number fixed since the 1790 creation and still in universal administrative, postal and vehicle-plate use today. Maine-et-Loire's INSEE code is 49. Using this number rather than inventing a letter code (e.g. ML, which is not how this department is referred to in any French administrative or statistical source) follows the same logic as the ESP precedent -- use the subunit's own official, stable identifying code -- while respecting that France's own code system is numeric, not alphabetic. This choice sets the precedent for any sibling French department rows created in the same batch, which should follow the same INSEE-numeric convention.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1800-gap-uncontained

**Maine-et-Loire existed from 1790, but no FRA container covers 1790-1800**

The department was created on 4 March 1790 by the Revolutionary administrative reform and has existed continuously since, but this database's earliest national France row is FRA-1800-1871, so this entry's span was truncated to start at 1800 rather than 1790 (see decision d-start-year-1800-not-1790). If a pre-1800 French national polity is ever added to this database (covering the Revolutionary and Consulate period, roughly 1790-1800), this entry's start_year and its container list should be revisited to extend back to 1790, and any data rows for Maine-et-Loire dated 1790-1799 that are currently unroutable or dropped should be re-examined.

### oq-polygon-source-not-fetched

**The candidate polygon source (GADM 4.1 ADM1, full/global download) has not been acquired**

The routing decision found that the locally registered gadm-4.1-adm1 source is a curated 81-country subset that excludes France, so no registered source can currently produce a Maine-et-Loire feature; polygon_route was set to new_source_needed, recommending either the full/global GADM 4.1 ADM1 download or French IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS department boundaries. Neither has been fetched or placed in data/geodata/ as of this entry's creation, so polygon_status is unassigned and polygon_feature_id is null. Once one of these sources is downloaded and registered, this entry should be revisited to assign the actual feature, most likely under a stable ID keyed to INSEE code 49.

### oq-luh2-residual-share

**1.4% of this unit's rows used a luh2_residual method -- unclear if they predate 1790 or represent reconstructed values**

The routing decision's concerns flagged that 1.4% of Maine-et-Loire's data rows were derived via a luh2_residual method and were folded into 'proposed' without further investigation. It is unresolved whether these rows fall in years before the department existed (pre-1790, which would need back_cast treatment rather than direct routing to this polity) or in the 1790-1799 window this entry currently excludes (see oq-pre1800-gap-uncontained), or whether they are ordinary reconstructed values within 1800-2025 that need no special handling. This should be checked against the row-level year values before treating this entry's ingest as complete.
