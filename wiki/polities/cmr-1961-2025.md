# Cameroon

## Summary

Cameroon is a WHEP polity row covering the territory of modern Cameroon from 1961 to the present. The 1961 start date reflects the unification of French Cameroun (independent 1 January 1960) with the southern portion of British Cameroons on 1 October 1961. The two parts were federated in 1961 and unified in 1972.

## Territorial extent

Cameroon (area 183,568 sq. miles; 475,441 sq. km) is situated on the west coast of central Africa, bounded by Nigeria (1,050 miles; 1,690 km), Chad (680 miles; 1,094 km), Central African Republic (495 miles; 797 km), Congo (325 miles; 523 km), Gabon (185 miles; 298 km), and Equatorial Guinea (118 miles; 189 km). Capital: Yaounde. The polygon source is CShapes 2.0.

## Sourced claims

- "Cameroon was a German colony until World War I. Following Germany's defeat it was divided as a mandate between France and Britain" (p.135). The first political party to emerge was the UPC (Union des Populations du Cameroun), which demanded independence. [biger-1995 §cameroon](../sources/biger-1995.md#cameroon)
- "Today's Cameroon was originally the German protectorate Kamerun, which was proclaimed on 12 July 1884" (p.136). The border between the French and German spheres of influence was defined in a convention concluded on 15 March 1894. [biger-1995 §cameroon](../sources/biger-1995.md#cameroon)
- "The German colony of Kamerun was occupied by British and French forces during World War I, who later divided the area between them and held separate mandates" (p.136). French Cameroon became independent on 1 January 1960 while Oubangui-Chari became the Central African Republic on 13 August 1960. [biger-1995 §cameroon](../sources/biger-1995.md#cameroon)

## Open questions

### oq-british-cameroons-split

British Cameroons split. At the 1961 plebiscite, the northern part of British Cameroons voted to join Nigeria while the southern part joined Cameroon. This territorial addition distinguishes CMR-1961-2025 from CMR-1960-1961.
