# Omani Empire

## Summary

The Omani Empire covers the period of the unified Sultanate of Muscat and Oman, which at its height controlled not only the Arabian coastal heartland but also extensive East African territories including Zanzibar, Pemba, and the mainland coast (modern-day Kenya and Tanzania). Said bin Sultan (ruled 1806–1856) moved his capital to Zanzibar in 1840, making the East African coast the commercial and political centre of the empire. Upon his death in 1856, the empire split under a British-mediated succession arrangement: Zanzibar went to one son and Oman to another. The 1856 split is the end event for this WHEP row, creating two separate successor polities — OMN-1856-2025 (Sultanate of Oman) and ZAN-1856-1964 (Zanzibar). The Omani Empire had no COW code; CShapes does not cover this pre-1886 period.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Cliopatria v0.1.3 (Sultanate of Oman 1840–1855 time-step), representing approximately 176,000 km² at near-peak imperial extent including East African holdings. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §scope](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#scope)

The single-time-step limitation applies: WHEP extracts one Cliopatria snapshot. Territorial control along the East African coast varied significantly during 1800–1856, with substantial Portuguese-period legacy claims giving way to firmer Omani control as the 19th century progressed. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §single-time-step](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#single-time-step)

CShapes' 1886 floor places this entire period outside CShapes' scope. [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope)

## Open questions

### oq-zanzibar-page

The successor ZAN-1856-1964 is listed in the WHEP CSV (per the Cliopatria-sourced notes for OMN-1800-1856) but no wiki page for it has been created. This is a dangling reference. The next ingest should create the ZAN-1856-1964 page.

### oq-east-africa-coast-control

Omani control of the East African coast was exercised through the Busaidi dynasty's trading network and treaties, not through a continuous land-border polity in the European sense. Whether the Cliopatria polygon correctly represents the coastal strip vs. the interior is unknown. A future ingest should compare the Cliopatria polygon against historical map sources for 19th-century East Africa.

### oq-pre-1800-oman

Oman's territorial history before 1800 (including the early 18th-century Yaruba dynasty, Portuguese expulsion in 1650, and Persian occupation 1737–1744) is not captured by the WHEP 1800 start date. This is a structural limitation of the WHEP dataset, not a page-level issue.
