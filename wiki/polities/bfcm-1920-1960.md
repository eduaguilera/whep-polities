# British and French Cameroons (combined reporting unit, 1920-1960)

## Summary

The two League of Nations mandates into which German Kamerun was divided after the First World War:
French Cameroun (the east, about four fifths) and British Cameroons (two strips along the Nigerian
border, administered from Lagos). German Kamerun was occupied by British and French troops during
the war and "later mandated to each country by the League of Nations in 1922".
[wikipedia-french-africa-mandates-2026-08-13 §french-cameroon](../sources/wikipedia-french-africa-mandates-2026-08-13.md#french-cameroon)

This row is the **two mandates together, as one reporting unit**. Both also exist separately
([fcm-1920-1960](fcm-1920-1960.md), [bcm-1916-1961](bcm-1916-1961.md)), which is why it is typed
`aggregate`: the pattern of [gct-1919-1956](gct-1919-1956.md) (Gold Coast plus British Togoland) and
[syl-1944-1953](syl-1944-1953.md).

## Why this entry exists

Issue 687 matched every layer-B iia `cameroon` series cell by cell against the raw IIA extract
(`harmonized_data.xlsx`), on the exact `(unit, year or period, value)` triple. The raw extract has
three Cameroon headings: `french cameroon`, `british cameroon` and **`british-french cameroon`**, the
combined figure for both mandates. Six items of the layer-B `cameroon` label are the combined figure:

- **sesame seed**: 20 of 20 area cells and 20 of 20 production cells match `british-french cameroon`.
- **groundnuts, with shell**: 12 of 12 area and 4 of 4 production cells. (The 1932 area cell is the
  620,004,098 ha misread recorded as `cmr-1932-groundnut-area-620-million-ha`; it moves with its
  series and stays recorded.)
- **tobacco, unmanufactured**: 4 of 4 production cells; the 5 area cells are zeros.
- **cotton seed** (14 area, 13 production) and **cotton lint** (6 area, 5 production): every cell
  matches the combined heading, but the values are round (100, 200 and 1,000), which match many
  headings at once. What decides them is that the raw extract has **no** French-only or British-only
  cotton series at all: every Cameroon cotton figure the IIA printed is under the combined heading.
- **cacao, beans**: 15 of the 15 dated area cells match `british-french cameroon`. None of the three
  area period averages and none of the three production period averages matches the combined
  heading exactly, so they cannot be decided by fingerprint. The production control is an independent source: fao1952 gives
  1934-1938 averages of 24.8 thousand t for French and 4.6 for British Cameroons, 29.4 together,
  and the iia 1934-1938 figure is 30.6, 4% from the combined total and 23% above the French one.

Two items stay on FCM-1920-1960, because they match `french cameroon`: coffee (area 8 of 8) and
rubber (area 9 of 15, and the production cell).

The combined figures had been routed to FCM-1920-1960 (French Cameroun only), which overstated the
French mandate by the British one's output. Routing them to BCM or to both would have been worse.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status: `assigned`.** `constructed`: the union of CShapes 2.0 gwcode 471 (French
Cameroun, 423,069 km²) and gwcode 472 (British Cameroons, 87,444 km²), both sampled at 1930, inside
471's 1922-1959 step and 472's 1922-1961 step. The union measures **510,512 km²** (ESRI:54034).
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-dependencies](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-dependencies)

- **Corroboration.** It equals CShapes' own German Kamerun before the 1911 Neukamerun cession
  (gwcode 470, 1901-1912 step, 510,512 km²) to the km². The two mandates divided exactly
  Altkamerun, and Neukamerun went back to French Equatorial Africa in 1916, so the union is a
  territory CShapes itself draws, not an approximation.
- **Why `assigned`.** Both parts are CShapes' own mandate-era features and nothing is estimated.

## Polygon status

`assigned` — constructed union of two CShapes 2.0 features, 510,512 km².

**No declared area.** `polygon_area_km2` is left empty: the only figure at hand is the polygon's
own measurement (510,512 km²), and declaring it would compare the polygon with itself.

## Decisions

- **Span 1920-1960**, the span of French Cameroun's row. The earliest combined iia cell is 1923.
- **Item-scoped corrections, not an alias**, because the same label and years carry French-only
  coffee and rubber.
