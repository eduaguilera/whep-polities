# Meurthe-et-Moselle

## Summary

Meurthe-et-Moselle is a French département in the northeast of the country (prefecture Nancy, department number 54), created in 1871 when the arrondissements of the historic départements of Meurthe and Moselle that remained French after the Franco-Prussian War were merged into a single new unit -- the rest of Moselle, plus Alsace, having been annexed by the German Empire under the Treaty of Frankfurt. This entry is `type: subnational` because a département is an administrative subdivision of France, never a sovereign polity in its own right; sovereignty over this territory belongs throughout to the FRA national chain, which is why the entry is containered against FRA-1871-1919 and then FRA-1919-2025 rather than standing alone. The entry exists to receive department-level historical statistics (reported by mitchell and/or comparable series covering roughly 1862-2023 per the routing decision) that are too fine-grained for the national FRA row: routing them to FRA-1919-2025 alone would conflate one département's output with that of the whole country, a roughly hundred-fold difference in scale. The department has retained essentially the same boundary since 1871 (aside from being placed in the larger Grand Est administrative region in 2016, which does not alter the département's own extent), so a single span to the present is appropriate once the start year is corrected to the department's actual creation date.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: a historical statistical series (per the routing decision, apparently mitchell or a comparable compilation) reporting values for the administrative unit labelled FRF31 / "Meurthe-et-Moselle", spanning 1862-2023, 97.6% observed rather than back-cast, with no row count given in the decision payload itself. Previously this data had no dedicated polity: the routing decision found zero existing candidates matching this specific département by name, so absent this entry the rows would have to be forced onto one of the four national FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), each of which covers the entire country and would overstate the reporting territory by roughly two orders of magnitude. What confirms the entity is distinct: Meurthe-et-Moselle is one of France's 96 metropolitan départements, each independently administered and independently enumerated in French national statistics (INSEE) since the department's 1871 creation -- it is not a subdivision that shares reporting with neighboring départements, and its post-1871 boundary is a matter of historical record (Treaty of Frankfurt cession lines), distinguishing it clearly from the pre-1871 Meurthe it was formed from.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. **Proxy deliberately not copied from a department-shaped source because none exists in this repository** -- instead the whole-country France polygon (cshapes-2.0, feature FRA-1919-2025) is attached as a stand-in, per the routing decision, because no registered source carries French département-level boundaries locally (gadm-4.1-adm1 excludes FRA entirely; gadm-3.6, which would have adm2-level French departments, is not present on disk). This is recorded as `polygon_status: proxy` rather than `assigned` because the geometry is not actually Meurthe-et-Moselle's boundary at any scale -- it is roughly 105 times larger.

Territory description: Meurthe-et-Moselle sits in the Lorraine part of modern Grand Est, between Nancy (its prefecture) in the south and Longwy/the Luxembourg-Belgium border in the north. On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE department 54, bordered by Moselle to the east, Meuse to the west, Vosges to the south, and Belgium/Luxembourg to the north. Its actual area is approximately 5,246 km2 (INSEE). The polygon attached to this entry instead measures the whole of France: **551,695 km2** as computed from the attached cshapes-2.0 geometry in an equal-area projection -- this figure is a measurement of the substitute polygon, not evidence about Meurthe-et-Moselle's own size, and should not be read as the department's territory.

## Predecessors and successors

Meurthe-et-Moselle has no predecessor row and no successor row in this table. It was created in 1871 from the French-retained arrondissements of the historic départements of Meurthe and Moselle after the Franco-Prussian War, but the pre-1871 Meurthe département is not itself represented as a polity row here, so no predecessor code exists to cite. The department has continued under this name and this general extent from 1871 to the present, so it has no successor either; it remains an active French département today, part of the Grand Est region since 2016 (an administrative-region change above the department that does not alter the department's own boundary or code).

## Sourced claims

- Meurthe-et-Moselle was created by decree in 1871, merging the arrondissements of Nancy, Toul, Lunéville and Briey that remained French after the Treaty of Frankfurt ceded most of the historic Moselle département and part of Meurthe to the new German Empire.
- Mitchell's International Historical Statistics: Europe reports agricultural and industrial series for Meurthe-et-Moselle from the late 19th century, consistent with the routing decision's stated data range beginning in 1862 (partly predating the department's 1871 creation).
- INSEE assigns Meurthe-et-Moselle department code 54, and gives its area as approximately 5,246 km2, with prefecture Nancy.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1871-not-1790

**Start year corrected to 1871, the department's actual creation, not 1790**

The routing decision this page implements asserted Meurthe-et-Moselle is an original 1790 Revolutionary département still in its present form, and proposed start_year 1790 on that basis. That is factually wrong: Meurthe-et-Moselle did not exist in 1790. The 1790 department covering this area was named Meurthe. Meurthe-et-Moselle was created in 1871, after the Franco-Prussian War, by merging the arrondissements of the historic Meurthe and Moselle departments that remained French once Germany annexed the rest of Moselle plus the Alsace departments (Bas-Rhin, Haut-Rhin) and the eastern cantons of Meurthe under the Treaty of Frankfurt. The pre-1871 department (Meurthe) is a distinct administrative entity with different boundaries and has no row in this table. Using 1790 here would misattribute pre-1871 Meurthe history to a polity that is really the post-1871 rump. start_year is therefore set to 1871, which also happens to be exactly where the FRA container chain in the table splits (FRA-1800-1871 / FRA-1871-1919), so the corrected span tiles cleanly against existing container rows without leaving any uncontained years.

### d-polygon-code-department-number

**Polity code uses the INSEE department number (54), not an invented abbreviation**

The dominant subnational pattern is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. For French départements the standard, unambiguous subunit identifier is the INSEE department number (Meurthe-et-Moselle is 54), not a name-derived abbreviation, since France has several départements that could produce colliding two/three-letter abbreviations (e.g. Meurthe-et-Moselle vs Moselle vs Meuse). FRA-54-1871-2025 follows the country's own official numbering rather than inventing a new abbreviation scheme, since this is the first FRA subnational row and sets precedent for siblings.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-status-downgraded

**polygon_status was `proxy` with no geometry attached**

the previous binding was cshapes-2.0 / FRA-1919-2025, which is the outline of FRANCE. A département cannot have its country's boundary as its own geometry — every sibling could claim it equally — so the binding is dropped rather than restated as a proxy.


### oq-pre1871-mitchell-rows

**Do any routed data rows fall before 1871, when Meurthe-et-Moselle did not yet exist?**

The routing decision reported data spanning 1862-2023 for this unit, mostly observed (97.6%). If any of those rows are dated 1862-1870, they predate the department's creation in 1871 and cannot describe Meurthe-et-Moselle as such -- they would belong, if anywhere, to the pre-1871 department of Meurthe, which has no row in this table and a different territorial extent (it included the eastern cantons later annexed by Germany). This page's start_year of 1871 means any pre-1871 rows currently routed to FRA-54-1871-2025 fall outside its span and the routing/aggregation logic needs to either exclude them, route them elsewhere, or an earlier Meurthe-1790-1871 entry needs to be created. This was not checked against the underlying row-level data, only inferred from the routing decision's stated year range.

### oq-department-polygon-never-built

**No department-level polygon source is registered for France; the whole-France proxy massively over-covers**

The attached polygon (cshapes-2.0, feature FRA-1919-2025) is the entire country of France (~551,695 km2 in metropolitan extent), used here only because no registered source in this repository carries French département-level boundaries (gadm-4.1-adm1 excludes FRA, gadm-3.6 is not present locally). Meurthe-et-Moselle's actual extent is about 5,246 km2 -- roughly 1/105th of the polygon attached to this row. Any area-based check, choropleth, or spatial join against this row's geometry will silently represent Meurthe-et-Moselle as the whole of France until a real French department boundary source is registered and this polygon is replaced. This is the first FRA subnational entry, so the same gap will recur for every other French département row created after it.
