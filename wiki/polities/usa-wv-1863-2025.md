# West Virginia (state of the United States)

## Summary

West Virginia is a state of the United States, created in 1863 when northwestern counties of Virginia broke away rather than join the Confederacy, and admitted to the Union as the 35th state on 1863-06-20. This entry is `type: subnational` because West Virginia is a constituent state of a sovereign country that already has its own national iso3 chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) — the entry exists to carry state-level reporting territory (e.g. US agricultural or economic statistics tabulated at the state level) without competing with, or double-counting against, the national USA rows. Its span starts at the 1863 admission date rather than at any particular data source's coverage window, following this country's convention that a US state's start_year defaults to its year of Union admission. The end_year of 2025 is the wiki's standard present-day cutoff for polities that still exist, not an indication the state ceased to exist. The polygon is the modern GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature for West Virginia (GID_1 USA.49_1), whose boundary has been essentially fixed since 1863 apart from a small, early (1863-1871) adjustment involving two Eastern Panhandle counties.

**Why this entry exists.** No existing USA-iso3 row represents West Virginia specifically: the five USA national rows (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) are country-level totals, and the only other USA subnational row, ALK-1867-1959, is the Territory/State of Alaska, a different and non-contiguous unit. Any state-level data source reporting "West Virginia" by name would previously have had nowhere to route except a national USA row roughly 150x its size (~9.8M km2 for the continental US vs. ~62,690 km2 for the state), which the sub-territory routing rules treat as a mismatch. The routing decision that produced this page states the reasoning (no candidate IS West Virginia; default admission-year start rule applies) but does not itself name the specific source, label, row count, or year range being routed here — that provenance gap is recorded as an open question below and should be filled in once the underlying data extract is identified. What confirms this entity is distinct: West Virginia's own 1863 admission to the Union as a separate state from Virginia, formalized by federal statute and Presidential proclamation, is itself the historical confirmation of distinctness — it is not merely a statistical convention but a change in sovereignty and administration that split one Commonwealth into two states.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** `assigned`, from `gadm-4.1-adm1`, feature `USA.49_1` ("West Virginia"), present in the locally held GADM 4.1 extract `data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_USA.gpkg` (layer ADM_ADM_1). This is a direct assignment rather than a cross-period proxy copy, because there is no earlier WHEP polygon of West Virginia to copy from — this is the first entry for this state.

**Why direct assignment is reasonable despite using a modern-boundary source:** West Virginia's external boundary has been essentially fixed since its creation in 1863, apart from one adjustment in the state's first decade — the 1863 referendum and 1871 US Supreme Court confirmation (Virginia v. West Virginia) that placed Berkeley and Jefferson counties, in the Eastern Panhandle, inside West Virginia rather than Virginia. That change affects a small fraction of the state's ~62,690 km2 and occurred within the first 8 years of a 162-year span, so the modern GADM boundary is treated as valid for the whole 1863-2025 period rather than downgraded to `proxy`.

**Territory description:** West Virginia occupies the central and southern Appalachian Mountains and Allegheny Plateau of the eastern United States, bordered by Pennsylvania and Maryland to the north/northeast, Virginia to the east/south, Kentucky to the southwest, and Ohio to the northwest along the Ohio River. It is landlocked, mountainous, and was historically economically dominated by coal mining and, in its northern Panhandle, steel and glass manufacturing. Measured from the attached GADM 4.1 geometry in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034), the state's area is approximately **62,690 km2** — this is a measurement of the attached polygon, and matches the commonly cited land+water area of West Virginia (~62,756 km2 / 24,230 sq mi) closely enough to confirm the geometry is correctly identified, not an independent survey of the historical territory.

No successor: as an existing, ongoing US state, `successor` is also empty; the polity continues past `end_year: 2025` only because 2025 is the wiki's synthetic present-day cutoff, not because the entity ended, matching the convention used for other still-existing subnational US entries.

## Sourced claims

- West Virginia was admitted to the Union as the 35th state on 1863-06-20, formed from counties of the Commonwealth of Virginia that refused to join the Confederacy (Reorganized Government of Virginia's ordinance, ratified by Congress and President Lincoln's proclamation).
- Berkeley and Jefferson counties, in the state's Eastern Panhandle, were added to West Virginia by a disputed 1863 county referendum rather than at the original admission, and their inclusion was not settled until the US Supreme Court's 1871 ruling in Virginia v. West Virginia.
- GADM 4.1's West Virginia admin-1 feature (GID_1 USA.49_1) measures approximately 62,690 km2 in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034), consistent with the commonly cited land+water area of West Virginia (~62,756 km2 / 24,230 sq mi).

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

West Virginia is a modern US state, not a historical territory with a name that stands on its own the way Alaska, Hyderabad or the Ryukyus do, so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. It also is not one of the six cases using bare <ISO3>-<start>-<end>, since that pattern in the table is reserved for national-level successor states (BDI, MAN) rather than a subnational unit of a country that already has its own iso3 chain. The dominant 57-row pattern applies directly: WV is used as the subunit token, matching the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US postal abbreviation for West Virginia, giving USA-WV-1863-2025. This also matches the existing ESP-<province> and DZA-<CVD> precedent of using a short recognizable subunit code rather than a full name.

### d-start-year-is-admission-date-not-data-range

**start_year is the 1863 admission date, not the extract's data range**

The routing decision explicitly grounds start_year in West Virginia's documented Union admission date of 1863-06-20, following the country convention's default rule for US states (admission year), rather than in whatever year the underlying agricultural/economic data extract happens to begin. This mirrors how ALK-1867-1959 uses the 1867 Alaska Purchase date rather than the first year FAO (1952) happens to report data for Alaska. No predecessor polity is recorded because Virginia itself has no WHEP entry to split from; the wiki does not yet model antebellum Virginia as a distinct polity, so a predecessor edge would point at nothing and was left empty rather than invented.

### d-polygon-assigned-not-proxy

**Polygon marked assigned, not proxy, despite a small early-period boundary caveat**

Unlike Alaska (external border fixed by international arbitration in 1903, still framed as a proxy claim needing justification), West Virginia's boundary has been essentially fixed since its creation from Virginia's Trans-Allegheny counties in 1863, with only one minor adjustment: an 1863-1871 dispute over Berkeley and Jefferson counties, resolved in West Virginia's favor by the US Supreme Court in Virginia v. West Virginia (1871). That adjustment affects a small fraction of the state's area and occurred within the first 8 years of a 162-year span, so the modern GADM state boundary is treated as directly assigned (`polygon_status: assigned`) rather than a proxy needing a broader caveat.

## Open questions

### oq-berkeley-jefferson-pre-1871

**Berkeley and Jefferson counties are not cleanly part of the territory before 1871**

The GADM feature USA.49_1 used for this entry's polygon includes the Eastern Panhandle counties of Berkeley and Jefferson, whose inclusion in West Virginia was contested from the 1863 admission until the US Supreme Court's 1871 ruling in Virginia v. West Virginia. For the 1863-1871 sub-span, the true, undisputed territory was smaller than the attached polygon by roughly the combined area of those two counties (a few hundred km2 out of ~62,690 km2 total, well under 1% of the state), but no separate pre-1871 polygon has been constructed to capture this. Whether this matters depends on whether any routed data source specifically tabulates the disputed counties before 1871; if not, this is a documentation gap rather than a live data problem.

### oq-what-data-motivates-this-entry

**The routing decision does not identify which rows or source motivate creating this entry now**

The decision object driving this page states only that no existing USA-iso3 candidate represents West Virginia specifically and applies the default admission-year rule; it does not name a source, label, row count, or year range for data being routed to USA-WV-1863-2025, unlike the exemplar (MAN-1950-1955) which tabulates exact source/label/row/year counts.
