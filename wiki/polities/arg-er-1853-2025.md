# Entre Ríos (province of Argentina)

## Summary

Entre Ríos is one of Argentina's original historic provinces, one of the fourteen that signed onto the 1853 Constitution as the basis of the federal republic — it is a mesopotamian province, bounded by the Paraná river to the west and the Uruguay river to the east, and was never part of the Buenos Aires/Confederation split that affected Buenos Aires province itself. type is subnational because this row is not a claim to sovereignty: Argentina held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1853-2025, and the ARG national chain (ARG-1800-1899, ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025) already carries that. What this entry exists to carry instead is the reporting/administrative unit "Entre Ríos province," which statistical sources tabulate separately from national Argentina totals. Entre Ríos is not one of the four departure cases this pipeline's country convention enumerates for Argentina (the ex-territorios nacionales Chaco/Formosa/La Pampa/Misiones/Neuquén/Río Negro, the Patagonian pair Chubut/Santa Cruz, Tierra del Fuego, or Ciudad de Buenos Aires) — it is an original historic province, so the default start rule of 1853 applies directly, and because it remains a current first-order administrative division, end_year is the open convention date of 2025 rather than a data-driven cutoff.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the routing decision for unit_id ARG-ENTRERIOS (admin_name "Entre Ríos", country Argentina) produced by the agent-harness routing pipeline. The pipeline found zero existing candidate polities for this admin1 unit: zero name candidates and zero boundary matches, meaning no wiki page previously represented Entre Ríos province as a distinct reporting unit; the unit's own data span (1900-2023) was previously falling through to the national ARG rows, which are roughly 50 times the area and conflate the province's statistics with those of the other provinces. The historical source confirming this is a distinct, persistently tabulated unit is administrative continuity: Entre Ríos has been one of Argentina's constitutionally recognized provinces since the 1853 Constitution, with its own governor, legislature and provincial statistical apparatus, and remains today a first-order Argentine province under which INDEC and its predecessors report population, production and area figures — the same class of justification used for the sibling Buenos Aires entry (ARG-BA-1853-2025), which this page follows structurally.

**Territory description:** Entre Ríos province occupies the Argentine Mesopotamia, the area between the Paraná river (west) and the Uruguay river (east, forming the border with Uruguay), bordered to the north by Corrientes province and reaching south to the Río de la Plata delta near Buenos Aires. Its capital is Paraná. Its modern area is approximately 78,781 km2, a figure taken from Argentine official sources describing the current province, not a measurement of a geometry attached to this entry — no geometry is attached yet. Entre Ríos's provincial boundary has been comparatively stable since 1853, unlike the Patagonian and Chaco territories that were only later organized as provinces; the main boundary-relevant episode is 19th-century adjustment of its border with Corrientes along minor Paraná tributaries, which is not expected to materially affect a present-day ADM1 proxy once fetched.

## Sourced claims

- Entre Ríos was one of the provinces represented at the 1853 Constituent Congress in Santa Fe and one of the signatories of the 1853 Argentine Constitution, unlike Buenos Aires province which remained outside the Confederation until 1860.
- Entre Ríos's capital, Paraná, briefly served as the national capital of the Argentine Confederation from 1854 to 1861, reflecting the province's central role in the mid-19th-century constitutional settlement.
