# Utah Territory

## Summary

Utah Territory was organised under the Compromise of 1850 (act of 9 September 1850) and originally reached from the Sierra Nevada to the Continental Divide. It was cut down repeatedly: Nevada and Colorado territories took its western and eastern parts in 1861, Nevada's eastern line was moved a degree east in 1862 and another in 1866, and the Wyoming Organic Act of 25 July 1868 took the north-eastern corner east of the 111th meridian. From 1868 the territory had the outline of the present state. Statehood was delayed for decades by the conflict over plural marriage and came, after the 1890 Manifesto, on 4 January 1896 (45th state).

This row carries the territory from 1868 to 1896 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-utah-1896-2025](usa-utah-1896-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span starts in 1868, when the last cession fixed the boundary this row carries; the 1850-1868 territory was several different, larger territories and receives no data. It ends in 1896 (exclusive), where USA-UTAH-1896-2025 begins.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-UTAH`, label `UTAH`),
reports 270 rows for 1879-1895 (17 year(s)) — wheat, oats, barley, rye, maize and potatoes (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as Utah Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The Great Basin and Colorado Plateau state between Idaho/Wyoming (42°N and 41°N) and Arizona (37°N), from Nevada (114°03'W) east to Colorado (109°03'W), with the notch in the north-east corner that the 1868 Wyoming line left: the modern state of Utah.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.45_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 219,833 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1868
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  No boundary change is known between 1868 and admission. The polygon is recorded as `assigned`.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. Utah Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1896 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1879-1895; the row spans 1868-1896, the years this boundary
existed as Utah Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-UTT-1868-1896` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-territorial-series-provenance

**Were the 1879-1895 figures published for the territory at the time, or rebuilt later?**

Quick Stats serves them under the state name as `SURVEY` data; the provenance of the individual estimates is not established here. The frame is the same either way.

### oq-pre-1868-territory

**No row exists for Utah Territory before 1868**

Its 1850-1861, 1861-1862, 1862-1866 and 1866-1868 extents all differ from this one. Created only if a source reports them.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
