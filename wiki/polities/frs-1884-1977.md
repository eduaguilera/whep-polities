# French Somaliland (1884–1977)

Authoritative WHEP row for colonial Djibouti. Polygon: CShapes 2.0 feature 522 (vintage 1900, ~23,192 km²), reassigned from retired DJI-1886-2025. Borders fixed by 1897 Franco-Ethiopian treaty. Successor: FRS-1977-2025.

## Known data-quality issues

### iia-coffee-is-ethiopian-transit-trade

**The IIA "green coffee" figures under this label are re-export, not
production.** Found 2026-07-24 by assertion verification of
`djibouti|iia|1929-1945` (two independent agents).

The series runs at a median of **~14,114 t/yr** — impossible for French
Somaliland, whose arid terrain supports essentially no coffee cultivation.
These are Ethiopian beans (grown around Harar and in southern Ethiopia)
moving through the port on the Djibouti–Addis Ababa railway, completed 1917;
France held a de facto monopoly on Ethiopia's export trade through Djibouti
until Assab opened in the mid-1950s.

**Re-measured 2026-08-14 against consolidated layer B.** The series is
**5 rows** — 1930, 1931, 1932, 1933 and one undated — not the 1929–1945 span
the assertion key `djibouti|iia|1929-1945` implies; the key names the window
that was *screened*, not the years that carry data. The median (14,113.7 t) is
confirmed exactly. Values 12,514–21,106 t.

**They are the same numbers as Ethiopia's own crop.** Mitchell's
`ethiopia` green-coffee series reads 14,100 t (1930) and 18,100 t (1931)
against 14,113.7 and 18,098.2 t here — agreement to Mitchell's 100 t rounding
in the two years where it is closest, so this is not merely an implausible
magnitude but a recognisable duplicate of the origin's series. 1932 and 1933
diverge (21,106 vs 22,800; 12,514 vs 16,200), as an export volume should when
a crop is partly held back or shipped elsewhere.

**Double-count risk, quantified.** Over 1930–1933 these rows add **65,832 t**
of green coffee on top of the 71,200 t Ethiopia already reports for the same
years — a **+92%** inflation of Ethiopian coffee in that window, and 0.5–0.9%
of all coffee recorded in layer B per year. Treat the rows as entrepôt/transit
trade attributable to the territory's *port function*, not to its agriculture.
The routing itself is correct — the source did record it under this label —
but any production aggregate must exclude it.

Recorded source-wide in
`pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/source_conventions.csv`
(`flow_type=entrepot_transit`, `origin_iso3=ETH`) and published for consumers
in `data/final/source_flow_flags.csv`, so an aggregation can exclude the rows
by joining on source/label/item instead of reading this paragraph.

**Still the only flagged flow, and now measured as to why.** Issue 14 called
this a general class and named Singapore, Hong Kong, Beira/Lourenço Marques,
Trieste and Rotterdam as candidates. `13_trade_entrepot_direction.py` classifies
5,839 modern reporter-item-years as entrepôt flows and its re-export class is
led by exactly that list; `14_entrepot_census.py` crosses that classification
against this flag file and promotes **0** of it. Three measured reasons, all of
them about the discriminator rather than about effort: the classification is
keyed on (reporter, item, year) summed over every partner, so it can never
supply the `origin_iso3` that makes this flag actionable; its years (1986–2021)
share none with layer B (1850–1960), where a transit tonnage booked inside a
*production* series is the actual harm; and on this very territory it names
Djibouti in five items (molasses, dry beans, pears, green chillies, raw sugar,
2014–2021) and **never in coffee**, while classifying `coffee, green` for 24
other reporters. Of the 193 layer-B production series that share a label and an
item with a classified flow, not one reaches 8× its item's median intensity
(the highest is 7.4×, IIA Belgian linseed). The census is
`pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/entrepot_census.csv`; what a second flag
needs is a named origin series, which no intensity or availability screen in
this repository produces.
- [2026-07-29] iso3 corrected FRS→DJI. The territory is modern Djibouti, whose ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 is DJI; FRS is not an ISO code. Same reasoning as frs-1977-2025 and ang-1905-1975: the prefix is the chain key, `iso3` names the territory. Without this, FAOSTAT area 72's colonial mapping had no ISO-bearing row covering pre-1977 years.
