# Missouri (state of the United States)

## Summary

Missouri is a U.S. state at the confluence of the Mississippi and Missouri Rivers in the American Midwest, admitted to the Union on August 10, 1821 as the 24th state — the slaveholding half of the 1820 Missouri Compromise, which paired its admission with Maine's as a free state to preserve the Senate's sectional balance. This entry covers the state from admission to the present. `type: subnational` is deliberate: Missouri is a constituent state of a sovereign country, not itself sovereign, and statehood is what distinguishes it from the preceding Missouri Territory (organized 1812 out of the Louisiana Purchase), which is why the span starts at 1821 rather than at the earliest year any data source happens to report on the area. Missouri's external boundary has been essentially stable since admission, apart from minor river-boundary adjustments typical of Mississippi/Missouri River meander states and the 1837 Platte Purchase, which added a strip of land in the northwest corner before most of the state's statistical history begins and is treated as already folded into the polygon used here.

**Why this entry exists.** Data from `juan-subnational` reports Missouri as a distinct sub-national reporting unit, with no existing USA-* polity representing a U.S. state specifically — the only prior USA rows (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) are national-total rows spanning the whole country, each roughly 15-20x Missouri's own area. The routing decision's candidate list was empty for exactly this reason: no state-level row existed to receive this data, and routing it to a national container would misrepresent the reporting scope. Missouri's distinctness as an administrative and reporting unit is not in question — it has been one of the states of the federal union since 1821, with its own government, statutes, and continuous corporate existence; the only judgment call is the start year, resolved by the country convention's default rule (span begins at admission to the Union, 1821) rather than by whatever year the data source happens to begin reporting Missouri separately.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.26_1`, matched directly by name ("Missouri") in the already-registered GADM 4.1 admin-1 layer, the same source and route already used for other USA state-level units (ALK-1867-1959, USA-IL-1818-2025). `polygon_status: proxy` rather than `assigned` because the feature reflects present-day (2025) state boundaries applied back to 1821; this is a low-risk proxy since Missouri's external boundary has been essentially unchanged since the 1837 Platte Purchase (itself well before the bulk of this entry's routed data), with no subsequent state-level boundary change of note.

**Territory description:** Missouri sits in the American Midwest, bordered by Iowa to the north, Illinois, Kentucky and Tennessee across the Mississippi River to the east and southeast, Arkansas to the south, and Oklahoma, Kansas and Nebraska to the west — readily located on a modern map as the state containing St. Louis (at the Mississippi/Missouri confluence), Kansas City, and the state capital Jefferson City. The commonly cited figure for the state's area is approximately 180,540 km2 (69,707 sq mi); the GADM feature's own measured area is consistent with this figure (labelled here as the geometry's own measurement, not an independent territorial claim), so no material under- or over-coverage is expected relative to the reporting unit the routed data describes.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity rows are declared. Before 1821, the area was part of the Missouri Territory (organized 1812 from the Louisiana Purchase lands), an unincorporated federal territory not itself represented as a WHEP polity, so there is no predecessor row to link — the territorial phase is out of scope for this subnational-state entry, which begins at statehood by design, consistent with the sibling USA-IL-1818-2025 entry's treatment of the pre-statehood Illinois Territory. There is no successor: Missouri continues to the present (end_year 2025, open/current per convention) with no scheduled dissolution or reorganization. Missouri's 1821 admission was notably delayed to 1821 (Congress passed the enabling act in 1820) by the slavery-extension dispute resolved through the Missouri Compromise, which is why the state's own admission year (1821) rather than the compromise or enabling-act year (1820) is used as start_year, following the convention of dating from formal admission.

## Decisions

### d-follow-illinois-precedent

**Follow the sibling USA-state verdicts' pattern for code and span convention**

This entry follows the same code shape and span-basis reasoning already established for sibling USA-<SUBUNIT> entries (e.g. USA-IL-1818-2025): code is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, the dominant pattern for 57 of 66 subnational rows in the table. start_year is the year of admission to the Union (1821) rather than the earliest data year, and end_year is the open/current convention value 2025. No bespoke code is used, because Missouri is an ordinary constituent state with no separate historical-territory identity of its own of the kind that justifies a bespoke code (unlike ALK-1867-1959, which names a distinct historical Territory of Alaska prior to statehood, or HYD-1724-1948, which names a princely state). Missouri's pre-statehood existence as part of the Louisiana Purchase and then Missouri Territory is out of scope for this subnational-state row.

### d-four-container-edges

**Container chain uses four edges to tile 1821-2025 with no gap**

The polity table's USA national-era chain is USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, and USA-1959-2025. Missouri's own span (1821-2025) crosses four of these national eras, so the container list declares one edge per era it overlaps: USA-1803-1848 (1821-1848), USA-1848-1867 (1848-1867), USA-1867-1959 (1867-1959), and USA-1959-2025 (1959-2025), rather than the single container_code (USA-1867-1959) proposed in the routing decision. A single edge would leave 1821-1867 uncontained (46 years), which the containment gate rejects since an edge cannot fall outside either party's span. This mirrors the identical fix already applied to sibling state entries such as USA-IL-1818-2025.

## Open questions

### oq-boundary-river-shifts

**Whether Mississippi/Missouri River meander shifts have altered the state outline at a scale that matters here**

Missouri's eastern and western borders follow the Mississippi and (in part) the Missouri River, and river-boundary states are subject to small avulsion-driven boundary disputes over time (e.g. the historic Kaskaskia-area disputes on the Illinois side of the Mississippi). The GADM 4.1 feature used here reflects the present-day (2025) legally settled boundary applied back to 1821. Whether any such shift is large enough to matter for area or containment purposes over a 204-year span has not been checked against USGS or state-boundary-history sources in this session, and is left open.
