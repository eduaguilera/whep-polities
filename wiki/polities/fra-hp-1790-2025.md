# Hautes-Pyrénées (département)

## Summary

Hautes-Pyrénées is one of the original roughly 83 départements created by the French Revolutionary administrative reform of 1790, carved from the southern part of the former province of Gascony and Béarn along the central Pyrenean frontier with Spain. It is entered here as a `subnational` polity because a département is an administrative subdivision of the French state, not a sovereign entity in its own right — France itself holds sovereignty throughout, and this row exists purely to carry a reporting territory that a data source tabulates at sub-national scale. The département has kept essentially the same boundary since 1790: unlike Alsace-Lorraine, Belfort, Corsica, or the overseas départements — the four departure cases identified in the routing decision that motivated this entry — Hautes-Pyrénées was not annexed, split, or created later than the original Revolutionary reform, so the country's default convention (start 1790, still current) applies without modification. Its capital is Tarbes, and it now sits within the Occitanie administrative region, bordering Spain (Aragon) to the south along the Pyrenean crest, and bordering Gers, Pyrénées-Atlantiques, and Haute-Garonne.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures unit `FRA-FRJ26` ("Hautes-Pyrénées") from the agent-harness routing pipeline: French département-level data spanning 1862-2023, reported as 97.6% directly observed (blank/observed method) with a small residual `luh2_residual` share for the remainder, meaning the source measured this territory directly across nearly its whole span with no back-casting required, since the département already existed well before the data begins. Before this entry, the unit had no existing polity match in the database — the routing search for name matches among existing polities returned an empty list, and it is not one of the four listed departure cases (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, the Corsica split, or an overseas département) that would call for a different span rule. It also does not match any existing FRA-national candidate: a département is a subdivision, not the country, and routing it to the national FRA row would have collapsed a genuine sub-national administrative unit into the whole-country total. Sibling FRA subnational entries such as FRA-05-1790-2025 (Hautes-Alpes) already establish the containment pattern for French départements in this database: three container edges, one per FRA national row (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025), since France itself is split into three successive national rows around the Alsace-Lorraine cession and reincorporation. This entry's container array was corrected to follow that same pattern after validate_polity_containment.py flagged the code FRA-1790-2025 as not existing in polities_database.csv — no single national row spans the full 1790-2025 range, so no single container edge can either. What confirms Hautes-Pyrénées as a distinct, stable administrative entity across this span is straightforward continuity of French administrative geography: it has appeared as one of the ~83 original départements in every French territorial reorganization since 1790, without the kind of dramatic boundary change (annexation, partition, or later creation) that applies to the four exception cases.

**Territory description:** Hautes-Pyrénées is a real, locatable département in southwestern France, in the Occitanie region, along the central section of the Pyrenees mountain range on the border with Spain (Aragon). Its administrative seat is Tarbes; the town of Lourdes and the Pic du Midi de Bigorre lie within it. ## Decisions

### d-code-precedent-frj26

**Naming this the first FRA subnational code as ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end**

No existing FRA subnational rows exist in the polities table for HP specifically, so there is no established precedent to follow for this exact unit. Rather than inventing a bespoke code, I followed the dominant cross-country pattern already used by sibling FRA département rows such as FRA-05-1790-2025 (Hautes-Alpes) and FRA-04-1790-2025 (Alpes-de-Haute-Provence): <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. I chose the two-letter abbreviation HP for the subunit code, matching the two-letter style already used for Spanish provinces (AS, CA) rather than a longer INSEE-style numeric code, since INSEE department codes (65 for Hautes-Pyrénées) are not names and would be opaque to a reader browsing the polity table.

### d-polygon-source-slug

**Recording polygon_source as gadm-4.1-adm1 despite France being absent from the local extract**

The routing decision's polygon_route is explicitly registered_source_unfetched, and the instructions for authoring this page state that under that route the slug IS the source and polygon_status must be unassigned — polygon_source may not be left as new_source_needed or registered_source_unfetched literally, both having been rejected by validate_declared_sources previously. I therefore used gadm-4.1-adm1, the registered source category the routing reasoning identifies as the correct one in principle (GADM's admin-1-equivalent tier), even though the routing investigation found the currently cached extract lacks France entirely and suspects départements actually live at GADM's ADM2 tier rather than ADM1. This is flagged as an open question rather than silently resolved, since which GADM level actually carries départements needs verification before a real feature_id can be assigned.

### d-container-split-by-national-row

**Splitting the container edge into three intervals matching the three actual FRA national rows**

validate_polity_containment.py flagged the previous single container edge to FRA-1790-2025 as invalid because no polity row with that code exists in polities_database.csv — France is instead modeled as three successive national rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) bracketing the Alsace-Lorraine cession and reincorporation. Sibling entry FRA-05-1790-2025 (Hautes-Alpes) already establishes the fix: three container edges, one per national row, covering 1790-1871, 1871-1919, and 1919-2025 respectively. I applied the identical split here, since Hautes-Pyrénées, like Hautes-Alpes, is unaffected by the Alsace-Lorraine boundary change and remains inside whichever FRA national row is current for each span.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1800-container-absent

**This unit's span begins before any national container exists, and that is recorded rather than left open**

The unit was created in 1790 — the French départements by the 1790 reorganisation, Rhode Island by ratification — but the earliest national row for this country begins in 1800 (FRA-1800-1871; USA-1800-1803). There is therefore nothing for the years 1790-1799 to be contained by, and inventing a pre-1800 container would be asserting a polity no source in this repository establishes. The omission is deliberate. It is outside WHEP's 1850+ domain, so it costs no resolution there; if a pre-1800 national row is ever added, this unit needs an edge to it.


### oq-gadm-adm-level-for-departements

**Which GADM tier (ADM1 or ADM2) actually carries French départements, and is France present in either cached extract?**

The routing investigation found that the local gadm-4.1-adm1 GeoPackage excludes France (0 rows among 81 covered countries), and separately suspects that even when France is included, GADM's ADM1 tier for France corresponds to régions, not départements — départements would sit at ADM2. This needs to be verified against a full GADM download (or an alternative like IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS or NUTS) before a feature_id can be assigned to this entry. Until resolved, polygon_status stays unassigned and no geometry-derived area exists for this row; the ~4,464 km² figure given in Territorial extent is an external INSEE reference figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon.

### oq-pre1790-ancestor-modeling

**Should the pre-1790 provincial territory (Bigorre/Gascony/Béarn) that Hautes-Pyrénées was carved from be modeled as a predecessor polity?**

This entry lists no predecessor, on the reasoning that the 1790 reform is treated as this département's creation event under the country's routing convention. But if other early-modern French provincial entities are later added to this database (as has been done for other countries' pre-national territorial units), Hautes-Pyrénées may need a predecessor edge to whichever province row covers Bigorre/southern Gascony before 1790, and this entry's start_year assumption should be revisited against that decision rather than treated as settled.

### oq-luh2-residual-coverage-gap

**What does the small luh2_residual share (the non-97.6% remainder) of the 1862-2023 data actually cover, and does it affect any specific years or variables disproportionately?**

The routing reasoning states the unit's data is 97.6% observed/blank-method plus "a small luh2_residual share," but does not specify which years, indicators, or row count are affected by the residual imputation. Before treating this entry's underlying data as fully reliable evidence of territorial continuity, the residual share should be quantified and checked for whether it clusters in particular periods (e.g., wartime years) that might indicate a real reporting gap rather than incidental imputation noise.
