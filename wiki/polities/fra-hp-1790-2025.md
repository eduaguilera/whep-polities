# Hautes-Pyrénées (département)

## Summary

Hautes-Pyrénées is one of the original roughly 83 départements created by the French Revolutionary administrative reform of 1790, carved from the southern part of the former province of Gascony and Béarn along the central Pyrenean frontier with Spain. It is entered here as a `subnational` polity because a département is an administrative subdivision of the French state, not a sovereign entity in its own right — France itself holds sovereignty throughout, and this row exists purely to carry a reporting territory that a data source tabulates at sub-national scale. The département has kept essentially the same boundary since 1790: unlike Alsace-Lorraine, Belfort, Corsica, or the overseas départements — the four departure cases identified in the routing decision that motivated this entry — Hautes-Pyrénées was not annexed, split, or created later than the original Revolutionary reform, so the country's default convention (start 1790, still current) applies without modification. Its capital is Tarbes, and it now sits within the Occitanie administrative region, bordering Spain (Aragon) to the south along the Pyrenean crest, and the French départements of Gers, Hautes-Alpes… no — bordering Gers, Pyrénées-Atlantiques, and Haute-Garonne.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures unit `FRA-FRJ26` ("Hautes-Pyrénées") from the agent-harness routing pipeline: French département-level data spanning 1862-2023, reported as 97.6% directly observed (blank/observed method) with a small residual `luh2_residual` share for the remainder, meaning the source measured this territory directly across nearly its whole span with no back-casting required, since the département already existed well before the data begins. Before this entry, the unit had no existing polity match in the database — the routing search for name matches among existing polities returned an empty list, and it is not one of the four listed departure cases (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, the Corsica split, or an overseas département) that would call for a different span rule. It also does not match any existing FRA-national candidate: a département is a subdivision, not the country, and routing it to the national FRA row would have collapsed a genuine sub-national administrative unit into the whole-country total. No prior FRA subnational rows exist in the table (0 of 66 subnational rows are FRA), so this entry sets the naming precedent for French départements in this database rather than following one. What confirms Hautes-Pyrénées as a distinct, stable administrative entity across this span is straightforward continuity of French administrative geography: it has appeared as one of the ~83 original départements in every French territorial reorganization since 1790, without the kind of dramatic boundary change (annexation, partition, or later creation) that applies to the four exception cases.

## Decisions

### d-code-precedent-frj26

**Naming this the first FRA subnational code as ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end**

No existing FRA subnational rows exist in the polities table (0 of 66), so there is no established precedent to follow for this country specifically. Rather than inventing a bespoke code, I followed the dominant cross-country pattern documented in the routing note — 57 of 66 subnational rows use <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919 — because Hautes-Pyrénées is an ordinary administrative subdivision (a département) with no name of its own distinguishing it as a historical territory in the way Alaska Territory or Hyderabad are. I chose the two-letter abbreviation HP for the subunit code, matching the two-letter style already used for Spanish provinces (AS, CA) rather than a longer INSEE-style numeric code, since INSEE department codes (65 for Hautes-Pyrénées) are not names and would be opaque to a reader browsing the polity table.

### d-polygon-source-slug

**Recording polygon_source as gadm-4.1-adm1 despite France being absent from the local extract**

The routing decision's polygon_route is explicitly registered_source_unfetched, and the instructions for authoring this page state that under that route the slug IS the source and polygon_status must be unassigned — polygon_source may not be left as new_source_needed or registered_source_unfetched literally, both having been rejected by validate_declared_sources previously. I therefore used gadm-4.1-adm1, the registered source category the routing reasoning identifies as the correct one in principle (GADM's admin-1-equivalent tier), even though the routing investigation found the currently cached extract lacks France entirely and suspects départements actually live at GADM's ADM2 tier rather than ADM1. This is flagged as an open question rather than silently resolved, since which GADM level actually carries départements needs verification before a real feature_id can be assigned.

### oq-pre1790-ancestor-modeling

**Should the pre-1790 provincial territory (Bigorre/Gascony/Béarn) that Hautes-Pyrénées was carved from be modeled as a predecessor polity?**

This entry lists no predecessor, on the reasoning that the 1790 reform is treated as this département's creation event under the country's routing convention.
