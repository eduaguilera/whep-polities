# Maule (region of Chile)

## Summary

Maule is one of Chile's sixteen regions, a central-Chilean administrative unit created by the 1974 regionalization decree-laws (DL 573 and DL 575) and made operative in 1976 under Pinochet's reorganisation of the country from provinces into regions. It sits on the coast and central valley south of the Metropolitan Region and Ohiggins, and north of Ñuble/Biobío, with its capital at Talca. `type: subnational` is used because Maule is a territorial subdivision of a sovereign state (Chile), not an independent polity: sovereignty over this territory never changed hands in this window, only the administrative tier reporting statistics about it. The row exists to receive data tabulated at the region level, which the national CHL row cannot represent without conflating it with the other fifteen regions.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Chilean regional-level data for Maule (unit id CHL-ML in the routing pipeline's country/admin tables) for the period the region has existed, 1976 to the present (open end_year 2025 per the project's convention for still-current units). Before this entry, any data tabulated under the label "Maule" or an equivalent region code had nowhere to route except the national CHL row, which is roughly fifty times Maule's territory and would silently blend Maule's figures with Arica-to-Magallanes totals — a wrong match because the source is reporting at admin-1 granularity, not national. No prior polity row in this database named or covered Maule specifically; the routing verdict recorded finding zero existing subnational CHL codes at all, so this country sets its own precedent (57 of 66 existing subnational rows elsewhere follow the ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, which this entry follows). That Maule is a genuine, distinct administrative unit — not a proxy label for something else — is confirmed by Chilean administrative law itself: DL 573/575 (1974) and the region's continuous listing in INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadísticas) regional statistics from 1976 onward, the same body that is the presumed source of region-level series feeding this pipeline.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision investigated this directly: `gadm-4.1-adm1`, the only registered source with admin-1 granularity, is present locally only as an 81-country subset that excludes Chile entirely (zero CHL features), so even though GADM's global release would carry Chilean regions, the fetched copy in this repository cannot be used. No other registered source (cshapes-2.0, paine-2024, cliopatria, constructed, reporting-areas) digitises Chilean sub-national boundaries at any period; the existing CHL rows in this database are all national-level (cshapes-2.0) or a single historical constructed polity unrelated to the modern regions. Maule is also not expressible as a union or difference of any registered feature, so `construct_from_registered` does not apply either. `polygon_source: none` and `polygon_status: unassigned` follow directly: the route decided was `new_source_needed`, meaning a real boundary exists and is well documented outside this repository, but nothing registered here carries it yet.

**Territory a reader can locate:** Región del Maule occupies central Chile's coast-to-Andes strip immediately south of the Libertador General Bernardo O'Higgins region and immediately north of Ñuble (split off from Biobío in 2018) and Biobío proper. Its capital is Talca; other principal towns include Curicó, Linares, and Constitución on the coast. The region covers roughly 30,300 km² (the figure commonly cited for Región del Maule in modern Chilean government statistics) — comparable in size to Belgium. Its boundaries have been stable since the 1976 regionalization; the one adjacent administrative change since then, the 2018 creation of Ñuble, carved territory out of the neighbouring Biobío region, not out of Maule, so Maule's own extent has not changed across its entire 1976-2025 span.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor polity code is set. Maule did not succeed an earlier subnational entity of the same name in this database — Chile had no prior region-level administrative tier recorded here, only provinces under the pre-1974 system, and this routing verdict explicitly declined to attribute pre-1976 data (years 1900-1975) to Maule without separate evidence that an earlier provincial-era unit shared its boundaries. No successor is set because the region remains current (`end_year: 2025`, open convention) and has not been split, renamed, or absorbed as of the routing decision.

## Sourced claims

- DL 573 and DL 575 of 1974 established Chile's regionalization into thirteen (later expanded) regions, with the new administrative structure taking operative effect in 1976 — the basis the routing verdict used for this entry's start_year.
- Chile's INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadísticas) has published region-level statistics for Región del Maule (region VII in the original 1976 numbering) continuously since the regionalization, distinct from national totals and from the neighbouring O'Higgins and Biobío/Ñuble regions.

## Decisions

### d-follow-dominant-code-pattern

**Use CHL-ML-1976-2025, following the 57-row ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern**

Maule is a standard first-order administrative region with no historical name or identity independent of its status as a Chilean region (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State, which predate or exceed their administrative-unit status and justify a bespoke code). There is no existing CHL subnational precedent to match, so this entry sets the country's convention going forward: <ISO3>-<2-letter subunit>-<start>-<end>, mirroring ESP-AS-1833-2025 and ESP-CA-1833-2025 in structure. 'ML' was chosen as a short, unambiguous abbreviation for Maule since no ISO 3166-2 or existing project convention for Chilean regions was found in this database to reuse verbatim.

### d-reject-pre-1976-attribution

**Left 1900-1975 data unrouted to this entry rather than back-dating start_year**

The routing verdict is explicit that data years 1900-1975 predate the region's legal existence and cannot be attributed to it under Chile's administrative history, since Maule as a bounded administrative unit did not exist before DL 573/575 took effect. Extending start_year backward to capture that data would require evidence of a pre-1974 provincial or departmental unit with matching boundaries, which was not established here; that question is left open rather than resolved by assumption.

## Open questions

### oq-no-adm1-source-registered

**No registered polygon source currently covers any Chilean region, including Maule**

The locally-fetched gadm-4.1-adm1 subset excludes Chile entirely (0 of its features), even though GADM's full global release does digitise Chilean admin-1 boundaries. Until either that subset is re-fetched to include CHL, or another source (e.g. geoBoundaries CHL ADM1, or Chile's own BCN/IDE cartographic service) is registered and its licence checked, Maule and every other Chilean region will remain polygon-unassigned in this database. This is a repository-wide gap, not specific to Maule, and resolving it once would likely unblock all sixteen regions at once.

### oq-pre1976-provincial-continuity

**Whether a pre-1976 provincial unit should feed into Maule as a predecessor**

Before 1976, the territory now called Región del Maule was covered by several Chilean provinces (including historical Talca, Linares, Curicó and Maule provinces under the pre-regionalization system). If a data source in this pipeline reports at that provincial level for 1900-1975 and its provinces map cleanly onto modern Maule's boundaries, a case could be made for either a distinct pre-1976 polity with its own code and a predecessor link to this entry, or for treating the old province-level data as out of scope. This routing verdict deliberately did not resolve that; it only declined to stretch this entry's start_year backward without that evidence being gathered first.

### oq-nuble-2018-boundary-check

**Confirm Maule's boundary was genuinely untouched by the 2018 Ñuble split**

The 2018 law creating the Ñuble region carved its territory from Biobío, not Maule, per the general account of that reform — but this was not independently verified against a primary Chilean legal or cartographic source in this session. If any adjustment to Maule's southern boundary occurred as part of that reform (e.g. a minor commune-level realignment), the single container edge and the 'boundaries stable since 1976' claim in the territorial extent section would need revision, and a second era might be needed either in this entry or as a boundary note once a real polygon source is registered.
