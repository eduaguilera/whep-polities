# Oruro (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

Oruro is a department (admin-1 unit) of west-central Bolivia, occupying a broad stretch of the Andean altiplano south of Lake Poopo and centered on the mining city of Oruro, historically Bolivia's most important tin- and silver-mining region. Oruro is one of the departments organized at the republic's founding in 1825/1826, not one of the three later-created departures documented elsewhere in this table (Tarija 1831, Beni 1842, Pando 1938), so its span here starts in 1825 rather than at a documented later separation date, following the routing decision's default rule. This entry is `type: subnational` because Oruro has never been sovereign in its own right: it is carried as a first-level administrative reporting unit inside Bolivia, following the convention already applied to Beni (BOL-BEN-1842-2025), Chuquisaca (BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025), Cochabamba (BOL-COCH-1825-2025), and La Paz (BOL-LP-1825-2025) -- a department-level admin-1 unit that appears as a distinct reporting unit in agricultural, trade, or census statistics gets its own polity row rather than being folded into the national total. Its end_year of 2025 reflects that Oruro remains an active, current Bolivian department with no dissolution or absorption event, so the row's upper bound is the open/current end used throughout this table rather than a historical terminus.

**Why this entry exists.** This row is created directly from a routing decision (unit_id `BOL-ORURO`) rather than from a specific data extract examined here: the routing stage identified Oruro, a Bolivian department, as a first-level administrative unit that this repository's country convention for Bolivia requires to have its own polity row, following the precedent set by BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and continued by BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025, BOL-COCH-1825-2025, and BOL-LP-1825-2025. The routing_reasoning confirms no existing candidate polity represents Oruro specifically: the only polities sharing ISO3 BOL are the four national-era rows (BOL-1825-1903, BOL-1903-1909, BOL-1909-1938, BOL-1938-2025), all whole-country totals rather than the department, so any Oruro-specific statistic previously routed to one of those rows would have been misattributed to the whole country rather than to this smaller mining region. What confirms Oruro is a distinct, long-standing administrative entity rather than an artefact of routing is Bolivia's own department history: it is one of the departments organized at the republic's founding, is not among the three documented later departures (Tarija 1831, Beni 1842, Pando 1938), and has been administered separately from La Paz, Cochabamba, Potosi, and the other departments ever since, with its own distinct economic identity as the country's historic tin- and silver-mining core. As with the four prior Bolivian department entries, no specific dataset or row count has yet been examined for this entry; it was created purely from the routing-stage convention, and what data currently routes here should be checked once the row exists (see open questions).

**Territory description:** Oruro department occupies west-central Bolivia on the high Andean altiplano, bordering Chile to the west, and the Bolivian departments of La Paz to the north, Cochabamba and Potosi to the east and southeast, and Potosi again to the south. It is centered on Lake Poopo (a large but shallow saline lake fed by the Desaguadero river out of Lake Titicaca) and the city of Oruro, historically the country's leading center of tin and silver mining and a major railway junction. On a modern map, present-day Oruro department covers roughly 53,588 km2, one of Bolivia's smaller departments by area, though this figure is drawn from general reference to the modern department rather than measured from any attached geometry, since none is yet assigned. ## Decisions

### d-code-format-oruro-three-letter

**Used BOL-ORU-1825-2025 rather than a two-letter or ISO 3166-2 code**

Bolivia's ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for Oruro is BO-O, a bare letter that collides with no other Bolivian subunit but is too terse to be legible in this table. The dominant table pattern for this country's subnational rows, set by BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and continued in BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025, BOL-COCH-1825-2025, and BOL-LP-1825-2025, is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. This entry uses ORU (three letters, the standard Bolivian department abbreviation for Oruro used in national statistical yearbooks and matching the length convention of BEN/CHUQ/COCH), rather than the two-letter form LP used for La Paz, since La Paz's two-letter choice was itself flagged in that entry's own decisions as a departure worth reconsidering, and ORU does not collide with any existing BOL subunit code or with any other ISO3 in this table.

### d-container-tiled-four-eras-oruro

**Tiled the container across all four BOL national eras, not just BOL-1825-1903 as the routing decision proposed**

The routing decision proposed only container_code BOL-1825-1903 with end_year 2025, which would leave 1903-2025 uncontained and be rejected by the containment gate, since BOL-1825-1903 only exists as a row through 1903. This mirrors the same structural gap already identified and fixed for BOL-BEN-1842-2025, BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025, BOL-COCH-1825-2025, and BOL-LP-1825-2025: the routing stage only ever proposes the first-overlapping national era, never the full chain a long-lived admin-1 unit needs. Reading the polity table's four BOL national-era rows (BOL-1825-1903, BOL-1903-1909, BOL-1909-1938, BOL-1938-2025) and tiling Oruro's full 1825-2025 span across all four resolves the gap identically to the four prior Bolivian department entries.
