# Territory of Papua (1906–1949)

## Territorial Extent

The Territory of Papua comprised the southeastern quarter of New Guinea island, administered by Australia from 1906 (transferred from British New Guinea established 1884). Area approximately 224,148 km² per CShapes 2.0 feature 740 (1920 vintage). The Territory of Papua is DISTINCT from the Territory of New Guinea (TNGU-1920-1949, ~237,536 km²), which covered the northeastern quarter of New Guinea island.

## Data Routing

### FAO 1952 (high confidence)

Label "Papua" (FAO 1952, 1937–1949, 17 rows) disaggregated for this territory alone -> TPAP-1906-1949, high confidence.

### IIA series — the combined-series warning below is DISPROVEN, and the real problem is different (2026-08-18)

**The evidence for a TPAP+TNGU combined series does not survive checking.** The warning kept below
rests on a tobacco step-change "~15.7x (6->94 t) ... consistent with TNGU plantation output (Rabaul
tobacco) entering the series post-1920". Measured in layer B, the IIA tobacco series for this label
runs **1910-1917 and stops**: thirteen values, none after 1917, three years before the mandate it is
supposed to evidence. The step it describes happens entirely inside the pre-1914 period.

Cacao, the other cited series, is **flat across 1920** — 388 ha in 1914, 393 ha in 1922, 402 ha in
1930 — which is what a constant reporting territory looks like, not a doubling.

**The real defect is one nobody had looked for.** Fingerprinting this label's 46 dated values against
the raw IIA extract (`state/iia_assertion_provenance.csv`) gives:

    dutch new guinea                     72%   (supplies 1910-1914 and 1922-1940)
    australian papua and new guinea      28%   (supplies 1915-1917 only)

Dutch New Guinea is **West** Papua — the western half of the island, Dutch until 1962 and Indonesian
since — not the Australian Territory of Papua at all. So for the great majority of these years the
series describes a different country on the other side of the island, and the question of whether
Papua and New Guinea were reported together is beside the point.

That also explains why the reasoning below went wrong: it assumed the label was an Australian
combined series and looked for an Australian territorial event to explain a step. The label is mostly
a Dutch series.

**Status:** `papua new guinea|iia|1910-1940` is quarantined on this measurement
(`state/quarantine.csv`, issue 315). TPAP-1906-1949 remains the routing target only because it is the
sole live PNG-family polity across the span; the scope is wrong, not merely undetermined. The FAO 1952
routing above is unaffected — that label is genuinely disaggregated for this territory.

### IIA combined series — COMBINED-SERIES WARNING (low confidence) — SUPERSEDED, see above

IIA label "papua new guinea" (54 rows, 1910–1940: cacao, rubber, tobacco) is a COMBINED series covering BOTH Territory of Papua and the adjacent NE New Guinea territory (GNGU-1884-1914 pre-1914; TNGU-1920-1949 from 1920). Australian statistical publications routinely reported both territories together under a combined label.

**Routing decisions:**
- **1910–1914 rows**: routed to GNGU-1884-1914 (low confidence) — German New Guinea was the NE component in this sub-period.
- **1920–1940 rows**: routed to TPAP-1906-1949 (low confidence) as administrative anchor only. Data represents a TPAP+TNGU combined aggregate; CANNOT be attributed to TPAP alone. Tobacco step-change ~15.7× (6→94 t) and cacao ~2.6× are consistent with TNGU plantation output (Rabaul tobacco) entering the series post-1920. FAO 1952 confirms Papua and New Guinea were distinct reporting units. The IIA series overstates TPAP-only output by approximately 2× and is NOT comparable to FAO data or post-1949 PNG series.
