# Acre

## Summary

Acre is a Brazilian state in the far southwest of the country, bordering Bolivia and Peru, occupying the upper Amazon basin around the Acre and Purus river valleys. This entry covers Acre from 1962, when it was elevated from federal-territory to full statehood, to the present. `type: subnational` is used because Acre has never been a sovereign polity — it was Bolivian territory until 1903, then a Brazilian federal territory (a subnational unit administered directly by the federal government, without its own legislature) until 1962, then a state (a subnational unit with its own elected government) from 1962 onward. This entry captures only the state-era span; the federal-territory era (1903-1961) is a separate administrative status not covered by the routing decision's proposed span, and is flagged as an open question for a possible predecessor row.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision this page implements found that Acre had no existing polity match at all in the database (unit_id BRA-ACRE, no proposed match). Prior to this decision, source rows describing Acre — a LatAm Subnational dataset spanning roughly 1900-2023, heavily back-cast via scaled/interpolated/fallback methods for 1903-1961 (97.6% combined per the decision's own method breakdown) — had nowhere correct to route: the whole span was at risk of falling into the national BRA-1909-2025 row, a container roughly ten times Acre's territory, which would misrepresent state-level production/trade data as national. The country convention for BRA departs from the default 1889 (republic-founding) start specifically because Acre's history under Brazilian sovereignty begins later and separately: Bolivian territory until the 1903 Treaty of Petrópolis, then federal territory, then state from 1962. This entry captures the state-era segment (1962-2025), the only span for which the routing decision proposed a discrete polity object; 1900-1902 (pre-cession) is explicitly left unroutable, and 1903-1961 (federal-territory era) is discussed in the decision's reasoning but not given its own proposed span, hence the open question below. What confirms Acre is a distinct reporting unit rather than folded into national Brazil figures is the source's own practice of reporting it as a named subnational unit across a century-long span (1900-2023), which is why a country-level convention exception was written for it in the first place.

**Territory description:** modern Acre covers approximately 164,000 km2 in Brazil's Norte region — a landlocked state in the far southwest of the country bordering Bolivia to the south/east and Peru to the west, with Amazonas state to its north/northeast. Its capital and largest city is Rio Branco. The state occupies the upper Amazon basin along the Acre and Purus rivers. Its boundary has been administratively stable since the 1904 creation of the federal territory (fixed by the 1903 Treaty of Petrópolis cession from Bolivia, with a minor further adjustment via the 1904 Treaty of Rio de Janeiro with Peru), so the modern administrative boundary should apply across the full 1962-2025 span with low vintage risk once fetched — a claim carried over from the routing decision's own polygon_reasoning that remains unverified against the actual file (see open question oq-polygon-vintage-once-fetched). ### d-code-iso3-start-end-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

Used BRA-ACRE-1962-2025 rather than a bespoke code. Acre is a first-order administrative division (a Brazilian state) with no name of its own outside that administrative role — unlike Alaska-as-territory or Hyderabad, it isn't a historical polity that predates and outlives its container's administrative scheme, it IS the administrative scheme.
