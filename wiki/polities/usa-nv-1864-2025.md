# Nevada (state of the United States)

## Summary

Nevada is one of the fifty United States, admitted to the Union on October 31, 1864 -- the 36th state, and one of the fastest territory-to-statehood transitions in US history, driven by Civil War politics (Union electoral votes and support for the Thirteenth Amendment) rather than population thresholds. Before statehood the same area was organized as Nevada Territory (created 1861 out of Utah Territory during the Civil War, prompted by the Comstock Lode silver rush). This entry is type: subnational because Nevada is a constituent state of the sovereign United States, not a sovereign polity itself -- the USA held sovereignty throughout, and the row exists purely to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate. No existing polity represented Nevada specifically before this decision: the only USA candidates in the table were national-total rows (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) and the unrelated Alaska territory entry (ALK-1867-1959), so absent this row Nevada's data would collapse into a national total roughly 30x its own area.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id USA-NEVADA) captures valued statistical rows for the administrative unit "Nevada", country United States of America, under the default state-start rule of the USA country convention -- Nevada is one of the 37 post-1791 states but, per the convention, uses its own actual admission year (1864) as start_year, exactly as decided for California and Colorado, rather than a generic post-1791 placeholder. Previously matched to: nothing dedicated -- no existing polity represented Nevada specifically; absent this entry, Nevada's rows would fall back to whichever USA national-era row spans the data year (USA-1848-1867 or USA-1867-1959 for pre-1959 data, USA-1959-2025 thereafter), conflating a single state with the entire country. What confirms distinctness: Nevada has been continuously tabulated as a distinct first-order administrative unit by the U.S. Census Bureau and federal agricultural/mining/economic statistical series since territorial days, and it is exactly the same order of statistical unit as other US states already present in this database (e.g. Colorado, USA-CO-1876-2025; Alaska, ALK-1867-1959) -- a state is not its country, and federal series report at state granularity as a matter of course.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Copied from the registered gadm-4.1-adm1 source, feature USA.29_1 (a direct source-feature assignment, not a proxy copy from a sibling polity period). gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered in scripts/sources.yaml and the local extract at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_USA.gpkg contains, in its ADM_ADM_1 layer, exactly one feature with GID_1 "USA.29_1", NAME_1 "Nevada", VARNAME_1 "NV|Nev.": an unambiguous match. polygon_status is therefore assigned, not proxy.

Vintage risk is low for most of the span but not negligible at its start: Nevada's boundary was extended twice after admission -- eastward in May 1866 (annexing a strip from Utah Territory) and southward in January 1867 (the Pah-Ute strip from Arizona Territory) -- before settling into the modern shape that GADM captures. So the present-day boundary very slightly over-covers the state's actual territory for 1864-1867 (about the first three years of this entry's span), but has been effectively stable since 1867, a span now approaching 160 years.

Territory description: Nevada occupies most of the Great Basin in the western United States, bordered by Oregon and Idaho to the north, Utah to the east, Arizona to the southeast (across Lake Mead/the Colorado River), and California to the west and south -- it is famously the driest US state, dominated by basin-and-range desert topography, with Las Vegas in the far southeast and the capital Carson City near the western border by Lake Tahoe. Its area is approximately 286,285 km2 as measured from the attached gadm-4.1-adm1 geometry in an equal-area projection (ESRI:54034) -- this figure is a measurement of the attached polygon, not an independently sourced official area, though it is consistent with commonly cited state-area figures for Nevada (~286,400 km2), which corroborates rather than substitutes for the geometric measurement.

## Predecessors and successors

Nevada has no predecessor polity in this database: before October 1864 the same territory was organized as Nevada Territory (created 1861 out of Utah Territory), but this database does not model that pre-statehood territorial phase as a distinct row, so predecessor is left empty, matching the treatment of Colorado's pre-admission territorial phase. It has no successor either: Nevada remains a current, active US state as of 2025, hence the open end_year of 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention used for other still-active USA subnational rows such as USA-CO-1876-2025.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-state-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter postal abbreviation NV, matching the dominant subnational pattern**

The dominant subnational pattern measured from the polity table is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, covering 57 of 66 subnational rows (e.g. ESP-AS, USA-CO). This page follows that convention, minting USA-NV-1864-2025 with NV as the standard USPS/ISO 3166-2 postal abbreviation for Nevada. A bespoke code (the ALK/HYD/RYU pattern) was rejected because Nevada, unlike the Territory of Alaska, is not a historical territory with a distinct name and identity separate from its role as a present-day US state -- it is one of the fifty states admitted under the ordinary process, so the standard <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT> pattern applies directly.

### d-three-container-edges-for-usa-national-eras

**Split the container into three edges to tile Nevada's full 1864-2025 span across three USA national-era rows**

Nevada's admission year, 1864, falls inside the USA-1848-1867 national-era row (1848-1867), and its span (1864-2025) then crosses two further national-era boundaries in the polity table: USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025. A single or double container edge would leave part of Nevada's span uncontained and would be rejected by the containment gate, which requires an edge to fall entirely within both parties' spans. This page therefore uses three container edges -- USA-1848-1867 for 1864-1867, USA-1867-1959 for 1867-1959, and USA-1959-2025 for 1959-2025 -- together tiling the full 1864-2025 span with no gap, following the same one-edge-per-containing-era practice used on USA-CO-1876-2025 (two edges) and other sibling state pages.

## Open questions

### oq-1861-1864-territorial-data-not-covered

**Nevada Territory (1861-1864) data, if any exists in the source panels, is not covered by this entry**

Nevada was organized as a US territory in 1861 (carved out of Utah Territory during the Civil War, largely to secure Union-aligned silver-mining votes and revenue from the Comstock Lode) and admitted to statehood on October 31, 1864 -- unusually fast, driven by Lincoln's desire for additional pro-Union electoral votes and support for the Thirteenth Amendment. This entry's start_year is the 1864 admission date per the default USA country-convention rule, matching the Colorado (USA-CO) and California precedents. If any source panel (juan-subnational or otherwise) carries pre-1864 Nevada Territory data, it is not currently routed to this polity and no predecessor row exists to catch it; whoever next extends this polity's sourced data should check whether such rows exist and, if so, whether back-casting them here (as was done for Colorado's 1869-1875 territorial-era rows) is appropriate, given that Nevada Territory's 1861 boundary was smaller than the modern state -- it did not yet include the eastern strip annexed from Utah Territory in 1866 or the southern Pah-Ute County strip annexed from Arizona Territory in 1867, both after statehood.

### oq-post-admission-boundary-changes-1866-1867

**The 1866 and 1867 boundary extensions mean the state's final shape postdates its own admission by up to three years**

Nevada's boundary was not fixed at admission in 1864: in May 1866 the state's eastern border was moved one degree of longitude further east (annexing land from Utah Territory), and in January 1867 the state acquired the southern tip below the 37th parallel (the Pah-Ute strip) from Arizona Territory, arriving at the modern boundary reflected in the attached GADM feature USA.29_1. This means the polygon slightly over-covers the state's actual territory for 1864-1867 (roughly the first three years of this entry's span), a small vintage mismatch of the same kind flagged on USA-CO-1876-2025 for its own early surveying disputes, but here affecting the state's very beginning rather than a minor edge correction. No attempt is made in this entry to construct a smaller 1864-1866 polygon; this is left as a known, minor approximation.
