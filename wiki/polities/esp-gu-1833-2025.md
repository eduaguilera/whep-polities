# Guadalajara (province of Spain)

## Summary

Guadalajara is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and since 1981 part of the Castile-La Mancha autonomous community (unlike Asturias or Madrid, Guadalajara is NOT coextensive with a single-province autonomous community: it is one of five provinces -- with Albacete, Ciudad Real, Cuenca, and Toledo -- that make up Castile-La Mancha, so its 1833 boundary and its post-1981 administrative role are two distinct levels, not one). This entry is type: subnational because Guadalajara is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025, and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Guadalajara separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES424, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES424) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES424 ("Guadalajara"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to sibling NUTS3 Spanish units (A Coruna/ES111, Madrid/ES-MD, Asturias/ES120, and onward through the ES1xx-ES4xx range), following a system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Guadalajara is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states there is "no existing polity" representing Guadalajara specifically, and rules out that it could be A Coruna or Madrid under a different name; the fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, collapsing a province-level series into a country-level one, rejected for the same reason it was rejected for the six already-created sibling provinces. What confirms distinctness: Guadalajara's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES424) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform is the same evidentiary basis already accepted for the six sibling rows -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is about 41x the area of Guadalajara province (~12,190 km2).

Territory description: Guadalajara province sits in central Spain, in the northeast of Castile-La Mancha, bordering the Community of Madrid to the west, Castile and Leon (Segovia, Soria) to the north, Aragon (Zaragoza, Teruel) to the east, and Cuenca to the south. Its capital, also called Guadalajara, lies on the Henares river and has grown substantially since the 1990s as a commuter/logistics satellite of Madrid, alongside older towns such as Siguenza and Molina de Aragon. On a modern map its outline matches the current Guadalajara province exactly, since -- unlike Asturias or Madrid -- it was never itself split, but it is also not equivalent to any single autonomous community, being one of five provinces making up Castile-La Mancha. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 12,190 km2, making it one of the larger, more sparsely populated Spanish provinces rather than a small one -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is confirmed present, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the sibling province rows.

## Predecessors and successors

Guadalajara has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory was organized under the historic Antiguo Regimen partido/corregimiento structure of the Crown of Castile, which does not correspond cleanly to the 1833 province's administrative form, and this database does not model sub-kingdom Castilian divisions at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to the sibling ESP province rows. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1981, a constituent province of Castile-La Mancha) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Guadalajara as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES424) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- Unlike Asturias, Cantabria, or Madrid (single-province autonomous communities), Guadalajara is one of five provinces (with Albacete, Ciudad Real, Cuenca, Toledo) making up the Castile-La Mancha autonomous community formed in 1981-1982, so its province-level and community-level boundaries are not identical -- a distinction the routing_reasoning does not need to resolve since the row tracks the 1833 province, not the 1981 community.
- scripts/sources.yaml records mapspain-ign as present_locally=False, a flag that appears to conflict with the six sibling ESP province pages (esp-as/ca/co/lu/or/po-1833-2025.md) already built in this session citing that same source for their proxy polygon route.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-gu-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation GU rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-AS-1833-2025 (Asturias), ESP-CA-1833-2025 (Cantabria), ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna), and ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES424) or the INE province numeric code (19). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-GU-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so that the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and any future province entry can be predicted from the same rule without a lookup table. GU was chosen as the standard ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-GU) and the everyday abbreviation for the province, avoiding collision with any other already-created sibling code.

### oq-guadalajara-area-figure-discrepancy

**Older or cached descriptions may understate Guadalajara's area relative to its actual ~12,190 km2 INE figure**

Guadalajara is sometimes casually described as one of Spain's smaller provinces by area, which is incorrect: at approximately 12,190 km2 it is one of the larger Spanish provinces, ranking above several sibling NUTS3 units already created in this session, despite having one of the lowest population densities in mainland Spain. Since no polygon is yet attached, this figure cannot be cross-checked against a measured geometry; whoever assigns the mapspain-ign feature to this entry should confirm the area against the INE administrative boundary once the cpro=19 feature is fetched, and correct this page's territory description if the confirmed figure differs.
