# Gipuzkoa (province of Spain)

## Summary

Gipuzkoa is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created by the Javier de Burgos administrative reform of 30 November 1833 alongside every other Spanish province, and since 1980 also (with modified competences reflecting its historic foral status) one of the three Basque Historical Territories composing the Basque Autonomous Community, together with Biscay/Bizkaia and Alava/Araba. This entry is type: subnational because Gipuzkoa is a sub-unit of the sovereign Spanish state, not a state in its own right: Spain held sovereignty over this territory throughout 1833-2025 (notwithstanding its distinct fiscal concierto economico arrangement), and the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography smaller than the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025 and distinguishable from it because Spanish and EU statistical agencies (INE, Eurostat/GISCO) have tabulated Gipuzkoa separately from the national total, at NUTS3 code ES212, for over a century and a half.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision (unit_id ESP-ES212) captures valued statistical rows for unit ES212 ("Gipuzkoa"), country Spain, under the same country convention already applied to the sibling batch of NUTS3 Spanish units (ES111/A Coruna, ES120/Asturias, and onward through ES241), all following the system default start_year of 1833 (the Javier de Burgos provincial reform, applied because Gipuzkoa is not one of the Canary Islands departure units that take a different start) and an open end_year of 2025 for still-current units. Previously matched to: nothing -- the routing_reasoning states explicitly that candidates by name are empty and the three ESP-sharing polities already in the table (A Coruna, Madrid, and national Spain) are none of them this province, so unlike A Coruna (which found a pre-existing but undocumented row) this unit had no candidate at all before this decision. The fallback in the absence of a dedicated row would have been the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which would collapse a province-level series into a country-level one and was rejected for the same reason it was rejected for the other six ESP province rows. What confirms distinctness: Gipuzkoa's status as a first-order Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES212) and an INE statistical division reporting at province granularity since the 1833 reform, further reinforced by its status as one of the three Basque Historical Territories with its own foral fiscal administration (the Diputacion Foral de Gipuzkoa collects most taxes directly under the concierto economico, unlike an ordinary Spanish province) -- continuous, separately-tabulated provincial statistics distinguish it from the national ESP-1800-2025 aggregate, which at roughly 505,000 km2 is well over 250x the area of Gipuzkoa.

## Territorial extent

Polygon status: Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_feature pointing at mapspain-ign with id_column=cpro (the Spanish INE province numeric code), under which Gipuzkoa is conventionally cpro 20, but the polygon_reasoning states plainly that mapspain-ign, while registered with the correct schema and id_column, "is not present_locally": the provinces.gpkg file has not been downloaded or placed on disk, so the route cannot be executed until that data is fetched, exactly as recorded for the other six routed ESP province rows for the same unmet precondition. gadm-4.1-adm1 was checked and returns zero name matches for ESP entirely (it models Spain at the 17-autonomous-community level, and even where a community and province do not coincide, as is the case here since the Basque Country autonomous community comprises three provinces, adm1 does not carry province-level attribution), so it was correctly ruled out rather than substituted. Territory description: Gipuzkoa is the smallest and easternmost of Spain's mainland provinces, occupying the eastern portion of the Basque Country on the Bay of Biscay coast, bordered by Biscay/Bizkaia to the west, Alava/Araba and Navarre to the south, and France (across the Bidasoa river/Pyrenees) to the east. Its capital is Donostia-San Sebastian, with Irun on the French border and the industrial towns of the Deba and Urola valleys (Eibar, Arrasate/Mondragon, Bergara) as other significant centres. On a modern map it is the same outline as the Gipuzkoa Historical Territory / Basque province, essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform. Its administrative area, per INE, is approximately 1,980 km2 -- this is the region's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached polygon, since none is attached yet. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially unchanged since the 1833 reform, so once the IGN provinces layer is acquired, vintage risk across the full 1833-2025 span should be low, matching the assessment already made for the other routed ESP province rows.

## Predecessors and successors

Gipuzkoa has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the territory it now covers was organized as the Provincia de Guipuzcoa under its own foral regime (customary Basque law, a separate Juntas Generales assembly, and tax exemption from the Castilian Crown) rather than as an ordinary province, distinct in administrative form from the 1833 province even though the name and rough boundary persisted, and this database does not model that pre-1833 foral structure at that granularity, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the same treatment given to the other ESP province rows. It has no successor either: the province remains a current, active administrative and statistical unit of Spain (and, since 1980, one of the three Historical Territories composing the Basque Autonomous Community) as of 2025, hence the open end_year 2025 (exclusive), matching the country convention already established for the other Spanish NUTS3 units.

## Sourced claims

- INE (Instituto Nacional de Estadistica) and Eurostat both report Gipuzkoa as a NUTS3 statistical unit (ES212) with continuous province-level data collection since the 19th century, consistent with its creation as one of the 50 provinces in the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform.
- The routing decision for unit ESP-ES212 records that no existing polity IS Gipuzkoa, that the three ESP-sharing polities already in the table are A Coruna, Madrid, and national Spain, and that Gipuzkoa follows the country's default 1833 start rule since it is not one of the enumerated departures (only the Canary Islands split in 1927 departs).
- The polygon_reasoning for this decision records that mapspain-ign's id_column is cpro (INE province numeric codes), that Gipuzkoa's conventional INE province code is 20, and that mapspain-ign is registered but not present_locally, matching the treatment of the other routed ESP province rows.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-gi-abbreviation

**Used the two-letter abbreviation GI rather than the province numeric code, matching the ESP house convention**

The dominant subnational pattern for Spanish provinces already established by ESP-CO-1833-2025 (A Coruna), ESP-MD-1833-2025 (Madrid), ESP-AS-1833-2025 (Asturias) and the other six ESP province rows is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short letter abbreviation of the unit name rather than the NUTS3 digits (ES212) or the INE province numeric code (20). This page follows that convention exactly, minting ESP-GI-1833-2025 rather than a numeric-coded alternative, so the sibling set of Spanish province rows stays internally consistent and a future author can predict the code for any remaining province without a lookup table. GI was chosen over alternatives like SS (from Basque-era or older Spanish abbreviations tied to the province capital San Sebastian/Donostia) because GI is both the modern ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (ES-SS uses SS in some vocabularies but the province itself is widely coded GI in Basque-government and cartographic usage) -- this repository follows the pattern of using the Basque/Euskera province name Gipuzkoa (rather than the historic Castilian Guipuzcoa) since that is the name used in the current official designation and by mapspain-ign's own province listing, and abbreviates it to GI for the same reason AS was chosen for Asturias over the older Oviedo-derived alternative: it reduces ambiguity for a future author matching a new data source against this row.

## Open questions

Whoever acquires mapspain-ign should verify there is exactly one province-level feature for Gipuzkoa, matching the modern Basque Autonomous Community's Gipuzkoa territorial historical outline, before assigning it here.

### oq-pre-1833-fueros-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Provincia de Guipuzcoa under its own foral regime has no distinct predecessor polity in this database**

Before the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, Gipuzkoa (together with Biscay and Alava) held a distinct foral status within the Spanish Crown -- the Provincia de Guipuzcoa exercised its own fueros (customary law, tax exemption, and a separate Juntas Generales assembly) rather than being administered as an ordinary Castilian province, a jurisdictional form quite unlike the pre-1833 territories of A Coruna, Madrid, or Asturias. This database does not model that distinct foral structure as a separate polity row, so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row, following the same treatment given to the other ESP province rows. Whether the pre-1833 (and post-1833 through the 1876 abolition of the fueros, and the later 1878 concierto economico arrangement that persists in modified form to this day as the Basque Economic Agreement) foral Gipuzkoa should eventually be modeled as its own polity -- given it carried genuine fiscal and legal autonomy unlike a bare Castilian province -- is an open question for the ESP country convention generally, and is more pressing here than for Asturias because the foral/concierto economico distinction has ongoing statistical consequences (the Basque treasury, not the Spanish state treasury, collects most taxes in Gipuzkoa) that a future author reconciling fiscal or tax-revenue data sources may need to route differently from the plain provincial-administrative series this entry captures.
