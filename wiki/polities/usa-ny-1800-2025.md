# New York (state of the United States)

## Summary

New York, one of the thirteen original U.S. states, ratified the Constitution on 26 July 1788 and has been a constituent state of the federal union ever since. `type: subnational` because New York never held sovereignty independent of the union -- it is a state, not a national polity -- and this row exists purely to give juan-subnational's state-level New York data (previously absorbed into the country-wide USA-* national rows) a container sized to the state rather than the roughly 3.8-million-km2 national territory those rows represent at any given era. The entry's start_year is set to 1800, not New York's actual 1788 admission: the polity table's earliest national USA row (USA-1800-1803) itself only begins in 1800, and the containment gate rejects any edge whose span falls outside its container's own recorded span, so 1800 is the earliest year any USA state-level row can currently be containered against. This mirrors the identical compromise already made for Connecticut, Delaware and Georgia, the other original-13 states processed in this same batch.

**Why this entry exists.** **Input data:** juan-subnational rows for New York ("New York" / USA subnational label), part of the same batch that produced usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co, usa-ct, usa-de and usa-ga in this session; the routing decision's own reasoning cites the underlying extract's data years as running from 1866, though the exact row count for New York specifically was not inspected in authoring this page (see open questions). **Previously matched to:** the country-wide USA national rows (USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025, etc.), which is wrong because those rows represent the entire United States (~3.8 million km2 at any given era) rather than the ~141,300 km2 New York actually reports on -- a roughly 27x territory mismatch. **Confirmation of distinctness:** New York is a constitutionally and administratively distinct U.S. state, one of the thirteen original states, with its own state government since 1777 (pre-dating even the federal Constitution), and juan-subnational tabulates it as a separate reporting unit from both the national total and from neighboring states, which is the same evidentiary bar already applied to create the sibling state-level entries in this batch.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1788

**start_year set to 1800, departing from the proposed 1788 ratification year**

The routing decision that motivated this page proposed start_year=1788, New York's ratification date (26 July 1788, the eleventh of the original 13). But the polity table's earliest national USA row is USA-1800-1803 ("United States (original)"), and the container-tiling rule this harness enforces rejects any container edge whose span falls outside the container's own recorded span (1800-1803 at the earliest). There is no national USA row covering 1788-1800 to attach an edge to, so a 1788 start would leave a 12-year gap the containment gate cannot pass. Rather than invent an unbacked container row, this page follows the identical compromise already made for Connecticut (USA-CT-1800-2025), Delaware (USA-DE-1800-2025) and Georgia (USA-GA-1800-2025), all original-13 states whose pages also start at 1800 for the same reason: the earliest available container bounds the earliest state row, not the state's own admission history. This also directly resolves the routing decision's own flagged concern about whether an earlier container than USA-1800-1803 exists -- it does not, at least not yet in this table. If USA gets an earlier national row in a future ingest (e.g. USA-1787-1800 or USA-1788-1800), New York's start_year should be revisited alongside every other original-13 state page that made the same compromise.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1800-gap

**Is there a case for a USA-1787-1800 (or similar) national row that would let original-13 states start at their true admission years?**

New York (ratified 1788) joins Connecticut, Delaware and Georgia in having its subnational start_year pinned to 1800 purely because that is where the polity table's national USA chain begins (USA-1800-1803). If a future ingest adds an earlier national USA row covering the Confederation period or the 1787-1800 span under the same country code, every original-13 state page, including this one, should be re-spanned to its actual ratification year rather than the shared 1800 floor. Until then, the 12 years 1788-1800 of New York's actual statehood are simply not represented by any polity row in this database.

### oq-juan-subnational-coverage

**What does the juan-subnational source actually cover for New York, and does it justify the full 1800-2025 span?**

This entry's sole listed source is juan-subnational, and the page was authored to create a container/routing home for that source's New York rows, not because a specific row count was inspected here. The routing decision's own reasoning describes the raw data's span as running 1866-2025, which may just reflect the extract window rather than New York's continuous reporting history. It is unconfirmed whether juan-subnational has dense coverage across the full 1800-2025 span or only a subset of years (e.g. post-1866, as flagged for other original-13 states in this same batch). If the source is sparse before 1866, that should be noted rather than implying dense reporting back to 1800.

### oq-boundary-vintage-and-nyc-consolidation

**Does the GADM 4.1 New York boundary match the state's boundary and internal composition at each point since 1800?**

New York's external state boundary (with the exception of small adjustments -- e.g. minor Vermont/New York and New Jersey/New York boundary litigation in the 19th and 20th centuries, and the 1908 fixing of the Hudson River boundary with New Jersey) has been substantially stable since well before 1800, so vintage risk for the outer polygon is low. But New York City itself was not consolidated into its present five-borough form until 1898 (Brooklyn, Queens, and Staten Island were previously separate or differently organized jurisdictions), which does not change the state's outer boundary but does mean the state's internal administrative geography looked materially different for roughly the first century of this entry's span. This is noted rather than resolved, since the polygon here covers the whole state, not sub-state administrative units.
