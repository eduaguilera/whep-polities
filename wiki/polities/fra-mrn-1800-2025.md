# Marne (département)

## Summary

Marne is a French département in the historic Champagne region of north-eastern France, with its préfecture at Châlons-en-Champagne and its largest city at Reims. It is one of the original ~83 départements created by the National Constituent Assembly's reform of 4 March 1790, which replaced the pre-Revolutionary provinces with a uniform grid of administrative units; Marne was assembled chiefly from parts of the former province of Champagne. This is a `type: subnational` entry, not a national polity: sovereignty over this territory has been French throughout, and the entry exists only to carry a reporting/administrative unit smaller than the country, mirroring how ESP-AS or ESP-CA carry Spanish provinces rather than competing with the ESP national chain. The département has not been one of the exceptional cases in French territorial reorganisation — it is not Belfort (split from Haut-Rhin in 1871), not one of the Alsace-Lorraine départements ceded in 1871 and returned in 1919, not a product of the 1975 Corsican split, and not an overseas département — so its own boundaries have plausibly been continuous since creation, tracking only the general national-level shifts already represented by the containing FRA chain. No predecessor or successor polity applies; only the containing national polity's identity changes across this span.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures France's national-level statistical/administrative vocabulary at the département tier for Marne, a unit that until now had no dedicated polity row: the three existing FRA-* rows in the polities table (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025, plus FRA-1800-1919) are all national totals for the whole of France, and a département is not equivalent to its country — any data keyed specifically to "Marne" rather than to "France" was previously either mismatched onto one of those national rows (silently inflating national territory coverage with what is actually a ~8,160 km² sub-unit) or left unmatched. Historical administrative sources confirm Marne as a distinct, persistent statistical/administrative unit: it is one of the ~83 départements established by the 4 March 1790 reform and has been separately enumerated in French national statistics (population censuses, agricultural statistics, INSEE series) as its own unit continuously since. This entry was proposed by the agent-harness routing pipeline (unit_id FRA-FRF23, admin_name FRF23) specifically because no existing FRA-* candidate represented Marne at the correct administrative granularity, and Marne is not one of the four listed departures from the default per-département routing rule (Belfort, the Alsace-Lorraine départements, the Corsican split, or the overseas départements).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is available in the GeoPackage for this period, and no registered source in scripts/sources.yaml currently carries French département-level (ADM1) boundaries locally: cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe digitise France only at country level, and gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered as a source but the file staged in this repository is an 81-country subset that does not include France. A full GADM 4.1 download or an IGN ADMIN EXPRESS extract would supply the boundary; neither has been staged yet. `polygon_area_km2` is left null rather than estimated.

**Territory description:** Marne covers roughly 8,160 km² in the Grand Est region of north-eastern France, bordered by the départements of Ardennes, Meuse, Haute-Marne, Aube, Seine-et-Marne, and Aisne. On a modern map it sits between Reims (its largest city, in the north-west of the département) and Châlons-en-Champagne (the préfecture, roughly central). The territory is the historic core of Champagne wine country, encompassing most of the Champagne AOC vineyard area along the Marne river valley. Its outline has not changed since the 1790 creation of the départements, so a present-day administrative boundary (once staged) would apply essentially unchanged across nearly all of this entry's 1800-2025 span.

## Predecessors and successors

Marne has no predecessor polity in this table: it was created directly by the 1790 Revolutionary départemental reform from parts of the former province of Champagne (chiefly the Reims and Châlons regions) and has had no distinct predecessor or successor state of its own since — it is a subdivision of France throughout, not a state that was absorbed into or split from another. No predecessor or successor codes are set. The containing national polity does change identity across this span (FRA-1800-1871 with Alsace-Lorraine, FRA-1871-1919 without it after the 1871 cession, FRA-1919-2025 reunified), but Marne itself, lying in Champagne well inland from Alsace-Lorraine, is untouched by that cession and return; the container edges above exist to satisfy tiling, not because Marne changed hands.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the proposed 1790**

The routing decision argued for start_year=1790 on the grounds that Marne is one of the ~83 départements created by the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform and has kept its shape since. That is true as administrative history, but the container-tiling requirement binds this page to what the polity table can actually supply: the earliest FRA row in data/final/polities_database.csv is FRA-1800-1871 (France with Alsace-Lorraine, 1800-1871). There is no FRA-* polity covering 1790-1800, so a container edge opening at 1790 would fall outside every candidate container's own span and the containment gate would reject it. Rather than leave a silent 10-year gap or invent an edge that points at a non-existent container span, this page starts Marne's WHEP entry in 1800, matching the earliest year the containing chain can actually vouch for. The département's true administrative continuity back to 1790 is preserved in the summary as historical fact; it just isn't representable as a container edge yet. If a pre-1800 FRA polity is added later, this page's start_year could be revisited.

### d-code-uses-mrn-not-numeric-insee

**Subunit token MRN chosen over the numeric INSEE code 51**

France's INSEE département code for Marne is the two-digit number 51, and French GIS/statistical sources routinely key départements by that number rather than a letter abbreviation. The dominant WHEP subnational pattern (57 of 66 rows) uses a short alphabetic subunit token, e.g. ESP-AS, ESP-CA, DZA-CVD. A bare numeric token (FRA-51-1800-2025) would be harder to read as a place name and risks collision with a year-like token in future parsing. MRN was chosen as a three-letter mnemonic for Marne, consistent with the alphabetic convention and with no existing FRA-* subnational row to conflict with, since this country currently sets its own precedent (0 existing subnational FRA codes).

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-not-sourced

**No polygon is attached; France ADM1 boundaries are not yet staged locally**

polygon_source is `none` because none of the registered sources in scripts/sources.yaml currently carry a French département-level feature: cshapes-2.0 and cshapes-europe are country-level only, and gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered but the locally staged file is only an 81-country subset that excludes France entirely. The full GADM 4.1 release does publish France at ADM1/département granularity (Marne would appear as FRA.x_1), and IGN's ADMIN EXPRESS dataset is the official French alternative; either would need to be downloaded and staged before this entry could move from `unassigned` to `assigned`. Licence terms for both have not been checked. Until then this row carries no geometry and polygon_area_km2 is left null rather than guessed.

### oq-boundary-stability-unverified

**Marne's boundary continuity since 1790 was assumed, not checked against a dedicated boundary history**

The routing reasoning and this page both assert that Marne's borders have been stable since the 1790 creation of the French départements, aside from the general national-level changes already captured by the FRA container chain (Alsace-Lorraine cession and return). This has not been independently verified against a French administrative boundary history (e.g. INSEE's own canton/arrondissement change records, which do note occasional inter-département boundary adjustments for individual communes). If a boundary source later shows a material change to Marne's extent at some point in this span, the single container-chain-wide span used here may need to be split, and the polygon (once sourced) may need a period-specific feature rather than one reused across the full 1800-2025 range.
