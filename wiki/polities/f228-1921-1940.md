# RSFSR/USSR (1921-1940)


> **`iso3` set to `SUN` on 2026-08-13 (issue 55).** This row previously carried none, so it
> belonged to no ISO family at all and could not be reached by `matchlib.pick_by_year`'s ISO
> route -- only by an explicit alias. The consumer's `regions_full` and
> `polity_area_crosswalk` both reference `SUN`, and resolving it through `polities` returned
> nothing even though the polity plainly existed.
>
> `SUN` is the **ISO 3166-3** former alpha-3 code for the USSR (3166-3 entry `SUHH`), and
> using it follows the rule already applied to `CSK`, `YUG`, `DDR`, `ANT` and `SCG`: a
> dissolved entity takes its own 3166-3 code when its polity family terminates -- when it
> split into several successors -- and inherits its successor's code only when the family
> itself continues into a live polity (which is why Burma's rows carry `MMR` and Southern
> Rhodesia's carry `ZWE`). The USSR dissolved into **15** successors, so no successor's code
> is the right one.
>
> Why this was deferred by the first pass (PR #129) and is safe now: the objection was that
> the F228 prefix spans two entities -- the Russian Empire and the USSR -- so no single code
> covers the family. True, but `iso3` is a **per-row** field over periodised rows, so the two
> entities can be coded separately. Only the three USSR-era rows take `SUN`; the Russian
> Empire rows are untouched (`F228-1800-1856` and `F228-1856-1905` keep `RUS`, and the five
> 1905-1921 rows keep their blank).
>
> Verified before applying: this adds **no** ISO collision. `SUN` is carried by exactly the
> three consecutive USSR rows and by no other polity, so no pair shares it over overlapping
> years -- the check that matters because a wrong `iso3` is what put a Belgium-Luxembourg row
> in Belgium's family (issue 40) and made "Malaysia" resolve to British North Borneo
> (issue 44).

> This row is named "RSFSR/USSR (1921-1940)" and starts one year before the USSR was
> established (30 December 1922). **19 of its 20 years are USSR**, so `SUN` is the code that
> describes almost all of it; the alternative -- leaving the row blank to avoid a one-year
> overstatement -- keeps it unreachable for the whole span, which is the larger error.

## Summary

This row covers **20 years** spanning the final year of the RSFSR
and the first 18 years of the USSR. The USSR was formally
established on 30 December 1922; before that date the polity was
still the RSFSR. The row covers the NEP era, Stalin's rise to
power, collectivization, the Great Purge, and the
Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact. The 1940 end date corresponds to the
Soviet annexation of the Baltic states, Bessarabia, and Northern
Bukovina — a major territorial expansion.

Predecessor: [f228-1920-1921](f228-1920-1921.md).
Successor: [f228-1940-1945](f228-1940-1945.md).

## Territorial extent

- **30 December 1922: USSR formally established.** The RSFSR,
  Ukrainian SSR, Belorussian SSR, and Transcaucasian SFSR signed
  the Treaty of Creation of the USSR. This is a political
  reorganization, not a territorial change per se — the
  constituent republics were already under Bolshevik control.
- **1939-08-23: Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact.** Secret protocol
  divided Eastern Europe into German and Soviet spheres of
  influence. This set the stage for the 1939-1940 Soviet
  territorial expansion.
- **1939-09: Soviet invasion of eastern Poland.** Following the
  German invasion from the west, the USSR annexed western
  Ukraine and western Belarus (the former Kresy lost at Riga
  in 1921).
- **1939-11 to 1940-03: Winter War with Finland.** The USSR
  gained the Karelian Isthmus, Salla, and a lease on the
  Hanko peninsula.
- **1940-06: Soviet annexation of Bessarabia and Northern
  Bukovina** from Romania.
- **1940-06 to 1940-08: Soviet annexation of the Baltic states**
  (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania). These were incorporated as
  SSRs of the USSR.
- The 1940 end date thus marks a **major territorial expansion**
  that justifies the CSV split.

## Known data-quality issues

### iia-russian-federation-label-is-whole-ussr

The IIA yearbooks label their Soviet series **"Russian Federation" /
"Russia"** but report **whole-USSR** territory, not the RSFSR proper.
Confirmed 2026-07-24 by assertion verification of
`russian federation|iia|1922-1940` (435 rows): the cotton lint and
cotton seed areas (medians ~796,000 ha lint, ~1.5M ha seed) are far too
large for the RSFSR alone — Soviet cotton was grown overwhelmingly in
Central Asia and Transcaucasia, outside the RSFSR. Rye area (~22M ha) is
likewise union-scale. This matches the row's documented RSFSR-to-USSR
scope, so the routing is correct; the label is simply anachronistic.

Treat "Russian Federation" in IIA-era (pre-1945) Soviet data as
whole-USSR by default, and check magnitudes before routing any such
label to a Russia-proper polity.
