# Finistère

## Summary

Finistère is a French département occupying the westernmost tip of Brittany, on the Atlantic coast of northwestern France, with its préfecture at Quimper and its largest city at Brest. It was one of roughly 83 départements created in a single Revolutionary administrative reform on 4 March 1790, when the National Constituent Assembly replaced the old provinces (here, western Brittany) with a grid of new units sized so no resident lived more than a day's ride from the seat of government. Finistère's boundary has not been redrawn since: unlike Belfort (split from Haut-Rhin in 1871), the Alsace-Lorraine départements (annexed by Germany 1871-1919), Corsica (split into two départements in 1975), or the overseas départements (created 1946), Finistère is not one of this table's listed departure cases from the default rule, so its span is the default: creation to present. This page is typed `subnational` because Finistère was never a sovereign state -- it is, and always has been, a second-tier administrative division of France, first of the Kingdom/Republic/Empire in its various 1790-1870 forms, then of the Third through Fifth Republics. The container chain reflects only the *national* row's own era-slicing (pre/post Alsace-Lorraine, pre/post 1919 reunification), not any change to Finistère itself.

**Territory description:** Finistère covers the far western end of the Brittany peninsula, bounded by the English Channel to the north, the Atlantic (Iroise Sea) to the west, the Bay of Biscay to the south, and the départements of Côtes-d'Armor and Morbihan to the east. It is roughly the land west of a line from Morlaix to Concarneau. A reader can locate it on a modern map as the department containing Brest, Quimper, Douarnenez, and the Crozon peninsula. Its modern area is approximately **6,733 km²**, per INSEE's current published figure for the département -- this is a stated reference figure, not a measurement of any polygon attached to this page, since no polygon is attached (see polygon status above).

## Sourced claims

- INSEE (France's national statistics institute) lists Finistère as département code 29, préfecture Quimper, with a current land area of approximately 6,733 km² -- a figure a reader can check against INSEE's own département fact sheet.
- The Revolutionary law of 4 March 1790 (loi du 4 mars 1790, later refined by the decree of 26 February 1790 taking effect 4 March) divided France into approximately 83 départements including Finistère, carved from the former province of Brittany; this creation date and mechanism can be checked against standard French administrative-history references (e.g. Ozouf-Marignier's work on the 1790 division, or the Archives Nationales' record of the decree).
- Alsace-Lorraine's départements were annexed by the German Empire from 1871 to 1919 under the Treaty of Frankfurt and returned under the Treaty of Versailles, which is why this table slices the France row at 1871 and 1919 -- checkable against the treaty texts -- but Finistère, outside Alsace-Lorraine, was unaffected by either boundary change.

## Decisions

### d-code-and-abbreviation

**Code uses FIN, the common short form, not the INSEE digit code 29**

Following the dominant 57-of-66 subnational pattern <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, this page is coded FRA-FIN-1800-2025. Finistère's INSEE department number is 29, but the existing ESP-* precedent (ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-GC...) uses letter abbreviations rather than numeric codes, and a bare '29' reads badly as a subunit token next to years. FIN is unambiguous for Finistère and matches the letter-code convention already established for Spanish provinces in this table, so it was chosen over the INSEE number.

### d-start-year-truncated-to-container-floor

**start_year truncated from the proposed 1790 to 1800, the container chain's floor**

Stage 1's routing_reasoning proposed start_year=1790, citing the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary creation of the ~83 original départements. But the FRA container chain's earliest era on file is FRA-1800-1871 (start_year 1800) -- FRA-1800-1919 exists as a row but is marked status=superseded and is not a valid container target. Per the container-floor rule (a unit cannot be contained before its container exists), this page is truncated to start_year=1800 rather than silently carrying the 1790 territorial-origin date into the frontmatter, where it would create a containment gap the gate rejects. The 1790 creation date is preserved in the summary as historical fact, distinct from the page's start_year.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap-in-fra-container-chain

**No FRA container row exists for 1790-1800, so Finistère's true origin predates its container floor**

Finistère was created on 4 March 1790, ten years before the earliest FRA national row (FRA-1800-1871) begins. This page's start_year is truncated to 1800 to satisfy the container-floor rule, which means the 1790-1800 decade of Finistère's existence -- the Revolutionary and First Republic period -- has no polity row at all in this table, national or subnational. If a FRA-1790-1800 (or similarly bounded) national row is ever added, this page's start_year should be reconsidered and pulled back to 1790 to match.
