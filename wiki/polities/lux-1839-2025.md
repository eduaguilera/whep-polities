# Luxembourg

## Summary

Luxembourg is tracked as a distinct national polity from **1839** to the
database truncation year **2025**. The 1839 start is the **territorial
event**: Biger gives the precise date and area. The
**Treaty of 19 April 1839** [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg)
established the limits of the "Eastern Duchy of Luxembourg" at
**998 sq mi (2,584 sq km) out of a prior 2,700 sq mi (7,000 sq km)**
— i.e. Luxembourg retained about **37%** of its territory after
the western (Oïl-speaking) part was ceded to Belgium
[biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg),
[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1839](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1839). Under WHEP's
territorial-economic definition of a polity
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition), this is the trigger for a
new polity row; WHEP does not require legal independence by any
external state-system criterion.

The row is continuous from 1839 through 2025 across the 1848
constitutional-settlement moment when Luxembourg became
"independent in its own right" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg), the 1867
severance of the German Confederation ties after which the
Luxembourg-Prussia boundary became an international boundary
[biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg), the 1890 end of the personal union with
the Netherlands [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1890](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1890), the 1842
Zollverein entry [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1842](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1842), the two
German wartime occupations (1914–1918 and 1940–1944), and the 1921
Belgium-Luxembourg Economic Union
[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1921](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1921). None of these events
substantially altered Luxembourg's territory, so none triggers a
WHEP split. Biger explicitly confirms: "Since 1867 no changes in
the boundary line were made" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). The end
year is the WHEP database truncation, not an end of sovereignty.

A parallel aggregate row `BLX-1800-2025` "Belgium-Luxembourg" exists in
the database for trade/FAOSTAT accounting, because FAOSTAT did not
disaggregate Belgium and Luxembourg until 1999 [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). That
aggregate is NOT this polity and the two must not be conflated in
analysis.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` per the CSV (`polygon_source`).
  CShapes temporal coverage begins in 1886 [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope), so
  CShapes polygons for Luxembourg **1839–1885** are not CShapes
  observations at all — they are either back-projections (CShapes
  backdates static borders to 1886 [cshapes-2.0 §geocoding](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#geocoding)) or
  come from a different source like the CShapes-Europe extension.
  See [oq-polygon-provenance](#oq-polygon-provenance).
- **Static polygon assumption.** Luxembourg's borders did not change
  after 1839 in ways CShapes would have coded: the 1867 Treaty of
  London did not alter borders, only sovereignty status
  [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1867](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1867), and no later territorial
  transfer approached the CShapes 100 × 100 km threshold
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes). Luxembourg is therefore almost
  certainly one of the 152 CShapes "countries with a single polygon
  during the entire period" [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope), meaning its
  polygon is a single geometry copied across 1886–2019. This is a
  reasonable simplification for Luxembourg specifically, but only
  because no large transfer occurred — the lint rule is that small
  European transfers are silently absent from CShapes.
- **1839 partition.** The Treaty of London left Luxembourg as
  "predominantly German" — it lost the geographically larger western
  (Oïl-speaking) part of the duchy to Belgium
  [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1839](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1839). This partition is
  pre-1886 and thus outside CShapes entirely [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope);
  any pre-1839 Luxembourg polygon must come from another source.
- **CShapes version: COW-based, confirmed.** WHEP loads the COW
  variant of CShapes 2.0, not the GW variant. This was confirmed by
  direct inspection of `data/geodata/cshapes2_full.gpkg` and then
  by bit-equivalent regeneration from
  `cshapes::cshp(useGW = FALSE, dependencies = TRUE)` — see
  `log.md 2026-04-11 decision-cshapes-is-cow-based` and
  `log.md 2026-04-11 cshapes-reproducibility-verified`. For
  Luxembourg this matters because COW and GW disagree about
  pre-1945 state-system membership [cshapes-2.0 §coding-states](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-states);
  the concrete consequences for Luxembourg's CShapes row timeline
  are laid out under *Sourced claims*.
- **Post-2019.** CShapes 2.0 ends in 2019 [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope).
  Luxembourg's borders after 2019 are assumed unchanged by WHEP, which
  is a safe assumption for this polity but is called out here to keep
  the lint honest.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [nld-1800-1830](nld-1800-1830.md) — *United Kingdom of the
  Netherlands*, which encompassed Luxembourg prior to the 1830 Belgian
  Revolution and the 1839 settlement [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The predecessor
  link is political, not a pure territorial split: Luxembourg's
  separation was negotiated at the Treaty of London, and a separate
  `nld-1830-2025` row tracks the rump Netherlands
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- **Successor:** NA (Luxembourg is still active at the database end
  year of 2025).

## Sourced claims

- **Pre-1815 background**: Luxembourg "became a separate duchy in 1354"
  and was "ceded to France in 1797" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). Under
  French rule 1797–1815.
- The 1815 Congress of Vienna "gave formal autonomy to Luxembourg" and
  created the Grand Duchy in personal union with the Netherlands
  [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1815](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1815). Biger adds: "The limits
  of Luxembourg were first delimited by this treaty and the grand
  duchy (which was considered an independent state) had special
  relations in eastern boundary" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). Per the
  Vienna settlement, "areas in eastern Luxembourg were transferred
  to Prussia (Germany)" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg).
- **1831 Belgian Revolution**: "Belgium achieved independence on
  20 January 1831 — including in it the entire territory of
  Luxembourg" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). The Netherlands refused to
  accept this and "its army entered the newly established state"
  [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg).
- **15 November 1831 London Treaty**: French and British mediation
  produced a treaty that "establish[ed] the present Belgian
  boundaries, leaving Luxembourg within Netherlands"
  [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). This partition was not yet accepted by
  all parties; Dutch recognition came in **1838**
  [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg).
- **19 April 1839 Treaty** (the WHEP row start): "The limits of
  Eastern Duchy of Luxembourg (998 sq. miles [2,584 sq. km.] out of
  2,700 sq. miles [7,000 sq. km.]) were established by the Treaty
  of 19 April 1839" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). Luxembourg retained
  ~37% of its prior area; ~63% went to Belgium. Wikipedia
  corroborates the partition more loosely as the cession of the
  "geographically larger western part of the duchy"
  [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1839](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1839).
- In 1842 Luxembourg "integrated it into the German Zollverein"
  [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1842](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1842). This matters for trade
  analysis: from 1842 until World War I, Luxembourg's external trade
  regime was German, not independent.
- **1848** — "The area became independent in its own right in 1848"
  [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). This is a *new* date relative to the
  Wikipedia source; Biger places Luxembourg's full legal
  independence at 1848 (the constitutional-settlement moment),
  distinct from both the 1839 territorial partition and the 1867
  international-boundary event. Under the WHEP polity definition
  rule this is a regime change, not a territorial change, and
  therefore does not trigger a split.
- **1867** — Biger: "The severance of the German Confederation in
  1867 led to the dissolution of ties between Luxembourg and
  Prussia and the dividing line became an international boundary
  between the two states" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). Wikipedia
  corroborates with the specific mechanism — the Second Treaty of
  London "guaranteed the perpetual independence and neutrality of
  the state"; the Prussian garrison withdrew and the fortress was
  dismantled [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1867](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1867). (Note:
  Biger's phrasing "severance of the German Confederation in 1867"
  is slightly loose — the Confederation was formally dissolved in
  1866 and the 1867 event was the Treaty of London itself; see
  [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg)'s §known-limitations.)
- The personal union with the Netherlands ended in 1890 when the
  "grand duchy passed to the House of Nassau-Weilburg" under Salic
  succession [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1890](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1890).
- In 1921 Luxembourg "entered into an economic and monetary union with
  Belgium" — the Belgium-Luxembourg Economic Union (BLEU)
  [wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1921](../sources/wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11.md#1921). This is the historical
  basis for the `BLX-1800-2025` Belgium-Luxembourg aggregate row in
  the database, though FAOSTAT's use of the aggregate persisted until
  1999 [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- COW country code 212 [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). ISO3 `LUX` [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- **COW state-system membership for Luxembourg** (direct read of
  `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv`): two tenure rows,
  `1920-11-15 → 1940-05-10` and `1944-09-10 → 2024-12-31`
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates). Luxembourg is absent from
  COW entirely before 1920-11-15 and during the 1940–1944 German
  occupation. The 1920 date is post-WWI League of Nations
  admission; the gap is the suspension of independent diplomatic
  action during occupation [cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion).
- **CShapes-COW row timeline for Luxembourg** (direct inspection of
  `data/geodata/cshapes2_full.gpkg`):

  | window | CShapes-COW status |
  |---|---|
  | 1893-01-01 – 1914-08-01 | `N/A` |
  | 1914-08-02 – 1918-11-11 | `occupied` |
  | 1918-11-12 – 1920-11-14 | `N/A` |
  | 1920-11-15 – 1940-05-10 | `independent` |
  | 1940-05-11 – 1944-09-09 | `occupied` |
  | 1944-09-10 – 2019-12-31 | `independent` |

  The `independent` windows match COW's membership dates exactly —
  confirming again that CShapes uses COW state-system membership as
  its independence criterion [cshapes-2.0 §coding-states](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-states). The
  two `N/A` windows (1893–1914 and 1918–1920) are periods where
  CShapes carries a polygon for Luxembourg even though COW does not
  count it as a system member — so these must come from CShapes's
  dependency-tracking layer (Territorial Change Dataset +
  Biger 1995 + Brownlie & Burns 1979
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)), not from COW membership.
- Under WHEP's territorial-economic polity definition
  [log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition), the 1839–1920
  window is still a single continuous Luxembourg polity, regardless
  of COW's 1920 start — Luxembourg's territory did not change
  substantially between 1839 and 1920, trade data for the duchy
  exists in that window, and COW's absence is a consequence of
  COW's diplomatic-recognition criterion rather than a statement
  about whether Luxembourg existed.
  [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg)
- **Territorial stability since 1867 — directly corroborated.**
  Biger: "Since 1867 no changes in the boundary line were made.
  The political changes that brought about major transformations
  in the Western European boundary lines never affected the
  Germany–Luxembourg line and it is one of the most peaceful
  boundaries in the world today" [biger-1995 §luxembourg](../sources/biger-1995.md#luxembourg). This
  is the academic statement the WHEP continuity rule requires
  — the 1867–1995 period is covered directly, the 1839–1867
  period by the 1839 treaty itself. The two German wartime
  occupations (1914–1918, 1940–1944) interrupted sovereignty but
  did not alter final borders, which is why the WHEP row remains
  unbroken across them.

## Open questions


### oq-polygon-provenance

Pre-1886 polygon provenance. What
polygon actually represents Luxembourg in the WHEP pipeline for
1839–1885? The 1839–1885 window is fully outside CShapes' stated
temporal scope [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope) and outside its actual
Luxembourg coverage (which starts 1893). Possibilities: CShapes
back-projection, a CShapes-Europe entry, a GADM modern proxy.
Needs verification against `R/05_cross_reference.R` and
`data/analysis/polygon_matching_report.csv`.

### oq-cshapes-1893-start

Why CShapes's first Luxembourg row
starts 1893-01-01, not 1886-01-01. Not driven by COW membership
(COW does not list Luxembourg until 1920-11-15
[cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates)). Must therefore come from
CShapes's dependency-tracking sources (Territorial Change Dataset,
Biger 1995, Brownlie & Burns 1979 [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)).
The specific 1893 event is unknown — candidates include a
Zollverein-era customs change, a population-threshold crossing
in the Territorial Change Dataset, or a neighboring-border
delineation. Needs another source ingest or inspection of the
cshapes package internals to resolve.

### oq-bleu-faostat

BLEU vs `BLX-1800-2025`. BLEU began in 1921,
but the FAOSTAT `BLX-1800-2025` aggregate row in the database
runs 1800–1999. Document why the aggregate starts in 1800
(pre-BLEU) — almost certainly a FAOSTAT back-projection
convention, but it should be cited, not inferred.
