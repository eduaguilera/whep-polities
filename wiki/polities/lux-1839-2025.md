# Luxembourg

## Summary

Luxembourg is tracked as a distinct national polity from **1839** to the
database truncation year **2025**. The 1839 start is the **territorial
event**: the Treaty of London reduced the Grand Duchy to roughly half
its prior area when the western (Oïl-speaking) part was ceded to
Belgium `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1839]`. Under WHEP's
territorial-economic definition of a polity
(`log.md 2026-04-11 decision-whep-polity-definition`), this is the
trigger for a new polity row; WHEP does not require legal independence
by any external state-system criterion.

The row is continuous from 1839 through 2025 across the 1890 end of
the personal union with the Netherlands
`[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1890]`, the 1842 Zollverein entry
`[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1842]`, the two German wartime
occupations (1914–1918 and 1940–1944), and the 1921 Belgium-Luxembourg
Economic Union `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1921]`. None of
these events substantially altered Luxembourg's territory, so none
triggers a WHEP split. The end year is the WHEP database truncation,
not an end of sovereignty.

A parallel aggregate row `F15-1800-1999` "Belgium-Luxembourg" exists in
the database for trade/FAOSTAT accounting, because FAOSTAT did not
disaggregate Belgium and Luxembourg until 1999 `[database]`. That
aggregate is NOT this polity and the two must not be conflated in
analysis.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` per the CSV (`polygon_source`).
  CShapes temporal coverage begins in 1886 `[cshapes-2.0 §scope]`, so
  CShapes polygons for Luxembourg **1839–1885** are not CShapes
  observations at all — they are either back-projections (CShapes
  backdates static borders to 1886 `[cshapes-2.0 §geocoding]`) or
  come from a different source like the CShapes-Europe extension.
  See open question 5.
- **Static polygon assumption.** Luxembourg's borders did not change
  after 1839 in ways CShapes would have coded: the 1867 Treaty of
  London did not alter borders, only sovereignty status
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1867]`, and no later territorial
  transfer approached the CShapes 100 × 100 km threshold
  `[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes]`. Luxembourg is therefore almost
  certainly one of the 152 CShapes "countries with a single polygon
  during the entire period" `[cshapes-2.0 §scope]`, meaning its
  polygon is a single geometry copied across 1886–2019. This is a
  reasonable simplification for Luxembourg specifically, but only
  because no large transfer occurred — the lint rule is that small
  European transfers are silently absent from CShapES.
- **1839 partition.** The Treaty of London left Luxembourg as
  "predominantly German" — it lost the geographically larger western
  (Oïl-speaking) part of the duchy to Belgium
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1839]`. This partition is
  pre-1886 and thus outside CShapes entirely `[cshapes-2.0 §scope]`;
  any pre-1839 Luxembourg polygon must come from another source.
- **COW vs GW coding.** CShapes ships in two versions that differ on
  pre-1945 independence dates `[cshapes-2.0 §coding-states]`. The
  repo's CSV records COW code 212 for Luxembourg `[database]` but does
  not record which CShapes version was loaded. For Luxembourg this
  likely does not matter (1839 is well-attested under both schemes)
  but the convention should be explicit — see open question 6.
- **Post-2019.** CShapes 2.0 ends in 2019 `[cshapes-2.0 §scope]`.
  Luxembourg's borders after 2019 are assumed unchanged by WHEP, which
  is a safe assumption for this polity but is called out here to keep
  the lint honest.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [[nld-1800-1830]] — *United Kingdom of the
  Netherlands*, which encompassed Luxembourg prior to the 1830 Belgian
  Revolution and the 1839 settlement `[database]`. The predecessor
  link is political, not a pure territorial split: Luxembourg's
  separation was negotiated at the Treaty of London, and a separate
  `nld-1830-2025` row tracks the rump Netherlands
  `[database]`.
- **Successor:** NA (Luxembourg is still active at the database end
  year of 2025).

## Sourced claims

- The 1815 Congress of Vienna "gave formal autonomy to Luxembourg" and
  created the Grand Duchy in personal union with the Netherlands
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1815]`.
- The 1839 Treaty of London ceded the "geographically larger western
  part of the duchy... to Belgium"; the remaining territory is the
  polity tracked in this row
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1839]`.
- In 1842 Luxembourg "integrated it into the German Zollverein"
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1842]`. This matters for trade
  analysis: from 1842 until World War I, Luxembourg's external trade
  regime was German, not independent.
- The 1867 Second Treaty of London "guaranteed the perpetual
  independence and neutrality of the state"; the Prussian garrison
  withdrew and the fortress was dismantled
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1867]`.
- The personal union with the Netherlands ended in 1890 when the
  "grand duchy passed to the House of Nassau-Weilburg" under Salic
  succession `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1890]`.
- In 1921 Luxembourg "entered into an economic and monetary union with
  Belgium" — the Belgium-Luxembourg Economic Union (BLEU)
  `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1921]`. This is the historical
  basis for the `F15-1800-1999` Belgium-Luxembourg aggregate row in
  the database, though FAOSTAT's use of the aggregate persisted until
  1999 `[database]`.
- COW country code 212 `[database]`. ISO3 `LUX` `[database]`.
- **COW state-system membership for Luxembourg** (direct read of
  `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv`): two tenure rows,
  `1920-11-15 → 1940-05-10` and `1944-09-10 → 2024-12-31`
  `[cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates]`. Luxembourg is absent from
  COW entirely before 1920-11-15 and during the 1940–1944 German
  occupation. The 1920 date is post-WWI League of Nations
  admission; the gap is the suspension of independent diplomatic
  action during occupation `[cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion]`.
- **CShapes-COW row timeline for Luxembourg** (direct inspection of
  `data/geodata/cshapes2_full.gpkg`):

  | window | CShapes-COW status |
  |---|---|
  | 1893-01-01 – 1914-08-01 | `N/A` |
  | 1914-08-02 – 1918-11-11 | `occupied` |
  | 1918-11-12 – 1920-11-14 | `N/A` |
  | 1920-11-15 – 1940-05-10 | `independent` |
  | 1940-05-11 – 1944-09-09 | `occupied` |
  | 1944-09-10 – 2019-12-31 | `independent` |

  The `independent` windows match COW's membership dates exactly —
  confirming again that CShapes uses COW state-system membership as
  its independence criterion `[cshapes-2.0 §coding-states]`. The
  two `N/A` windows (1893–1914 and 1918–1920) are periods where
  CShapes carries a polygon for Luxembourg even though COW does not
  count it as a system member — so these must come from CShapes's
  dependency-tracking layer (Territorial Change Dataset +
  Biger 1995 + Brownlie & Burns 1979
  `[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes]`), not from COW membership.
- Under WHEP's territorial-economic polity definition
  `[log 2026-04-11 decision-whep-polity-definition]`, the 1839–1920
  window is still a single continuous Luxembourg polity, regardless
  of COW's 1920 start — Luxembourg's territory did not change
  substantially between 1839 and 1920, trade data for the duchy
  exists in that window, and COW's absence is a consequence of
  COW's diplomatic-recognition criterion rather than a statement
  about whether Luxembourg existed.

## Open questions

1. **Pre-1886 polygon provenance.** What polygon actually represents
   Luxembourg in the WHEP pipeline for 1839–1885? The 1839–1885
   window is fully outside CShapes' stated temporal scope
   `[cshapes-2.0 §scope]` and outside its actual Luxembourg coverage
   (which starts 1893). Possibilities: CShapes back-projection, a
   CShapes-Europe entry, a GADM modern proxy. Needs verification
   against `R/05_cross_reference.R` and
   `data/analysis/polygon_matching_report.csv`.
2. **Territorial stability 1839–2025.** The WHEP continuity rule
   `[log 2026-04-11 decision-whep-polity-definition]` requires that
   the territory did not substantially change across the row. This
   is almost certainly true for Luxembourg post-1839 but has not
   been formally verified against a historical atlas. The two
   German occupations (1914–1918, 1940–1944) did not alter the
   final border per `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11 §1867]`'s
   successor sources, but this should be cross-checked.
3. **Academic corroboration for 1839.** The 1839 territorial event
   itself rests on `[wikipedia-luxembourg-2026-04-11]` alone. One
   academic or reference-work source (Britannica, a national
   history) would be enough to move this page to `reviewed`.
4. **CShapes 1886–1892 gap narrowed, not resolved.** The 1893-01-01
   start of the CShapes Luxembourg row is NOT driven by COW
   membership (COW does not list Luxembourg until 1920-11-15
   `[cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates]`). It must therefore come
   from CShapes's dependency-tracking sources (Territorial Change
   Dataset, Biger 1995, Brownlie & Burns 1979
   `[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes]`). The specific event on or near
   1893-01-01 that caused a new non-member row to be created is
   unknown — possible candidates are a Zollverein-era customs
   change, a population-threshold crossing in the Territorial
   Change Dataset, or a border delineation elsewhere on
   Luxembourg's frontier. Needs either another source ingest or
   inspection of the CShapes codebase to resolve.
5. **BLEU vs `F15-1800-1999`.** BLEU began in 1921, but the FAOSTAT
   `F15-1800-1999` aggregate row in the database runs 1800–1999.
   Document why the aggregate starts in 1800 (pre-BLEU) — almost
   certainly a FAOSTAT back-projection convention, but it should be
   cited, not inferred.
