# Denmark (to 1920)

## Summary

This row tracks reduced Denmark (without Schleswig-Holstein) for
**57 years**, from the **1864** Second Schleswig War to the **1920**
Northern Schleswig plebiscite [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
The CSV notes: "Split from DNK-1800-1920. Post-Second Schleswig War
Denmark without Schleswig-Holstein (-33% territory ~38496 km2)."

At 1864, Denmark had just lost Schleswig-Holstein to Prussia and
Austria -- a ~33% territorial loss (~57K km² to ~38K km²). Biger
confirms: "In 1864 Denmark lost the whole of Schleswig-Holstein
(representing 40% of its territory) to Prussia"
[biger-1995 §denmark](../sources/biger-1995.md#denmark).

The 1920 split is the Northern Schleswig return via plebiscite (a
Treaty of Versailles provision). After WWI: "only the Danish-populated
zones of northern Schleswig reverted to Denmark after a referendum"
[biger-1995 §denmark](../sources/biger-1995.md#denmark).

War (1864).
Successor: [dnk-1920-2025](dnk-1920-2025.md) --

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0 + CShapes-Europe` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). CShapes 2.0
  starts at 1886, so the 1864-1885 period would use CShapes-Europe.
  Area ~38,496 km².
- **Metropolitan composition.** Denmark proper (Jutland, Zealand,
  Funen, and associated islands) without Schleswig-Holstein. Also
  includes Iceland (until independence 1918/1944), the Faroe Islands,

## Open questions

### oq-1920-plebiscite-date

Exact date of the Schleswig plebiscites and territory transfer. The
CShapes split date would pin this. The Treaty of Versailles mandated
plebiscites in Schleswig; historically, Zone 1 (northern) voted on
10 February 1920, Zone 2 (central) on 14 March 1920. The formal
transfer of northern Schleswig to Denmark took place on 15 June 1920.
The CSV uses 1920 as the end year, consistent with all of these dates.

### oq-iceland-1918

Iceland became a sovereign state in personal union with Denmark on
1 December 1918 (Act of Union). This is a substantial territorial
change within the row window -- though Iceland may not be counted in
the ~38K km² metropolitan area figure. The relationship between
Iceland's status change and Denmark's WHEP row structure needs
clarification.
