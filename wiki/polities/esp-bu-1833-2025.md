# Burgos (province of Spain)

## Summary

Burgos is one of Spain's 50 provinces, located in the northern plateau (Meseta) of the historic region of Old Castile, in what is now the autonomous community of Castilla y León. It was created as a first-level administrative division by the 30 November 1833 royal decree ("división de Javier de Burgos"), which redrew Spain's internal boundaries into provinces largely along the lines of the old Bourbon intendencias, replacing looser historical kingdoms and lordships. Burgos city, on the Arlanzón river, had been the historic capital of the Kingdom of Castile in the medieval period, giving the province an identity distinct from a purely administrative creation. This entry is `type: subnational` because Burgos is, and has always been, a constituent unit of the sovereign Spanish state (Kingdom of Spain, First and Second Republics, Francoist State, restored monarchy) rather than an independent or quasi-sovereign entity — it never had separate international recognition, its own currency, or a period of self-government outside the Spanish state, unlike bespoke-coded historical territories such as Hyderabad or the Ryukyu Islands. The 1833 start date is used because Burgos is not one of the Canary Islands provinces that departed from the mainland provincial map in the 1927 split (which produced Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas from the single prior Canarias province) — for all non-Canary provinces the country convention applies the 1833 creation date directly. The province has retained essentially the same boundaries since 1833 and continues as a live administrative and statistical unit through the open end_year of 2025.

No specific row count or dataset year range was included in the routing decision payload provided to this authoring pass — that evidence trail (source name, row counts, years) lives in the pipeline's evidence snapshot rather than in the decision JSON itself, which is flagged as an open question below. What confirms Burgos is a distinct statistical/administrative entity is its independent existence as one of Spain's 50 provinces since the 1833 territorial division — a division standard in Spanish administrative history and reflected in INE's provincial code system (cpro=09) and Eurostat's NUTS3 classification (ES412), both of which report Burgos as a discrete unit separate from all other provinces.

**Territory description:** Burgos province occupies the northeastern part of Castilla y León, in the historic region of Old Castile, on Spain's northern Meseta plateau. It borders Cantabria and the Basque province of Álava to the north, La Rioja and Soria to the east, Segovia and Valladolid to the south, and Palencia to the west. The provincial capital is the city of Burgos, on the Arlanzón river, roughly 240 km north of Madrid. The province corresponds to modern NUTS3 code ES412.

## Predecessors and successors

Burgos has no predecessor polity: it was created directly by the 1833 Javier de Burgos royal decree that divided Spain into 49 (later 50) provinces, superseding the older, larger historical region/intendencia of Burgos rather than continuing a pre-existing statistical unit tracked in this database. It has no successor: the province remains a current first-level administrative and statistical unit of Spain through the open end_year of 2025, unaffected by the later NUTS/autonomous-community reorganization since Castilla y León (of which Burgos is a constituent province) was formed from existing untouched provincial boundaries in 1983.
