# Poland (1921-1945)

## Summary

This row tracks Poland for **25 years**, the interwar and WWII period
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Interwar Poland (Second Polish Republic) with borders settled by Treaty
of Riga (1921). ~389,000 km² (~50.7 sq deg in CShapes). September 1939
invasion by Germany and USSR partitioned Poland. COW gap 1939-09-27 to
1945-06-28. Government-in-exile in London. End date 1945 marks
liberation and establishment of communist government.

Predecessor: [pol-1920-1921](pol-1920-1921.md) -- Treaty of Riga.
Successor: [pol-1945-2025](pol-1945-2025.md) -- post-WWII Poland.

### oq-wartime-reporting-basis

**What territory do 1939-1945 agricultural figures actually cover?**
Raised 2026-07-24 by assertion verification of `poland|juan|1922-1945`.

The `juan` series (rye, wheat, oats, barley, potatoes; ha and tonnes)
shows a roughly parallel ~20-30% decline across all five staples from
1939 into a 1940-1944 plateau, then a sharp 1945 collapse (rye area
-92% year-on-year) as the front crossed the country January-May 1945.
A uniform proportional fall across crops with very different regional
distributions within pre-war Poland reads as occupation-era production
collapse (requisitioning, labour/input/livestock losses) reported on a
constant pre-war territorial basis — a hard boundary cut would hit the
crops unevenly. On that reading the data belongs to this row, which was
deliberately designed as the single container for the 1921-1939
stable-border period plus the 1939-1945 disruption.

But the wartime reporting convention could not be confirmed from the
source: whole pre-war Poland, General Government only, or German- and
Soviet-annexed areas excluded are all consistent with a ~25% drop. The
assertion is therefore banked as `best_available` rather than
`verified_equal` — no alternative WHEP polity exists for an
"occupied/reduced-territory wartime Poland" to reroute or split to. If
such a polity is ever created, the banked verdict reopens automatically
(the assertion evidence hash covers the polity family's structure).

Resolving this needs the source's own documentation of its 1939-1945
Polish coverage, or an external series with known territorial basis to
compare against.
