# Braga (NUTS III region of Portugal)

## Summary

PRT-BRA-1986-2025 captures Braga, a NUTS III statistical and administrative district in the north of continental Portugal, from the 1986 introduction of Portugal's NUTS regional classification (following 1986 EU accession) through the present. This is a subnational entry (`type: subnational`): Braga has never been a sovereign territory, and Portugal held sovereignty over the district throughout its existence as a distinct administrative and statistical unit. It exists as a wiki entry, rather than being folded into the national PRT-1800-2025 row, because the source data separately tabulates Braga at district granularity in years overlapping with — and continuing after — a prior verdict for the sibling unit PTFA (Faro) that was explicitly rejected on the grounds that a subnational unit is not interchangeable with its national container. The pattern confirmed by six further sibling verdicts (PTAV, PTBJ, PTEV, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG), all found unroutable to any existing polity for 1880-1988, establishes that no polity currently represents district-level Portuguese administrative units before the NUTS framework existed in 1986; this entry supplies that missing polity for the post-1986 span specifically for Braga.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the PRT_PTBR unit reports 18,561 valued rows spanning 1880-1988 for the district labeled Braga, with a 96.2% observed/blank method share indicating direct district-level observations rather than reconstructions; a further span of data continues to be tabulated at district granularity through the present (end_year 2025 in this entry). Previously, the sibling unit PTFA (Faro) was matched to the national container PRT-1800-2025, and that verdict was rejected because a subnational statistical unit is not the same territory as its national container; the same reasoning applies to Braga, and is corroborated by six further sibling units (PTAV, PTBJ, PTEV, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG) all being independently found unroutable to any existing polity for 1880-1988, confirming that no current polity represents Portuguese NUTS III districts. What confirms this entity is distinct: the existence of Portugal's own NUTS III statistical/administrative classification since 1986, under which Braga is enumerated as a named, bounded reporting unit by Eurostat/GISCO and by Portuguese national statistics (INE), separate from all-Portugal totals and from neighbouring districts such as Porto and Viana do Castelo.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision classified this as `new_source_needed`: no registered, locally-present source carries Portuguese NUTS III boundaries. `gadm-4.1-adm1` (the only registered adm1-level source) is missing Portugal from its local subset entirely; `gadm-3.6` is registered globally but not present in this repository's local data; `cshapes-europe` is present and registered but has not been checked for whether its attribute table resolves to NUTS III (district) granularity for Portugal versus only country- or NUTS I/II-level boundaries. The correct defining source — Eurostat/GISCO's NUTS boundary files (NUTS2021 vintage), which explicitly digitise Braga as a NUTS III region — is not currently registered in `sources.yaml`, so it cannot be declared here; it is recorded as an open question instead.

**Territory description:** Braga district covers the far northwest of continental Portugal, on the Spanish border, bounded by Viana do Castelo to the northwest, Vila Real to the east, and Porto district to the south. It includes the city of Braga itself (Portugal's third-largest city) and municipalities such as Guimarães, Barcelos, and Vila Nova de Famalicão. Modern Braga district covers approximately 2,673 km2 (a widely cited figure for the current NUTS III/administrative district, not measured from any polygon attached to this entry — no polygon is attached). It is one of Portugal's most densely populated districts outside the Lisbon and Porto metropolitan areas.

## Decisions

### d-follow-dominant-code-pattern

**Use ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end rather than a bespoke code**

Braga is a standard NUTS III statistical/administrative district, not a historical territory with its own name distinct from the modern administrative geography (unlike ALK or HYD). The dominant pattern for subnational rows (57 of 66) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and PRT currently sets no precedent of its own (0 existing subnational PRT codes), so PRT-BRA-1986-2025 was chosen over a bespoke code. SUBUNIT 'BRA' follows the same district-abbreviation style used by sibling Portuguese verdicts (PTAV, PTBJ, PTBR in routing_verdicts.csv), just without the leading 'PT' since that is already carried by the iso3 field.

### d-start-year-1986

**Start year fixed at 1986 per country convention default rule**

The routing decision's proposed span (1986-2025) is adopted unchanged. Portugal's NUTS regional classification, including NUTS III Braga, was introduced in 1986 following EU accession, and no departure from the default start-year rule applies to Braga the way it does for Lisboa (2015 revision) or Aveiro (historic-district exception) per country_conventions.json. Pre-1986 Braga-labelled data (1880-1988, 18,561 valued rows for the wider PT_BR unit) is NOT captured by this entry and remains routed per the sibling PTAV/PTBJ/PTEV/PTGU/PTLE/PTPG pattern as unroutable for those years, consistent with routing_concerns flagging this as unresolved rather than settled here.

### d-polygon-none-not-new-source-needed-slug

**polygon_source set to none, not a route name or unregistered slug**

The routing decision's polygon_route is new_source_needed with a candidate (Eurostat GISCO NUTS boundaries) that is not a registered source in sources.yaml. Per the authoring instructions, new_source_needed maps to polygon_source: none and polygon_status: unassigned, with the desired source (GISCO NUTS III Braga) recorded as an open question rather than written into polygon_source, since registered_source_unfetched and new_source_needed slugs are both rejected by validate_declared_sources when used as if they were registered.

## Open questions

### oq-gisco-registration

**Should Eurostat GISCO NUTS boundaries be registered as a source for Braga and its sibling districts?**

No registered, locally-present source carries Portuguese NUTS III/district-level boundaries: gadm-4.1-adm1 is missing Portugal from its local subset, gadm-3.6 is not present locally, and cshapes-europe's attribute table has not been checked for NUTS III granularity. The real-world defining source for this unit — Eurostat/GISCO's NUTS boundary files (NUTS2021 vintage) — explicitly digitises Braga for exactly this polity's span (1986-2025), so vintage risk would be low if it were registered, but licence terms have not been verified (licence_known: false in the routing decision). Registering GISCO would resolve the polygon gap not just for PRT-BRA-1986-2025 but for the other five unrouted sibling districts (PTAV, PTBJ, PTEV, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG) simultaneously.

### oq-pre1986-braga-data-disposition

**What happens to the 1880-1988 Braga-labelled data that predates this entry's start year?**

The source reports 18,561 valued rows for the PT_BR unit across 1880-1988 with a 96.2% observed/blank method share, suggesting these are direct district-level observations rather than reconstructions. Under the current sibling pattern (PTAV, PTBJ, PTEV, PTGU, PTLE, PTPG all unroutable for 1880-1988), that pre-1986 span is left unrouted rather than matched to any polity, since no polity currently represents district-level Braga before the NUTS framework existed. If a historic 'Braga district' polity is later created (mirroring the Aveiro historic-district exception noted in country_conventions.json), this entry's predecessor field should be updated to point to it, and this open question should be revisited alongside all six sibling districts together rather than one at a time, since revising the pattern for one implies revising it for all.

### oq-1986-vs-1989-start-year

**Does Braga's NUTS classification actually begin in 1986, or later like some peer proposals suggest?**

The routing decision's routing_reasoning text states the NUTS framework applies '1989 (first full year the NUTS system applies per the country convention's default rule)' in one place, but the proposed span and span_basis both use 1986 (EU accession year). This entry follows the proposed span_basis (1986) since it is the more specific, sourced figure, but the 1989 reference in the reasoning text was not independently reconciled against country_conventions.json in this session — if the convention's default rule in fact resolves to 1989 for first-full-year application, this polity's start_year and code would need to shift, along with the container edge's start_year.
