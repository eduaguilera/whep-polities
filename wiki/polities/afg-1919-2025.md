# Afghanistan

## Summary

Afghanistan as tracked by WHEP from 1919, following the end of the Third Anglo-Afghan War and the Treaty of Rawalpindi (8 August 1919) which granted Afghanistan full independence in foreign affairs. The country's borders were largely established through 19th-century treaties between the British and Russian empires, with the Durand Line (1893) defining the eastern boundary and the Wakhan Corridor separating Russian and British spheres.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is CShapes 2.0. Afghanistan is a landlocked state bounded by Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Iran, Pakistan, and China. The Durand Line boundary with Pakistan and the Iran boundary were settled between 1872 and 1935.

## Sourced claims

- Afghanistan first found unity as a nation under Ahmad Shah Abdal in 1747; it was subsequently colonized by the British after the First Afghan War (1838--1842). [biger-1995 §afghanistan](../sources/biger-1995.md#afghanistan)
- The Durand Line with Pakistan was established by treaty on 12 November 1893, reaffirmed in 1905, and finalized by treaty on 22 November 1921. [biger-1995 §afghanistan](../sources/biger-1995.md#afghanistan)
- Afghanistan acquired a common boundary with China via the 1895 Anglo-Russian agreement creating the Wakhan Corridor. [biger-1995 §afghanistan](../sources/biger-1995.md#afghanistan)

### oq-durand-line-dispute

The Durand Line remains disputed by Afghanistan, which has questioned its legitimacy since the transfer of sovereignty from Britain to Pakistan in 1947.
