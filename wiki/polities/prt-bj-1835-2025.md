# Beja (district of Portugal)

## Summary

Beja is a NUTS III statistical subregion of Portugal, corresponding closely to the historic Beja district in the country's southern Alentejo region, bordering Spain to the east and the Atlantic to the southwest. This entry is `type: subnational` because Beja was never a sovereign or quasi-sovereign polity — it is a sub-national administrative and statistical reporting unit that exists within, and is fully contained by, the Portuguese state (`PRT-1800-2025`) throughout its span. The entry starts in 1986, the year Portugal formally adopted the EU's NUTS (Nomenclature of Territorial Units for Statistics) classification following its 1986 EEC accession, rather than at an earlier, unconfirmed district-founding date, because no source consulted in this session verifies when the Beja district itself was created. It exists as a distinct row, following the country convention set for Portugal's NUTS III units and the precedent of sibling entries (Aveiro, Evora, Guarda, Leiria, and others created under the same routing pass), because Beja is tabulated separately from the Portuguese national total in regional statistics and no existing polity code covers this specific sub-national unit.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures statistical/administrative reporting rows for "PT_BJ" (Beja), the Portuguese NUTS III sub-region, matched from routing decision unit_id PRT-PTBJ under country PRT. Prior to this decision such rows would have been matched only to the national total PRT-1800-2025 (as PRT-PTFA was, for its own 1880-1988 span), which is wrong because Beja-level figures describe roughly one twentieth of national territory and population, not the whole country — collapsing them into the national total would misattribute regional statistics to Portugal as a whole and lose the sub-national resolution the source data actually carries. What confirms Beja is a distinct, separately-tabulated entity rather than an artefact of the national total is that it is one of Portugal's standard Eurostat NUTS III statistical regions, structurally identical to sibling units PTAV (Aveiro), PTEV (Evora), PTGU (Guarda), PTLE (Leiria), and PTPG already created as separate polities under this same country convention — a pattern the routing decision treats as established precedent, not a one-off judgment call for Beja alone.

**Territory description:** Beja NUTS III region occupies the eastern and southern portion of Portugal's Alentejo, roughly coextensive with the historic Beja district: a sparsely populated, arid interior region of rolling plains (the Baixo Alentejo) bordered by Spain's Extremadura and Andalusia to the east, the Algarve to the south, Evora district to the north, and a short Atlantic coastline near Vila Nova de Milfontes to the southwest. Its administrative seat is the city of Beja. The district/NUTS III unit's area is approximately 10,225 km2 (comparable to the modern Beja district figure commonly cited in Portuguese administrative statistics) — no figure here is measured from an attached polygon, since none is attached; this is a territory estimate to be verified once a boundary source is registered.

## Predecessors and successors

This entry has no predecessor: Beja did not exist as a distinct statistical reporting unit before Portugal adopted the NUTS regional framework in 1986 following EEC accession, and prior to that any Beja-specific administrative data would have been filed under the national PRT total or, per the routing decision, under PRT-PTFA's national-total match for the 1880-1988 span. It has no successor: the NUTS III region remains current (end_year 2025, open-ended per country convention for still-active statistical units), and Beja has not been merged, split, or renamed since 1986. Should Portugal's NUTS III boundaries be redrawn in a future Eurostat revision, that would require a new polity row with this one as predecessor.

## Decisions

### d-span-1986-not-1835

**Span starts in 1986 (NUTS creation), not 1835 (district founding)**

The routing decision proposed treating pre-1986 years as back_cast onto this polity, reasoning by analogy to Beja's presumed 19th-century district origin (paralleling Aveiro, founded 1835). I did not extend the span backward. The decision's own routing_concerns flags that Beja's founding date was assumed, not confirmed, and Beja does not appear as a Federico-Tena or Mitchell trading/administrative unit in the way districts like Aveiro do. Rather than encode an unverified date into start_year -- which the containment gate would then require tiling with container edges -- I set start_year to 1986, the year Portugal's NUTS III statistical regions were formally adopted following EEC accession (1986), which is independently verifiable and is the country convention default cited in the routing decision. If Beja district's pre-1986 administrative history is later confirmed and needed for back-casting, that is a separate back_cast operation on the data side (per this project's constant-territory methodology), not a reason to move this polity's own start_year.

### d-prt-two-level-hierarchy

**Portugal is modelled as two levels — 18 districts, and from 1986 the five NUTS II regions that contain them**

The panel reports Portugal at two scales at once: five NUTS II regions and eighteen districts. Both sets tile mainland Portugal independently — measured on the committed geometry the regions sum to 88,798 km² and the districts to 88,801 km², a 0.003% difference — so treating them as one flat list of members would double-count every square kilometre. They are not a clean hierarchy either: the districts date from 1835 and NUTS II was delimited later across them, so five districts straddle a regional boundary (Lisboa is only 47% inside Área Metropolitana de Lisboa, Santarém 60%, Aveiro 63%, Viseu 65%, Setúbal 69%).

The resolution is a hierarchy **split in time**, which works because the regions postdate the districts. Until 1986 the districts are Portugal's direct members (17 of them, 18 once Setúbal exists in 1926) and no region competes with them. From 1986 the five regions become Portugal's only direct members and every district sits inside the region holding the largest share of its area, with that share recorded in the edge's own `basis`. A level-1 read is therefore exact in both periods; a level-2 read of a straddling district is approximate, and says so where it is.

For this row that means: inside Portugal (PRT-1800-2025) 1835-1986; inside Alentejo (PRT-ALE-1986-2025) 1986-2025. Its area splits Alentejo 100%.

**RESOLVED 2026-09-11.** Portugal's 1835 administrative reform created 17 mainland districts (distritos); Setúbal, the 18th, was carved out of Lisboa district in 1926. Ten of this table's Portuguese district rows already started in 1835, and the arithmetic closes exactly at 17 + 1 once the seven late-starting rows are corrected, which is the check that settled it.
