# Granada (province of Spain)

## Summary

Granada is one of Spain's 50 provinces, created in the single administrative stroke of the Javier de Burgos territorial reform of 30 November 1833, and since then has occupied the same inland-and-coastal swath of eastern Andalusia stretching from the Sierra Nevada to a short Mediterranean coastline (the Costa Tropical). This entry is type: subnational, not national, because sovereignty over the territory belongs to Spain throughout 1833-2025; the row exists only to carry a statistical reporting geography below the national aggregate ESP-1800-2025, on the same basis already established for the six other Spanish NUTS3 rows in this database (ESP-AS-1833-2025 Asturias, ESP-CA-1833-2025 Cantabria, ESP-CO-1833-2025 A Coruña, ESP-LU-1833-2025 Lugo, ESP-MD-1833-2025 Madrid, ESP-OR-1833-2025 Ourense, ESP-PO-1833-2025 Pontevedra) -- INE and Eurostat/GISCO have tabulated Granada separately from the national total, as NUTS3 code ES614, for as long as such series have existed. Since 1978 the province has also been a constituent unit of the Andalusia autonomous community together with seven other Andalusian provinces, but that is a later administrative layer above the 1833 province, not a redefinition of it: the boundary set in 1833 is the one this entry carries end to end.

Territory description: Granada province sits in eastern Andalusia, bordered by Jaén and Albacete to the north, Almería to the east, the Mediterranean (a roughly 80 km stretch of the Costa Tropical) to the south, and Málaga and Córdoba to the west. It contains the Sierra Nevada, mainland Spain's highest range (Mulhacén, 3,479 m), rising directly behind the city of Granada in the Vega depression. Its capital and largest city is Granada, with Motril (on the coast) and Almuñécar as the next largest municipalities. On a modern map its outline is the same as the province used both for the 1833 administrative division and, since 1978, as one of the eight constituent provinces of the Andalusia autonomous community. Its area, per INE, is approximately 12,647 km2 -- this is the province's own official area figure, not a measurement of any attached geometry; if a km2 figure is later derived from the mapspain-ign polygon itself, it should be labelled explicitly as a measurement of that geometry rather than presented as independent territorial evidence.

## Predecessors and successors

Granada has no predecessor polity in this database: before 1833 the same broad territory was governed under the historic Kingdom of Granada, an Antiguo Régimen jurisdiction whose boundary does not correspond to the 1833 province (it was both differently shaped and, in parts, larger, having later shed territory to the newly created Málaga and Almería provinces), so predecessor is left empty rather than pointing at a non-existent row -- the treatment already given to the sibling ESP rows. It has no successor either: Granada remains a current, active INE province and Eurostat NUTS3 unit (ES614) as of 2025, hence the open end_year of 2025 (exclusive), capped at the containing national row ESP-1800-2025's own end_year.

## Decisions

### d-code-gr-natural-form

**Minted ESP-GR-1833-2025 using the natural two-letter form, since GR is unclaimed**

The dominant ESP subnational pattern abbreviates the province name to two letters (ESP-AS for Asturias, ESP-MD for Madrid, ESP-OR for Ourense). Granada's natural two-letter abbreviation, GR, is also its historic DGT vehicle-registration province code, and unlike Córdoba (whose natural CO collided with A Coruña's prior claim on that code, forcing ESP-COR) or Cádiz (whose natural CA collided with Cantabria's prior claim, forcing ESP-CDZ), no existing ESP row currently holds GR. Of the 7 existing ESP subnational codes (AS, CA, CO, LU, MD, OR, PO), none is GR, so this entry can use the natural short form without the lengthening workaround those two siblings needed. This keeps the code set's general rule -- two-letter form unless already taken -- intact rather than defaulting to a longer bespoke code where it isn't required.

### oq-pre-1833-kingdom-of-granada-unmodeled

**The pre-1833 Kingdom of Granada and its Antiguo Régimen boundary are not modeled as a predecessor**

Before the 1833 reform, the broader area was organized under the historic Kingdom of Granada, the last Nasrid territory absorbed into Castile in 1492 and later one of the Reinos of Antiguo Régimen Andalusia, with a jurisdictional footprint substantially larger than the modern province (it historically included parts of what are now Málaga and Almería provinces, both created as separate units in 1833). This database leaves predecessor empty for this entry rather than pointing at a non-existent row, mirroring the treatment given to ESP-COR-1833-2025 and other Andalusian siblings. Whether a pre-1833 Kingdom of Granada should eventually be modeled as its own bespoke-coded polity (in the manner of ALK/HYD/RYU), given how much larger and how differently bounded that historic kingdom was compared to the 1833 province, is a live question for the ESP country convention generally and is not resolved by this entry.
