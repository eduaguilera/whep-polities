# Bizkaia (province of Spain)

## Summary

Bizkaia is one of Spain's 50 provinces and one of the three historical territories of the Basque Country, located on the northern (Cantabrian) coast around the city of Bilbao. This entry captures it as a `subnational` unit -- a reporting/administrative division inside the sovereign Spanish state, never itself sovereign -- covering the province from its creation in the 1833 Javier de Burgos territorial reform through to the present day (open end_year 2025, as used elsewhere in this database for still-current polities). It follows the same NUTS3-to-province routing pattern as the seven other ESP provincial rows already in this batch (Asturias, Cantabria, A Coruna, Lugo, Madrid, Ourense, Pontevedra): the routing decision found no existing ESP polity -- national Spain, A Coruna, or Madrid -- that represents this specific territory, and the sibling provinces in the same NUTS batch have consistently resolved to create_new rather than a match, confirming that Bizkaia (NUTS unit ES213) needs its own row rather than being folded into a broader Spain-wide series.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Eurostat NUTS3 unit ES213 (Bizkaia, admin_name 'ES213', country Spain) from a batch of Spain NUTS-level routing decisions being processed by the agent-harness pipeline; the harness batch has already produced 7 sibling ESP province rows (ESP-AS, ESP-CA, ESP-CO, ESP-LU, ESP-MD, ESP-OR, ESP-PO) from the same NUTS reform pattern, and this is the next one in that sequence. No row count or year range for underlying source data rows is given in the routing decision itself -- the decision is a NUTS-unit routing/creation call, not a data-ingest match, so unlike the MAN-1950-1955 exemplar (which cites specific mitchell/fao1952 row counts) this page cannot cite a row count without fabricating one; this gap is itself worth flagging rather than papering over. Previously, per the routing_reasoning, no polity in the database represented Bizkaia specifically: the only ESP candidates considered were A Coruna, Madrid, and the national Spain polity, none of which is this territory, and matching Bizkaia data to any of those would have been wrong by conflating a single province with either an unrelated province or the whole country. What confirms this entity is a distinct, separately-reported unit is administrative history itself: Bizkaia has been one of Spain's formally constituted provinces since the 1833 Javier de Burgos reform, the identical constitutional event that separately created the seven sibling ESP provincial rows already in this database, each of which is treated by this harness as a distinct reporting/administrative unit warranting its own row.

## Territorial extent

No polygon feature has been resolved for this period, because the routing decision that produced this page names `mapspain-ign` as the intended source by analogy with the seven sibling ESP provinces already sourced from it, but supplies no feature id and flags that its own read of the sources table shows `mapspain-ign` as `present_locally=False` -- a state it itself calls likely stale or inconsistent with those 7 already-produced rows. **Territory description:** Bizkaia (Biscay in English, Vizcaya in Castilian) is the westernmost and most populous of the three historical territories of the Basque Autonomous Community, on Spain's northern Cantabrian coast, bordered by Cantabria to the west, Burgos and Araba/Alava to the south, Gipuzkoa to the east, and the Bay of Biscay to the north. Its provincial capital and largest city is Bilbao. On a modern map it corresponds to Eurostat NUTS3 unit ES213 and to Spain's INE province code 48. Its land area is approximately 2,217 km2 -- among the smallest Spanish provinces by area, though one of the most densely populated owing to Bilbao's industrial and port economy. Provincial boundaries in Spain have been essentially geodetically stable since the 1833 reform, so a present-day province boundary is expected to serve as an adequate proxy for the full historical span once the mapspain-ign feature is actually resolved -- the same low-vintage-risk reasoning already applied to the 7 sibling provinces.

Note: Bizkaia additionally retained a distinct pre-1876 foral (charter) regime -- the fueros -- which set its fiscal and administrative relationship with the central Spanish state apart from ordinary provinces until the Fueros Abolition Law of 1876, and a modified fiscal arrangement (concierto economico) continues today. This affects the polity's administrative history but not, as far as is established here, its territorial boundary, so it does not change the polygon proxy reasoning above.

### oq-1833-start-vs-fueros-transition

**Does 1833 actually mark administrative continuity for Bizkaia, given the foral regime?**

The 1833 Javier de Burgos reform nominally created Bizkaia as a province alongside the rest of Spain, but Bizkaia (like the other Basque territories) retained its historical fueros (a distinct fiscal and administrative charter) until 1876, and a further concierto economico arrangement persists in modified form today. Unlike provinces created from scratch by the 1833 reform, Bizkaia had a pre-existing distinct territorial identity as the Senorio de Vizcaya. This page treats 1833 as the start year per the country convention's default rule, matching the other ESP provinces, but does not investigate whether the pre-1833 Senorio de Vizcaya should instead be a predecessor entry with its own span, which would change predecessor from [] to a bespoke pre-1833 code.
