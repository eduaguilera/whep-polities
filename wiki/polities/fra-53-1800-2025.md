# Mayenne

## Summary

Mayenne is a French département in the Pays de la Loire region of north-western France, with its préfecture at Laval. It was created in the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform that replaced the ancien régime provinces with a nationwide grid of roughly 83 départements, carved primarily from territory formerly belonging to the provinces of Maine and Anjou. It has kept its present form since, making it -- per the country convention recorded for FRA in pipelines/agent-harness/state/country_conventions.json -- one of the "default" départements whose span is governed by the system-wide 1790 start and open 2025 end, rather than by one of the four documented departure events (Belfort's 1871/1922 creation, Alsace-Lorraine's 1871-1919 absence, the 1975 Corse split, or the 1946 overseas incorporations). This page is `type: subnational` because Mayenne is an administrative division of France, not a sovereign state: it sits inside the FRA national row chain (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) for its entire span, and the type reflects that a département-level statistical series needs its own row without competing with the national FRA rows for sovereignty-ranked matching.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures a département-level historical statistical panel for Mayenne: the routing decision (unit_id FRA-FRG03, admin_name FRG03) describes a France-country data source with a per-unit series spanning data years 1862-2023 for a unit the routing reasoning identifies as the département of Mayenne. No row count is given in the decision payload itself, but the routing_reasoning explicitly states no existing FRA polity matched this unit: the three existing FRA-* rows (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025) are national totals for all of France, not this single département, so match_existing was correctly ruled inapplicable and this data would otherwise have had no home short of a wrong match to a national row (diluting a department-level series into a 550,000 km2+ national aggregate). What confirms Mayenne is a distinct, statistically-tracked unit rather than an arbitrary carve-out: it is one of France's officially constituted départements under the Revolutionary 1790 reform, a first-order administrative division that has been the standard unit of French statistical reporting (agricultural censuses, INSEE data) for over two centuries -- the same administrative-division logic that justifies every other <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> row in this table (e.g. ESP-AS, DZA-CVD).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision's polygon_reasoning notes GADM 4.1 adm1 would normally carry French département boundaries, but the gadm-4.1-adm1 source file present in this repository is a subset covering only 81 countries and excludes France entirely (0 rows for FRA). No other registered polygon source (cshapes, cliopatria, paine, histogis-habsburg, reporting-areas, constructed) digitises French sub-national units, so no executable polygon route exists today; see open question oq-no-polygon-source.

**Territory description, for a reader locating this on a modern map:** Mayenne (INSEE department code 53) lies in north-western France, part of the Pays de la Loire administrative region, bordering Normandy to the north. Its préfecture is the city of Laval; other principal towns include Mayenne and Château-Gontier. The modern département covers approximately 5,175 km2 -- this figure is the department's own published administrative area (INSEE), not a measurement of any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached. Its boundaries have been stable since the 1790 creation of the département system, subject to the caveat in oq-territorial-reorg-since-1800 that no canton-level source was consulted to confirm this specifically for Mayenne.

## Predecessors and successors

Mayenne has no predecessor or successor polity row: it was created directly in the 1790 Revolutionary reform (predating this page's 1800 start, see open question oq-1790-1800-gap) from parts of the ancien régime provinces of Maine and Anjou, and it has not been merged, split, or abolished since -- unlike Territoire de Belfort (carved out in 1871) or Corse (split in 1975), Mayenne is not named among the country convention's unit_start_exceptions. It persists to the present day as an active French département, so the page's end_year (2025) is the container's own open end rather than a terminal event.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the country convention's 1790 system floor**

pipelines/agent-harness/state/country_conventions.json records system_start_year 1790 for the French département system (the 1790 Revolutionary reform), and Mayenne is an original département with no listed unit_start_exception, so the convention's default would put start_year at 1790. But the polity table has no France row earlier than FRA-1800-1871 (data/final/polities_database.csv), and the container gate rejects an edge whose interval falls outside the container party's own span. A 1790-2025 span containered starting at FRA-1800-1871 would leave 1790-1800 uncontained and fail that gate. Rather than inventing an uncontained decade or a phantom pre-1800 France row, start_year is pinned to 1800, matching the earliest container actually present in the table, and the three container edges above tile 1800-2025 with no gap. The 1790-1800 gap is carried forward as an open question rather than silently absorbed.

### d-subunit-code-53

**Subunit code chosen as the INSEE département number, not a postal abbreviation**

The dominant subnational pattern is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, and the routing decision's admin_name field carries 'FRG03', which is neither a département code nor a name a reader would recognise (it appears to be a data-source internal region/unit identifier). French départements have a stable, universally used two-digit INSEE code, and Mayenne's is 53. FRA-53-1800-2025 was chosen over FRA-MAY-1800-2025 or similar because the INSEE numeric code is the canonical French administrative identifier for this unit (used on vehicle plates, postal codes, and all French statistical publishing), unlike a bespoke three-letter abbreviation that has no external convention to anchor it.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap

**The 1790-1800 decade of Mayenne's existence is not containered by any polity row**

The country convention's own system_start_basis dates the département system to the 4 March 1790 Revolutionary reform, and Mayenne was among the original ~83 départements created then. This page starts at 1800 only because the earliest France national row in the polity table, FRA-1800-1871, does not reach back further. If a pre-1800 France container row is ever added (e.g. FRA-1790-1800 or an extension of FRA-1800-1871's start_year to 1790), this page's start_year and code should be revised to 1790 to match. Until then, 1790-1800 is a real gap in coverage, not a deliberate exclusion.

### oq-no-polygon-source

**No GADM or other registered source carries French département boundaries locally**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning states that GADM 4.1 adm1 would normally carry this feature, but the locally present gadm-4.1-adm1 file excludes FRA entirely (a documented subset of 81 countries), and no other registered source (cshapes, cliopatria, paine, histogis-habsburg, reporting-areas, constructed) digitises French sub-national units. polygon_status is therefore 'unassigned' with no polygon_source, not a proxy or estimate. Obtaining the full/global GADM 4.1 adm1 layer (or an FRA-specific adm1 extract) would resolve this for Mayenne and, presumably, for the rest of the FRA département rows this harness will go on to create -- worth doing once rather than per-département.

### oq-territorial-reorg-since-1800

**Whether Mayenne's boundary has been perfectly stable since 1800 is asserted, not verified against a canton-level source**

The country convention treats Mayenne as unaffected by any of the four listed departure events (Belfort, Alsace-Lorraine, Corse split, overseas départements creation), which is why this page defaults to the system's stable-boundary assumption. But French département boundaries have seen minor canton and commune-level adjustments over two centuries that a country-level convention would not surface. No canton-level historical boundary source was consulted to confirm Mayenne specifically saw no such adjustment between 1800 and 2025; this is inherited confidence from the convention, not a Mayenne-specific check.
