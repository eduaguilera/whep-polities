# California (US state)

## Summary

California as a constituent state of the United States, from its admission to the Union on September 9, 1850 (the 31st state, under the Compromise of 1850) through the open end of this database's series in 2025. type is subnational because California never held sovereignty in its own right during this span, it is a first-order administrative division of the United States throughout, and the entry exists purely to carry a reporting territory distinct from the national USA rows, mirroring the existing USA-subnational precedent set by Alaska (ALK-1867-1959). The 175-year span is a single continuous statehood with no administrative reorganization of the state itself; what changes across it is only which national polity contains it, since the USA rows in this table are periodized by other national-level milestones (1848, 1867, 1959) that do not correspond to any change in California's own boundary or status.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision, the unit is USA-CALIFORNIA ('California', country United States of America); the decision itself does not state a row count or source name for the data series that triggered this polity's creation, only that no existing polity represents California specifically. What confirms distinctness: no existing table row is California, the nearest candidates share only the iso3 USA and are either national-level United States rows (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) or the unrelated Alaska entry (ALK-1867-1959); none narrows to California's own territory. Previously, any California-labeled data would have had no dedicated home and would fall through to whichever national USA row spans its year, conflating a single state's figures with all-U.S. totals, structurally the same problem that justified the Alaska entry (ALK-1867-1959) as a distinct subnational row rather than folding Alaska into the national chain. Historically, California's distinctness as an administrative unit is not in question: it has been a constituent state of the Union, with its own state government, since the 1850 Compromise of 1850 admission, a formal dated event with no ambiguity.

## Territorial extent

Polygon: gadm-4.1-adm1 source, feature USA.5_1, California's present-day state boundary reused across the entire 1850-2025 span. Proxy, not assigned (see decision d-polygon-proxy-not-assigned): GADM 4.1 reflects a present-day survey backdated up to 176 years, and while California's external boundary is understood to have been essentially stable since admission, no period-dated historical polygon was available or consulted to confirm this year-by-year (see open question oq-gadm-boundary-history). No copy-from-another-period-of-this-polity note applies since this is the polity's only polygon reference.

Territory: the U.S. state of California, occupying the Pacific coast of the western United States between Oregon (north), Nevada and Arizona (east), and the Mexican state of Baja California (south), bounded by the Pacific Ocean on the west. On a modern map this is the area from roughly 32.5N to 42N latitude and 114W to 124.5W longitude, encompassing the Central Valley, the Sierra Nevada, the Mojave and Colorado deserts, and the coastal ranges from the Oregon border to the San Diego area. The state's official land-plus-water area is approximately 423,970 km2 (about 163,700 sq mi), the third-largest U.S. state by area. The figure attached to this entry as polygon_area_km2 is measured directly from the GADM 4.1 geometry described above and is a measurement of that polygon, not independent evidence about the state's actual historical extent.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor row is set. California had no prior statehood-era polity entry in this table (it enters directly as an admitted state, not via a preceding territorial-government row split out separately), and it remains a current U.S. state at the open end of the series (2025 exclusive, per the open-end convention), so no successor exists either. See open question oq-preadmission-territory-data for whether a pre-1850 predecessor should eventually be added.

## Sourced claims

- California was admitted to the Union as the 31st state on September 9, 1850, as part of the Compromise of 1850, following its cession by Mexico under the 1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo.
- GADM 4.1's admin-1 feature USA.5_1 gives California's present-day state boundary, which this page reuses as a proxy for the full 1850-2025 span; the same source is already used in this table for Alaska (ALK-1867-1959), establishing the precedent of routing U.S. states through gadm-4.1-adm1 rather than cshapes-2.0 or cliopatria, which carry only whole-country USA boundaries.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-no-subunit-abbrev

**Code uses bespoke short mnemonic, not the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern**

57 of 66 subnational rows follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. ESP-CA-1833-2025), and the routing decision that created this page proposed exactly that shape with a two-letter subunit code. But the one existing USA subnational precedent, ALK-1867-1959 (Alaska), is bespoke: <BESPOKE>-<start>-<end> with no ISO3 prefix at all, using the territory's own short name (ALK). Rather than mixing conventions within the same country (ALK bespoke, California ISO3-prefixed), this page follows the closer within-country precedent and uses a short bespoke code, CAL-1850-2025, matching the ALK-1867-1959 shape. This keeps USA subnational codes internally consistent even though it departs from the cross-country majority pattern.

### d-polygon-proxy-not-assigned

**polygon_status is proxy, not assigned, despite low vintage risk**

The routing decision explicitly flags GADM 4.1's admin-1 California boundary as a modern survey being backdated 176 years to 1850, even though the external state line has been stable since statehood. Per the polygon proxy rules, reusing a present-day boundary across a period where no historical vintage was consulted to confirm zero change year-by-year is the case for polygon_status: proxy rather than assigned. assigned is reserved for a source polygon actually surveyed/dated to the polity's own period; this one is dated to the present and only assumed stable.

## Open questions

### oq-preadmission-territory-data

**Does any data series reference California territory before 1850 statehood?**

start_year is pinned to the 1850-09-09 admission date per the country convention (a state's start_year is its Union admission year, not the data's extent). But California was an organized U.S. territory (ceded by Mexico in 1848 under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo) and briefly the unrecognized California Republic in 1846, both years inside the container USA-1848-1867's own span. If any production/trade source reports 'California' data for 1848-1850, it currently has no explicit home: it would fall to the national USA-1848-1867 row by default rather than this entry. Nobody has checked whether such rows exist; if they do, this page's start_year may need to move earlier, or the pre-1850 span needs its own predecessor entry.

### oq-gadm-boundary-history

**Has California's external boundary actually been constant since 1850, or only approximately?**

The routing decision asserts the boundary is stable 'since statehood' but this is asserted, not sourced against a historical atlas. California's current shape reflects at least one 1863-1864 boundary-survey correction along the eastern Sierra line with Nevada, and the Colorado River channel against Arizona has shifted over the 20th century. These are almost certainly too small to matter at the km2 scale used below, but no historical boundary source has actually been checked to confirm 'essentially unchanged' rather than merely 'unchanged at map scale'.
