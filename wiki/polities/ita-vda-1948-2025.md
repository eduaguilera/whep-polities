# Valle d'Aosta (region of Italy)

## Summary

Valle d'Aosta (Vallée d'Aoste) is Italy's smallest region, a bilingual (Italian/French) Alpine territory in the country's extreme northwest, bordering France and Switzerland, with Aosta as its capital. It is one of Italy's five special-statute regions (regioni a statuto speciale), granted autonomy by constitutional law in 1948 in recognition of its French-speaking minority. This entry is `type: subnational`, matching the convention used for Italy's ordinary regions (e.g. ita-pie-1970-2025.md): Italy held sovereignty over this territory throughout the period, and the row exists to carry a reporting/administrative territory beneath the national ITA polity, not a claim of statehood. Unlike the 15 ordinary-statute regions, whose regional councils and administrative apparatus only became operative in 1970, Valle d'Aosta's special statute (Legge costituzionale 26 febbraio 1948, n. 4) took effect immediately in 1948, giving it its own regional government, budget and legislative competences decades before the ordinary regions existed as functioning tiers. This entry's start_year therefore departs from the 1970 convention used for Piemonte and the other ordinary regions, and follows the special-statute timeline instead.

**Why this entry exists.** This row was NOT created to receive a specific data series from the panel; the routing decision states explicitly that no reporting unit routes to it ("unit_id: none — no reporting unit asks for this row"). It exists so that the ITA-1919-2025 container's set of member regions tiles the whole of Italy without gaps: Valle d'Aosta is one of Italy's 20 constitutionally defined regions, and if its row is missing while its 19 siblings (Piemonte, Lombardia, etc.) exist, any exhaustive accounting of Italian territory by region would silently omit it. Previously, any Valle d'Aosta-labelled statistics (were there any in this panel) would have had nowhere distinct to land except the national ITA row, folding a small, historically and linguistically distinct alpine territory into the national total. What confirms this is a distinct administrative entity, independent of any data source: Valle d'Aosta's own special-statute constitutional law (1948), its unique bilingual status among Italian regions, and its continuous existence as a first-order NUTS2/administrative unit (ITC2) reported by Istat and Eurostat separately from other regions, exactly as Piemonte and the other regional rows in this table are.

**Territory description:** Valle d'Aosta occupies the extreme northwestern corner of Italy, in the Alps, surrounded by France (Haute-Savoie) to the west/south, Switzerland (Valais) to the north, and the Piemonte region to the south/east. It contains Mont Blanc's Italian slopes and the upper Dora Baltea valley, with Aosta as its capital and largest town. It is Italy's smallest and least populous region, with a present-day administrative area of approximately **3,263 km²**. The 3,263 km2 measured from the attached GADM geometry matches this widely cited present-day administrative figure, a reasonable cross-check given the region's boundaries have been stable since the 1948 statute; this figure is a measurement of the attached polygon, cross-checked against (not derived independently of) common present-day area statistics.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1948-not-1970

**Start year 1948 (special-statute grant), not 1970 (ordinary-region convention)**

ita-pie-1970-2025.md and the other ordinary-statute regions use 1970 as their start_year because that is when Italy's 15 ordinary regions held their first regional council elections and became operative administrative tiers. Valle d'Aosta is explicitly excluded from that convention: it is one of the five special-statute regions, which received constitutional autonomy and functioning regional government in 1948 (Legge costituzionale n. 4/1948), 22 years before the ordinary regions. Using 1970 here would misrepresent this region's actual administrative history and contradict the routing decision's own era_of_this_page value of 1948-2025. The container edge is dated 1948-2025 to match, tiling the ITA-1919-2025 container without a gap for this member from its actual point of administrative distinctness.

### d-code-pattern-subunit

**Use ITA-VDA-1948-2025, following the ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern set by Piemonte**

Consistent with 428 of 439 subnational rows and with the ITA-PIE precedent set in ita-pie-1970-2025.md, this entry uses <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. VDA is the natural three-letter abbreviation for Valle d'Aosta, already the common English/Italian shorthand used for the region (e.g. in NUTS ITC2 documentation). No bespoke-code case applies: Valle d'Aosta has no name-of-its-own historical identity independent of being an Italian region (unlike Manchuria or Hyderabad); it is an ordinary administrative subdivision, just one with an earlier and special constitutional status.
