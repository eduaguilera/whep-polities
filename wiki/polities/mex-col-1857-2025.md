# Colima

## Summary

This entry covers the Mexican state of Colima, one of the 32 first-order federal entities of Mexico, located on the Pacific coast in west-central Mexico between Jalisco and Michoacán. Colima began the 19th century as a minor administrative unit and was organized as a federal territory before being admitted as a full state of the Mexican federation in 1857 under the Constitution of that year, following the country's default state-admission convention (which does not list Colima among the three exceptions — Baja California, Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo — that were admitted much later as former federal territories). Its type is `subnational` because it is a constituent unit of the sovereign state of Mexico (MEX), never itself sovereign; the national polity chain MEX-1800-1848 → MEX-1848-2025 already carries Mexico's sovereignty and international status, and this entry exists purely to let production/trade or demographic data reported at the state level route to the correct sub-national territory rather than being force-fit to the national total. Colima remains a current Mexican state today, so its end_year is left open at 2025 per the table's convention for still-existing subnational units. No production, trade, or other dataset row is cited as the direct trigger for this entry in the routing decision provided; the entry was created as part of a systematic pass adding Mexican states as subnational candidates so that any state-level Mexican data encountered by future ingests has a home distinct from the national MEX row.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Colima as a distinct Mexican federal state (iso3 MEX), created not in response to a specific dataset row cited in the routing decision but as part of a systematic effort to give each of Mexico's 32 states its own subnational polity row, since none previously existed (0 existing MEX subnational rows in the table before this one). Previously, any Mexico-labelled data at state granularity — whether historical production statistics, trade records, or demographic series that name "Colima" specifically — would have had nowhere to route except the national MEX-1848-2025 row, which aggregates the entire country (roughly 1.96 million km² today) and would silently misattribute state-level figures to the whole nation, inflating apparent per-area or per-capita magnitudes by roughly two orders of magnitude relative to Colima's true ~5,627 km². What confirms Colima is a genuine, distinct administrative entity — not a duplicate of an existing candidate — is that no MEX candidate in the table names Colima or any other state (the two existing MEX rows are purely national totals for 1800-1848 and 1848-2025), and Colima's separate statehood is a matter of settled Mexican constitutional history: it is one of the federation's enumerated states under Article 43 of the Mexican Constitution, with its own governor, legislature, and INEGI state code (06), administratively and statistically distinct from Jalisco (from which its territory was originally carved) and from the national government.

**Territory description:** Colima state occupies Mexico's Pacific coast in the west-central part of the country, bordered by Jalisco to the north, east, and west, and Michoacán to the southeast, with a short Pacific coastline including the port city of Manzanillo. Its capital is Colima city. It is one of the smallest Mexican states by area, at approximately 5,627 km² (INEGI's published figure for the modern state), and includes the Colima Volcano (Volcán de Fuego) on its border with Jalisco. This is a citable published figure from Mexican national statistics, not a measurement from any geometry attached to this entry, since no geometry is attached yet.

The state's administrative boundary has been broadly stable since the 19th century admission as a federal entity, though this continuity has not been independently checked against a historical atlas — see the open question on modern-vs-historical boundary vintage.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the 57/66 <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern**

Colima is an ordinary Mexican federal state with no bespoke historical identity distinct from its administrative status (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad, which had names and institutions independent of any modern country subdivision code). It is the first MEX subnational entry, so there is no existing MEX precedent to follow or depart from. The dominant pattern in the table, <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>, applies directly: MEX-COL-1857-2025, using COL as a two/three-letter subunit abbreviation distinguishing it from Colombia's ISO3 (COL) — since this field is a subunit code within MEX, not a country code, there is no collision in the table's namespace. I did not use INEGI's numeric state code (06) or the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code (MX-COL) since neither appears elsewhere in the table's subunit style, which uses short mnemonic letters (e.g. ESP-CA, ESP-AS).

## Open questions

### oq-1857-admission-date-unverified

**1857 admission date for Colima as a full state is not independently sourced here**

The routing decision itself flags that the 1857 start_year came from the country convention's default admission rule rather than a primary source consulted in this session. Colima was a federal territory before statehood; various secondary sources place its promotion to full state status at 1857 under the Constitution of 1857, but this has not been cross-checked against INEGI, the Mexican federal decree record, or a historical atlas. If the underlying country-convention research used a different date (some accounts cite an 1856-1857 transition or a slightly earlier legislative act), start_year and the container edge's start_year should both be corrected together, since the gate requires them to match.

### oq-gadm-mexico-not-fetched

**GADM 4.1 Mexico features are not present in the local geodata subset**

data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg is a curated 81-country subset and Mexico is absent from it entirely (0 features), even though GADM 4.1 publishes Mexican state boundaries globally and MEX.6_1 should correspond to Colima under standard GADM numbering. Until the local file is refetched/extended to include Mexico, polygon_status stays unassigned and no area can be computed or verified for this entry. Whoever refetches should confirm MEX.6_1 indeed resolves to Colima and not a neighboring state, since GADM's GID_1 numbering can shift between vintages.

### oq-modern-boundary-vs-historical

**A modern GADM boundary is not verified against Colima's actual 19th-century territorial extent**

Colima state's borders have been described as broadly stable since the 19th century, but this has not been checked against a historical atlas or gazetteer for boundary changes (e.g., minor municipal boundary adjustments with Jalisco). Once gadm-4.1-adm1 is fetched, the vintage risk noted in the routing decision (modern ~2020s boundary applied back to 1857) should be revisited to see whether any documented adjustment is large enough to warrant a proxy caveat rather than a straight assignment.
