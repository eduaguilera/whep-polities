# Indonesia (to 1889)

## Summary

CSV row for Indonesia from **1800** to **1889** — a **90-year row**
covering the Dutch East Indies from the database floor
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW code 750
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

**CSV oddity: cow=750 is shared with India** (IND rows also use
cow=750). This is likely a CSV error — Indonesia should use cow=850.
See [oq-cow-750-shared](#oq-cow-750-shared).

## Territorial extent

This row's polygon is bound to CShapes feature id 850 at
**feature_year=1886**, area **1,877,243 km²** (independently
measured from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`, equal-area
ESRI:54034, as **1,876,795 km²** — the ~450 km² gap is rounding/
projection noise, not a discrepancy). 1886 is not a coincidence: it
is the earliest year CShapes covers at all — "1886 is a hard floor.
Anything before 1886 is out of scope of the paper entirely"
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders).
Concretely, that means **the entire 1800–1885 portion of this row's
span is not an observation** — it is a single 1886 polygon
retrojected across 86 years, per CShapes' own admission that many
units carry "a single polygon for the whole 1886–2019 window; for
these, the '1886 polygon' is the same geometry as the latest
observation" [cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders).
CShapes also flags pre-definition regions with a dummy "placeholder
polygon" variable for years when borders were not yet settled
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders);
whether that flag is set for feature 850 has not been checked in this
pass and remains an open question (see below).

The polygon corresponds, in real territorial terms, to the Dutch
East Indies as bounded by the **Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824**, which
divided the Malay archipelago into British and Dutch spheres: the
Netherlands ceded Malacca and withdrew objections to British
Singapore, in exchange for full British withdrawal from Sumatra —
after 1824, "the Dutch Indonesian regions" of the archipelago sat on
one side of a line from British Malaya on the other
[biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia). Biger's
Indonesia entry itself only describes the country in its post-1949
configuration and 1963/1975 expansions
[biger-1995 §indonesia](../sources/biger-1995.md#indonesia); it has no
entry describing the 1800–1889 colonial boundary directly, so the
1824 treaty framing above is corroborated only via the Malaysia
cross-reference, not a dedicated Indonesia passage.

The 1886 CShapes snapshot understates how unsettled Dutch control
still was even at the row's own 1889 end date: the **Aceh War**
(started 1873) was still ongoing in northern Sumatra, and full Dutch
sovereignty over the outer islands (Aceh, parts of Borneo, the
southern archipelago) was not consolidated until the early
20th-century "Pacification" campaigns — i.e. this row's successor,
IDN-1889-1945, itself binds its polygon to a **1900** CShapes feature
year, not 1889, per the CSV
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). This is uncited
general history (not found in an ingested source) and should be
treated as background, not a sourced claim.

**Predates the polygon:** the years 1811–1816 saw a British military
occupation and administration of Java (the "British interregnum,"
under Lieutenant-Governor Raffles) following the Napoleonic-era
capture of Java, with the colony restored to the Netherlands by 1816.
This is likewise uncited general history — no ingested source
documents it — but it means that for roughly five years inside this
row's span, the *de facto* administering power was Britain, not the
Netherlands, even though CShapes/COW (which code by recognized
sovereignty/state-system membership, not occupation) would not treat
this as a change of state
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)
("we also excluded wartime territorial changes, unless they were
made permanent in treaties signed after the war" — the interregnum
was reversed, not made permanent). See
[oq-1811-1816-interregnum](#oq-1811-1816-interregnum) below.

## Sourced claims

- The polygon bound to this row (CShapes feature id 850,
  feature_year 1886) reflects the earliest year CShapes covers at
  all — the dataset's documented hard floor —
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders).
- CShapes routinely backdates a single polygon across its whole
  1886–2019 coverage for units where no border change was coded, so
  a 1886-dated polygon standing in for 1800–1885 is expected
  behaviour of the source, not an error specific to this row —
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders).
- CShapes flags dependent territories only above a 250,000-population
  threshold and border changes only above a 100×100 km size
  threshold; any sub-threshold 19th-century adjustment to the Dutch
  East Indies' boundary would not be visible in CShapes at all —
  [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes).
- The Anglo-Dutch division of the Malay archipelago (Dutch East
  Indies vs. British Malaya/Singapore) is attested indirectly: Biger
  describes the later Indonesia–Malaysia border on Borneo as
  separating "the Dutch Indonesian regions of East and West
  Kalimantan from the Malaysian states of Sarawak and Sabah" —
  [biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia).
- Biger's only dedicated Indonesia narrative describes post-1949
  events (independence 1949, West New Guinea 1963, East Timor
  invasion — Biger misdates it "1955", almost certainly a typo for
  1975) and says nothing about the 1800–1889 window —
  [biger-1995 §indonesia](../sources/biger-1995.md#indonesia).
- **CSV oddity confirmed, not merely suspected:** the ingested COW
  state-system data file itself (`statelist2024.csv`, part of the
  cow-state-system-v2024 source's local data) records Indonesia
  under ccode **850** (row `INS,850,Indonesia,1949,12,27,...`) and
  India separately under ccode **750** (row
  `IND,750,India,1947,8,15,...`). This row's `cow=750` in
  `polities_database.csv` is therefore Indonesia's row wrongly
  carrying India's COW code, consistent with the open question
  already on this page —
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §schema](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#schema)
  documents the `ccode` column that was checked directly against the
  raw file to make this comparison.
- COW does not consider Indonesia a state-system member before its
  1949 recognition (`INS` entry begins 1949-12-27, per the same raw
  file) — reinforcing that COW membership dates are the wrong lens
  for a colonial-era row like this one, per WHEP's own
  territorial-economic polity definition —
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates).

## Open questions

### oq-cow-750-shared

**Confirmed, not just suspected**, against the raw COW state list:
COW code 750 is assigned to both the early Indonesia rows
(IDN-1800-1889, IDN-1889-1945) and all India rows, but
`statelist2024.csv` gives India ccode=750 and Indonesia ccode=850 —
see the sourced claim above citing
[cow-state-system-v2024 §schema](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#schema).
This is a CSV error in `polities_database.csv`; the fix (changing
`cow` to 850 for both IDN-1800-1889 and IDN-1889-1945) is a data-file
change outside the scope of this wiki-only pass and is left for the
next ingest that touches `polities_database.csv`.

### oq-1811-1816-interregnum

1811–1816 British military occupation/administration of Java
("British interregnum," Raffles as Lieutenant-Governor) sits inside
this row's span but is not documented in any ingested source — see
Territorial extent above. Open question: does WHEP's
territorial-economic polity definition warrant splitting this row at
1811 and 1816 to reflect a genuine (if temporary) change of
administering power, even though CShapes/COW would not code it as a
state change? Needs a source that actually covers Java 1811–1816
before this can be resolved either way.

### oq-1889-cut-basis

What actually motivates the 1889 cut between IDN-1800-1889 and
IDN-1889-1945? The page previously asserted an "1889 Anglo-Dutch
boundary treaty" but the only Anglo-Dutch Borneo boundary convention
this pass could find dates to 1891, and the successor row's own
CShapes polygon binds to feature_year 1900 — neither matches 1889.
No ingested source explains the 1889 date. Needs the original
rationale for the CSV's 1889 split point, or a correction of the
cut year itself.

citations at all** — its territorial content is a stub. The `reviewed` label is
supposed to mean a human checked the claims against sources, so carrying it here
was misleading: it invited both readers and the verification pipeline to treat

is what they actually are. Nothing about the polity's data or polygon changed;
only the honesty of the label.

claims** sections filled from real sources (Biger 1995, CShapes 2.0, the COW
state-system list) with inline citations, and the specific territorial changes
