# Deux-Sèvres (département)

## Summary

Deux-Sèvres is a French département (a NUTS3-equivalent sub-national administrative unit, hence type: subnational) in western France, with its prefecture at Niort, in the modern Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It was created in March 1790 during the Revolutionary reorganization of France into départements, assembled from parts of the former provinces of Poitou, Aunis and Saintonge, and its boundary has remained essentially stable since — it is not one of the departments affected by the major territorial disruptions tracked elsewhere in this table (the 1871-1919 loss and return of Alsace-Lorraine, the Corsican departmental split, the separation of Territoire de Belfort, or the overseas départements). This entry gives Deux-Sèvres its own polity row, distinct from the national FRA rows that contain but do not equal it, so that any data reported specifically at this department's granularity (INSEE statistics, historical agricultural censuses, etc.) has somewhere correct to be routed rather than defaulting to the whole of France. The span is set to 1800-2025 rather than the department's true 1790 origin because the earliest available FRA national container row begins in 1800 (see decisions and open questions on this gap).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: none specifically cited yet — this row was created structurally by the pipeline's decision for admin unit FRA-FRI33 (INSEE department 79, Deux-Sèvres), one of the set of French départements being given individual polity rows, rather than in response to a specific unmatched dataset (see open question oq-source-data-not-yet-specified). Previously, any data reported at Deux-Sèvres granularity would have had nowhere to go but the national FRA rows (FRA-1800-1871 / FRA-1871-1919 / FRA-1919-2025), which are roughly 32x the area (551,695 km2 metropolitan France vs. 5,999 km2 for this department) and would silently blend department-level statistics into national totals. That is wrong whenever a source specifically tabulates at department level, as INSEE historical series and some European agricultural censuses do. The entity's distinctness is confirmed by INSEE's own department-numbering system, under which Deux-Sèvres (code 79) has been a discrete statistical and administrative reporting unit continuously since the 1790 Revolutionary creation of the département system — this is the same kind of administrative-unit evidence that justifies the other 57 <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT> rows in the table, such as Spain's provinces.

## Decisions

### d-code-fra-79

**Code uses the INSEE department number, not a two/three-letter abbreviation**

The dominant subnational pattern in the table (57 of 66 rows) is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> using a short alphabetic subunit code, as with ESP-AS or ESP-CA. French départements, however, do not have a standard alphabetic abbreviation in general use — they are universally identified by their INSEE numeric code, which is printed on every French license plate, postal code prefix, and administrative document. Deux-Sèvres is INSEE department 79. Using FRA-79 rather than inventing a two-letter abbreviation (there is no established one, unlike Spain's provincial letter codes) keeps the code recognizable to anyone who knows French administrative geography, and there are zero existing FRA subnational rows to conflict with or set a rival precedent.

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**Start year moved from the proposed 1790 to 1800, to satisfy the containment-tiling gate**

The routing decision that generated this page proposed start_year=1790, reasoning that Deux-Sèvres is an original Revolutionary département created in 1790 and has been stable since. That is true as a matter of administrative history — the department's boundary has not materially changed since its 1790 creation. But the polity table's earliest FRA national container row is FRA-1800-1871; there is no FRA row of any kind covering 1790-1800 (checked: only FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1871-1919, FRA-1919-2025 exist). A containment edge cannot fall outside the container's own span, so a 1790-start department would leave 1790-1799 uncontainable and fail the tiling gate. This page therefore uses start_year=1800 to align with the earliest available FRA container, at the cost of not representing the full 1790-1800 decade. That gap is recorded as an open question below rather than silently absorbed.

## Open questions

### oq-1790-1800-gap

**The 1790-1800 decade of this département's existence has no polity row at all**

Deux-Sèvres was created in March 1790 as one of the original 83 Revolutionary départements (from part of the former province of Poitou), a full decade before the earliest FRA national container row (FRA-1800-1871) begins. This page starts at 1800 to satisfy the containment-tiling gate, which means the 1790-1800 window is currently represented by no polity row for this department at all — not this one, and not any national FRA row either, since none exists before 1800. If a pre-1800 FRA national container is ever added (covering the Revolutionary and Directory periods, 1790/1792-1800), this department's start_year should be moved back to 1790 to match, and the container list extended with the corresponding edge.

### oq-no-polygon-fetched

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 has not been fetched for France, so no polygon can be assigned yet**

The registered source for department-level boundaries, gadm-4.1-adm1 (id column GID_1), is the right granularity for this entry, but the local on-disk subset of that source covers only 81 countries and France is not among them (0 rows for FRA in the current extract). Until the FRA-inclusive GADM 4.1 adm1 file is fetched, polygon_feature_id stays null and polygon_status stays unassigned. Once fetched, the correct feature should be the GID_1 row for Deux-Sèvres (FRA department code 79), and boundary stability since 1790 (per the decision above) means a single present-day feature should serve as a reasonable proxy for the whole 1800-2025 span with low vintage risk — but that should be confirmed against a historical INSEE boundary source rather than assumed, since even administratively stable French departments have had minor commune-level boundary adjustments (e.g. canton reorganizations) that could shift the adm1 polygon slightly from its 1800 extent.
