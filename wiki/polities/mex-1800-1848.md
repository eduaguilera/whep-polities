# Mexico (to 1848)

## Summary

This row tracks Mexico from the **database floor (1800)** to
**1848** — a **49-year row**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). At the 1800
start, the territory was New Spain under Spanish colonial rule;
Mexican independence came on 27 September 1821. The row ends at
1848, corresponding to the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo
(2 February 1848), which ceded roughly 55% of Mexico's territory
to the United States.

Pre-Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo Mexico included Central America
until 1823 (~4.4M km2), then ~3.2M km2 after the secession of
the Central American provinces. Texas was lost de facto in 1836
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). The Treaty
of Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848-02-02) ceded California, Nevada, Utah,
Arizona, and New Mexico to the US — roughly 55% of Mexican
territory [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Predecessor: NA (database floor — at 1800, New Spain under Spanish
colonial rule). Successor:
[mex-1848-2025](mex-1848-2025.md).

## Sourced claims

- **1821-09-27: Mexican independence.** Mexico declared independence
  from Spain. The First Mexican Empire was established under
  Agustín de Iturbide.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1823: Central American secession.** The Central American
  provinces (Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica)
  separated from Mexico, forming the Federal Republic of Central
  America. Mexico reduced from ~4.4M km2 to ~3.2M km2.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1824: Federal Republic.** The 1824 Constitution established
  Mexico as a federal republic.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1836: Texas independence.** Texas declared independence from
  Mexico (de facto lost)
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).
- **1846-1848: Mexican-American War.** War between Mexico and the
  United States, ending with the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1848-02-02: Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo.** Mexico ceded
  California, Nevada, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico, and parts of
  Colorado, Wyoming, Kansas, and Oklahoma to the United States.
  ~55% territorial loss
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). This is
  the end-of-row event.

## Open questions

### oq-independence-1821

Mexican independence from Spain (1821) is a major event within
this row, not a CSV split. The 1800 start is the database floor,
not an independence date. The row therefore spans two very
different political entities (Spanish colony 1800-1821, independent
Mexico 1821-1848). Under a strict reading of the polity-definition
rule, this could be a candidate mid-row split point.

### oq-cow-code-na

The CSV has `cow = NA` for this row
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW lists
Mexico (`MEX`, `ccode=70`) with system entry on 1831-01-01. The
1800-1831 period is pre-COW for Mexico. Worth verifying whether
COW 70 should be assigned to this row for the 1831-1848 portion.
