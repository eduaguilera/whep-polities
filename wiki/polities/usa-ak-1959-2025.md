# Alaska (state of the United States)

## Summary

Alaska as a U.S. state, from admission to the Union on January 3, 1959 through the present (end_year 2025 per the repository's open/current-unit convention). Congress passed the Alaska Statehood Act in 1958; the territory was formally admitted as the 49th state in January 1959, succeeding the Territory of Alaska (1867-1959, entry ALK-1867-1959) with no change in external boundary. type is subnational because Alaska is a constituent state of the sovereign United States (USA-1959-2025 carries national sovereignty); this row exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory, exactly as USA-AL-1819-2025, USA-AZ-1863-2025 and the other 47 state rows already in the table do. Unlike the Territory of Alaska predecessor or Hawaii's pre-statehood Kingdom/Republic/Territory phases, there is no distinct pre-statehood identity bundled into this entry — it begins exactly at statehood and runs to the present, which is why it takes the dominant USA-SUBUNIT naming pattern rather than a bespoke code.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures state-of-Alaska-level statistical/production data reported from 1959 (formal statehood) onward — the routing decision (unit_id USA-ALASKA) describes source rows spanning 1960-2025, entirely after statehood, with no pre-statehood segment needing back-casting. Previously such data, if matched at all, would fall either to the national USA-1959-2025 row (losing the state-level granularity the source actually reports) or incorrectly to the predecessor ALK-1867-1959 territorial entry, which is scoped to end in 1959 and legally ceased to exist at statehood — routing post-1959 Alaska data there would misrepresent a territory as still existing after it was dissolved into a state. What confirms this is a distinct reporting entity: Alaska has held its own two-letter postal/statistical code (AK) and been tabulated separately in US state-level series continuously since 1959, exactly like every other US state already present in the table (USA-AL, USA-AZ, etc.), and the routing decision explicitly treats it as needing its own new polity because no ALK-1959-2025 or equivalent row already existed to receive this data.

**Area:** approximately 1,717,854 km2 measured from the attached GADM 4.1 geometry in an equal-area projection — this is a measurement of the attached polygon, not an independently sourced territory figure. For context on locating this on a modern map: Alaska occupies the far northwest of North America, bordered by Canada's Yukon and British Columbia to the east, the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Bering Strait/Sea to the west, and the Gulf of Alaska/Pacific Ocean to the south, including the Aleutian Islands chain extending west and the panhandle extending southeast along the Pacific coast — the largest U.S. state by area, roughly 2.5 times the size of Texas, matching the standard modern cartographic depiction of the state with no territorial change since 1959.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-not-bespoke

**Used the dominant USA-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern instead of continuing the bespoke ALK code**

The routing decision proposed reusing the bespoke ALK prefix from the predecessor Territory of Alaska entry (ALK-1867-1959), but 49 of USA's 50 existing subnational codes already follow USA-STATE-start-end (e.g. USA-AL-1819-2025, USA-AZ-1863-2025), and Alaska as a state is administratively identical to every other state row in that set: a two-letter postal abbreviation, admitted by Congress, no separate historical identity distinct from statehood itself. ALK, CAL and HAW are bespoke precisely because they name a territory/republic phase with its own identity (Territory of Alaska, Republic of California, Kingdom/Territory of Hawaii) predating or distinct from statehood; this row is the statehood phase itself, so it takes the dominant pattern: USA-AK-1959-2025.

### d-no-predecessor-claim

**Declared no predecessor to ALK-1867-1959, and flagged the asymmetry as an open question instead**

A prior version of this page declared predecessor: [ALK-1867-1959], but that page declares successor: ['USA-1959-2025'], the national polity code, not this row's code. validate_chain_integrity checks edges bidirectionally against ALK-1867-1959's own baseline, and I cannot edit that page from here. Rather than assert an edge the other page contradicts (which would fail the gate) or silently drop the historical continuity, I removed the predecessor field on this page and recorded the exact one-line edit ALK-1867-1959 needs (change successor from ['USA-1959-2025'] to ['USA-AK-1959-2025']) as an open question, so a maintainer with write access to that page can close the loop.
