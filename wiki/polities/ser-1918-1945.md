# Serbia within Yugoslavia (1918-1945)


> **Polygon attached 2026-08-05 — and the decision this page had recorded was wrong.**
>
> The Polygon decision section below concluded: *"The 1918 CShapes geometry (cowcode=345,
> feature_year=1918) approximating pre-merger Serbia is used as a period proxy"*. Two problems.
> It was never implemented — the frontmatter said `polygon_status: unassigned`,
> `polygon_feature_id: NA` — and **the geometry it names is not pre-merger Serbia**. Measured:
>
> ```
> CShapes 345 @ 1918   157,995 km²   contains Sarajevo, Banja Luka, Podgorica
> this page's estimate  88,000 km²
> ratio                       1.8x
> ```
>
> That step is the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes at its formation, including Bosnia and
> Montenegro. Implementing the decision as written would have attached a 1.8× overstatement to
> **476 data rows** — the largest single block of layer-B rows reaching any polity with no geometry
> (issue 155). This is the fourth prose-versus-frontmatter contradiction found this week, and the
> first where following the prose would have made things worse than ignoring it.
>
> **What is attached instead:** `cshapes-europe` feature 340 at its 1915-1917 step, **90,599 km²** —
> within **3%** of the 88,000 km² this page derived independently by subtracting the other Yugoslav
> republics. Sarajevo, Banja Luka and Podgorica are outside it; Belgrade, Niš, Priština and Skopje
> are inside.
>
> **`polygon_status: proxy`, not `assigned`, and the vintage is deliberately out of span.** Interwar
> Yugoslavia had no "Serbia" as a mapped administrative unit — after 1929 it was divided into
> banovinas that deliberately cut across the old provincial lines — so no contemporaneous polygon
> for this row can exist in any source. A back-projection is the honest option and is labelled as
> one.
>
> **The one thing genuinely unresolved** is which territory the sources mean by "serbia". This page
> says "modern Serbia including Kosovo and Vojvodina", but the polygon attached is the pre-1918
> kingdom, which **includes Vardar Macedonia and excludes Vojvodina** — a different territory of
> almost identical area:
>
> ```
> pre-1918 kingdom (attached)     90,599 km²   Serbia + Kosovo + Macedonia, no Vojvodina
> modern Serbia + Kosovo          87,933 km²   Serbia + Kosovo + Vojvodina, no Macedonia
> ```
>
> Both are within 3% of 88,000, so **area cannot discriminate between them** — only knowing which
> unit IIA and Mitchell tabulated can. The contemporaneous boundary is chosen because Yugoslavia
> retained the pre-war provincial divisions until 1929, covering most of this row's data years.
> Recorded rather than presented as settled.
## Summary

A **27-year row** covering the Serbian administrative territory within the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (1918-1929) and the Kingdom of Yugoslavia (1929-1945). Serbia was not an independent state during this period — it was a constituent territory within Yugoslavia.

> **This row now carries NO DATA, and the premise it was created on was wrong (2026-08-18).** The
> IIA `serbia` series it existed to hold is the **all-Yugoslavia** series, and 573 rows were rerouted
> to `F248-1920-1947`. The paragraph that stood here claimed the data "refers to this sub-territory
> (~88,000 km²), not to the whole Yugoslav state (~255,000 km²)" and that routing it to
> F248-1920-1947 "would overstate the territorial basis ~3x". Both halves are false: routing it here
> **understated** the territory by ~2.8x. It also claimed Mitchell as a second source for the
> reading, and Mitchell's `serbia` label ends in **1909** — it has no 1921-1945 rows at all, and
> carries `yugoslav sfr` separately. Same for `juan`, whose `serbia` ends in 1912. Only IIA'"'"'s label
> spanned past 1918, so the alias served one source. See "Why this polity was created" below for the
> four independent measurements, and `oq-serbia-is-yugoslavia` for the trail.

The row is **retained rather than retired**: it is still the only polity covering wartime occupied
Serbia (1941-1945), which #311 routes here for want of an occupation-administration polity, and
retiring a row is a separate decision from correcting a routing.

Predecessor: [ser-1913-1918](ser-1913-1918.md) — independent Serbia (1913-1918), which merged into Yugoslavia on 1 December 1918.
Successor chain: [srb-2006-2008](srb-2006-2008.md) via the post-WWII Yugoslav period (no granular Serbia polity exists for 1945-2006 at this time). Retargeted 2026-08-05: this pointed at `SER-2006-2008`, retired as a duplicate in issue 43. The stopgap is unchanged in kind — it is still the next Serbia row, and it is still a 61-year jump over a period with no Serbian republic polity, which is the gap worth recording rather than the link worth trusting.

## Territorial extent

- **Territory.** The Serbian lands within Yugoslavia covered roughly modern Serbia including Kosovo and Vojvodina, corresponding to the pre-1918 Kingdom of Serbia territory plus the areas acquired in the Balkan Wars (1912-1913). Estimated area ~88,000 km².
- **Comparison.** Yugoslavia as a whole covered ~255,000 km² (F248-1920-1947). Serbia's share was approximately 34% of the Yugoslav total.
- **Administrative unit.** Within interwar Yugoslavia, "Serbia" as reported in IIA statistics corresponded to the historic Serbian crownland territory. The 1929 reorganization into banovinas fragmented historical regions (Serbia proper was divided among Moravska, Zetska, Vardar, and Dunavska banovinas), but IIA data continued to be reported under "serbia" as a geographic label.
- **Polygon status.** `estimate` — no exact historical GIS polygon for this sub-territory exists in CShapes 2.0 or Cliopatria. The estimate is constructed conceptually as Yugoslavia minus Croatia (~56,538 km²), Slovenia (~20,273 km²), Bosnia-Herzegovina (~51,197 km²), Montenegro (~13,812 km²), and Macedonia (~25,713 km²), yielding ~87,000-90,000 km². This approximation is documented here; the actual figure may vary slightly by source and year.
- **Polygon approximation direction.** The ~88,000 km² figure is consistent with the post-1913 Kingdom of Serbia territory. No border changes within the Serbian territory occurred 1918-1945 that would require splitting.

## Polygon decision

No dedicated historical GIS polygon for "Serbia within interwar Yugoslavia" exists in standard sources (CShapes 2.0, Cliopatria, CHGIS, GADM). Options considered:

1. **Exact historical GIS.** Not available for sub-national units of Yugoslavia at this granularity.
2. **Composed union.** Could be composed as Yugoslavia minus the other constituent territories, but these sub-polygons are also not individually available in CShapes.
3. **Modern Serbia polygon.** Modern Serbia (SRB-2008-2025, CShapes 2006) covers ~77,474 km² — this understates the interwar territory because it excludes Kosovo (~10,908 km²). Not appropriate without adjustment.
4. **Period proxy.** The 1918 CShapes geometry for cowcode=345 (Serbia/Yugoslavia transition) approximates the Serbian territory at ~87,000-90,000 km² immediately before the Yugoslav merger. This is the closest available proxy.

**Decision:** `polygon_status=estimate`. The 1918 CShapes geometry (cowcode=345, feature_year=1918) approximating pre-merger Serbia is used as a period proxy, with documented ~10-15% uncertainty in the sub-territory boundary. The approximation understates the true Serbian administrative territory within Yugoslavia by an unknown but probably small margin (boundary ambiguities in Kosovo/Macedonia).

## Sourced claims

- **IIA agricultural statistics.** Reports "serbia" as a sub-unit of Yugoslavia for 1921-1945, yielding 568 rows (sources iia/mitchell). The label refers to the Serbian constituent territory, not Yugoslavia as a whole.
- **1918-12-01: Kingdom of SCS formed.** Serbia merged into the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (renamed Kingdom of Yugoslavia 1929). Serbia ceased to exist as an independent state.
- **1929: Renamed Yugoslavia.** Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes renamed to Kingdom of Yugoslavia. Internal reorganization into banovinas.
- **1941-04-06: Axis invasion.** Yugoslavia invaded and partitioned. "Serbia" under German occupation (the Territory of the Military Commander in Serbia, administered from August 1941 by Milan Nedic's Government of National Salvation) was **substantially smaller than this row's territory**, not "roughly the same": Kosovo went to Italian-sponsored Greater Albania, western Vojvodina to Hungary, the Banat to a separate German administration, and the Vranje/Bosilegrad/Pirot area to Bulgaria. What remained was close to the pre-1912 "old Serbia" core. Corrected 2026-08-18 — the previous wording here claimed the occupied territory matched "the historical Serbian core", which read as agreement with this page's ~88,000-90,599 km2 headline while actually describing a much smaller unit. See `oq-wwii-occupation-shrinkage`.
- **1944-10-20: Liberation of Belgrade.** Tito's partisan forces liberated Belgrade. Yugoslav sovereignty restored.
- **1945: SFRY established.** Federal People's Republic of Yugoslavia proclaimed. Serbia became a constituent republic (Federalna Republika Srbija) within SFRY.

## Why this polity was created

This polity was created to resolve an alias routing problem: data labelled "serbia" in IIA for
1921-1945 was being routed to SRB-2008-2025 (modern Serbia, ~77,000 km²), and an earlier alias
pointed to F248-1920-1991 (retired). The stated reason for the new row was that routing to
F248-1920-1947 (all Yugoslavia, ~255,000 km²) "would overstate the territory ~3x", so a granular
~88,000 km² polity was needed to match "the Serbian sub-territory that the data actually measures".

**That reason was wrong, and the data was rerouted to F248-1920-1947 on 2026-08-18.** Four
independent measurements, each of which would have sufficed on its own:

1. **Availability.** Raw IIA `serbia` is **28 rows, all imports/exports, zero production or area**,
   and only 1909-1912. It cannot have supplied 573 valued rows. Raw
   `kingdom of serbs, croats and slovenes` (1909-1925: 88 production + 73 area) and raw `yugoslavia`
   (1926-1945: 409 + 249) partition the period exactly, so the series is one Yugoslav run stitched
   across the 1929 renaming of the state.
2. **Per-item provenance.** All **23 of 23** attributable item series attribute to raw `yugoslavia`
   and none to anything else (`state/item_provenance.csv`, `label_is_mixed=no`). So this is a
   whole-label misrouting, not an item-level mixture — the class an alias can actually fix.
3. **The source'"'"'s own stated area.** IIA states **248,494 / 248,665 / 249,468 km²** for this entity
   in its 1929 / 1932 / 1933 editions: F248-1920-1947'"'"'s 255,000 to within **2.5%**, and this row'"'"'s
   90,599 out by **2.74x**. The same table reproduces the pre-1918 rows exactly — **48,302 km²** for
   data year 1911 against SER-1878-1913'"'"'s 48,000, and **87,776** for 1913 against SER-1913-1918'"'"'s
   90,599 — so the source itself draws the boundary at the state'"'"'s formation. That is why the
   pre-1918 rows keep their existing routing and only 1920-1944 moved.
4. **Direct agreement with another source.** IIA `serbia` and `juan`'"'"'s explicitly-labelled
   `yugoslav sfr` share **324 (item, unit, year) cells at a median ratio of 1.000**, 71% within 2%,
   with area series identical to the digit — beans dry 35,000 = 35,000 ha, 32,000 = 32,000,
   30,000 = 30,000 across seven years. Serbia was ~34% of Yugoslavia, so a genuine sub-territory
   series would read ~0.34 against Juan'"'"'s, not 1.000.

The counter-reading the earlier note left open — that the harmonisation deliberately extracted a
Serbian sub-series which merely *shares* values with the Yugoslav one — is what leg 4 rules out. A
sub-series would agree on **no** area cell, let alone on a median of exactly 1.000 across 324.

## Open questions

### oq-banovina-reorganization

The 1929 banovina reorganization divided historical Serbia among multiple administrative units (Moravska, Zetska, Vardar, Dunavska banovinas). It is unclear whether IIA statistics for "serbia" after 1929 continue to use the historical Serbian territory or adopt one of the banovina definitions. The ~88,000 km² estimate assumes continuity with pre-1929 borders. If IIA used banovina-based definitions after 1929, the territory could differ. This question remains open pending direct consultation of the IIA source methodology.

### oq-kosovo-vojvodina-boundary

The ~88,000 km² estimate includes Kosovo (~10,908 km²) and Vojvodina (~21,506 km²). Vojvodina was acquired from Austria-Hungary in 1918 and may or may not be included in IIA's "serbia" label. If Vojvodina is reported separately, the territory would be ~66,500 km². Pending source verification.

### oq-label-is-yugoslavia-not-serbia

**This page's reason for existing may be backwards, and the measurement is not close.** Measured
2026-08-18 against the raw IIA extract (see issue 315):

    raw IIA `serbia`                                28 rows, ALL imports/exports, ZERO production
    raw IIA `kingdom of serbs, croats and slovenes` 594 rows
    raw IIA `yugoslavia`                          2,462 rows

    layer B `iia/serbia`, 462 dated production values
      explained by `yugoslavia`                     317   (69%)
      explained by `kingdom of serbs, croats...`    111   (24%)
      combined                                      428   (93%)
      explained by raw `serbia`                       0   (0%)

The rye series partitions exactly — 6 values from the Kingdom label (pre-1929), 16 from
`yugoslavia` (after the state was renamed), 22 of 22, none from `serbia`. It is one Yugoslav
series stitched across the 1929 renaming.

If that holds, the "Why this polity was created" section above has it inverted. It says routing
this data to F248-1920-1947 (all Yugoslavia, ~255,000 km2) "would overstate the territorial basis
~3x". But the data appears to BE the all-Yugoslavia series, in which case routing it to this row's
90,599 km2 polygon **understates** the territory by ~2.8x, and F248-1920-1947 was the right target
all along.

**RESOLVED 2026-08-18, and acted on.** This note was right and its caution was the correct
posture at the time: one fingerprint measurement is not grounds to move 573 rows. Three further
independent measurements were then taken — the source'"'"'s own stated areas, per-item provenance, and
direct agreement with `juan`'"'"'s `yugoslav sfr` at a median ratio of 1.000 over 324 shared cells —
and the alias was rerouted to F248-1920-1947. The alternative reading this note preserved (a
deliberately extracted sub-series that merely shares values) is what the Juan comparison rules out.
See "Why this polity was created" above.

One thing remains unexplained and is worth separating from the routing question: while the AREA
series agree to the digit, IIA'"'"'s `beans dry` **production** runs about **0.24x** Juan'"'"'s for the same
years (30,600 t against 116,600 in 1933, and similarly through 1939) even though their areas are
identical. That is a production-basis or unit discrepancy between two sources for the same
territory, not evidence about which territory it is, and it sits with the recorded
`iia-layerb-magnitude-scale-inconsistent` error.

Note what this does to the other evidence on this page: the 2026-07-24 note argued from the absence
of a discontinuity at the 1929 banovina reorganisation. If the label is Yugoslavia throughout, then
there would be no discontinuity to find — the observation is equally consistent with the opposite
conclusion, so it supports neither.

### oq-wwii-occupation-shrinkage

**The one territorial discontinuity inside this row's own data span, and neither other open
question mentions it.** `oq-banovina-reorganization` asks whether IIA switched to banovina
definitions after 1929 and `oq-kosovo-vojvodina-boundary` asks what the label includes at the
edges; both are about which *stable* unit is meant. This one is different: from April 1941 to
late 1944 the territory administered as "Serbia" was **actually smaller**, having lost Kosovo,
most of Vojvodina, the Banat and the Bulgarian-annexed east (see the 1941-04-06 sourced claim).

It matters because `serbia|iia|1920-1944` — 573 rows — spans the occupation years. If IIA kept
tabulating under "serbia" through 1941-1944, those years describe a materially smaller reporting
territory than the ~90,599 km2 polygon attached here, and the row silently mixes two territories.

**Measured 2026-08-18 — the exposure is 21 rows, not 573.** IIA does keep tabulating "serbia"
through the occupation, but thinly: 6 rows in 1941, 5 in each of 1942, 1943 and 1944, against
35-40 rows per year for 1936-1939. So the mixed-territory problem is real but small, and it is
confined to oats, olive oil, rapeseed, rye, soybeans and raw sugar.

**And it cannot be settled by magnitudes, which is the part worth recording.** The obvious test
— do the occupation years step down? — is unavailable here. Those 21 rows are internally
incoherent: oats at 23,741,000 tonnes in 1942 against a 318,100-tonne pre-war median; rye area
swinging 78,000 (1941) -> 861,000 (1942) -> 26,000 (1943) -> 915,000 (1944) ha; rye in 1943
giving 26,000 ha and 1,536,000 tonnes, a yield of 59 t/ha. That is not a territorial signal, it
is the already-recorded `iia-layerb-magnitude-scale-inconsistent` data error
(`state/data_errors.csv`, status `pending_audit`), which names **this very assertion** —
`serbia|iia|1920-1944` — as the largest one it affects and states that the
`magnitude_continuity` check is unusable for IIA.

So the question is answerable only from the IIA source methodology directly, not from the panel,
and it stays open for that reason rather than for want of looking.

No granular WHEP polity exists for wartime occupied Serbia, so routing those rows here remains
the best available option — which is why the 2026-08-18 verification recorded
`confirm_kind=best_available` rather than `verified_equal`. Creating one would need a decision on
whether a three-year occupation administration is a polity in this database's sense.

### oq-post-1945-granular-polity

No granular "Serbia within SFRY (1945-1991)" polity exists in WHEP. Data for "serbia" after 1945 and before 1992 (SCG) would need a separate polity (SER-1945-1991 or similar). This is out of scope for the current issue but noted for future work.
