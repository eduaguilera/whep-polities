# Metropolitana (region of Chile)

## Summary

The Region Metropolitana de Santiago (RM) is one of Chile's sixteen first-order administrative regions and by far its most populous: it contains the national capital, Santiago, and roughly 40% of Chile's population despite covering only about 15,400 km2, making it one of the smallest regions by area. This entry captures it as a subnational reporting unit rather than a sovereign polity: `type: subnational` because Chile held sovereignty throughout, but statistics reported at the regional level under a "Metropolitana" or "Santiago region" label describe a territory a small fraction of national Chile, and must not be matched to the national CHL row. The routing decision behind this page found no existing polity that IS this region -- the CHL national rows contain it but are not it -- the same structural gap already documented for sibling regions Magallanes (CHL-MA) and O'Higgins (CHL-LI). The entry starts in 1976, when Chile's current Regiones system (fixed by DL 573/575 of 1974) took effect and gave the area its modern administrative form and name (Region Metropolitana), replacing the older Provincia de Santiago. No predecessor province with a matching boundary is confirmed in hand, so 1900-1975 data is deliberately left unrouted rather than guessed at, per the routing decision's own note.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: rows labelled with Chile's "Metropolitana" administrative unit (CHL-RM in the routing pipeline's unit table), reported at regional level, spanning years for which Chile's Regiones system was in force (1976 onward for this unit under the country's default-start convention). Row counts and exact source labels are held in the routing pipeline's state, not restated in this page. Previously this data risked being matched to CHL-1902-2025, the national Chilean polity row, which is wrong because Metropolitana is roughly 15,400 km2 against continental Chile's roughly 756,000 km2 -- folding regional statistics into the national total would misattribute a Santiago-region figure as a whole-country one, or double count it against other regions' data already correctly separated (as with CHL-MA, CHL-LI). Confirmation that this is a distinct, formally constituted entity comes from Chile's own regionalization law, Decreto Ley 573/575 of 1974 (effective 1976), which created the sixteen-region structure -- Region Metropolitana de Santiago being Region XIII in that scheme -- around which Chile's national statistical agency (INE) has organized regional reporting ever since.

Territory description: Region Metropolitana de Santiago is Chile's smallest region by area but its administrative and demographic core, comprising the city of Santiago (the national capital) and the central valley basin around it, bordered by the Andes to the east and the Coastal Range to the west, within Chile's central zone between the regions of Valparaiso (north/west) and O'Higgins (south). On a modern map it corresponds closely to the Santiago metropolitan area and its surrounding rural comunas. Its area is commonly cited at approximately 15,400 km2. No polygon is attached yet, so this figure is a description of the territory from general reference geography, not a measurement of any geometry in this repository.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor polity is listed: the pre-1976 administrative territory known as the Provincia de Santiago is not represented by any existing row with a confirmed matching boundary, and that gap is recorded as an open question rather than bridged with a guess -- the province and the modern region are not necessarily coextensive, since provincial boundaries were reorganized more than once before the 1974 regionalization. No successor is listed either, since the region remains current at end_year 2025, matching the open end used for the national CHL-1902-2025 row and for sibling region entries CHL-MA-1976-2025 and CHL-LI (O'Higgins).

## Sourced claims

- Chile's Regiones administrative system, under which the Region Metropolitana de Santiago became a first-order region (Region XIII), was established by Decreto Ley 573 and 575 of 1974 and became operative from 1976, replacing the earlier provincial system.
- The Region Metropolitana de Santiago is Chile's smallest region by area, at approximately 15,400 km2, yet contains roughly 40% of the national population, centered on the capital city of Santiago.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern

**Followed the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern**

Chose CHL-RM-1976-2025 following the 57-of-66 dominant subnational pattern (ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end), matching the precedent already set within this same country by CHL-MA-1976-2025 (Magallanes) even though this is only the second CHL subnational code created. Region Metropolitana de Santiago is a standard first-order administrative region, not a historical territory with a bespoke name of its own like Alaska or Hyderabad, so the 3-of-66 bespoke-code pattern does not apply here. RM is the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for this region (CL-RM), matching how CHL-MA used CL-MA's code MA.

### d-polygon-source-none

**Set polygon_source to none rather than the registered slug**

The routing decision's polygon_detail proposed gadm-4.1-adm1 be fetched as a new source, but the locally staged gadm-4.1-adm1 file excludes Chile entirely (0 features), so the registered slug is not actually usable here today. Per the harness's own rule, new_source_needed and registered_source_unfetched routes must not be written verbatim into polygon_source; since the correct source is not currently fetched/available in this repo's data, polygon_source is set to none and the desired source (a full global GADM 4.1 or Chile-specific admin1 boundary set) is recorded as an open question instead.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-chile-missing

**No admin-1 boundary source for Chile is currently staged**

The natural registered source for this region's polygon is gadm-4.1-adm1, matching how other countries' first-order subdivisions are sourced in this repo, but the locally staged GADM 4.1 file is limited to about 81 countries and does not include Chile. Fetching the full global GADM 4.1 admin-1 release (or a Chile-specific slice from it) would resolve this directly and should be prioritized since it would also resolve the identical gap already open on CHL-MA-1976-2025 and any other Chilean regions created in this same batch.

### oq-predecessor-province

**Whether a pre-1976 Provincia de Santiago should be linked as predecessor**

Before the 1974/1976 regionalization, the Santiago area was organized under Chile's older provincial system, and it is plausible that a Provincia de Santiago existed with a boundary substantially overlapping the modern Region Metropolitana. No such polity row currently exists in this database, and no source is in hand confirming the exact boundary match, so no predecessor edge is drawn. If evidence of a matching provincial boundary and administrative continuity turns up, a predecessor polity should be created and linked rather than leaving 1900-1975 data permanently unrouted.

### oq-neighbouring-region-overlap

**Boundary overlap with newly created neighbouring CHL regions is unverified**

This region borders Valparaiso and O'Higgins regions, at least one of which (O'Higgins, CHL-LI) is referenced as a sibling entry created under the same routing batch. Since no polygon is yet attached to either region, it is not possible to verify from geometry alone that the eventual boundaries will tile without gap or overlap; this should be re-checked once GADM Chile data is fetched and polygons are assigned to all Chilean regions in this batch.
