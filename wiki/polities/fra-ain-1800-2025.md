# Ain (département of France)

## Summary

This entry is the French département of Ain, a subnational administrative unit in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of eastern France, bordering Switzerland along Lac Léman and the Jura range to the northeast, with its préfecture at Bourg-en-Bresse. `type: subnational` because Ain has never been a sovereign entity: national sovereignty over this territory sat with France throughout, first as a constitutional monarchy and later republics, and the entry exists purely to carry a reporting/administrative territory that a data source keys by département rather than by country. This is the same rationale documented for other département- and province-level subnational rows already in the table (e.g. the ESP-AS/ESP-CA Spanish province entries): a source publishes at a finer administrative grain than the national polity, and routing those rows to the national FRA container instead would misrepresent the reporting scope even though it would not misrepresent sovereignty. Ain is one of the original 1790 Revolutionary départements and, unlike Alsace-Lorraine, was never ceded to or reincorporated from Germany, never split (unlike Corsica's later division), and is not an overseas département — it is the plain default case the country convention anticipates, which is why its start followed the convention's 1790 rule in the routing decision before being adjusted to 1800 here for containment reasons (see decisions).

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: rows keyed to unit id FRA-FRK21 (admin_name FRK21, i.e. Ain) for France, spanning 1862-2023 per the routing decision; the decision does not give a row count, which is itself a gap — see open questions if this needs resolving before the ingest gate accepts the mapping. Previously, these FRA-FRK21 rows would have matched only against the national FRA container chain (FRA-1800-1871 / FRA-1871-1919 / FRA-1919-2025), which is wrong because those rows are explicitly keyed at département granularity, not national totals, and folding them into the national row would either double-count against other French department-level rows already split out, or silently misrepresent a sub-national reporting unit as a national one. What confirms Ain is a distinct, addressable administrative entity rather than an artefact of the source's coding scheme: it is one of the 83 original 1790 Revolutionary départements, a stable, named, continuously-existing unit of French public administration for over two centuries, with its own INSEE code (01) and préfecture — not a source-specific aggregation invented for this dataset.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: Ain has existed continuously as a French département since the 1790 Revolutionary administrative reform, which replaced the pre-revolutionary provinces (this area was drawn mostly from the province of Bresse, with parts of Bugey, Valromey, Pays de Gex and the Dombes) with a uniform grid of départements. No successor: the département remains a live administrative unit of the modern French Republic as of 2025, and this entry's end_year=2025 is an open-ended present, not a historical termination. Ain was never annexed, split, or renamed at any point across the 1800-2025 span, unlike neighbouring French entries that were affected by the 1871/1919 Alsace-Lorraine cession and reincorporation — that event changed which national FRA container row applies (see the container list) but did not alter Ain's own borders, since Ain does not border Alsace-Lorraine.

## Sourced claims

- Ain was created as one of the original 83 départements in the French Revolutionary administrative reform of 4 March 1790 (law of 22 December 1789 / 26 February-4 March 1790), assembled principally from the former province of Bresse together with Bugey, Valromey, the Pays de Gex, and the Dombes.
- Two independent series (per the routing decision this entry implements) tabulate data keyed to unit id FRA-FRK21 / admin_name FRK21 for Ain across 1862-2023, which is the evidentiary basis for creating this row rather than leaving the rows matched only to the national FRA container.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1790

**start_year set to 1800, not the 1790 default the routing decision proposed**

The routing decision reasoned that Ain is an original Revolutionary département (created 1790) and is not one of the four listed departure cases, so the country convention's default start_year=1790 should apply. That reasoning about the département's own creation date is correct, but it cannot be implemented as written: the containment gate requires every year of a member's span to fall inside some container edge, and the earliest national FRA row in the table is FRA-1800-1871, which starts in 1800. There is no FRA polity row covering 1790-1800 to container against. Rather than leave a 10-year uncontained gap (which the gate rejects) or invent a container edge for a national polity that does not exist in the table, this entry's start_year is pinned to 1800, matching the earliest available container. The département's actual 1790 creation is noted in the summary as historical fact distinct from this entry's span, and the gap is flagged as an open question in case a pre-1800 FRA national row is added later, at which point this entry's start_year should move to 1790.

### d-code-includes-full-span-not-just-data-years

**Polity code spans 1800-2025, not just the 1862-2023 data window**

The routing decision's proposed span (1790-2025, adjusted here to 1800-2025) covers the department's entire existence as an administrative container, not only the 1862-2023 window where mitchell/whichever source rows actually land. This follows the same convention as other FRA-style subnational entries in the table (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, ESP-CA-1833-2025): the code names the polity's full administrative lifetime so that later data landing in gaps before 1862 or after 2023 does not require a new polity or a re-span, which issue 308 flagged as a way banked verdicts get orphaned.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1800-gap-uncontained

**1790-1800 gap: Ain existed as a département before any FRA container row does**

Ain was created in the January 1790 Revolutionary reform, ten years before the earliest FRA national row in the current table (FRA-1800-1871). This entry's start_year is pinned to 1800 to satisfy the containment gate rather than left at the historically correct 1790, per decision d-start-year-1800-not-1790. If a pre-1800 FRA national polity (e.g. FRA-1790-1800 or an extension of FRA-1800-1871's start) is ever added to the table, this entry's start_year and code should be revisited and moved back to 1790 to match the département's actual creation date.

### oq-polygon-source-needed

**No registered source currently supplies a département-level Ain polygon**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning confirms that the locally fetched gadm-4.1-adm1 file is a curated subset that omits France entirely (0 features for FRA), so it cannot be cited as polygon_source even though GADM as a product is registered. No French-specific administrative layer (e.g. IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS, analogous to the mapspain-ign source used for Spain's provinces) is registered in sources.yaml at all. A correct polygon would come from either (a) fetching GADM's full/global release at ADM2 level, which corresponds to French départements, or (b) registering an IGN ADMIN-EXPRESS départements layer. Licence terms for either have not been checked (licence_known=false in the routing decision). Until one is registered and fetched, polygon_source is left as none and polygon_status as unassigned rather than citing a route name, per the harness instructions that new_source_needed is not itself a valid polygon_source value.

### oq-departement-boundary-stability-unverified

**Assumed shape stability of Ain 1790-2025 has not been checked against a source**

The routing decision's polygon_reasoning states French départements have been largely stable in shape since the Revolutionary reform and estimates vintage risk as low, but this is an assumption, not a sourced check. French département boundaries have in fact shifted at the margins over two centuries (arrondissement and canton reorganizations, minor commune reassignments between neighbouring départements). Once a polygon source is fetched (see oq-polygon-source-needed), whoever assigns it should confirm whether the modern boundary is being used as a proxy for the full 1800-2025 span or whether any known boundary change specifically affected Ain's borders with its seven neighbouring départements (Saône-et-Loire, Jura, Rhône, Isère, Savoie, Haute-Savoie, and Puy-de-Dôme at various points).
