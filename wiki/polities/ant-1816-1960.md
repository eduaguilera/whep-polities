# Dutch Caribbean (colonial)

## Summary

This row tracks the Dutch Caribbean island group — Curaçao, Aruba, Bonaire, Sint Maarten (Dutch half), Sint Eustatius, and Saba — during Dutch colonial administration, from the formal re-establishment of Dutch sovereignty in 1816 (following the Napoleonic-era British occupation) through to 1960, when the successor statistical unit ANT-1961-2010 begins in WHEP. The Charter of the Kingdom of the Netherlands (in force 15 December 1954) converted the Netherlands Antilles from a colony into an autonomous country within the Kingdom, but no territorial change occurred at 1954 or 1961; the 1961 break follows the existing ANT-1961-2010 aggregate row. Suriname (South American mainland) was reported separately by FAO and is covered by SUR-1886-2025. This row fills the gap for 21 rows of fao1952 livestock/agricultural data labelled 'Netherlands W Indies' for years 1936-1951.

## Territorial Extent

- **Polygon source:** GADM 4.1 ADM0 islands, composed union. The six constituent islands are small Caribbean islands whose boundaries have not changed since the 1816 restitution. ESTIMATE flag: no historical GIS source (CShapes 2.0, Cliopatria, Paine-2024) covers this territory at any colonial-era date. Modern island boundaries are a faithful proxy for the colonial period given island geography.
- **Polygon method:** composed union of current island ADM0 polygons (ESTIMATE).
- **Area:** approximately 960 km² total (Curaçao ~444 km², Aruba ~180 km², Bonaire ~288 km², Sint Maarten Dutch half ~34 km², Sint Eustatius ~21 km², Saba ~13 km²).
- **Confidence: high.** Island geography makes modern polygons a faithful proxy for historical extent.

## Sourced Claims

- The Charter of the Kingdom of the Netherlands came into force on 15 December 1954, converting the Netherlands Antilles from a colony into an autonomous country within the Kingdom (Wikipedia, Netherlands Antilles).
- The islands were restored to the Netherlands under the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814 and Congress of Vienna 1815; Dutch colonial administration resumed in 1816 (Wikipedia, Netherlands Antilles).
- Suriname was administered separately and reported as a distinct FAO unit; covered by SUR-1886-2025.
- Total land area approximately 960 km²: Curaçao 444 km², Aruba 180 km², Bonaire 288 km², Sint Maarten (Dutch) 34 km², Sint Eustatius 21 km², Saba 13 km² (Wikipedia individual island articles).

## Decisions

- **Code ANT-1816-1960.** Uses the same ISO3 prefix ANT as the successor to signal statistical continuity. Start year 1816 reflects formal restoration of Dutch sovereignty post-Napoleonic occupation. End year 1960 is the final year before the existing ANT-1961-2010 row begins.
- **Type: colonial.** Islands were a Dutch colony/overseas territory throughout this period (formally autonomous within the Kingdom from 1954, but territory and reporting structure unchanged).
- **Polygon: composed-union ESTIMATE.** No historical GIS source covers these small Caribbean islands for the colonial period. GADM 4.1 modern island polygons are the appropriate proxy; island geography ensures modern borders are identical to historical borders.

## Open Questions

### oq-ant-1816-start-year

The 1816 start year reflects the restoration of Dutch administration after the Napoleonic period. If WHEP sources contain data for years before 1816, the start year may need extension to 1800 (WHEP temporal boundary).

### oq-ant-pre-1816-polity

No WHEP row currently covers the Dutch Caribbean islands before 1816. If data from the 1800–1815 period is found, a separate row covering the British-occupation period (1807–1816) and/or the earlier Dutch colonial period (1800–1807) would be needed.
