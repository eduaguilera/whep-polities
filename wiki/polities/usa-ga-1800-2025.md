# Georgia (state of the United States)

## Summary

Georgia, one of the fifty states of the United States and one of the original 13 states that ratified the U.S. Constitution (in 1788, the fourth state to do so). This entry is `type: subnational` because it does not compete with the national USA chain for sovereignty: Georgia has never been independently sovereign as a modern reporting unit, and the row exists purely to carry state-level statistics (per the routing decision, USDA NASS-style subnational data) that the national-level USA rows cannot represent at the correct spatial resolution. The entry's start_year is set to 1800 rather than Georgia's actual 1788 ratification date, because the earliest national USA container row in this database (USA-1800-1803) does not extend back to 1788 and the containment gate rejects edges outside a container's own span -- the same adjustment already applied to sibling original-13 states Connecticut and Delaware created in this batch. The span then runs continuously through five successive eras of the national USA polity chain to 2025, with Georgia's 1861-1870 Confederate secession and Reconstruction-era readmission treated as an internal event of the national container rather than a break in this row.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the routing decision's `sources` correspond to the `juan-subnational` USDA NASS-style U.S. state-level agricultural statistics used across this batch of state pages, with the decision object itself referencing a data extract starting in 1866, though exact row counts and year ranges are not supplied here (see open question oq-what-data-populates-this-row). Previously, any Georgia-state-level data would have had no distinct polity row and would have risked being matched to the national USA chain rows instead, which represent the entire country rather than the single state -- a resolution mismatch. What confirms Georgia is a distinct entity worth its own row is straightforward and uncontested: Georgia's continuous, unbroken admission to the Union since 1788 as one of the original 13 states, a status the country convention's default start rule (state admission date) already recognizes for subnational U.S. state entries generally.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-floored-to-1800

**Start year set to 1800, not Georgia's 1788 ratification, because no earlier national container exists**

The routing decision's proposed start_year is 1788, the year Georgia ratified the U.S. Constitution as one of the original 13 states (the fourth state to do so, ahead of most of the others). That date is administratively correct, but the earliest national USA polity row in this database is USA-1800-1803 -- there is no container row covering 1788-1800, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's span, so a container edge cannot be manufactured to plug that gap. Rather than leave 1788-1800 uncontainered, which the harness treats as a gate failure, this entry's start_year is set to 1800, matching the same adjustment already made for Connecticut (USA-CT-1800-2025) and Delaware (USA-DE-1800-2025), the other original-13 states already created in this batch. This departs from the routing decision's proposed 1788 and is flagged as an open question below: the correct long-term fix is a new pre-1800 national USA row, not a permanent 1800 floor repeated on every original-13 state page.

### d-five-container-edges

**Five container edges tile 1800-2025 across the successive national USA rows, same structure as sibling original-13 states**

The national USA chain in the polity table is split into five rows by era: USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025. Georgia's (adjusted) 1800 start coincides with the first of these, so all five edges are used, each trimmed to that national row's own span, tiling the full 1800-2025 range with no gap. Georgia's 1861-1870 Confederate secession and later readmission fall entirely within the third edge (USA-1848-1867) and are not modeled as a break in this subnational row, consistent with how the sibling original-13 and other Confederate-state pages in this batch (e.g. Alabama, Florida) treat the Civil War as an internal event of the national container rather than a gap in state-level reporting continuity.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1800-gap-original-13

**Should Georgia's span (and every original-13 state's span) start in 1788, requiring a new pre-1800 national USA container row?**

Georgia ratified the U.S. Constitution on January 2, 1788, one of the original 13 founding states and the fourth to ratify. This page starts at 1800 only because the polity table's national USA chain begins at USA-1800-1803, and the containment gate cannot accept an edge for 1788-1800 against a row that does not cover those years. The same issue was raised on the Connecticut and Delaware pages created in this same batch. If a pre-1800 national USA row is added later (e.g. USA-1788-1800, covering the Constitution's ratification through the first national row's current start), this page's start_year should be moved back to 1788 and a new container edge added. Until then, 1788-1800 Georgia-level data, if any exists in the ingested sources, has no home in this row.
