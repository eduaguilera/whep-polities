# Greater Nepal

## Summary

Greater Nepal covers the Gurkha kingdom at or near its greatest territorial extent, before the catastrophic losses of the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–1816). At its peak, Greater Nepal stretched from the Sutlej River in the west to the Tista/Mechi River in the east, encompassing approximately 227,000 km² and including the present-day Indian states of Sikkim, Kumaon, Garhwal, and the western Terai. The Treaty of Sugauli (4 March 1816) ceded Sikkim, Kumaon, Garhwal, and much of the Terai to British India, reducing Nepal's territory by approximately 34% to ~149,000 km². Nepal had no COW code during this period; it was never formally colonized and maintained internal sovereignty, but Britain controlled key boundary commissions. CShapes starts in 1886 and does not cover this period.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is Cliopatria v0.1.3 (Nepal 1800–1802 time-step). A single snapshot is applied across the 1800–1816 period, representing Greater Nepal at near-peak extent. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §scope](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#scope)

The single-time-step limitation applies: WHEP extracts one snapshot from Cliopatria's Nepal sequence. The polygon may not accurately reflect the gradual Gurkha expansion that peaked around 1814 and the 1815–1816 British advance. CShapes' 1886 floor places this entire period outside CShapes' scope. [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope)

## Sourced claims

- A war between Britain and Nepal in 1814–1815 ended with Nepal's defeat; the peace treaty established the Kali and Mechi rivers as the eastern and western boundaries of Nepal. [biger-1995 §nepal](../sources/biger-1995.md#nepal)
- In December 1816 the Terai area between the Rapti and Kosi rivers was returned to Nepal (the Terai restoration was a partial concession, not a reversal of the full cession). [biger-1995 §nepal](../sources/biger-1995.md#nepal)
- Nepal is bounded by China (768 miles; 1,236 km) in the north and India (1,050 miles; 1,690 km) in the east, south and west — dimensions reflecting the post-1816 boundaries. [biger-1995 §nepal](../sources/biger-1995.md#nepal)
- Cliopatria v0.1.3 provides the polygon for this period (Nepal 1800–1802 time-step), representing ~227,000 km² at near-peak Gurkha extent. [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §scope](../sources/cliopatria-v0.1.3.md#scope)
- Greater Nepal had approximately 227,000 km² at peak (1814); Treaty of Sugauli (1816) caused a ~34% territorial loss. [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)

## Open questions

### oq-gurkha-expansion-peak

The Cliopatria Nepal 1800–1802 snapshot may underrepresent the Gurkha kingdom's maximum extent (~227,000 km², reached around 1814). A more accurate representation would use a time-step closer to 1814. The next ingest should investigate whether a later Cliopatria time-step (e.g. 1810–1815) better represents peak Greater Nepal and whether WHEP should prefer it.

### oq-terai-restoration-1816

Biger notes that in December 1816 part of the Terai was returned to Nepal. It is unclear whether this restoration is reflected in the Cliopatria polygon for NPL-1816-2025 or in any separate territorial adjustment. A future ingest should determine whether the restored Terai territory was captured in the CShapes or Cliopatria coverage.
