# Bornu Empire

## Summary

The Bornu Empire from the 1800 database floor to Rabih az-Zubayr's conquest in 1893. Bornu is the post-1380 western continuation of the Kanem-Bornu state (after the Sayfawa dynasty was expelled from Kanem by the Bilala). At its 19th-century extent it covered northeastern Nigeria, southeastern Niger, northern Cameroon, and western Chad. Rabih's 1893 conquest ended Bornu's independence; Rabih's own defeat at Kousséri on 22 April 1900 handed the territory to the British–French–German colonial partition, which was effectively completed by 1902.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from Paine et al. (2024). Heartland around Lake Chad with Kukawa as capital in the 19th century. Area ~227,106 km² per CSV notes. Cliopatria 0.1.3 records 8 time-steps for BNU 1636–1894.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** None in WHEP. The 1800 start is a database floor; the empire dates to at least c.1380 (and the broader Kanem-Bornu polity to c.700).
- **Successor:** [nni-1899-1904](nni-1899-1904.md) — Northern Nigeria Protectorate, which inherits the British share of the partition. The French share became part of French Equatorial Africa / Niger; the German share became Kamerun. WHEP currently tracks only the Nigerian successor chain.

## Contradictions

End-year ambiguity. 1893 marks the loss of Bornu independence to Rabih, but Rabih's regime lasted until 1900. The WHEP row ends at 1893 because the successor NNI row begins at 1899 and the Rabih period is not modelled separately. A conservative reading would split BNU-1800-1893 from a RAB-1893-1900 row; flagged as open question.

## Open questions

### oq-bnu-rabih-gap

The 1893–1899 Rabih period is not captured by any WHEP row. Bornu under Rabih was a distinct political entity from both the Sayfawa-era Bornu Empire and the colonial successors. Whether to add a RAB-1893-1900 row (or extend BNU's end_year) is an open modelling decision.
