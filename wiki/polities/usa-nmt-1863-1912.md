# New Mexico Territory

## Summary

New Mexico Territory was organised under the Compromise of 1850 (act of 9 September 1850) from land ceded by Mexico in 1848 and claimed by Texas; the Gadsden Purchase (1853-54) added the strip south of the Gila. Colorado Territory took the part north of 37°N in 1861, and the Arizona Organic Act of 24 February 1863 divided the territory along the 109th meridian, the western half becoming Arizona Territory. From 1863 New Mexico Territory had the outline of the present state. It was admitted as the 47th state on 6 January 1912, after the longest territorial period of any contiguous state.

This row carries the territory from 1863 to 1912 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-nm-1912-2025](usa-nm-1912-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span starts in 1863, when the split from Arizona fixed the boundary this row carries; the 1850-1863 territory included Arizona and is a different territory. It ends in 1912 (exclusive), where USA-NM-1912-2025 begins. The sibling row [usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the same 1863 division.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-NEWMEXICO`, label `NEW MEXICO`),
reports 486 rows for 1879-1911 (33 year(s)) — wheat, oats, barley, maize, dry beans, potatoes and hay (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as New Mexico Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The south-western state between Colorado (37°N) and Mexico, from Arizona (109°03'W) east to the Texas and Oklahoma lines (103°W, with the 32°N line and the Rio Grande in the south-east): the modern state of New Mexico.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.32_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 314,880 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1863
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  The eastern line with Texas was surveyed more than once (the Clark survey of 1859-60 ran west of the 103rd meridian, and later surveys and litigation adjusted the line and the Rio Grande reach near El Paso). Those are strips along the edge, far under 1% of the territory, so the polygon is recorded as `assigned`.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

  of the same size as the year-to-year changes around it, with no step, which is what a series

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. New Mexico Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1912 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1879-1911; the row spans 1863-1912, the years this boundary
existed as New Mexico Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-NMT-1863-1912` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-territorial-series-provenance

**Were the 1879-1911 figures published for the territory at the time, or rebuilt later?**

Quick Stats serves 33 territorial years under the state name as `SURVEY` data. The provenance of the individual estimates is not established here; the frame is the same either way.

### oq-texas-line

**Texas-New Mexico line adjustments are not measured**

Believed small, but not checked against a year-stamped boundary source; `usaboundaries-newberry` is registered but not fetched here.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
