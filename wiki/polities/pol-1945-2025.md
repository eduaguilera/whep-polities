# Poland (1945-2025)

## Summary

This row tracks Poland for **81 years**, from post-WWII to the present
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Post-WWII Poland. Borders shifted dramatically westward: lost eastern
territories (Kresy) to USSR, gained German territories east of
Oder-Neisse line (Silesia, Pomerania, southern East Prussia). Communist
period 1945-1989. Solidarity movement. 1989 democratic transition. EU
member 2004. Modern Poland ~312,700 km².

Predecessor: [pol-1921-1945](pol-1921-1945.md) -- WWII partition.
Successor: NA (still active).

## Sourced claims

- **1945: Post-WWII borders.** Poland lost eastern territories to USSR
  (Kresy) and gained Silesia, Pomerania, and southern East Prussia from
  Germany. Net loss of ~77,000 km². This is the start-of-row event.
- **1945-1989: Communist period.** People's Republic of Poland. Soviet
  satellite state.
- **1980-1981: Solidarity.** Independent trade union led by Lech Walesa.
  Martial law declared December 1981.
- **1989: Democratic transition.** Round Table Agreement. First
  semi-free elections. End of communist rule.
- **2004-05-01: EU accession.** Poland joined the European Union.
- **COW state-system membership.** COW code 290, from 1945-06-28
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates).
- **Area and borders.** Biger: area 120,628 sq. miles (312,683
  sq. km.), pop. 38,500,000 (1994). Bounded by Baltic Sea,
  Kaliningrad (432 km.), Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine, Slovakia,
  Czech Republic, Germany. Capital is Warsaw (pp.436--437)
  [biger-1995 §poland](../sources/biger-1995.md#poland).
- **Post-WWII border shift.** "After the war Poland's borders
  underwent a drastic change. The eastern territories inhabited
  mainly by Ukrainians, Byelorussians and Lithuanians were annexed
  to the Soviet Union" (p.439)
  [biger-1995 §poland](../sources/biger-1995.md#poland).
- **Kaliningrad exclave.** "The Russian region of Kaliningrad
  (Konigsberg) covers about 5,790 sq. miles (15,000 sq. km.) and
  is populated by approximately 871,000 people" (p.440)
  [biger-1995 §poland](../sources/biger-1995.md#poland).
