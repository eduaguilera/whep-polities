# British Honduras

## Summary

British Honduras under British colonial administration, from the first year CShapes covers (1886) through 1970. All **66 layer-B rows** for the BLZ polity family — years 1909-1951 — fall inside this row's span (`pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/territory_basis.csv`). The colony's borders did not change across the 84-year span; what changed was its constitutional status (Crown Colony 1862, internal self-government 1964) and, at the very end, its capital. Guatemala maintained a claim to the whole territory throughout without ever exercising control over any of it.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** CShapes 2.0 feature `80`, time-step **1886**, `polygon_status: assigned`. Measured in an equal-area CRS (ESRI:54034) from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`: **22,076 km²** on the Caribbean coast of Central America. The conventionally cited area of Belize is 22,966 km² — which is also the value this row's frontmatter declares — so the attached polygon is **890 km² (3.9%) smaller** than the declared figure; see *Contradictions*.

**The 1886-vintage polygon and the 1909-1951 data are 23 to 65 years apart, and that is fine here — but for a reason that has to be argued, not assumed.** CShapes has no time-step for feature 80 between 1886 and Belize's independence, which under CShapes' own method means no coded change occurred: where a unit has no coded change, "we simply backdated their borders to 1886" ([cshapes-2.0 §geocoding](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#geocoding)). That is an argument from CShapes' silence, and CShapes' silence is only informative where CShapes would have spoken. Here it would have: at 22,076 km² the colony is above the sub-250,000-population dependency floor only marginally, but the relevant test for *change* is the 100 × 100 km (10,000 km²) size threshold ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)) — so CShapes would have recorded any transfer amounting to 45% or more of the colony, and nothing smaller. **The vintage gap is therefore safe against large changes and blind to small ones**, which is the honest statement of what the polygon supports.

**Independent corroboration that the borders did not move: the Guatemalan claim was never settled during the span.** Biger's Belize material is thin — the country appears mainly on a regional boundary map with its capital at Belmopan, and the Belize-Guatemala boundary is treated in the Guatemala-Mexico context rather than in its own entry ([biger-1995 §belize](../sources/biger-1995.md#belize)). But the thinness itself is informative: Biger's *Encyclopedia of International Boundaries* records no Belize boundary event, and an unresolved claim is by definition not a boundary change. Guatemala's claim was a claim to the whole colony, so had it ever succeeded even partially the change would have been far above CShapes' threshold and impossible to miss.

**Territorial changes inside 1886-1970: none found. The row should not be split.** The in-span events, and why each is not a row boundary:

- **1862** — British Honduras becomes a Crown Colony. Predates the row.
- **1964-01-01** — internal self-government. Constitutional, not territorial; under the WHEP polity definition a change of regime does not open a row ([log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition)).
- **1970-08-01** — the capital moves from Belize City to the newly built Belmopan, after Hurricane Hattie destroyed much of the old capital in 1961. This is the row's end boundary and it is worth being explicit that **it is not a territorial event either** — a capital relocation changes nothing about the colony's extent. The 1970 boundary exists because the successor row was cut there, not because territory moved. Biger's map caption confirms Belmopan as the capital by 1995 ([biger-1995 §belize](../sources/biger-1995.md#belize)).

The one caveat on the *start* boundary: 1886 is the CShapes coverage floor ([cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope)), not a historical event. The predecessor [BLZ-1800-1886](blz-1800-1886.md) carries `polygon_source: none` and `polygon_status: unassigned` precisely because nothing on disk maps the pre-1886 settlement, so the 1886 split is a data-availability artefact and carries no historical claim.

## Sourced claims

- Belize (formerly British Honduras) appears in Biger on the regional map of "the boundaries of Belize, El Salvador, Guatemala and Honduras" with its capital at Belmopan, and the Belize-Guatemala boundary is discussed only in the Guatemala-Mexico section context ([biger-1995 §belize](../sources/biger-1995.md#belize)). Biger records **no** Belize boundary change.
- "The Central American colonies separated from Europe in 1821 and in 1823 combined to form the United Provinces of Central America" ([biger-1995 §belize](../sources/biger-1995.md#belize)) — context for the region, and notably British Honduras was *not* part of that process, which is why it has a separate colonial chain in this database rather than being folded into a Central American federation row.
- **COW's Belize tenure begins `1981-09-21` and runs to `2024-12-31`** — a single row for `ccode=80` in `wiki/sources/data/cow-v2024/statelist2024.csv` ([cow-state-system-v2024 §schema](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#schema) for columns). That start is **11 years after this row ends**, so COW says nothing about any part of this span. The frontmatter `cow: 80` is a code assignment for the Belize family, not a COW membership claim; colonial dependencies are absent from COW by design ([cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion), and limitation 1 of [cow-state-system-v2024 §known-limitations](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#known-limitations)).
- Where a unit has no coded territorial change, CShapes backdates its borders to 1886 rather than observing them ([cshapes-2.0 §geocoding](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#geocoding)) — so this row's polygon is an inference from stability, and its 1886 label should not be read as an 1886 measurement.
- CShapes codes border adjustments only above 100 × 100 km ([cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)) — 10,000 km², i.e. 45% of this colony. Any smaller in-span change would be invisible to CShapes, which bounds how strong the stability claim above can be.
- Geometric measurement (this ingest, not an external source): this row **22,076 km²** in ESRI:54034 from `data/final/polities_database.gpkg`; the predecessor [BLZ-1800-1886](blz-1800-1886.md) has no geometry to measure.
- Data footprint (this ingest): **66 layer-B rows** route to this code per `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/territory_basis.csv`, all in 1909-1951 and therefore all inside the span.

## Contradictions

- **Declared 22,966 km² vs measured 22,076 km².** The frontmatter `polygon_area_km2` and the previous version of this page both said ~22,966 km², which is the standard figure for Belize's land area. The polygon actually attached measures **22,076 km²** — 890 km² (3.9%) smaller. The likely explanation is the same one that recurs across this database: a published country area was written into a field that is meant to describe the *polygon*, or the CShapes coastline excludes cayes and lagoon area that the published figure includes. The declared value is left unchanged here because other gates baseline it, but the measured figure is the one this page's reasoning uses, per [log decision-csv-is-evidence-not-authority](../log.md#decision-csv-is-evidence-not-authority). See [oq-blz-declared-area](#oq-blz-declared-area).
