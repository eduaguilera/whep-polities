# Papua New Guinea

## Summary

This row tracks Papua New Guinea for **51 years** from independence
(1975) to the database truncation year
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Papua New Guinea
gained independence on 16 September 1975, adopting the 1973 boundary
line with Indonesia as its international land boundary
[biger-1995 §papua-new-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#papua-new-guinea).
The Indonesia-Papua New Guinea boundary is 510 miles (820 km) long,
running north-south across the island of New Guinea along two
meridians and the thalweg of the Fly River.

## Sourced claims

- Papua New Guinea became fully independent on 16 September 1975,
  adopting the 1973 line as its land boundary with Indonesia.
  [biger-1995 §papua-new-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#papua-new-guinea) (p.297)
- The Indonesia-Papua New Guinea boundary is 510 miles (820 km) long,
  follows two meridians and the thalweg of the Fly River, and is
  demarcated by 14 meridian monuments.
  [biger-1995 §papua-new-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#papua-new-guinea) (p.296)
- The London Agreement of 1885 determined the border between German
  and British colonial lands on the island of New Guinea.
  [biger-1995 §papua-new-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#papua-new-guinea) (p.297)
- Following the Papua Act of 1905, British New Guinea became the
  Territory of Papua under Australian administration.
  [biger-1995 §papua-new-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#papua-new-guinea) (p.297)
- No disputes concerning the Indonesia-Papua New Guinea border.
  [biger-1995 §papua-new-guinea](../sources/biger-1995.md#papua-new-guinea) (p.297)
