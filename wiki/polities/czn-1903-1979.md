# Panama Canal Zone (US-administered)

## Summary

This row tracks the **Panama Canal Zone** from its creation under the
**Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty (1903-11-18)** to its abolition under the
**Torrijos–Carter Treaties (effective 1979-10-01)**.

The row exists because IIA yearbook footnotes for **1960** report the
Canal Zone's data as recorded separately from Panama — it was a
US-administered federal enclave with its own census, budget, and
administration, and is therefore reported as a `separate_entity` from
Panama in the IIA series.

The Canal Zone was a roughly 16 km wide (five statute miles either side of
the canal centreline, i.e. a ten-mile width) strip of territory running
approximately 80 km across the Isthmus of Panama. This sentence used to read
"8 miles either side", which is a 26 km width and not the treaty's; the
width is 16.09 km and that is what the polygon builder uses. Under the 1903 treaty, the United States
received rights to administer the zone "in perpetuity" as if it were
sovereign, though Panama retained nominal sovereignty. The zone was
governed by a presidentially-appointed Canal Zone Governor and the
Panama Canal Company; it had its own law code (Canal Zone Code),
courts, postal system, and police force (Canal Zone Police). The 1950
US Census counted approximately 52,800 civilian residents in the Zone,
and the Zone maintained its own agricultural, statistical, and trade
reporting separate from the Republic of Panama.

Under the Torrijos–Carter Treaties signed 7 September 1977, the
surrounding Canal Zone territory was returned to Panama on
1 October 1979 (entry into force of the Panama Canal Treaty). The canal
itself and remaining US installations were handed over on
31 December 1999. The successor polity is [pan-1979-2025](pan-1979-2025.md).

The predecessor is [pan-1800-1979](pan-1800-1979.md), which covers
the Republic of Panama in the period when the Canal Zone operated as
an enclave within it; that row explicitly notes the Canal Zone as an
open question warranting its own row.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status: proxy**, constructed 2026-08-17 (issue 155). `polygon_source: constructed`,
`polygon_feature_id: CZN-1903-1979`, built by `build_czn_1903_1979` in
`scripts/sources/constructed/build.py`.

- **The recipe is the treaty.** Article II of the Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty grants the
  United States "a zone of land and land under water … of the width of ten miles
  extending to the distance of five miles on each side of the center line of the route of
  the Canal". The Zone is therefore *defined* as a five-statute-mile buffer of the canal
  centreline, and the only thing a builder needs from a source is that centreline. The
  polygon is a 8,046.72 m buffer taken in UTM zone 17N (EPSG:32617) and returned to WGS84.
- **Where the centreline came from, and why it was missed.** The four sources this page had
  queried genuinely lack the Zone: CShapes 2.0 carries Panama as gwcode 95 (1903–2019) as a
  single feature; Cliopatria has "Republic of Panama" (1905–2024) and no Zone entry;
  Paine-2024's `PCS.shp` has no match; and GADM 4.1's fetched set has **no Panama file at
  all** (re-measured 2026-08-17: adm0 99 countries, adm1 80, adm2 Indonesia only — no
  `PAN`). A **fifth** file nobody queried has it: Natural Earth 10m rivers and lake
  centrelines, distributed inside the Paine 2024 replication package
  (`data/geodata/paine-2024/AfricanBordersReplication/Shapefiles/Rivers/ne_10m_rivers_lake_centerlines.shp`),
  carries the **Panama Canal** as two line parts (`Lake Centerline` through Gatun Lake and
  `River` for the cut sections). This closes oq-polygon-construction, which had asked for
  exactly this and concluded no such source was present.
- **Measured, with the deviation stated rather than corrected.**

  ```
  NE centreline length            60.65 km
  5-mile buffer                1,176.3 km²   (ESRI:54034, as built)
  conventionally stated        1,432   km²   (553 sq mi)
  deviation                      -17.9%
  ```

  `polygon_area_km2: 1432` records the historically stated figure, not the polygon's own
  area, so that `validate_polygons` compares the geometry against something independent of
  it (a declared area within 0.1% of its own polygon is what that gate's self-reference
  check exists to reject).

- **Why the shortfall, in one direction and quantified.** It is not the buffer distance,
  which is the treaty's. It is the two things the Zone held *beyond* the strip:
  - **Gatun Lake.** The Zone included the whole lake (~425 km²), much of which lies more
    than five miles from the centreline. The 553 sq mi figure is land **and** water; the
    Zone's land area is usually given near 368 sq mi (~953 km²), and this land-plus-water
    strip of 1,176 km² sits between the two, as it should.
  - **The sea approaches**, three marine miles out from each coast. NE's line stops at the
    coast — it ends at 9.304 °N against Cristóbal's ~9.35 °N and at 8.960 °N against
    Balboa's ~8.93 °N — so roughly 9 km of centreline and ~145 km² of buffer are missing at
    the ends. Extending the line to invented termini, or widening the buffer until the area
    matched 1,432, would make the area check circular; the FEZ-1943-1951 rule applies.
- **The known error in the other direction.** The treaty excludes "the cities of Panama and
  Colón", which sit at the two ends inside the strip. No fetched source has either city's
  1903 limits, so they are **not** subtracted. Both were small in 1903, so the two errors
  partly cancel and the net is still −17.9%.
- **Confidence.** The location and shape are as good as the treaty definition; the *extent*
  is a documented −17.9% understatement. This is a `proxy`, not `assigned`, for that reason.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor.** [pan-1800-1979](pan-1800-1979.md) — the Republic
  of Panama, which contained the Canal Zone as a US-administered enclave
  for the full 1903–1979 period. The Zone was carved out of Panamanian
  territory at independence; the predecessor code reflects the territorial
  context immediately before the Zone was established.
- **Successor.** [pan-1979-2025](pan-1979-2025.md) — Panama after the
  1979 return of the Canal Zone territory. Upon entry into force of the
  Panama Canal Treaty (1 October 1979), the Zone ceased to exist as a
  distinct administrative unit; its territory was absorbed into the
  Republic of Panama.

## Contradictions

- **Sovereignty vs. administration.** The 1903 treaty left Panama with
  nominal sovereignty over the Zone while giving the US rights "as if"
  sovereign. This creates ambiguity in territorial classification: the
  Zone was neither a US territory (no formal annexation) nor Panamanian
  administered land. WHEP classifies it as `type: colonial` (a US-administered
  dependency) following the functional-control principle.
- **Start date ambiguity.** The Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty was signed
  18 November 1903 and ratified by the US Senate 23 February 1904. US
  administration effectively began in 1904. The `start_year: 1903` follows
  treaty date convention (consistent with how CShapes records Panama's
  independence in 1903).

## Decisions

- **2026-08-17 — polygon constructed from the treaty definition (issue 155).** Supersedes the
  decision below. The Zone was receiving 5 layer-B rows while carrying no territory, i.e.
  matched but spatially unusable, and the blocker turned out to be a source nobody had
  queried rather than a missing dataset. See Territorial extent for the recipe and the
  measured −17.9%.
- **Polygon unassigned (superseded 2026-08-17).** All four queried sources (CShapes 2.0,
  Cliopatria, Paine-2024, GADM 4.1 adm1) lack a dedicated Canal Zone
  feature. The `polygon_status: unassigned` was the honest documented
  outcome while that was the whole search; it was not, and a fifth fetched file had the
  canal centreline the treaty definition needs.
- **polity_code `CZN`.** No ISO 3166 code exists for the historical Canal
  Zone. The UN used the code `CZ` (later retired); `CZN` is used here
  as a sensible 3-letter WHEP abbreviation to avoid collision with the
  Czech Republic (CZE).
- **iso3 = PAN.** The Zone was geographically within Panama and Panama
  retained nominal sovereignty; `PAN` is the most accurate modern ISO
  assignment. There is no ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code for the Zone itself.
- **cow = null.** The COW state system does not assign the Canal Zone
  a separate state number; it is treated as part of Panama (ccode 95).
- **end_year 1979.** The Canal Zone ceased to exist as a distinct
  administrative unit on 1 October 1979 under the Panama Canal Treaty.
  The US retained operational control of the canal itself until
  31 December 1999, but that residual presence does not constitute a
  distinct territorial entity for WHEP purposes.
- **type: colonial.** The Zone was a US-administered dependency with
  distinct governance structures; `colonial` best captures the functional
  status even though Panama retained nominal sovereignty.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-construction — CLOSED 2026-08-17

This asked for a polygon constructed from the treaty description (a strip centred on the canal
centreline) and said the WHEP data directory contained no source for it. It did: Natural Earth
10m rivers, inside the Paine 2024 bundle. Built; see Territorial extent. The question that
remains is narrower and is recorded separately as oq-czn-lake-and-approaches.

Two corrections to this question's own wording, for the record. It said the Zone was "16 km
wide (8 miles either side of the canal centreline)" in the Summary above — 8 miles either side
is a 26 km width. The treaty is five statute miles each side, a 16.09 km width, which is what
the builder uses.

### oq-czn-lake-and-approaches

The buffer measures 1,176 km² against a stated 1,432 (−17.9%), because the Zone also held Gatun
Lake beyond the five-mile line and three marine miles of sea at each end, and because Natural
Earth's centreline stops ~9 km short of the two harbour entrances. Closing this needs either a
Gatun Lake polygon (no fetched source has one — the lake shapefiles in the Paine bundle are
Africa-only) or a digitised Zone boundary. Until then the polygon is a proxy understating the
Zone by a known and one-directional 18%.

### oq-czn-pan-double-count

`PAN-1903-1979` says its CShapes feature 95 covers Panama "excluding the US-administered Canal
Zone strip". Measured on 2026-08-17 against the new polygon: **95.9% of the Zone (1,127 of
1,176 km²) is inside CShapes 95**, and the 4.1% outside is the sea approaches, not an excluded
enclave. So the two rows overlap almost completely for 1903–1979 and any consumer summing both
counts the Zone twice. Geometry was attached anyway — 5 rows with no territory is worse than 5
rows a hypothetical double-sum would duplicate, which is the AOI-1936-1941 precedent — but
which row a consumer should drop is not settled here. The false sentence on `pan-1903-1979.md`
was corrected in the same change.

### oq-1904-vs-1903-start

The Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty was signed November 1903 but US
administration effectively began in 1904 after Senate ratification.
If the WHEP convention for this period shifts to ratification date,
`start_year` should be updated to 1904.

### oq-1999-continuation

From 1 October 1979 to 31 December 1999, the US retained a "Canal Zone"
in the sense of operating the canal and maintaining military bases under
the Panama Canal Treaty. Whether this residual presence (1979–1999)
warrants a separate WHEP row for the canal operating area is open. The
current row ends at 1979 when the Zone's civilian administration
dissolved.
