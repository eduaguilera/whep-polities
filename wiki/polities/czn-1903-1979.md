# Panama Canal Zone (US-administered)

## Summary

This row tracks the **Panama Canal Zone** from its creation under the
**Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty (1903-11-18)** to its abolition under the
**Torrijos–Carter Treaties (effective 1979-10-01)**.

The row exists because IIA yearbook footnotes for **1960** report the
Canal Zone's data as recorded separately from Panama — it was a
US-administered federal enclave with its own census, budget, and
administration, and is therefore reported as a `separate_entity` from
Panama in the IIA series.

The Canal Zone was a roughly 16 km wide (8 miles either side of the
canal centreline) strip of territory running approximately 80 km across
the Isthmus of Panama. Under the 1903 treaty, the United States
received rights to administer the zone "in perpetuity" as if it were
sovereign, though Panama retained nominal sovereignty. The zone was
governed by a presidentially-appointed Canal Zone Governor and the
Panama Canal Company; it had its own law code (Canal Zone Code),
courts, postal system, and police force (Canal Zone Police). The 1950
US Census counted approximately 52,800 civilian residents in the Zone,
and the Zone maintained its own agricultural, statistical, and trade
reporting separate from the Republic of Panama.

Under the Torrijos–Carter Treaties signed 7 September 1977, the
surrounding Canal Zone territory was returned to Panama on
1 October 1979 (entry into force of the Panama Canal Treaty). The canal
itself and remaining US installations were handed over on
31 December 1999. The successor polity is [pan-1979-2025](pan-1979-2025.md).

The predecessor is [pan-1800-1979](pan-1800-1979.md), which covers
the Republic of Panama in the period when the Canal Zone operated as
an enclave within it; that row explicitly notes the Canal Zone as an
open question warranting its own row.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** No faithful-vintage polygon for the Canal Zone
  as a distinct entity has been found in any of the queried sources:
  - **CShapes 2.0** (`data/geodata/cshapes-2.0/CShapes-2.0.shp`)
    contains Panama as gwcode 95 (1903–2019) as a single feature
    covering the whole republic; no separate Canal Zone feature exists.
  - **Cliopatria** (`data/geodata/cliopatria/cliopatria_polities_only.geojson`)
    contains "Republic of Panama" entries (1905–2024) but no distinct
    Canal Zone entry.
  - **Paine-2024** (`data/geodata/paine-2024/PCS.shp`) was queried
    with no Canal Zone or Panama Zone match.
  - **GADM 4.1 adm1** (`data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg`) does
    not include Panama in the available 583-feature subset.
- **Method.** No polygon assigned (`polygon_status: unassigned`). The
  Canal Zone was a narrow strip (~1,400 km²) running along the canal;
  no historical GIS source in the WHEP geodata repository records it
  as a distinct administrative polygon.
- **Area.** Historically documented at approximately **1,432 km²**
  (553 sq mi), based on the conventionally reported zone dimensions of
  ~16 km wide × ~80 km long. This figure is from published historical
  accounts, not from a polygon query, and is given here as context only;
  it is NOT entered in `polygon_area_km2` because the polygon is
  unassigned.
- **Confidence.** Unassigned status is certain: CShapes 2.0 and
  Cliopatria were both queried and confirmed to lack a dedicated feature.
  A constructed polygon could in principle be derived from OpenStreetMap
  historical data or a digitised historical map of the Zone, but no
  such source is currently in the WHEP geodata repository.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor.** [pan-1800-1979](pan-1800-1979.md) — the Republic
  of Panama, which contained the Canal Zone as a US-administered enclave
  for the full 1903–1979 period. The Zone was carved out of Panamanian
  territory at independence; the predecessor code reflects the territorial
  context immediately before the Zone was established.
- **Successor.** [pan-1979-2025](pan-1979-2025.md) — Panama after the
  1979 return of the Canal Zone territory. Upon entry into force of the
  Panama Canal Treaty (1 October 1979), the Zone ceased to exist as a
  distinct administrative unit; its territory was absorbed into the
  Republic of Panama.

## Contradictions

- **Sovereignty vs. administration.** The 1903 treaty left Panama with
  nominal sovereignty over the Zone while giving the US rights "as if"
  sovereign. This creates ambiguity in territorial classification: the
  Zone was neither a US territory (no formal annexation) nor Panamanian
  administered land. WHEP classifies it as `type: colonial` (a US-administered
  dependency) following the functional-control principle.
- **Start date ambiguity.** The Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty was signed
  18 November 1903 and ratified by the US Senate 23 February 1904. US
  administration effectively began in 1904. The `start_year: 1903` follows
  treaty date convention (consistent with how CShapes records Panama's
  independence in 1903).

## Decisions

- **Polygon unassigned.** All four queried sources (CShapes 2.0,
  Cliopatria, Paine-2024, GADM 4.1 adm1) lack a dedicated Canal Zone
  feature. The `polygon_status: unassigned` is the honest documented
  outcome; this is an accepted WHEP pattern.
- **polity_code `CZN`.** No ISO 3166 code exists for the historical Canal
  Zone. The UN used the code `CZ` (later retired); `CZN` is used here
  as a sensible 3-letter WHEP abbreviation to avoid collision with the
  Czech Republic (CZE).
- **iso3 = PAN.** The Zone was geographically within Panama and Panama
  retained nominal sovereignty; `PAN` is the most accurate modern ISO
  assignment. There is no ISO 3166-1 alpha-3 code for the Zone itself.
- **cow = null.** The COW state system does not assign the Canal Zone
  a separate state number; it is treated as part of Panama (ccode 95).
- **end_year 1979.** The Canal Zone ceased to exist as a distinct
  administrative unit on 1 October 1979 under the Panama Canal Treaty.
  The US retained operational control of the canal itself until
  31 December 1999, but that residual presence does not constitute a
  distinct territorial entity for WHEP purposes.
- **type: colonial.** The Zone was a US-administered dependency with
  distinct governance structures; `colonial` best captures the functional
  status even though Panama retained nominal sovereignty.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-construction

A faithful polygon for the Canal Zone could be constructed from the
historical treaty description (16 km wide strip centred on the canal
centreline). The WHEP data directory does not currently contain a
digitised historical map of the Zone. If a high-quality GIS source
(e.g., historical USGS topographic data or a digitised State Department
boundary map) becomes available, the polygon_status should be updated to
`assigned` with an appropriate constructed or proxied feature.

### oq-1904-vs-1903-start

The Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty was signed November 1903 but US
administration effectively began in 1904 after Senate ratification.
If the WHEP convention for this period shifts to ratification date,
`start_year` should be updated to 1904.

### oq-1999-continuation

From 1 October 1979 to 31 December 1999, the US retained a "Canal Zone"
in the sense of operating the canal and maintaining military bases under
the Panama Canal Treaty. Whether this residual presence (1979–1999)
warrants a separate WHEP row for the canal operating area is open. The
current row ends at 1979 when the Zone's civilian administration
dissolved.
