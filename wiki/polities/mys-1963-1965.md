# Malaysia (1963-1965)

## Summary

Malaysia (1963-1965) covers the brief period when the Federation of Malaysia included Singapore. On 16 September 1963 the British colonies of Sabah, Sarawak, and Singapore joined the Federation of Malaya to form Malaysia, dramatically expanding the country's territory from approximately 107,112 km² (Malaya only) to approximately 267,887 km². Singapore was expelled from Malaysia on 9 August 1965, becoming an independent city-state; Malaysia's territory then shrank to approximately 267,444 km² (Singapore's small area subtracted). The two-year Malaysia-with-Singapore row is a distinct WHEP entry because it represents a real and documented territorial change at both ends.

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is CShapes 2.0. CShapes records Malaysia with COW code 820 throughout; the 1963-09-16 step shows a large area increase from ~107,112 km² (Malaya) to ~267,887 km² (Malaya + Sabah + Sarawak + Singapore). The 1965-08-09 step shows a minor reduction (~267,444 km²) when Singapore's territory is subtracted. [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)

The Federation includes two geographically separate landmasses: Peninsular Malaysia and the Borneo states (Sabah and Sarawak), separated by the South China Sea.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** [MYS-1957-1963](mys-1957-1963.md) — Federation of Malaya (Peninsular Malaysia only); Singapore, Sabah, Sarawak join on 16 September 1963
- **Successor:** [MYS-1965-2025](mys-1965-2025.md) — Singapore expelled 9 August 1965; Malaysia continues without Singapore

Note: Singapore (1965–2025) becomes an independent state at this transition. No separate Singapore WHEP row has been identified in the CSV for the 1963–1965 period (Singapore was administratively part of Malaysia). A gap may exist for Singapore 1963–1965.

## Sourced claims

- On 16 September 1963 Sabah and Sarawak joined the Independent Federation of Malaya, which became Malaysia. [biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia)
- The Indonesia–Malaysia boundary on Borneo separated "the Dutch Indonesian regions of East and West Kalimantan from the Malaysian states of Sarawak and Sabah." [biger-1995 §malaysia](../sources/biger-1995.md#malaysia)
- CShapes 2.0 records Malaysia (cowcode=820) with a polygon change on 1963-09-16, area increasing from ~107,112 km² to ~267,887 km², and a further change on 1965-08-09 reducing to ~267,444 km² (Singapore departure). [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)
- Singapore is described in Biger as an island state with no land boundaries that "separated from Malaysia in 1965." [biger-1995 §singapore](../sources/biger-1995.md#singapore)
- CShapes codes Malaysia as independent (owner=820) throughout this period, confirming state-system membership was continuous despite the territorial changes. [cshapes-2.0 §coding-states](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-states)

## Open questions

### oq-singapore-1963-1965

Singapore was part of Malaysia 1963–1965. The current WHEP CSV has no row for Singapore during this period — Singapore appears as a separate WHEP entry only from 1965. Whether Singapore's 1963–1965 trade statistics were recorded separately (and thus require a WHEP row) or are subsumed in the Malaysia row is unresolved. A future ingest should check FAOSTAT coverage for Singapore 1963–1965.

### oq-brunei-exclusion

Brunei was invited to join Malaysia but declined, retaining British protectorate status. Brunei's territory during 1963–1965 must be accounted for in either a separate Brunei WHEP row or in the British protectorate chain. This should be verified in a future ingest.
