# Lazio (region of Italy)

## Summary

Lazio is one of Italy's fifteen ordinary-statute regions (regioni a statuto ordinario), the NUTS-2 unit centred on Rome that also comprises the provinces of Latina, Frosinone, Rieti and Viterbo. This entry captures Lazio specifically as a regional administrative/statistical reporting unit, not as a claim to national sovereignty -- Italy's national polity (ITA-1919-2025 across this span) already carries sovereignty and the whole-country data. `type: subnational` reflects that this row exists purely to hold regionally-reported statistics, mirroring the treatment of Italy's other ordinary-statute regions being created in this same batch (e.g. ITA-EMR-1970-2025, ITA-MAR-1861-2025). The span starts in 1970 because that is when ordinary-statute regions, mandated by the 1948 Constitution but not actually implemented for over two decades, held their first regional council elections and became operative governmental and statistical entities; before that date, Lazio-labelled or Rome-region data has no distinct administrative referent apart from the national Italian state and belongs on the national chain instead. The end year is open (2025) because the region remains current.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Lazio as a distinct ordinary-statute Italian region for the period it has functioned as an operative administrative and statistical reporting unit (1970-2025), following the same batch logic applied to Italy's other NUTS-2 regions created alongside it (Emilia-Romagna, Marche, Veneto, and the historical macro-regions ITH1/ITH4/ITI1/ITI2). No specific ingested data rows were named in the routing decision under review for this unit beyond the general problem statement -- the decision documents the routing reasoning (unit_id ITA-ITI4 / admin_name ITI4, i.e. Lazio under its Eurostat NUTS code) and concludes matched_polity_code is null, meaning no existing polity in the table already represents Lazio. Previously, any Lazio-specific data would have had nowhere to route except the national ITA container, which over-attributes region-specific statistics to the whole country and is exactly the duplicate/over-broad-coverage problem the routing reasoning explicitly rejects (matching ITA-* for every one of Italy's twenty regions would make each region equally "claim" the same national container). What confirms Lazio is a distinct, real administrative entity rather than an arbitrary aggregation is Italy's own constitutional and electoral history: the 1948 Constitution provided for ordinary-statute regional government, implemented in practice only from 1970 with the first direct regional council elections, after which Lazio (like the other fourteen ordinary-statute regions) has had its own elected government, budget and, for many statistical series, its own reporting line separate from national totals.

**Territory description:** Lazio is the Italian region surrounding Rome, on the central-western (Tyrrhenian) coast of the Italian peninsula, bordered by Tuscany and Umbria to the north, Marche and Abruzzo to the east, Molise and Campania to the south, and the Tyrrhenian Sea to the west. It comprises the modern provinces of Roma Capitale, Latina, Frosinone, Rieti and Viterbo. Its present-day area is approximately **17,232 km²** (a commonly cited administrative figure for the region, not a figure measured from any polygon attached to this entry, since no polygon is yet attached). A reader can locate it on a modern map as the region containing Rome and the Alban Hills, running roughly from Lake Bolsena in the north to the Garigliano river and the border with Campania in the south.
