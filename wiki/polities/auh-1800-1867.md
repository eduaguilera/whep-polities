# Austrian Empire (to 1867)

## Summary

The current CSV tracks this row as the Austrian Empire from **1800**
to **1867** [database]. Both endpoints require hedging under
`[log decision-csv-is-evidence-not-authority]` and are flagged
in `[log proposal-auh-chain-audit]` (Findings 3 and 5).

- **Start year**: the CSV uses 1800 (the WHEP database floor), but
  the polity_name "Austrian Empire" is strictly valid only from
  **1804-08-11** onward, when Franz II proclaimed the Austrian
  Empire in response to Napoleon's French Empire declaration
  [wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11 §1804-08-11]. The 1800–1804
  window is pre-Empire and belongs to the Archduchy of Austria +
  associated Habsburg lands within the (still-existing) Holy
  Roman Empire. Biger says "no Austrian nation as such existed
  before the twentieth century" and describes the Austrian
  Empire as coming into its own "after the splitting of the
  Habsburg family estate among the Spanish and German branches
  in 1555" [biger-1995 §austria]. The 4-year label anachronism is
  filed as Finding 5 in the audit proposal.
- **End year**: the CSV uses 1867 as the split to AUH-1867-1908
  with the note "Dual monarchy established by Ausgleich 1867"
  [database]. The Ausgleich (30 March 1867, promulgated 28 July
  1867) was a **constitutional** change, not a territorial one
  [wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11 §1867]. Under the WHEP
  polity-definition rule [log decision-whep-polity-definition],
  splits trigger on substantial territorial change, not legal
  status. **This split is inconsistent with the rule as written**
  — see audit Finding 3.
- The **real** territorial events that shaped this row are the
  1859 loss of Lombardy and the 1866 loss of Venetia + exclusion
  from German affairs after Königgrätz. Under a strict reading
  of the WHEP rule, those (not 1867) would be the legitimate
  split candidates within the Austrian Empire period.

Predecessor: NA (pre-database floor).
Successor as currently in CSV: [auh-1867-1908].

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `Cliopatria (Seshat)` per the CSV, with the
  notes field specifying "Cliopatria polygon: Austrian Empire
  1815-1819" — a single time-step applied across 67 years
  [database]. Same single-time-step caveat as the Ottoman
  pre-1886 row and the Russian Empire pre-1886 rows
  [cliopatria-v0.1.3 §single-time-step]. The polygon represents
  the territorial configuration immediately after the 1815
  Congress of Vienna and before the 1859 Lombardy loss and the
  1866 Venetia loss — i.e. the empire at approximately its
  maximum 19th-century extent, applied across a period during
  which it actually lost ~90,000 km² in two separate wars.
- **Pre-1859 vs post-1866 extent.** Biger calls the
  mid-19th-century Austrian Empire "eight times the size of
  [modern Austria's] current territory, and ruling over Czechs,
  Slovaks, Hungarians, Croats, Serbs, Slovenes, Poles,
  Ruthenians, Jews, Bosnians, Romanians and many others"
  [biger-1995 §austria]. That scale includes the Italian
  territories (Lombardy until 1859, Venetia until 1866), so the
  "eight times" figure matches the mid-1850s extent, not the
  post-1866 extent.
- **No CShapes coverage pre-1886.** CShapes 2.0 starts in 1886
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope], so the Cliopatria polygon cannot be
  validated against CShapes for any part of this row.
- **Modern concordance.** COW code 300 [database]. No ISO3 for
  the Austrian Empire per se — AUH is a WHEP-internal code.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** NA — the WHEP database floor is 1800. The
  entity that existed immediately before the Austrian Empire
  was the Archduchy of Austria (under the Habsburg Monarchy)
  within the Holy Roman Empire, which itself dissolved in 1806.
- **Successor:** [auh-1867-1908] per the CSV, but see the audit
  proposal — under the strict WHEP rule the two rows should
  arguably be merged.

## Sourced claims

- **1804-08-11: Austrian Empire proclaimed** by Franz II in
  response to Napoleon's declaration of the French Empire
  [wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11 §1804-08-11]. Biger does
  not give an exact date; the 11 August 1804 date is from
  Wikipedia.
- **1806: Dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire.** Not directly
  cited in the current ingest; a future source (Biger p.47 only
  says "the splitting of the Habsburg family estate among the
  Spanish and German branches in 1555" as the framing for the
  Austrian Empire's origin). The 1806 date is standard
  historical fact; flagged as [oq-1806-hre-dissolution] below.
- **1815: Congress of Vienna territorial settlement.** Set the
  maximum 19th-century extent of the Austrian Empire. The
  Cliopatria polygon used by WHEP for this row is specifically
  labeled "1815–1819" per the CSV notes field, meaning it
  represents this configuration [database].
- **1848–1849: Revolutions and the Hungarian war of
  independence.** Hungary declared independence briefly in 1849;
  Austrian forces with Russian assistance reconquered the
  territory. Not a permanent territorial change under WHEP's
  rule, but worth noting for trade-data continuity. Flagged as
  [oq-1848-revolutions] below.
- **1859: Loss of Lombardy** in the Second Italian War of
  Independence. The Treaty of Zurich (10 November 1859) ceded
  Lombardy to France, which then transferred it to Sardinia.
  Substantial territorial loss within the row's window — one of
  the candidate mid-row split points under a strict WHEP rule
  reading [wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11 §1859].
- **1861: Italian unification** as the Kingdom of Italy. Biger
  cites "Italy acceded and became an independent country in
  1861" in the AUSTRIA–ITALY subsection [biger-1995 §austria].
  This is the Italian counterpart to the 1859 Lombardy event;
  Lombardy joined the new Italian state.
- **1866 — the real turning point.** The Austro-Prussian War
  ("six-week war") ended with the Peace of Prague. Austria was
  "defeat[ed] at Hradec Králové (Königgrätz) in 1866, ending the
  six-week Austro-Prussian war over the control of Germany"
  [biger-1995 §austria]. Consequences:
  - Austria excluded from German affairs; the "North German
    Confederation of German states under Prussian leadership"
    was formed without Austria [biger-1995 §austria].
  - Venetia ceded to the Kingdom of Italy by the Vienna Peace
    Treaty of **3 October 1866** [biger-1995 §austria] — another
    substantial territorial loss within the row.
  - Internal Hungarian pressure for autonomy leading directly
    to the 1867 Ausgleich.
- **1867-03-30: Austro-Hungarian Compromise (Ausgleich).** "On
  28 July, Franz Joseph, in his new capacity as King of Hungary,
  approved and promulgated the new laws, which officially gave
  birth to the Dual Monarchy"
  [wikipedia-austria-hungary-2026-04-11 §1867]. Biger
  corroborates: "In 1867 the Habsburg Empire became the dual
  monarchy of Austria-Hungary" [biger-1995 §austria]. This is
  the CSV's end-of-row event. **Note under critical-stance**:
  the Ausgleich was a constitutional reorganization, not a
  territorial change. No territory was added or lost by the
  Ausgleich itself. The CSV's use of 1867 as a split trigger is
  inconsistent with the WHEP polity-definition rule
  [log decision-whep-polity-definition]. See
  [log proposal-auh-chain-audit] Finding 3.
- **COW state-system membership.** COW lists Austria-Hungary
  (`AUH`, `ccode=300`) as a single continuous tenure from
  **1816-01-01** to **1918-11-12** [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates].
  No split at 1867 (Ausgleich), 1908 (Bosnia annexation), or any
  other date within the WHEP AUH chain. The COW end date
  1918-11-12 is one day after the Armistice of 11 November 1918.
  Successor states in COW: Austria (`ccode=305`, 1919-09-10 to
  1938-03-13, then 1955-07-27 onward) and Hungary (`ccode=310`,
  1918-11-16 onward). See [log autonomous-2-cow-auh-dates].

## Open questions

### oq-1806-hre-dissolution

The Holy Roman Empire dissolved on 6 August 1806 after the
Napoleonic victories. Franz II had already proclaimed himself
Franz I of Austria in 1804 in anticipation. For the period
1804–1806, the Austrian Empire and the Holy Roman Empire
coexisted; after 1806 the Austrian Empire was fully
independent. WHEP's AUH-1800-1867 row does not reflect this
double-attribution in 1804–1806. Minor but worth documenting.
A future source (Biger has limited coverage) or a brief
Wikipedia fetch would resolve it.

### oq-1848-revolutions

The 1848 revolutions briefly split Hungary from the Austrian
Empire (declaration of independence, April 1849) before the
Russian-assisted Austrian reconquest restored the status quo
ante. Temporary territorial / political change with no
lasting effect on the row's borders. Should be mentioned as a
sourced claim once a source with Biger-precision dates is
added. Biger treats 1848 as part of the continuous Habsburg
period and does not provide specific territorial claims.

### oq-pre-1859-polygon

The Cliopatria polygon used for this row is labeled
"Austrian Empire 1815-1819" per the CSV notes. It represents
the post-Vienna, pre-Lombardy-loss extent. Any analysis that
uses this row's polygon for the period 1859–1867 will
overstate the Austrian Empire's actual territory by Lombardy
(1859) and Venetia (1866). A future ingest should either
split the row at 1866, apply a different polygon for
1866–1867, or document the overstatement as a known
limitation.

### oq-cow-auh-dates

**Resolved 2026-04-12** by [log autonomous-2-cow-auh-dates].
COW `statelist2024.csv` has a single row for `AUH, ccode=300`:
1816-01-01 to 1918-11-12. No splits at 1867, 1908, or any
other date. Successor entries: Austria `ccode=305` (1919-09-10
to 1938-03-13; 1955-07-27 onward), Hungary `ccode=310`
(1918-11-16 onward).

### oq-1859-lombardy-exact-date

The Treaty of Zurich (10 November 1859) ended the Second
Italian War of Independence. The handover of Lombardy to
Sardinia was procedural — Austria ceded it to France, which
then transferred it to Sardinia. Exact date for the transfer
to Sardinia (and subsequently to the unified Kingdom of Italy
in 1861) not yet pinned to a verbatim quote.
