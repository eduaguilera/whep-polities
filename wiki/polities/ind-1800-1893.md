## Former title: India (to 1893)

## Summary

CSV row for India from **1800** to **1893** — a **94-year row**
covering British India from the database floor through East India
Company rule and Crown rule (from 1858)
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). COW code 750
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

The 1893 end-of-row event likely relates to the **Durand Line**
(12 November 1893), the boundary agreement between British India
and Afghanistan — a real territorial-delimitation event.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon binding.** This row is pinned to the CShapes 2.0 COW-based
feature for `cowcode=750`, timestep `1886-01-01 → 1893-11-11`
[cshapes-2.0 §which-version-is-on-disk-2026-04-11](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#which-version-is-on-disk-2026-04-11).
Measured directly from `data/geodata/cshapes2_full.gpkg` in the
equal-area CRS `ESRI:54034`, that feature is **4,652,712 km²**
(the CSV's own `polygon_area_km2` field for this row is empty, but
the number is reproducible from the source geometry). The very next
CShapes timestep, `1893-11-12 → 1898-12-31`, is **4,819,795 km²** —
a jump of **+167,083 km²** exactly at the row's end date
[cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes).
This confirms the 1893 cut is not arbitrary: it lands on a real,
CShapes-recorded boundary change, consistent with the Durand Line
agreement of 12 November 1893 between British India and Afghanistan
that this page's Summary already flags. The WHEP split at 1893
is correctly placed relative to that specific change.

**Coverage gap before 1886.** CShapes only maps borders back to 1886
[cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope), so the
polygon assigned to this row (a single static shape used for the
full 1800–1893 span) is a backdated copy of the 1886 shape, not
independent evidence about 1800–1885
[cshapes-2.0 §geocoding](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#geocoding). That
matters a great deal here, because British India's actual territorial
extent changed enormously over 1800–1885 through a long sequence of
wars and annexations (Second and Third Anglo-Maratha Wars 1803–1818;
Anglo-Nepalese War and the 1816 Sugauli cession; the 1826 Treaty of
Yandabo ceding Arakan, Manipur, Assam and Tenasserim from Konbaung
Burma
[wikipedia-europe-asia-gaps-2026-04-15 §what-it-adds](../sources/wikipedia-europe-asia-gaps-2026-04-15.md#what-it-adds);
the 1843 conquest of Sindh; the 1845–49 Anglo-Sikh Wars and annexation
of Punjab; the 1852 annexation of Lower Burma; the 1856 annexation of
Awadh; and the 1885–86 Third Anglo-Burmese War annexing Upper Burma,
which lands right at the CShapes 1886 start date). This is **uncited
general history** beyond the single Burma-cession claim that has an
ingested source — it is not backed by a WHEP-ingested polygon dataset
for the pre-1886 window, and it should not be read as CShapes evidence.
See [oq-pre-1886-territorial-changes](#oq-pre-1886-territorial-changes).

**State-system status, not territorial status.** COW does not list
India (`ccode=750`) as a state-system member until **1947-08-15**
[cow-state-system-v2024 §schema](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#schema),
consistent with the pre-1920 criterion of "diplomatic recognition by
Britain or France" plus a population threshold
[cow-state-system-v2024 §inclusion](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#inclusion).
This is expected and not a contradiction: COW's membership dates
track legal sovereignty, not territory, and this row is a WHEP
territorial-economic unit (British India as a colonial administrative
unit with its own trade/production statistics), which is exactly the
distinction the wiki's polity-definition rule draws between
territorial-extent sources and state-system-membership sources.

## A split IS feasible — Cliopatria has the features (2026-07-24)

This page records that the row covers the entire British conquest of India on a
single polygon because CShapes has no intermediate features. That is true of
CShapes but **not of the polygon priority stack as a whole**: **Cliopatria
v0.1.3**, already fetched under `data/geodata/cliopatria/`, carries **British
Raj** features at 3-5 year resolution through this span.

Measured from that source (equal-area, ESRI:54034):

| Cliopatria period | area |
|---|---|
| 1873-1876 | 4,188,571 km² |
| 1877-1879 | 4,188,399 km² |
| 1880-1884 | 4,209,917 km² |
| **1885-1889** | **4,816,534 km²** |
| 1890-1894 | 4,823,859 km² |

The step between 1880-1884 and 1885-1889 is **+606,617 km²** — the annexation of
**Upper Burma** (Third Anglo-Burmese War, 1885). So the single largest territorial
change inside this row's span is directly observable in an already-available
source.

This supersedes the assumption that no split was possible. Doing it properly is a
project rather than an edit: Cliopatria's periodisation would generate several
sub-rows, each needing its own page and polygon binding, and the earlier
expansions (Anglo-Maratha 1803-1818, Sugauli 1816, Yandabo 1826, Sindh 1843,
Anglo-Sikh 1845-1849) should be checked against Cliopatria's pre-1873 features
before choosing split years. Recorded for a decision on scope.

### Split at 1886 — the sources cover different periods, not the same event

Investigated 2026-07-24. An earlier version of this section claimed CShapes and
Cliopatria disagreed 3.6× about the Burma annexation. **That was an error** — it
compared two different events. Corrected:

- **CShapes 2.0's India coverage begins `1886-01-01`.** Its earliest feature
  (1886-01-01 → 1893-11-11, 4,652,712 km²) therefore *already includes* Upper
  Burma, annexed 1 January 1886.
- **CShapes' step at `1893-11-12`** (+167,089 km², to 4,819,795) is the **Durand
  Line** agreement of 12 November 1893 — the date matches exactly. Not Burma.
- **Cliopatria's step at 1885** (+606,617 km², 4,209,917 → 4,816,534) is **Upper
  Burma**.

So the two sources describe **two separate real events**, and they are broadly
consistent where they overlap: Cliopatria's 1885-89 figure (4,816,534 km²) sits
within 3.5% of CShapes' 1886-93 figure (4,652,712 km²).

**The actual problem with this row is different and simpler: CShapes has no
pre-1886 India at all**, so this row's polygon (CShapes @1886) includes Upper
Burma for the whole 1800-1893 span — overstating British India before 1886 by
roughly 440,000 km², and by far more before the mid-century conquests.

**Recommended split at 1886**, since that is where a real territorial change and
a source boundary coincide:

| row | span | source | area |
|---|---|---|---|
| IND-1800-1886 | 1800-1886 | **Cliopatria** "British Raj" @1880 | 4,209,917 km² |
| IND-1886-1893 | 1886-1893 | CShapes 750 @1890 | 4,652,712 km² |
| [ind-1893-1914](ind-1893-1914.md) | unchanged | CShapes 750 @1899 | 4,819,795 km² |

Using Cliopatria for the earlier row is **not** the source-mixing hazard flagged
before: CShapes simply does not cover those years, which is precisely what the
polygon priority stack exists to handle. The 1886 junction is a genuine event
(Upper Burma), not an artefact.

**Residual approximation, unavoidable:** Cliopatria's British Raj series itself
begins in **1859**, with no East India Company feature before that. So 1800-1859
remains a back-projection in any arrangement, and the conquest-era expansions
inside it (Anglo-Maratha 1803-1818, Sugauli 1816, Yandabo 1826, Sindh 1843,
Anglo-Sikh 1845-1849) cannot be split on either source.
