# Kansas (state of the United States)

## Summary

Kansas, one of the fifty states of the United States, admitted to the Union on January 29, 1861, as the 34th state, after a violent decade of contested territorial status ("Bleeding Kansas") during which pro-slavery and free-state factions fought for control before it entered as a free state. This entry is `type: subnational` for the same reason as the other U.S. state pages in this batch (e.g. Georgia, Alabama): Kansas has never been an independently sovereign reporting unit, and the row exists to carry state-level statistics that the national USA rows cannot represent at the correct spatial resolution. Unlike the original-13 states, Kansas's start_year is not adjusted down to fit an early national container row -- its 1861 admission date falls comfortably within the existing USA-1848-1867 national row's span, so no floor adjustment or pre-container gap is needed, in contrast to the Georgia/Connecticut/Delaware 1788-to-1800 problem.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision, the source is `juan-subnational` U.S. state-level agricultural statistics (the same USDA NASS-style dataset used across this batch of state pages), with the routing decision itself flagging that the data extract used starts in 1866, five years after the proposed 1861 start. No exact row count or full year range accompanies the routing object, which is recorded as an open question below, consistent with how the Georgia sibling page handled the same gap. Previously, any Kansas-level data would have had no distinct polity row and risked being matched to the national USA chain rows instead, which represent the entire country rather than a single state -- a resolution mismatch of the same kind documented in the Manchuria exemplar. What confirms Kansas is a distinct entity worth its own row is its status as a formally admitted U.S. state since January 29, 1861, a status the country convention's default admission-year start rule already recognizes for subnational U.S. state entries generally; the routing decision explicitly notes Kansas is not one of the three departure cases (Alaska, Hawaii, or the generic post-original-13 group) and so takes the default rule.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Copied from GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.17_1 (Kansas), assigned directly rather than via proxy -- GADM's present-day administrative boundary for the state is used across the full 1861-2025 span. Kansas's external state boundary has been fixed at admission (aside from minor surveyed-line corrections along the Missouri River with Missouri and Nebraska, which have not altered its overall extent materially), so vintage risk from using a present-day polygon over a 164-year span is low, the same reasoning applied to Kansas in the routing decision's `polygon_reasoning` and to sibling state Alaska (ALK-1867-1959, also GADM-sourced). `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Why this entry exists:** No existing polity row represented the U.S. state of Kansas; this is a routing gap of the same kind documented above, not a duplicate of any other row in the database. Kansas's status as a state distinct from the federal union, and from the antebellum Kansas Territory that preceded it, is uncontroversial and confirmed by its formal admission to the Union on January 29, 1861 -- it does not depend on a specialist historical reference the way a contested foreign territory would.

**Territory description:** The area covered is the modern U.S. state of Kansas, in the central United States (Great Plains), bordered by Nebraska to the north, Missouri to the east, Oklahoma to the south, and Colorado to the west. A reader can locate it on any modern map as the rectangular-ish state containing Wichita and Topeka. Its area is approximately 213,100 km2 (the modern state's commonly cited land+water area, per standard U.S. Census Bureau figures); no independent geometry measurement was computed for this response, so this figure is a reference value, not a measurement of the attached polygon.

## Predecessors and successors

Kansas has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database. The pre-statehood Kansas Territory (1854-1861), during which the "Bleeding Kansas" conflict over slavery played out, is not modeled as a distinct predecessor polity here -- it is treated, like the equivalent territorial-to-statehood transitions for other states in this batch, as outside this row's span rather than as a separate entry, since no data source currently routes territorial-era Kansas statistics to this database. Kansas has undergone no subsequent partition or merger since admission.

## Decisions

### d-no-start-year-floor-needed

**Start year kept at 1861 admission date, no containment floor adjustment required**

Unlike the original-13 state pages in this batch (Georgia, Connecticut, Delaware), which had to have their start_year floored to 1800 because no national USA container row exists before 1800, Kansas's actual admission date of January 29, 1861 already falls inside the existing USA-1848-1867 national row's 1848-1867 span. No adjustment away from the historically correct admission year is needed, and no pre-container gap is created. This is the routing decision's proposed start_year (1861) applied without modification, which is why the code, unlike Georgia's, does not depart from the country convention's default admission-year rule at all.

### d-three-container-edges

**Three container edges tile 1861-2025, one fewer than the five-edge original-13 pattern**

Because Kansas's span starts in 1861 rather than 1800, it only touches three of the five national USA rows in the polity table: USA-1848-1867 (trimmed to 1861-1867, since the row itself only covers up to 1867 and Kansas only exists as a state from 1861), USA-1867-1959, and USA-1959-2025. These three edges tile the full 1861-2025 range with no gap, following the same tiling principle as the five-edge original-13 state pages but with two fewer edges since Kansas's span is shorter.

### oq-boundary-stability-since-1861

**Has Kansas's external boundary been fully stable since 1861, including the Missouri River boundary disputes with Missouri and Nebraska?**

The polygon assigned (GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.17_1) captures Kansas's present-day boundary, roughly 213,100 km2 by commonly cited convention (not independently re-measured here). Kansas's eastern boundary along the meandering Missouri River has been subject to minor 19th- and 20th-century avulsion-driven adjustments and interstate boundary litigation with Missouri, and its northern boundary with Nebraska was also subject to early survey disputes. Whether any of these adjustments materially changed the state's area, or whether they only affected a negligible strip of land, has not been checked against a historical atlas or the relevant boundary-commission records.
