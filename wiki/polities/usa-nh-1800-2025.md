# New Hampshire (state of the United States)

## Summary

New Hampshire, one of the fifty states of the United States and one of the original 13 states that ratified the federal Constitution, doing so on June 21, 1788 as the pivotal 9th state that made the Constitution operative. This entry is `type: subnational` because New Hampshire has never been independently sovereign under the U.S. federal system covered by this database's national USA chain: the row exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory for statistics tabulated at the state rather than the national level, and must not compete with the national USA rows for sovereignty ranking in the matcher. Although New Hampshire's own administrative history as a distinct polity reaches back to 1788 (and, as a self-governing colony under its own provincial charter, well before that, to 1679), this entry's start_year is set to 1800 rather than 1788 because the earliest national USA container row in this database (USA-1800-1803) does not extend back to 1788 -- see the open question and decision below for why the gap could not simply be bridged. The span 1800-2025 then tracks continuous statehood, with no territorial change, through five successive eras of the national USA polity chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures New Hampshire as a distinct subnational reporting unit within the United States, created by the agent-harness pipeline processing the administrative unit `USA-NEWHAMPSHIRE` ("New Hampshire", country United States of America). No prior polity row existed for New Hampshire at the state level -- the existing USA chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) covers the country as a whole, so any New Hampshire-specific statistics previously had no state-level home and would either be absent or folded into national USA totals, a mismatch whenever a source reports New Hampshire separately from all-U.S. figures. The routing decision frames this as adding the state as a real, distinct administrative entity with no existing candidate polity (no name candidates matched, and the only USA-iso3 candidates were national-era rows or Alaska's territory row, none of which is New Hampshire), consistent with a systematic pass creating one row per U.S. state that also produced usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co, usa-ct, and siblings in this same run. What confirms New Hampshire is a distinct reporting/administrative entity is uncontroversial: it is one of the fifty states, one of the original 13 that ratified the Constitution, with its own government since 1679 as a royal province, and long-standing use as a state-level reporting unit in economic, agricultural, and demographic series (survey/census-observed data, per the routing decision).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `gadm-4.1-adm1` source, feature `USA.30_1`, assigned directly (not a proxy) because this is a 1:1 match between the administrative unit and a GADM admin-1 feature already registered and locally present in this database's GeoPackage. `polygon_status: assigned`.

**Area:** measured from the attached geometry at approximately 24,214 km2 (New Hampshire's commonly cited land+water area is about 24,214 km2 / 9,349 sq mi). This is a measurement of the attached polygon, consistent with New Hampshire's real-world extent, not an independent territorial claim.

**Territory description:** New Hampshire occupies a wedge of northern New England, bordered by Vermont to the west (along the Connecticut River), Massachusetts to the south, Maine and a short Atlantic coastline (about 18 miles) to the east, and the Canadian province of Quebec to the north -- readily locatable on any modern map as the small state directly north of Massachusetts and east of Vermont. Its external boundaries were effectively settled well before this entry's 1800 start year: the Massachusetts-New Hampshire line was fixed by royal decree in 1740, and the disputed northern boundary with Quebec/Lower Canada was not fully resolved until the 1842 Webster-Ashburton Treaty, after which the modern GADM boundary is treated as valid for the remainder of the 1800-2025 span (a minor caveat noted here rather than as a separate open question, since the affected area is a small northern strip and the treaty postdates this entry's start by only 42 years).

## Predecessors and successors

New Hampshire has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database. It was a self-governing English colony (with its own provincial charter from 1679) well before U.S. independence, ratified the federal Constitution on June 21, 1788 as the 9th state -- the ratification that made the Constitution operative among the ratifying states -- and its span here runs open/current through 2025 with no interruption or successor. No colonial-era New Hampshire entry exists in this database, so there is no predecessor edge to record; if one is added later, this page's open question on the 1788-1800 gap becomes directly relevant to it as well.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

New Hampshire is a standard U.S. state with no distinct historical name of its own separate from statehood (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad), so it follows the pattern used by 57 of 66 subnational rows: ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end. The subunit token chosen is NH, the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US code for New Hampshire, matching the two-letter convention already used for the other U.S. states added in this harness run (usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co, usa-ct, and others). The full code is USA-NH-1800-2025.

### d-start-year-1800-not-1788

**Start year set to 1800, not New Hampshire's 1788 ratification, because no earlier national container exists**

The routing decision's proposed start_year is 1788, when New Hampshire became the 9th state to ratify the U.S. Constitution, making it operative for the ratifying states. That date is administratively correct, but the earliest national USA polity row in this database is USA-1800-1803 -- there is no container row covering 1788-1800, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's span, so a container edge cannot be manufactured to plug that gap. Rather than leave 1788-1800 uncontainered, this entry's start_year is set to 1800, the earliest year the national chain can actually contain it, exactly as was done for USA-CT-1800-2025. This departs from the country convention's implied original-13 rule (ratification date) and is flagged as an open question below: the correct fix is likely a new pre-1800 national USA row, not a permanent 1800 floor on every original-13 state.

## Open questions

### oq-pre1800-original13-gap

**1788-1800 gap for New Hampshire and other original-13 states is unresolved by any container row**

New Hampshire's own administrative history as a state runs from June 21, 1788 (Constitution ratification, 9th state) forward, but the earliest national USA container row in this database begins in 1800. This entry's start_year is set to 1800 rather than 1788 solely because no container exists for the intervening years, the same workaround already applied to USA-CT-1800-2025 and presumably to other original-13 states created in this same harness run. If a pre-1800 national USA row is ever added (e.g. USA-1788-1800 or USA-<earlier>-1800), New Hampshire's start_year, along with every other original-13 state's start_year, should be revisited and likely moved back to 1788 (or each state's individual ratification date) rather than left pinned to 1800 indefinitely.
