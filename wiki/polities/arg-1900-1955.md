# Formosa (national territory of Argentina)

## Summary

Formosa, in Argentina's northeastern Chaco region bordering Paraguay along the Pilcomayo and Paraguay rivers, existed as a Territorio Nacional (a directly Buenos Aires-governed national territory rather than a self-governing province) from its creation under Ley 1532 in 1884 until Ley 14408 elevated it to full provincial status in 1955. This entry covers 1900-1955: the start year reflects the data floor of the routed statistical series rather than the territory's 1884 legal creation date, and the end year is the 1955 provincialization. type is subnational and iso3 is ARG because Argentina held sovereignty over this territory throughout; the national ARG chain (ARG-1899-1902, ARG-1902-2025) already carries that sovereignty claim. What this entry exists to carry instead is the reporting/administrative unit "Formosa," which is exactly the class of unit Argentina's country convention's departures list (territorios nacionales including Chaco, Formosa, La Pampa, Misiones, Neuquén, Río Negro) singles out as requiring its own polity distinct from the modern-province-shaped rows used for original 1853 provinces like Buenos Aires. This entry follows the same <ISO3>-<start>-<end> code pattern already used for the structurally identical ARG-CHACO entry (ARG-1884-1951), for the same reason: appending a subunit tag here would wrongly suggest continuity with a not-yet-created post-1955 province-era code.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: per the routing decision (unit_id ARG-FORMOSA), Argentine admin-1 data under the label "Formosa" from the country-convention pass on Argentine provinces was routed here; the row/year count is not itself restated in the decision payload, but the decision establishes the series covers 1900 onward within the territorio nacional period and follows the identical pattern to the sibling ARG-CHACO verdict, which used the juan-subnational source. This entry was previously implicitly matched (by the country convention's default provincial handling) to the national ARG chain (ARG-1899-1902 / ARG-1902-2025), which is wrong because those rows represent the whole of Argentina — orders of magnitude larger than Formosa's roughly 72,000 km2 provincial extent — the same category of scope mismatch the routing decision explicitly names for this exact case. What confirms Formosa was administratively distinct in this period: Ley 1532 (1884) is a primary legislative source establishing the Territorio Nacional de Formosa as a named, separately-governed national territory distinct from Argentina's provinces and from neighboring national territories, and Ley 14408 (1955) is the primary source confirming its status changed at the far end of this span, exactly mirroring the Chaco case (Ley 1532 / Ley 14037) already established in arg-1884-1951.md.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The correct source class is an Argentine first-level administrative division (province) boundary, which gadm-4.1-adm1 digitises globally, but the locally registered extract (data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg) is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Argentina at all (0 ARG features confirmed) — so despite gadm-4.1-adm1 being a registered slug, it carries no fetched content for this country, and cannot be declared as this entry's polygon_source without being rejected by validate_declared_sources. No existing ARG polygon can substitute either: cshapes-2.0 is country-level only (no internal province boundaries), and cliopatria/paine-2024/histogis-1860-habsburg/constructed have no existing Argentine sub-national features covering Formosa.

**Territory description:** Formosa is Argentina's northernmost mainland province, located in the northeast Gran Chaco lowlands, bounded by the Pilcomayo River (Paraguay/Bolivia border) to the north and the Paraguay River (Paraguay border) to the east, with Salta and Chaco provinces to the west and south. Its modern provincial area is approximately 72,066 km2 — the reader can locate it on a modern map as Argentina's province directly south of Paraguay's Pilcomayo river frontier. No polygon is attached to this entry, so no area is measured here; the 72,066 km2 figure is the modern province's stated area from general geographic reference, offered only to help a reader locate the territory, not as a claim about this entry's own historical boundary, which may have differed from the modern province during the 1900-1955 territorio nacional period (see open question on polygon proxy risk).

## Sourced claims

- Ley 1532 (1884) established the Territorio Nacional de Formosa as a directly-governed national territory of Argentina, distinct from the surrounding provinces, per the routing decision's routing_reasoning field which cites this law by number and treats the territory as administratively distinct from at least 1884.
- Ley 14408 (1955) elevated the Territorio Nacional de Formosa to provincial status as Formosa Province, ending the national-territory administrative regime this entry captures; per the routing decision's routing_reasoning field, which names this law as the event ending the territorio nacional stage in exactly the same way Ley 14037 (1951) ended Chaco's.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-arg-1900-1955

**Used the <ISO3>-<start>-<end> code pattern, not <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>**

Of ARG's existing subnational rows, ARG-CHACO's own page (arg-1884-1951.md) already established that a bounded historical administrative era of a territorio nacional — as opposed to a modern, ISO3-stable provincial subunit — takes the <ISO3>-<start>-<end> pattern instead of appending a SUBUNIT tag, because appending FORMOSA to a code that only covers the pre-province era would misleadingly suggest the tag denotes the same entity as the eventual post-1955 province code, when the two are different legal statuses of the same territory that will get two different polity rows. This entry follows that same precedent for exactly the same reason: Formosa's post-1955 provincial era is a separate, not-yet-created polity, and this row is deliberately only the 1900-1955 territorio nacional segment. The start year is 1900 rather than the 1884 creation date of the Territorio Nacional (Ley 1532) because 1900 is stated in the routing decision as the data floor within this administrative period, not the territory's legal creation date — the decision is explicit that the entity itself predates this row's start.

### d-polygon-none-not-unfetched-source

**polygon_source is none, not gadm-4.1-adm1, despite the routing decision naming that slug**

The routing decision's polygon_route is registered_source_unfetched and names gadm-4.1-adm1 as the source that would need fetching. But polygon_detail is explicit that the local extract data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg is a curated subset covering only 81 countries and confirms 0 total features for ARG — meaning gadm-4.1-adm1 is not actually registered with Argentine content in this repository's data, only in GADM's upstream global release. Per this prompt's own instruction, registered_source_unfetched only applies when the slug is registered and just not fetched here, with polygon_status: unassigned; since no ARG features exist under that slug in this repo at all, treating it as registered-but-unfetched would be recording a source this repository does not currently carry. I therefore set polygon_source: none and polygon_status: unassigned, and record the needed source (Argentina added to the local gadm-4.1-adm1 adm1 extract) as an open question rather than a declared source, consistent with the instruction that new_source_needed / registered_source_unfetched slugs get rejected by validate_declared_sources and the wanted source belongs in an open question instead.

## Open questions

### oq-formosa-creation-date-unconfirmed

**1884 creation date of the Territorio Nacional de Formosa is inferred, not sourced here**

The routing decision's routing_concerns explicitly flags that the exact creation date of the Territorio Nacional de Formosa under Ley 1532 (1884) is not itself in evidence in the decision payload, only inferred by analogy from the country convention's departures list (the same Ley 1532 that created Chaco, per arg-1884-1951.md, is a general national-territories law covering multiple named territories including Formosa). This page's start_year is 1900 (the data floor) rather than 1884, so the discrepancy does not affect this row's own span, but a primary legislative source (the text of Ley 1532 itself, or a secondary administrative history of Formosa specifically) should be checked before any future page uses 1884 as Formosa's own creation date.

### oq-formosa-polygon-source-not-registered

**No polygon can be assigned until Argentina is added to a registered adm1 source**

gadm-4.1-adm1 is the correct source class (first-level administrative division) for Formosa, but the local extract at data/geodata/gadm-4.1/gadm41_adm1.gpkg is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Argentina (0 features confirmed). No union or difference of already-registered ARG features can substitute: cshapes-2.0 only carries country-level Argentina, and cliopatria/paine-2024/histogis-1860-habsburg/constructed have no existing ARG sub-national pieces covering Formosa specifically. Until Argentina's provinces are fetched into gadm-4.1-adm1 (or another adm1-resolution source is registered for Argentina), this entry and its sibling ARG territorio-nacional rows must remain polygon_status: unassigned. A further open question, once a polygon is available: current Formosa provincial boundaries (settled largely by the 1955 provincialization) are being proposed here as a likely future proxy for the pre-1955 territorio nacional era, but that proxy decision has not been made or validated yet.
