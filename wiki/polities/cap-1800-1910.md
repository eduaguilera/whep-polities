# Cape Colony (to 1910)

## Summary

The British Cape Colony as a separate statistical / territorial unit from
the permanent British takeover in 1806 (Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1814 confirming
occupation) to the Union of South Africa on 31 May 1910, when Cape Colony
became the Cape Province within the new union. Reported in pre-1961
agricultural data variously as "Cape Province" or "Cape of Good Hope";
agricultural statistics were compiled separately from the other British South
African territories until union.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `cshapes-2.0` gwcode 561, time-step 1886-1895, area 600,802 km².
CShapes records two time-steps for the Cape Colony:

| Range | Area | Territory |
|---|---:|---|
| 1886-1895 | 600,802 km² | Core Cape Colony + Griqualand West (annexed 1880) |
| 1895-1910 | 716,102 km² | After annexation of British Bechuanaland (1895) |

The 1886-1895 polygon is used as the primary binding because it represents
the larger fraction of the 1852-1909 data window. Cliopatria also records
"British Cape Colony" with many time-steps 1796-1884, which could provide a
more accurate pre-1886 polygon if needed — current binding uses CShapes as
a stable proxy for the entire pre-union period.

**Extent approximation — polygon overstates colony for data years before ca. 1880:**
The CShapes 1886-1895 polygon (600,802 km²) already incorporates Griqualand
West, which was annexed in 1880, and reflects Transkei consolidation from the
mid-1880s. For data years prior to ca. 1880 (i.e. roughly 1854-1879 in the
source data), the polygon overstates the colony's actual extent. Key
territorial additions that the single polygon cannot reflect:

| Annexation | Year | Territory added | Approximate area |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Griqualand West | 1880 | Diamond-fields region around Kimberley | ~85,000 km² |
| British Bechuanaland | 1895 | Trans-Orange region | ~115,300 km² |

Data years 1854-1879 are therefore matched to a polygon that overstates
extent by roughly 85,000 km² (Griqualand West not yet annexed). Data years
1895-1909 should ideally use the larger 716,102 km² CShapes time-step; the
current binding uses the 1886-1895 step as a conservative proxy.

**Future split candidates:** If granular period-specific polygons become
available, the following year boundaries are recommended as split points:

- **1865** — approximate mid-point before Griqualand West; separates the
  earliest data from the pre-annexation colony extent.
- **1880** — Griqualand West incorporated; colony area expands materially.
- **1895** — British Bechuanaland annexed; colony reaches its maximum
  pre-union extent (716,102 km²).

Until such polygons are available, all data rows are matched to CAP-1800-1910
with the 1886-1895 CShapes polygon as an acknowledged approximation. This
approximation is flagged as `extent_approximation` in the issue record.

**Why this entry exists:** The pre-1961 input data reports "Cape Province" /
"Cape of Good Hope" for 195 rows spanning 1854-1898. Without this row the
matcher's `name_override` rules — which already map both strings to code
`CAP` — had nothing to resolve against.

## Data routing

### dec-south-africa-label-1852-1909

Data labelled **'south africa'** in Mitchell and IIA sources for years **1852–1909** (218 rows) is routed to CAP-1800-1910.

**Basis:** Cape Colony was the dominant British territorial entity in southern Africa throughout the nineteenth century and was routinely described as "South Africa" in European statistical publications before the Union. The Orange Free State (OFS) and the South African Republic (Transvaal) do not have WHEP polities covering this period, so no finer routing is available.

**Approximation risk:** If any source aggregate included OFS or Transvaal figures alongside Cape Colony figures under the label 'south africa', routing to CAP-1800-1910 (~600,802 km²) would understate the true territorial scope. This risk is accepted given the absence of the relevant granular polities.

**Alias added:** `south africa` 1852–1909 → CAP-1800-1910 in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv` (2026-07-02).

**Approximation risk resolved in favour of this routing (2026-07-24, assertion
verification).** The magnitudes rule out an OFS/Transvaal aggregate: this
source's median flock is 13,432,000 sheep and 4,939,000 goats, while the 1904
Cape Colony census independently recorded ~11.8M sheep (8,465,000 woolled +
3,353,000 other) — Cape-alone scale. A combined-provinces total would be far
larger; the Union's four provinces together held on the order of 25-30M sheep by
1911. So the label reports Cape Colony's own agricultural returns, not an
aggregate.

**But the assertion is banked `best_available`, not `verified_equal`,** because
the Cape's territory did not stay constant across 1852-1909: it annexed British
Kaffraria, Griqualand West (1880), the Transkeian territories (1885-1894) and
British Bechuanaland (1895). No single polygon vintage can equal the reporting
territory across the whole span, so the correct long-term fix is splitting this
row at the annexation years (per the WHEP territorial-change rule) — see
[oq-cape-expansion-splits](#oq-cape-expansion-splits).

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1886-territory

Cape Colony was smaller pre-1886 (before the 1885-1890 expansion into the
Transkei) and pre-1880 (before Griqualand West annexation). Using the
1886-1895 CShapes polygon as a proxy over-represents territory for 1854-1880
data by roughly 85,000 km² (Griqualand West). Cliopatria could provide
period-specific polygons if this becomes load-bearing. Split candidates at
1865, 1880, and 1895 are documented in the Territorial extent section above.

### oq-cape-expansion-splits

**The Cape Colony's territory grew substantially within this row's span.**
Raised 2026-07-24 by assertion verification of `south africa|mitchell|1852-1909`.

This row covers 1800-1910 with one polygon vintage, but the Cape annexed
British Kaffraria, Griqualand West (1880), the Transkeian territories
(1885-1894) and British Bechuanaland (1895). Data routed here from across the
span is therefore matched to a territory that differs from the reporting
territory in some years — the reason the assertion is banked `best_available`
rather than `verified_equal`.

The WHEP territorial-change rule points to splitting this row at the annexation
years, each period taking its own polygon. That needs a human decision plus
period polygons (CShapes coverage begins 1886, so the earlier boundaries would
need another source or a documented approximation). Until then the
approximation direction is known: the single polygon **overstates** the Cape's
territory in the early decades and matches it best late in the span.
