# Hungary (1947-2025)

## Summary

This row tracks Hungary for **79 years**, from post-WWII to the present
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Post-WWII Hungary at Trianon borders (~93,000 km²). Communist period
1947-1989. 1956 Revolution crushed by Soviet intervention. 1989
democratic transition (opened border with Austria, triggering fall of
Iron Curtain). EU member 2004.

Predecessor: [hun-1944-1947](hun-1944-1947.md) -- Treaty of Paris.
Successor: NA (still active).

## Sourced claims

- **1947-02-10: Treaty of Paris.** Restored Hungary's pre-1938 Trianon
  borders. This is the start-of-row event.
  [biger-1995 §hungary](../sources/biger-1995.md#hungary)
- **1956-10-23: Hungarian Revolution.** Anti-Soviet uprising crushed by
  Soviet military intervention (4 November 1956).
  [biger-1995 §hungary](../sources/biger-1995.md#hungary)
- **1989: Democratic transition.** Hungary opened its border with
  Austria (May 1989), enabling East German exodus and triggering the
  fall of the Iron Curtain.
  [biger-1995 §hungary](../sources/biger-1995.md#hungary)
- **2004-05-01: EU accession.** Hungary joined the European Union.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **COW state-system membership.** COW code 310, continuous
  [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates).
- **Area: 35,934 sq mi (93,033 sq km).** Landlocked, bounded by
  Slovakia (420 mi), Ukraine (64 mi), Romania (275 mi), Serbia
  (103 mi), Croatia (204 mi), Slovenia (63 mi), Austria (227 mi).
  [biger-1995 §hungary](../sources/biger-1995.md#hungary) (p.266)
- **1947-02-10: Treaty of Paris restored Trianon borders.** "the
  Treaty of Peace with Hungary, signed on 10 February 1947,
  declared the Vienna Award null and void and the Trianon boundary
  was reinstated."
  [biger-1995 §hungary](../sources/biger-1995.md#hungary) (p.268)
- **Post-Trianon Hungarian minorities abroad.** "out of 10,000,000
  Hungarians, 1,700,000 found themselves in Romania, 1,000,000 in
  Czechoslovakia, over 500,000 in Yugoslavia and 25,000 in Austria."
  [biger-1995 §hungary](../sources/biger-1995.md#hungary) (p.267)
