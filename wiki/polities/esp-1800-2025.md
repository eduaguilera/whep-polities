# Spain

## Summary

This row tracks Spain as a single continuous territorial-economic unit
from **1800** to **2025** — a **226-year unbroken row**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). Despite massive colonial losses (the
entire Latin American empire in the 1810s–1820s, Cuba/Philippines/
Puerto Rico/Guam in 1898), the CSV does not split at any point because
**metropolitan Spanish territory did not change substantially** during
the entire period. Spain's European borders have been essentially stable
since the Congress of Vienna (1815).

Under the WHEP polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition](../log.md#decision-whep-polity-definition),
splits trigger on substantial territorial change to the polity itself.
Colonial losses are tracked as separate WHEP rows (each colony has its
own row), so they don't trigger a split on the metropolitan row. This
is the same logic that keeps GBR-1800-1921 as a single row despite the
massive British colonial empire — the UK row tracks metropolitan
territory only.

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0` per the CSV
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). CShapes records
  Spain (`cowcode=230`) as a single polygon from 1886-01-01 to
  2019-12-31 with area ~53.60 sq degrees
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope). No polygon
  changes at all — Spain's borders are static across CShapes's entire
  coverage.
- **Pre-1886 extent.** CShapes does not cover before 1886. The CSV
  does not specify a CShapes-Europe supplement for Spain, suggesting
  the same polygon is back-projected.
- **Metropolitan stability.** Spain's European borders have not
  changed since 1815. The only minor exception is the Spanish
  enclaves in Morocco (Ceuta, Melilla) which predate 1800 and are
  still Spanish today. No European territorial gains or losses.

## Sourced claims

- **1808–1814: Peninsular War.** Napoleon invaded Spain in 1808;
  Joseph Bonaparte was designated king. Spain regained independence
  in 1814 after the Peninsular War
  [wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §1808-peninsular-war](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#1808-peninsular-war).
  Not a permanent territorial change for metropolitan Spain — the
  French occupation was temporary.
- **1810s–1820s: Loss of American colonies.** Argentina (1816),
  Chile (1817), Colombia (1819), Venezuela (1821), Mexico (1821),
  Peru (1824). By 1824 Spain had lost the entire American mainland
  empire. "Battle of Ayacucho...signified the end of the Spanish
  Empire on the American mainland"
  [wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §colonial-losses](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#colonial-losses). Each colony
  is tracked as a separate WHEP row.
- **1898-12-10: Treaty of Paris.** Spain ceded Puerto Rico, Guam,
  and the Philippines to the United States; Cuba gained independence.
  US paid $20 million to Spain
  [wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12 §1898-treaty](../sources/wikipedia-spain-2026-04-12.md#1898-treaty). End of the
  Spanish colonial empire. All ceded territories are tracked as
  separate WHEP rows.
- **Metropolitan borders unchanged.** CShapes records a single
  static polygon for Spain across its entire 1886–2019 coverage
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope). No European territorial changes.
- **COW state-system membership.** COW lists Spain (`SPN`,
  `ccode=230`) with a single continuous tenure from 1816-01-01 to
  2024-12-31 [cow-state-system-v2024 §key-dates](../sources/cow-state-system-v2024.md#key-dates). No breaks.

### oq-colonial-loss-split-candidates

The 1810s–1820s colonial losses and the 1898 Treaty of Paris are
massive territorial changes in absolute terms (millions of km²).
Under the WHEP rule, colonial territories are tracked as
separate rows and their independence doesn't affect the
metropolitan row. But should the *loss* of colonial trade data
trigger a WHEP split for trade-analysis purposes even when
metropolitan borders don't change? This is a WHEP-definition
question. Tier X.

### oq-civil-war-1936

The Spanish Civil War (1936–1939) and subsequent Franco regime
are within this row's window. While not a territorial change,
the civil war involved major economic disruption relevant to
trade data. COW records no break for Spain during this period.
Not a split candidate under the WHEP rule but worth documenting.
