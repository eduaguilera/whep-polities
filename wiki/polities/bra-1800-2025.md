## Summary

This row tracks Brazil from the **database floor (1800)** to
**1903** — a **104-year row**
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). At 1800,
the territory was Portuguese Brazil, the largest colony in South
America. Brazilian independence was proclaimed on 7 September 1822
by Dom Pedro I. The Empire of Brazil lasted 1822-1889, followed
by the Republic proclaimed on 15 November 1889.

The 1903 split likely corresponds to the Treaty of Petropolis
(17 November 1903), by which Bolivia ceded the Acre territory
(~190,000 km2) to Brazil during the rubber boom era. This is
the largest single territorial acquisition in Brazil's independent
history. `verification_status = VERIFIED`
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Predecessor: NA (database floor — Portuguese colony).
Successor: BRA-1903-1909 (this row).

## Sourced claims

- **1808: Portuguese court relocates.** The Portuguese royal family
  fled to Brazil following Napoleon's invasion of Portugal. Rio de
  Janeiro became the de facto capital of the Portuguese Empire.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1822-09-07: Brazilian independence.** Dom Pedro I proclaimed
  independence from Portugal ("Cry of Ipiranga"). The Empire of
  Brazil was established.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1824: First constitution.** The 1824 Constitution established
  Brazil as a constitutional monarchy.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1831: Abdication of Pedro I.** Dom Pedro I abdicated in favor
  of his son Pedro II (aged 5). Regency period 1831-1840.
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1840-1889: Pedro II.** Long reign. Abolition of slavery
  (Lei Aurea, 13 May 1888).
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1889-11-15: Republic proclaimed.** Military coup deposed Pedro
  II. The Republic of Brazil established (Republica Velha / Old
  Republic, 1889-1930).
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)
- **1903-11-17: Treaty of Petropolis.** Bolivia ceded the Acre
  territory (~190,000 km2) to Brazil. Rubber boom era. This is
  likely the end-of-row event. See
  [oq-1903-acre-treaty](#oq-1903-acre-treaty).
  [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv)

### oq-acre-split-not-applied

**This page describes a split the database never received.** Flagged
2026-07-24 by assertion verification of `brazil|mitchell|1879-1960`
(`page_suspect`): the body is titled "Brazil (to 1903)", asserts the row ends in
1903, and names a successor `BRA-1903-1909` that does not exist. The frontmatter
and `polities_database.csv` both carry one row, **BRA-1800-2025**, spanning 225
years.

The 1903 boundary was not arbitrary — the **Treaty of Petrópolis (17 November
1903)** transferred **Acre** (~152,000 km²) from Bolivia to Brazil, a real
territorial change inside the span. So the split was the right idea; it simply
was not executed, and the page was left describing the intended state.

Two ways to reconcile, needing a human decision:

1. **Apply the split** — `BRA-1800-1903` and `BRA-1903-2025`, each with its own
   polygon vintage, which is what the WHEP territorial-change rule prescribes
   and what the body already assumes.
2. **Keep one row** and rewrite the body to match, documenting the Acre
   acquisition as an in-span approximation (the single polygon overstates
   pre-1903 Brazil by ~152,000 km², about 1.8%).

Until then, treat pre-1903 Brazilian data as matched to a territory ~152,000 km²
too large. 1,148 layer-B rows are affected.
