# Utah (state of the United States)

## Summary

Utah is one of the fifty states of the United States, admitted to the Union on January 4, 1896 as the 45th state, after more than four decades as a US territory (Utah Territory, organized 1850, itself reduced repeatedly by the creation of Nevada, Colorado and Wyoming out of its original 1850 boundary). This entry captures Utah specifically as a subnational reporting territory inside the USA — `type: subnational`, matching the pattern already used for other admitted states — from admission through the present (2025, exclusive). No existing USA-family row could carry Utah-specific data: the USA rows in the polity table are all national totals or, in Alaska's case, a distinct pre-statehood territory (ALK-1867-1959) with its own bespoke code, and none of them is Utah. Utah's boundary has been fixed since 1896 and has not changed with any subsequent state admission, so there is no internal territorial-change event that would require splitting this entry's span.

**Why this entry exists.** Statistical series (agricultural/economic data keyed to the admin unit "UTAH", country USA) are reported at the state level and were previously falling through to the national USA row, which aggregates all fifty states and is roughly 45x Utah's area — an aggregation that would badly misattribute any Utah-specific figure if left unrouted. No candidate among the existing USA-family rows names Utah: the national rows are total-USA series and ALK-1867-1959 is the Alaska Territory, a geographically and administratively unrelated unit. Utah's separate statistical identity is confirmed by the fact that US state-level series (agricultural censuses, mineral production, population) have reported Utah as its own unit continuously since territorial days, and by the same country-convention logic already applied to other US states admitted post-original-thirteen: each gets its own polity row keyed to its own admission year, per the governing routing decision. The row count and exact source years for the Utah-labeled records were not enumerated to me directly in this task; the routing decision that produced this entry states data exists for Utah under this admin unit but does not give a specific row count, which is recorded as an open question below.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** `assigned`. Utah's modern state boundary has been essentially unchanged since admission on January 4, 1896, and GADM 4.1's admin-1 feature for Utah (`USA.45_1`) is used directly rather than as a proxy, per the routing decision's own assessment that vintage risk is negligible for a US state boundary that predates GADM's reference vintage by well over a century.

**Territory description:** Utah occupies the eastern Great Basin and the northern Colorado Plateau in the Mountain West of the United States, bordered by Idaho and Wyoming to the north, Colorado to the east, Arizona to the south, and Nevada to the west, with a short border with New Mexico at its southeast corner. It contains the Great Salt Lake, the Wasatch Range, and most of the Colorado Plateau's national parks (Zion, Bryce Canyon, Arches, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef). Its capital and largest city is Salt Lake City. A reader can locate it on any modern US map as the rectangular-cornered state directly south of Idaho and Wyoming, north of Arizona.

**Area:** the attached GADM 4.1 polygon (`USA.45_1`) measures approximately 219,887 km2 when computed from the geometry — this is a measurement of the attached polygon, not an independently sourced territorial figure, and it is consistent with commonly cited figures for the state of Utah (~219,900 km2 total area), since the boundary has not moved since 1896.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor row: prior to 1896 the area was Utah Territory (organized 1850), which is not separately represented in the polity table as a distinct polity code and is not claimed as a predecessor here since no such row exists to link to — this gap is noted as an open question. No successor: the entry runs to the open end (2025 exclusive) as Utah remains a current US state with no territorial change since admission.

## Sourced claims

- Utah was admitted to the Union as the 45th state on January 4, 1896, ending the territorial period that began with the Organic Act of 1850.
- Utah's state boundary has not changed since 1896; no subsequent state admission or federal boundary act altered its borders, unlike its territorial predecessor which was reduced by the creation of the Nevada, Colorado, and Wyoming territories.

## Decisions

### d-container-two-era-split

**Container split across USA-1867-1959 and USA-1959-2025 to tile the full 1896-2025 span**

The routing decision proposed a single container_code of USA-1867-1959 with end_year 2025 for the polity, but the polity table's own USA chain ends that container era at 1959 (the Alaska/Hawaii admission boundary implied by ALK-1867-1959's end_year and the naming of a successor era). Using only USA-1867-1959 as the container for the full 1896-2025 span would leave 1959-2025 uncontained and would produce a containment edge whose end_year (2025) falls outside the container polity's own span (which per the table ends at 1959), which the containment gate rejects as an edge outside either party's span. I therefore split the container into two edges, one per container era, together covering 1896 to 2025 with no gap: USA-1867-1959 for 1896-1959, and USA-1959-2025 for 1959-2025. This follows the same principle used elsewhere in the table where a subnational entry's containment must be re-edged whenever the container itself changes code across an era boundary, even though the underlying state (Utah) persists unchanged through that transition.

## Open questions

### oq-utah-territory-predecessor

**Should a Utah Territory (1850-1896) predecessor row be created?**

This entry has no predecessor because no polity row for Utah Territory (1850-1896) currently exists in the table. Utah Territory was a real, statistically distinct reporting unit prior to statehood — its boundaries were reduced repeatedly (loss of territory to Nevada in 1861/1862/1866, to Colorado in 1861, to Wyoming in 1868) which means any pre-1896 Utah-labeled data may correspond to a different, larger boundary than the modern state. If pre-1896 Utah-territory data exists in the source series this pipeline is routing, it should not be back-cast onto this entry's 1896-fixed GADM polygon without checking which territorial-boundary vintage applies to which year; a separate bespoke-coded predecessor entry (e.g., UTAH-T-1850-1896) with its own constructed polygon may be needed, mirroring the ALK-1867-1959 pattern for a pre-statehood US territory.
