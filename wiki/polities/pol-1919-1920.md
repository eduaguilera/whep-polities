# Poland (1919-1920)

## Summary

This row tracks Poland for **2 years**, during the border-settling
period [database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv).

Polish borders still being settled. The Treaty of Saint-Germain
(10 September 1919) incorporated Galicia (formerly Austrian territory)
into Poland. The Polish-Soviet War (1919–1921) was ongoing, with a
major Polish expansion following the armistice of October 1920.

Predecessor: [pol-1918-1919](pol-1918-1919.md) -- initial independence.
Successor: [pol-1920-1921](pol-1920-1921.md) -- Treaty of Riga period.

## Approximation note

The polygon (1919-09-10, ~256,575 km²) is accurate for January–September
1920 data but understates by ~11% for October–December 1920 data, when
Poland's territory was ~284,599 km² (post-Polish-Soviet-War expansion).

A sub-year split (e.g. POL-1919-1920a / POL-1919-1920b at 1920-10-07)
is not currently feasible under the year-keyed data model. This
approximation is documented here; the `polygon_vintage_drift` flag
signals that downstream users should account for this when using
late-1920 production figures.

## Contradictions

### the-polygon-this-page-rejects-is-the-polygon-this-row-carries

**RESOLVED 2026-08-17 (issue 100).** The row now carries the Saint-Germain step through
`constructed/POL-1919-1920`: **256,575 km²** attached against **256,575** declared. What
follows is the record of the defect.

**Measured 2026-08-13 (issue 100): the row carried 177,754 km², the candidate the table
above marks "No — excludes Galicia". The 256,575 km² Saint-Germain polygon this page names
four times as "the polygon for this row" had never been attached.** The row was therefore
~31% short as published — 78,813 km², i.e. Galicia, which is the ~44% understatement of the
Versailles extent this page's own rationale had already computed the other way round. The
shortfall was invisible to `validate_polygons` check A because `polygon_area_km2: 177754`
had been taken from the attached geometry rather than from the argument above (see the
2026-07-24 caveat below), so the check compared the wrong polygon against its own area and
passed.

Why it happened: three CShapes steps for gwcode 290 contain the year 1919 and two of them
start in 1919, so `build_database.find_feature` could not break the tie and fell back to
shapefile row order (issue 100's mechanism). `polygon_feature_date: 1919-09-10` was
recorded in the frontmatter, which reads as an attempt to pin the intended step — but at the
time **nothing in the pipeline consumed that key**; only `scripts/validate_references.py`
knew it existed, as a permitted field name. (That changed on 2026-08-17: `find_feature` now
narrows a temporal source's candidates by day. Had the key been live then, `1919-09-10`
would have bound this row directly, without the constructed indirection.)

Why no `polygon_feature_year` could fix it: `1919` is shared by all three candidates, and
`1920` resolves deterministically to the *next* step (1920-10-07, ~284,599 km²), which this
page assigns to the successor row and which falls outside this row's own period. The two
available remedies were a date-level binding (making `polygon_feature_date` load-bearing in
`find_feature`, which truncates CShapes' `gwsdate`/`gwedate` to a year) or a constructed
polygon.

**How it was fixed:** the constructed route, because it needs no pipeline change and because
this repo already had the mechanism — `_cshapes2_step(gwcode, start_year, end_year)` names a
CShapes step by its year bounds and raises if the bounds are not unique. `TUR-1913-1914` was
repaired the same way in issue 123. `290`'s `1919-1920` bounds are unique, so
`build_pol_1919_1920` returns exactly the intended feature and the binding no longer depends
on shapefile row order. A date-level binding was the more general remedy and was left open here;
it landed on 2026-08-17 and pinned issue 100's two remaining rows, `ROU-1919-1920` and
`F228-1920-1921`, neither of whose geometry changed. This row was not converted back to a
direct CShapes binding: the constructed feature is byte-for-byte the same geometry, and
swapping a working binding for a second working one would change the published GeoPackage's
provenance for no gain.

## Polygon caveat (added 2026-07-24, RETRACTED 2026-08-17)

The 2026-07-24 note recorded that `polygon_area_km2` had been "corrected" from
**256,573** to **177,754 km²** because `scripts/validate_polygons.py` found a 31%
divergence from the attached geometry, and reasoned that 177,754 "is what the CShapes
1919-1920 feature actually measures" and was plausible for a transitional row whose
eastern border was unsettled until the Treaty of Riga.

**That reasoning was backwards, and it is why the defect survived another three weeks.**
The divergence was real but the geometry was the wrong party: 177,754 km² is what the
*1919-06-28 / 1919-09-09* step measures — the step this page had already rejected by name —
not the 1919-09-10 / 1920-10-06 step it asked for. Aligning the declaration to the attached
geometry silenced the only check that could have caught the binding error. The 256,573
figure was the argued one all along; re-measured it is 256,575 km², and the row now attaches
that geometry.

The lesson, recorded here because it generalises: when a declared area and an attached
polygon disagree, the polygon is a candidate for being wrong. Overwriting the declaration
from the geometry makes the pair agree without establishing which one was right.

`polygon_status` remains `polygon_vintage_drift`, which correctly flags that borders were
moving throughout this window — see the ~11% October–December 1920 residual above.
