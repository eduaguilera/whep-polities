# Piemonte (region of Italy)

## Summary

Piemonte is one of Italy's 15 ordinary-statute regions (regioni a statuto ordinario), located in the country's northwest, bordering France and Switzerland, with Turin (Torino) as its capital. This entry is `type: subnational` because Piemonte never held sovereignty of its own in this period — Italy held sovereignty throughout 1970-2025 — the row exists purely to carry a *reporting/administrative territory* below the national level, the same rationale documented for other regional subnational rows in this table (e.g. the Manchuria region entries). Piemonte became an operative administrative and statistical unit in 1970, when Italy's ordinary-statute regions held their first regional council elections mandated by the 1948 Constitution; before that date, regional government existed on paper only and statistics were not compiled at this level, so this row's span begins in 1970 rather than at unification or at the Constitution's adoption. The end_year of 2025 is open — the region remains current.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: this row is created to receive statistical series reported under Italian administrative unit ITC1 (Piemonte) in NUTS/GADM-style subnational classifications — the routing decision names the unit as `ITA-ITC1`, admin_name `ITC1`, country Italy, with no row count given in the decision object itself but flagged for creation because the routing pipeline identified Piemonte-labelled rows needing a home distinct from the national ITA polity. Previously matched to: all such data was being matched to the national polities (ITA-1919-2025 and its predecessors), which is wrong for any year from 1970 onward because Piemonte-level reporting is a real, distinct statistical/administrative practice that the national total obscures — a regional figure folded into a national total cannot be recovered or distinguished from other regions' contributions. What confirms distinctness: Piemonte is a constitutionally recognized ordinary-statute region of Italy with its own directly elected regional council since 1970, its own regional government (Giunta regionale), and its own budget and statistical reporting apparatus — it is not a mere geographic label but a formal administrative tier that Italian and international statistical agencies (e.g. Istat, Eurostat under NUTS2 code ITC1) report on separately from national totals.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon is available in this repository's GeoPackage for Piemonte at any period — the correct registered source type (gadm-4.1-adm1) exists in sources.yaml, but the copy of that dataset present locally is an 81-country curated subset that does not include Italy, so ITA.13 (Piemonte) cannot be extracted yet. `polygon_source` is set to the registered slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` per the routing decision's `registered_source_unfetched` route (the source itself is not in question, only whether it has been fetched), and `polygon_status` is `unassigned` with `polygon_feature_id: null` until that fetch happens.

**Why this entry exists:** the routing decision for unit `ITA-ITC1` (admin_name ITC1, i.e. Piemonte) found that Piemonte-labelled data was previously matched only at the national ITA level, even though Italy already has subnational administrative rows for other purposes — meaning region-level Piemonte statistics were being folded into all-Italy totals rather than isolated as their own reporting unit. Piemonte is not an ad hoc statistical construct: it is a first-order administrative region (regione) of Italy with its own regional government, council, and administrative apparatus since 1970, making it a natural unit for regional-level statistical series (e.g. agricultural production, demographic data) reported separately from national Italian totals.

**Territory description:** Piemonte occupies Italy's northwest corner, bordered by France (across the Alps) to the west, Switzerland to the north, Valle d'Aosta and Lombardia to the north/east, Liguria to the south, and Emilia-Romagna to the southeast. It contains the Po river's upper basin and the city of Turin. Its present-day area is approximately **25,400 km²**, making it Italy's second-largest region after Sicilia. This figure is a widely cited present-day administrative-area statistic, not a measurement of any geometry attached to this entry (none is attached yet) — once the GADM ADM1 feature is fetched, `polygon_area_km2` should be populated from that geometry directly and can be checked against this figure.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-subunit

**Use ITA-PIE-1970-2025, the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-years pattern**

57 of 66 existing subnational rows follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. DZA-CVD-1902-1919, ESP-AS-1833-2025). Piemonte is an ordinary Italian administrative region with no name-of-its-own historical identity distinct from being a region of Italy (unlike Manchuria or Hyderabad, which earned bespoke codes as historical territories), so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. ITA has zero existing subnational rows, so this entry sets the country's precedent: PIE is the natural three-letter abbreviation for Piemonte and should be reused by any future Italian regional row (e.g. ITA-LOM-1970-2025 for Lombardia) for consistency.

### d-start-year-1970

**Start year 1970 matches the routing decision's convention default, not GADM vintage**

The routing decision fixes start_year at 1970 because that is when Italy's 15 ordinary-statute regions (Piemonte among them) held their first regional council elections and became operative administrative and statistical units; the five special-statute regions (Sicilia, Sardegna, Trentino-Alto Adige, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Valle d'Aosta) are excluded from this convention and are not implicated by this row. Pre-1970 Piemonte-labelled data has no distinct regional administration to attach to and falls to the national ITA container per the routing reasoning, so this entry does not retroactively claim any pre-1970 rows.

## Open questions

### oq-gadm-fetch-pending

**gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered but the local file excludes Italy**

sources.yaml already registers gadm-4.1-adm1 as the mechanism for constructing sub-national region polygons, and the routing decision confirms it is the correct source type for an ordinary Italian region (a standard GADM ADM1 unit). But the file present in this repo's data/geodata is explicitly an 81-country curated subset that does not include Italy (0 features returned for any ITA.x code). Until the full/global GADM ADM1 layer is fetched and Piemonte's ITA.x feature extracted, this row has polygon_status: unassigned and polygon_feature_id: null. When fetched, polygon_status should move to assigned and this open question can close.

### oq-pre1970-piemonte-rows

**Where does pre-1970 Piemonte-labelled data actually land, and is it currently double-counted with ITA national rows**

The routing reasoning says pre-1970 years fall to the national ITA polity current at the time per the container chain, meaning any historical statistical series labelled 'Piemonte' before 1970 should already be routed to ITA-1919-2025 (or its predecessor) rather than to this new row. This has not been independently verified against the routing_verdicts.csv ledger — it is asserted in the decision object, not confirmed by inspecting which rows currently carry a Piemonte label pre-1970. If any pre-1970 Piemonte series were previously mis-routed to a national row under the assumption Piemonte didn't exist as a reporting unit, or if a subnational reporting series predates 1970 (e.g. an old Kingdom of Sardinia-era Piemonte administrative unit), this entry's start_year could be too late and would need revisiting.

### oq-adjacent-region-boundary-overlap

**Piemonte's modern GADM boundary may not match its 1970 administrative boundary exactly**

Once the ADM1 feature is fetched, the geometry it provides will reflect present-day (post-1970-ish, largely stable) Italian regional boundaries. Italian regional boundaries have been essentially stable since 1970 with only minor municipal reassignments between neighbouring regions and provinces over the decades (e.g. small comune boundary adjustments near Liguria and Lombardia), so vintage risk is low but not zero. This has not been checked against a historical atlas of Italian regional boundary changes 1970-2025, so a reader should not assume the fetched polygon is pixel-exact for every year in this row's span.
