# Sonora

## Summary

Sonora is a Mexican federal state on the northwestern border with the United States (Arizona), on the Gulf of California. It was created in 1830 when the joint state of Estado de Occidente — formed at Mexican independence by merging the former colonial provinces of Sonora and Sinaloa — was split into two separate states by federal decree. Sonora has existed continuously as a Mexican state since, making it, like most of the other 31 current Mexican states, an entity that long predates and outlasts most of the data windows any single source reports it in. `type: subnational` is used, not `national`, because Sonora has never been sovereign: it is a first-order administrative division of Mexico throughout its existence, and the container edges below record that it sat inside pre-1848 Mexico and then inside post-Mexican-Cession Mexico, with no gap in between.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is created directly from a routing decision (unit_id MEX-SONORA) produced by the agent-harness pipeline while working through Mexican subnational administrative units that had no existing polity candidate in the table. The decision's own text states Sonora is "a genuine Mexican federal state with no existing polity candidate" — i.e., before this page, any data reported at the "Sonora" administrative level for Mexico would have had nowhere to route except the national MEX row (MEX-1848-2025 or its predecessor), which is wrong for any source that specifically distinguishes Sonora from the rest of Mexico, since the national row covers roughly 65x Sonora's own area and includes 31 other states. The routing decision does not itself quote a row count, source name, or year range for the specific data that prompted this unit's creation — that is flagged as an open question below, since it is the piece an economic-historian reviewer would check first to confirm a real (not merely plausible) mismatch existed. What is documented in the decision, and does confirm Sonora as a distinct, source-recognized reporting unit rather than an arbitrary subdivision, is the administrative history itself: Sonora's split from the Estado de Occidente in 1830 is a matter of Mexican constitutional/administrative record (state congresses and boundaries fixed by federal decree), independent of any single statistical source's labeling choice, and Sonora has been listed as one of Mexico's states in essentially every subsequent Mexican census and international statistical compilation (e.g., Mexican INEGI state series, and cross-country compilations such as GADM's admin-1 layer) — the country sets its own precedent here, since MEX previously had zero subnational codes in this table.

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. `polygon_status: unassigned` with `polygon_source: gadm-4.1-adm1` — this is the `registered_source_unfetched` route: GADM 4.1 admin-1 is a registered source (`sources.yaml`, present_locally=True) and is the correct source for a Mexican state boundary, but the routing evidence found that the copy of GADM 4.1 present on disk is a curated 81-country subset that does not include Mexico. No proxy from another period is copied, because there is no other Sonora-period feature anywhere in the GeoPackage to copy from — this is the first Sonora entry.

**Territory description:** Sonora is Mexico's second-largest state, on the northwestern mainland bordering Arizona and a short stretch of New Mexico (US) to the north, Chihuahua to the east, Sinaloa to the south, and the Gulf of California to the west, with Baja California across the Gulf. Its capital is Hermosillo. Modern Sonora covers approximately 179,000 km2 (Mexican federal government/INEGI figures put it at roughly 179,355 km2), the majority of it Sonoran Desert terrain plus the Sierra Madre Occidental foothills along its eastern edge. No area figure is asserted here from the attached geometry, since no polygon is yet attached to this entry (see polygon status above); the ~179,000 km2 figure is an independent, source-stated modern administrative area, not a measurement of any geometry in this GeoPackage.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern, not a bespoke code**

Chose MEX-SON-1830-2025 over a bespoke name because Sonora is an ordinary Mexican federal state with no independent historical identity of its own separate from being a subdivision of Mexico -- unlike the three bespoke-coded examples (Alaska Territory, Hyderabad, Ryukyu), which were each historically distinct polities (a US territory, a princely state, an occupied archipelago) before or instead of being an ordinary administrative unit of their respective countries. Sonora has never been anything other than a Mexican state, so it follows the 57-of-66 dominant pattern rather than the 3-of-66 bespoke pattern. The subunit code SON follows the ISO 3166-2:MX convention (MX-SON) minus the country prefix, for consistency with codes like ESP-AS and ESP-CA in the precedent table, which likewise use the country's own subdivision abbreviation rather than an invented one.

### d-container-split-at-1848

**Container edges split at 1848, using the table's actual pre-Cession Mexico code MEX-1800-1848**

Used two container edges instead of one, because the polity table's own containing-chain for Mexico changes at 1848 (the Mexican Cession redrew Mexico's national border after the Mexican-American War), and the containment gate requires one edge per era of the containing chain with no gap between start_year and end_year. A single edge naming only MEX-1848-2025 would leave 1830-1848 uncontained and would be rejected as falling outside that container polity's own span. The gate finding confirmed the placeholder code MEX-1821-1848 used in the previous version of this page does not exist in polities_database.csv; the actual pre-Cession Mexico row is MEX-1800-1848 (span 1800-1848, labeled 'Mexico (to 1848)'), so the first container edge is corrected to that code with start_year 1830 (Sonora's own creation year, which falls inside MEX-1800-1848's 1800-1848 span) and end_year 1848, matching the second edge's start_year with no gap.

## Open questions

### oq-son-admission-year

**Exact admission year: 1830 or 1831?**

The routing reasoning gives 1830 as Sonora's separation from the joint Estado de Occidente and admission as an independent federal state, but the routing_concerns field explicitly flags this as needing verification against a primary source on Mexican state history rather than general knowledge; some accounts date the definitive split and first separate state congress to 1831. This page uses 1830 per the routing decision, but start_year (and therefore the polity_code itself) should be revisited if a primary source, such as the text of the federal decree separating Sonora and Sinaloa, gives a different year.

### oq-son-polygon-fetch

**GADM 4.1 admin-1 needs to be fetched at full (non-subset) scope before a feature can be assigned**

polygon_route is registered_source_unfetched: gadm-4.1-adm1 is registered in sources.yaml as present_locally=True, but the local file is confirmed to be a curated 81-country subset that excludes Mexico entirely, so no GID_1 feature currently exists to assign to Sonora. This entry stays polygon_status: unassigned with no polygon_feature_id until the full global GADM 4.1 admin-1 dataset is fetched and a Sonora feature (expected GID_1 form similar to MEX.26_1, but unverified) is identified and attached.

### oq-son-boundary-stability

**Has Sonora's state boundary actually been stable since 1830?**

The routing decision's own polygon_detail flags a vintage risk: applying a present-day (c.2022) GADM boundary back to 1830 assumes state boundaries are effectively stable since admission, but Sonora's borders shifted somewhat in the 19th century relative to Baja California and Sinaloa after the Estado de Occidente split, and the broader northern Mexican border region was affected by the 1853 Gadsden Purchase. This has not been checked against a historical atlas of Mexican state boundaries; once a GADM feature is attached, whether a single modern polygon is an adequate proxy for the full 1830-2025 span (or whether an earlier sub-period needs its own polygon_status: proxy treatment with a documented km2 difference, per the proxy rules) should be assessed.
