# Haut-Rhin (département)

## Summary

Haut-Rhin is a French département in the Grand Est region, on the German and Swiss borders, with its préfecture at Colmar and its largest city Mulhouse. This entry is `type: subnational` because it is an ordinary administrative subdivision of the sovereign French state, not a state or historical territory with an identity independent of the modern département -- consistent with every other French département row created in this pipeline run. What makes Haut-Rhin different from an ordinary département, and why its start_year departs from the 1790 default that applies to siblings like Aisne or Calvados, is a documented territorial-sovereignty break: along with Bas-Rhin and Moselle, Haut-Rhin was ceded to the German Empire under the 1871 Treaty of Frankfurt following the Franco-Prussian War and administered as part of Reichsland Elsaß-Lothringen until the 1919 Treaty of Versailles returned it to France after the First World War. This is one of exactly four explicit departures from the default départemental history recorded in this pipeline's country convention for France (the others being the Territoire de Belfort split in 1871/1922, the 1975 Corse split, and the 1946 overseas départements), so its start_year is fixed at 1919 rather than 1790, and the FRA container chain's own 1871-1919 row (which is explicitly the "without Alsace-Lorraine" era of France) confirms the same sovereignty gap from the container side.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: the panel unit FRA-FRF12, labelled "Haut-Rhin" via nuts_code_names.csv, reports at 97.6% observed/blank by the routing decision's method, spanning 1862-2023 per the routing decision -- row count not independently re-verified in this authoring pass, flagged in sourced_claims. Before this entry, none of the three existing FRA candidate polities (FRA-1800-1871, FRA-1871-1919/FRA-1800-1919, FRA-1919-2025) could correctly hold this data: they are national totals for successive eras of the whole of France, not Haut-Rhin specifically, and a département is not its country -- routing département-scale series there would silently blend one département's figures into a national aggregate roughly ninety times larger in département-count terms. What confirms Haut-Rhin is a real, distinct, historically continuous administrative entity rather than a source-invented label: it is one of the original French départements (residual after the 1790 creation and the 1871 territorial changes), it carries its own INSEE code (68) and préfecture (Colmar), and its 1871-1919 absence from France is independently corroborated by this table's own FRA-1871-1919 row, which is explicitly defined as France "without Alsace-Lorraine" -- the container chain and the country convention agree on the same sovereignty break from two independent angles.

**Territory description:** Haut-Rhin ("Upper Rhine") occupies the southern part of Alsace, bordered by Germany (Baden-Württemberg) across the Rhine to the east, Switzerland (Basel-Landschaft and Jura cantons) to the south, and the French départements of Vosges and Territoire de Belfort to the west. Its préfecture is Colmar; its largest city and sub-préfecture is Mulhouse, a historic textile and industrial center. On a modern map it corresponds to INSEE département code 68, running along the Rhine plain and up into the Vosges mountains on its western edge, drained by the Ill river. Its modern area is approximately **3,525 km²** (a figure quoted from published French administrative statistics for the present-day département, not measured from any polygon attached to this entry, since none is attached), making it one of the smaller metropolitan départements -- consistent with it having been the residual portion of the pre-1871 Haut-Rhin left in France after the Territoire de Belfort was carved off and the rest annexed to Germany, until the whole territory was reunited with France in 1919.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1919-not-1790-departure-rule

**start_year set to 1919 per the explicit country-convention departure, not the 1790 system floor**

The default rule for French départements in this pipeline is start_year 1790, matching the Revolutionary creation of the départemental system (documented in country_conventions.json, system_start_year 1790). Haut-Rhin is one of exactly four named departures from that default: the convention's unit_start_exceptions entry for 'Bas-Rhin, Haut-Rhin, Moselle (Alsace-Lorraine départements)' sets start_year 1919 because these three were annexed by Germany 1871-1918 (per container row FRA-1871-1919, which excludes Alsace-Lorraine by name) and returned to France only under the 1919 Treaty of Versailles, matching container row FRA-1919-2025's own start. Unlike the ordinary départements entered elsewhere in this pipeline run (Aisne, Calvados, etc.), which start at 1790/1800 because they never left French sovereignty, Haut-Rhin was administratively absent from France for 48 years and its present-form départemental existence genuinely resumes in 1919, so 1919 is used as both the code year and the frontmatter start_year, satisfying the gate that the two must match.

### d-subunit-code-is-insee-number-68

**Subunit code '68' follows INSEE numbering, matching the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT pattern and every sibling département entry**

Haut-Rhin's INSEE département code is 68 (préfecture Colmar). This keeps the code inside the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> family (57 of 66 subnational rows, and the established convention for every French département entry created in this pipeline run so far: 02, 14, 18, 21, 25, 27, 28, 36, 37, 39, 41, 50, 58, 59, 60). Haut-Rhin has no name of its own distinct from the modern département in the way Alaska or Hyderabad do, so bespoke-code treatment was rejected even though its administrative history (annexation, return) is more eventful than an ordinary département's -- that history is captured by the start_year departure and the container basis, not by a bespoke code.

### d-no-back-cast-page-for-1862-1918

**This page covers only 1919-2025; the routing decision's proposed 1862-1918 back-cast is not represented by a second polity row**

The routing decision for FRA-FRF12 proposed handling the panel's earlier years (1862-1918, which include the 1871-1918 German-annexation span when no French Haut-Rhin département existed) via back_cast onto this new polity, not via a second polity entry. Per the task given to this pipeline stage (create the polity this decision calls for), that back-cast is a downstream data-routing operation on already-created rows, not a wiki page in its own right -- there is no France row spanning 1862-1918 for Haut-Rhin to container against in the first place (the German Empire held the territory as Alsace-Lorraine, Reichsland Elsaß-Lothringen, which is outside this table's FRA chain and outside this pipeline's polity-creation task), so no attempt is made here to construct a distinct 1862-1918 entry. This gap is recorded as an open question rather than resolved.

## Open questions

### oq-1862-1918-annexation-span-uncontained

**1862-1918 portion of the data panel (pre-creation and German-annexation years) has no polity row to route to**

The underlying panel for unit FRA-FRF12 extends back to 1862, and the routing decision explicitly proposes handling 1862-1918 via back_cast onto this new 1919-2025 polity rather than via a distinct row. But 1871-1918 is the exact span during which Haut-Rhin was part of the German Empire (Reichsland Elsaß-Lothringen), not France, and 1862-1871 is French Haut-Rhin before the FRA container chain's post-1871 split even applies. No row in this table represents German-administered Alsace-Lorraine, and this pipeline task was scoped to creating the polity implied by the decision, not to adjudicating the back-cast mechanism. Whether those 56 years should be silently absorbed into FRA-68-1919-2025 via back-casting (as proposed), left unrouted, or given a separate German-administration-era row has not been decided here and should be revisited before the back-cast is actually executed.

### oq-no-polygon-source-registered-68

**No registered, locally-present source can supply a Haut-Rhin boundary today, and none would carry the right vintage even if it could**

As with the other French département entries in this run, gadm-4.1-adm1 is the only registered source with département-level granularity for France, but the copy present in this repository is an 81-country subset that excludes France (0 FRA rows), so the route cannot execute. Beyond mere availability, the routing decision's own polygon_detail flags a vintage problem specific to this unit: any GADM or IGN département boundary obtainable today reflects Haut-Rhin's modern (post-1919, arguably post-1871-cession) shape, not a boundary specific to 1919. That is a smaller concern than for most départements since Haut-Rhin's external boundary has been stable since 1919 (only the internal Territoire de Belfort split, in 1871, predates this entry's start), but it has not been verified that no boundary adjustment occurred at the 1919 return itself. This should be revisited once gadm-4.1-adm1 is re-sourced as its full global layer, or IGN's ADMIN-EXPRESS is registered for France.
