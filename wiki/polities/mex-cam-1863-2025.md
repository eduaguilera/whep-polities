# Campeche

## Summary

Campeche is a state of the Mexican federation occupying the western, Gulf-of-Mexico-facing side of the Yucatán Peninsula. It was created out of the larger state of Yucatán and admitted to the federation by decree in 1863, following a period of separatist agitation in the 1850s driven by regional grievances against government in Mérida. This entry is `type: subnational` rather than `national` because Campeche has never been a sovereign state -- Mexico held (and holds) sovereignty over this territory throughout the entry's span -- and the entry exists in this database purely to give the state its own reporting-territory row, distinct from both the national MEX chain and its parent state Yucatán, which itself lacks a dedicated row in this table. The state's boundaries have been essentially stable since 1863: no subsequent partition, annexation, or federal reorganization has altered its territory in a way that would require splitting this single row into multiple periods.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is a subnational gap-fill, not a response to a specific dataset extract: the routing decision that created it (unit_id MEX-CAMPECHE) found that Campeche, a genuine Mexican federal state, had no representation in this database -- no name match to an existing polity, and the national row MEX-1848-2025 is the wrong territory (all of Mexico, not this one state). No source, label, or row count accompanies the decision, unlike the typical data-first creation pattern in this pipeline; this was instead identified as a missing administrative unit during a country-convention sweep of Mexican states, following the same rule already applied to give Baja California and Quintana Roo their own rows keyed to their admission years rather than a blanket 1824/1848 start. What confirms Campeche is a distinct entity worth its own row: it is a first-order administrative division of a federal republic with its own government, legislature, and admission date to the federation (1863) that differs from Mexico's own founding-era start years (1800/1824/1848) -- the same reasoning that already justifies dedicated rows for other Mexican states with non-default admission years.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The registered admin-1 source in this repository, gadm-4.1-adm1, is present only as an 81-country subset and Mexico is not among the included countries, so no existing GeoPackage feature can be assigned even as a rough proxy. The most plausible authoritative source for Mexican state boundaries is INEGI's Marco Geoestadístico Nacional, but it is not currently registered in sources.yaml, has not been fetched, and its licence terms have not been checked (see open question oq-no-polygon-source-identified). `polygon_source` is therefore recorded as `none` and `polygon_feature_id` as null rather than pointing at a placeholder.

**Territory description:** Campeche occupies the western third of the Yucatán Peninsula, fronting the Gulf of Mexico's Bay of Campeche. On a modern map it is bordered by the Mexican states of Yucatán (northeast), Quintana Roo (east), and Tabasco (southwest), and by Guatemala along a short southern stretch. Its capital is the port city of San Francisco de Campeche. The present-day state covers approximately 57,900 km2 (INEGI figures), making it roughly the size of Croatia or the U.S. state of West Virginia -- a mid-sized Mexican state, larger than Tabasco or Yucatán but smaller than its peninsula neighbor Quintana Roo. No polygon is attached to this entry, so no geometry-derived area figure exists to potentially conflate with this stated modern reference figure.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity rows exist in this table. Before 1863, the territory that became Campeche state was part of the state of Yucatán (itself represented in this table, if at all, only as part of the national MEX rows, since Yucatán has no dedicated subnational entry either). Campeche split from Yucatán by federal decree and has remained a constituent state of Mexico continuously since, with no subsequent territorial reorganization producing a successor entity -- hence both predecessor and successor lists are empty rather than pointing to sibling rows that do not exist in this database.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-iso3-subunit

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, using a 3-letter subunit abbreviation**

Of the 66 subnational rows precedent-checked for this harness, 57 follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> (e.g. ESP-AS-1833-2025, DZA-CVD-1902-1919). Mexico has no existing subnational rows, so there is no in-country precedent to match, but there is no reason to depart from the dominant cross-country pattern here: Campeche is an ordinary federal state, not a historical territory with a name and identity independent of the modern country the way ALK (Alaska Territory) or HYD (Hyderabad) are. I chose the 3-letter subunit code CAM (rather than a 2-letter code as used for Spanish provinces) because Mexico's states do not have an established 2-letter ISO 3166-2 style abbreviation in common use the way Spanish provinces do; CAM is unambiguous, matches the state's common abbreviation in Mexican federal usage, and leaves room for other Mexican states (e.g. a future COL for Colima) to use non-colliding 3-letter codes. Final code: MEX-CAM-1863-2025.

### d-single-container-era

**Single container edge suffices because neither party's span changes**

The container chain requires one edge per era of the containing chain, covering the full territory span with no gap. Campeche's span (1863-2025) falls entirely within MEX-1848-2025's own span (1848-2025) with no intervening national reorganization that would split the container into multiple polity rows in this table -- Mexico's federal structure and the state's federation membership have not changed across this window. A single container edge from 1863 to 2025, keyed to MEX-1848-2025, therefore tiles the whole span without gaps.

## Open questions

### oq-1863-admission-date-unverified

**The 1863 admission date has not been checked against a primary source**

The routing decision that created this entry explicitly flagged that the April 29, 1863 decree date separating Campeche from Yucatán and admitting it as a full federal state was taken from background knowledge, not verified against a primary or authoritative reference in that session (the same reference standard used for the Baja California / Quintana Roo departures from the default 1824/1848 start elsewhere in the Mexican state series). Some secondary sources instead cite the 1858 unilateral declaration of separation from Yucatán, with 1863 marking recognition rather than the original separatist act. If a primary source instead supports 1858 (or a different year for federal recognition), both the frontmatter start_year and the code MEX-CAM-1863-2025 would need to change together, since the years in the code must equal the frontmatter years.

### oq-no-polygon-source-identified

**No registered polygon source covers Mexican state boundaries**

The GeoPackage's registered admin-1 source (gadm-4.1-adm1) is present only as an 81-country subset that excludes Mexico entirely, so no route through GADM adm1 is viable for Campeche or any other Mexican state. The most likely authoritative source is INEGI's Marco Geoestadístico Nacional, but it is not registered in sources.yaml, its licence terms have not been checked, and it has not been fetched into this repository. Until that is resolved, this entry carries polygon_status: unassigned and no polygon_feature_id. Campeche's borders have been essentially stable since 1863 (no territorial disputes or boundary changes of consequence within the modern state), so once a source is registered a single feature should proxy the full 1863-2025 span without a vintage caveat -- but that is a claim to verify once a candidate geometry exists, not before.
