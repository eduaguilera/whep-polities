# Pennsylvania (state of the United States)

## Summary

Pennsylvania, one of the original thirteen colonies and the second state to ratify the U.S. Constitution (1787-12-12), as a first-order administrative subdivision of the United States. This entry is `type: subnational`: Pennsylvania was never a sovereign state distinct from the federal union it helped found, and the entry exists to carry state-level statistics, not sovereignty. `start_year` is set to 1800 rather than the historically correct 1787 ratification date, because the USA national container chain in this database begins at `USA-1800-1803` -- there is no earlier national container row for the containment gate to attach an 1787-1800 edge to, and an edge outside the container's own span is rejected. The 13-year gap between actual statehood and this entry's start is recorded below as an open question rather than silently absorbed. `end_year` is 2025, open/current, matching the terminal `USA-1959-2025` container row.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry is created directly from a harness routing decision (unit_id USA-PENNSYLVANIA) rather than from a specific dataset intake batch: no existing USA-iso3 candidate row represented Pennsylvania specifically -- the existing USA rows are all national totals (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) plus the unrelated bespoke territory ALK-1867-1959 (Alaska). Any state-level series for Pennsylvania (population, agricultural output, or similar state-tabulated statistics) would previously have had nowhere to route except the national USA row, which overstates the reporting unit by roughly 40x in area and folds 49 other states' data into what should be a single state's figures. Pennsylvania's status as a distinct reporting/administrative unit is uncontroversial and well documented: it is one of the original thirteen colonies, ratified the U.S. Constitution on 1787-12-12 as the second state to do so, and has been a first-order administrative and statistical unit of the United States (a U.S. Census Bureau state, with its own vital statistics, agricultural census, and economic accounts) continuously since admission. No historian's judgment call is required here in the way Federico-Tena or Cliopatria adjudicate contested polities -- Pennsylvania's distinctness as a statistical unit is a matter of public record (U.S. Census, USDA state-level series).

## Territorial extent

**Polygon:** `assigned` status. `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.39_1`, a direct single-feature match by name -- Pennsylvania's GADM admin-1 polygon -- registered and available locally, requiring no construction. Pennsylvania's external state boundary has been effectively stable since admission (aside from minor 19th-century surveying adjustments along the Ohio, Delaware, and New York borders, and the still-unresolved Delaware River boundary disputes with New Jersey), so using a present-day GADM boundary to represent the full 1800-2025 span is a low-risk, standard approximation, consistent with how every other unchanged-boundary U.S. state in this database is handled.

**Territory:** the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, in the northeastern United States, bordered by New York to the north, New Jersey and the Delaware River to the east, Delaware and Maryland to the south, West Virginia to the southwest, and Ohio and Lake Erie to the west. Its official land+water area is approximately **119,280 km2** (about the size of England and Wales combined), readily locatable on any modern map as a single contiguous state with no exclave or island territory.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor: Pennsylvania has no antecedent polity row in this database, since its pre-1787 colonial history is not separately modeled here and its statehood is treated as continuous from the federal era onward. No successor: Pennsylvania remains an active U.S. state as of 2025, so `end_year` is open/current and there is no successor code to record.

## Sourced claims

- Pennsylvania ratified the U.S. Constitution on 1787-12-12, becoming the second of the thirteen original states to do so (National Archives, Constitution ratification records).
- The U.S. Census Bureau reports Pennsylvania's total area as 119,283 km2 (46,054 sq mi), consistent with the ~119,280 km2 figure cited here and with the area implied by the GADM 4.1 admin-1 polygon USA.39_1.

## Decisions

### d-start-year-1800-not-1787

**Start year set to 1800, not the 1787 ratification date, to satisfy container tiling**

The routing decision's span_basis argues for start_year 1787, matching Pennsylvania's actual ratification of the U.S. Constitution and the country convention's stated 1787 floor for original-13 states. But the containment gate requires every container edge to fall within both the member's own span and the container's own registered span, and the earliest national USA container row in this database is USA-1800-1803 -- there is no national United States row covering 1787-1800 for an edge to attach to. Rather than leave a 13-year uncontained gap (which the gate rejects) or invent an unregistered container row, this entry's start_year was set to 1800 to match the earliest available container. This is a database-completeness compromise, not a historical claim that Pennsylvania was not a state before 1800; it should be revisited if an earlier USA national container row covering 1787-1800 is added to the table.

## Open questions

### oq-1787-1800-gap-uncontained

**Pennsylvania's actual 1787-1800 statehood is not represented by any container row**

Pennsylvania ratified the Constitution in 1787, thirteen years before this entry's forced start_year of 1800. If any historical dataset carries state-level Pennsylvania figures for 1787-1800 (e.g., early federal censuses of 1790), those rows currently have no valid polity to route to: this entry cannot absorb them without violating the containment gate, and the national USA-1800-1803 row would misrepresent them at roughly 40x the correct territory. Resolving this requires either adding an earlier national USA container row (1787-1800) or explicitly deciding that pre-1800 state-level data does not exist in the ingested sources and this gap is moot in practice.

### oq-boundary-vintage-risk

**GADM 4.1 present-day boundary used across a 225-year span**

The polygon feature USA.39_1 reflects Pennsylvania's boundary as surveyed and digitized in the present day, applied uniformly back to 1800. Pennsylvania's external boundary is widely described as stable since admission, but this has not been independently verified against a historical atlas for this entry -- in particular, the Erie Triangle (purchased by Pennsylvania from the federal government in 1792, giving Pennsylvania its Lake Erie frontage) predates 1800 and is assumed already reflected in the modern boundary, but this assumption has not been checked against a period map.
