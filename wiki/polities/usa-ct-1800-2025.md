# Connecticut (state of the United States)

## Summary

Connecticut, one of the fifty states of the United States and one of the original 13 states that ratified the federal Constitution in 1788. This entry is `type: subnational` because Connecticut has never been independently sovereign under the U.S. federal system covered by this database's national USA chain: the row exists purely to carry state-level reporting territory for statistics tabulated at the state rather than the national level, and must not compete with the national USA rows for sovereignty ranking in the matcher. Although Connecticut's own administrative history as a distinct polity reaches back to 1788 (and, as a self-governing colony, well before that), this entry's `start_year` is set to 1800 rather than 1788 because the earliest national USA container row in this database (USA-1800-1803) does not extend back to 1788 -- see the open question and decision below for why the gap could not simply be bridged. The span 1800-2025 then tracks continuous statehood, with no territorial change, through five successive eras of the national USA polity chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025).

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures Connecticut as a distinct subnational reporting unit within the United States, created by the agent-harness pipeline processing the administrative unit `USA-CONNECTICUT` ("Connecticut", country United States of America), tagged with source `juan-subnational`. No prior polity row existed for Connecticut at the state level -- the existing USA chain (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) covers the country as a whole, so any Connecticut-specific statistics previously had no state-level home and would either be absent or folded into national USA totals, a mismatch whenever a source reports Connecticut separately from all-U.S. figures. The routing decision does not name a specific source row count or year range of data driving this creation (unlike the Manchuria exemplar, which cites 20 rows across two named sources) -- it instead frames this as adding the state as a real, distinct administrative entity with no existing candidate polity, consistent with a systematic pass creating one row per U.S. state (following usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co in this same run). What confirms Connecticut is a distinct reporting/administrative entity is uncontroversial: it is one of the fifty states, with its own government since 1788 (and colonial self-government since 1662), and long-standing use as a state-level reporting unit in economic and demographic series.

**Territory description:** Connecticut occupies the southwestern corner of New England, bordered by New York to the west, Massachusetts to the north, Rhode Island to the east, and Long Island Sound to the south -- readily locatable on any modern map as the small coastal state directly northeast of New York City. Its external boundaries have been effectively fixed since its 1786 cession of western land claims (the "Connecticut Western Reserve" in present-day Ohio) resolved its last major territorial dispute, well before both its 1788 ratification and this entry's adjusted 1800 start year, so the modern GADM boundary is treated as valid for the full 1800-2025 span.

## Predecessors and successors

Connecticut has no predecessor or successor polity row in this database. It was a self-governing English colony under its own royal charter (1662) well before U.S. independence, ratified the federal Constitution in 1788 as one of the original 13 states, and its span here runs open/current through 2025 with no interruption or successor. No colonial-era Connecticut entry exists in this database, so there is no predecessor edge to record; if one is added later, this page's open question on the 1788-1800 gap becomes directly relevant to it as well.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-iso3-subunit

**Used the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern, not a bespoke code**

Connecticut is a standard U.S. state with no distinct historical name of its own separate from statehood (unlike Alaska Territory or Hyderabad), so it follows the pattern used by 57 of 66 subnational rows: ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end. The subunit token chosen is CT, the USPS/ISO 3166-2:US code for Connecticut, matching the two-letter convention already used for the other U.S. states added in this harness run (usa-al, usa-ar, usa-az, usa-co). The full code is USA-CT-1800-2025.

### d-start-year-1800-not-1788

**Start year set to 1800, not Connecticut's 1788 ratification, because no earlier national container exists**

The routing decision's proposed start_year is 1788, Connecticut's ratification of the U.S. Constitution as one of the original 13 states. That date is administratively correct, but the earliest national USA polity row in this database is USA-1800-1803 -- there is no container row covering 1788-1800, and the containment gate rejects an edge that falls outside either party's span, so a container edge cannot be manufactured to plug that gap. Rather than leave 1788-1800 uncontainered (which the harness explicitly treats as a gate failure), this entry's start_year is set to 1800, the earliest year the national chain can actually contain it. This departs from the country convention's implied original-13 rule (ratification date) and is flagged as an open question below: the correct fix is likely a new pre-1800 national USA row, not a permanent 1800 floor on every original-13 state.

### d-five-container-edges

**Five container edges tile 1800-2025 across the successive national USA rows**

The national USA chain in the polity table is split into five rows by era: USA-1800-1803, USA-1803-1848, USA-1848-1867, USA-1867-1959, USA-1959-2025. Connecticut's (adjusted) 1800 start coincides with the first of these, so all five edges are used, each trimmed to that national row's own span, tiling the full 1800-2025 range with no gap.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1800-gap-original-13

**Should Connecticut's span (and every original-13 state's span) start in 1788, requiring a new pre-1800 national USA container row?**

Connecticut ratified the U.S. Constitution on January 9, 1788, one of the original 13 founding states. This page starts at 1800 only because the polity table's national USA chain begins at USA-1800-1803, and the containment gate cannot accept an edge for 1788-1800 against a row that does not cover those years. The routing decision itself flagged this: 'the listed container chain's earliest entry is USA-1800-1803, which does not cover 1788-1800.' If a pre-1800 national USA row is added later (e.g. USA-1788-1800, covering the Constitution's ratification through the first national row's current start), this page's start_year should be moved back to 1788 and a new container edge added. Until then, 1788-1800 Connecticut-level data, if any exists in the ingested sources, has no home in this row.

### oq-boundary-stability-since-1788

**Has Connecticut's external boundary been stable since 1788, or only since some later date?**

The polygon assigned (GADM 4.1 admin-1 feature USA.7_1) captures Connecticut's present-day boundary. Connecticut settled its western land claims (the Connecticut Western Reserve, ceded to the federal government in 1786, and remaining claims resolved by 1800) before this entry's adjusted 1800 start year, so no boundary change is expected to fall inside 1800-2025.
