# Montana Territory

## Summary

Montana Territory was organised by the Organic Act of 26 May 1864 from the north-eastern part of Idaho Territory, in the middle of the Bannack and Alder Gulch gold rushes. It kept its original boundaries throughout — the 49th parallel on the north, the 104th meridian on the east, the 45th parallel and the Continental Divide / Bitterroot Range on the south and west — and was admitted as the 41st state on 8 November 1889 under the Enabling Act of 22 February 1889.

This row carries the territory from 1864 to 1889 (end exclusive) as a
`type: subnational` unit of the United States, alongside the state row that succeeds it,
[usa-mt-1889-2025](usa-mt-1889-2025.md). It follows the precedent of the Territory of Alaska
([alk-1867-1959](alk-1867-1959.md)) and of the Australian colonies
([nsw-1800-1901](nsw-1800-1901.md) and its siblings): the pre-statehood administration is its
own row, and the state row starts at admission.

The span runs from organisation in 1864 to admission in 1889 (exclusive), where USA-MT-1889-2025 begins. The territory's outline did not change, so no boundary break falls inside the span.

**Why this entry exists.** USDA NASS Quick Stats, ingested through the `juan-subnational` panel
(source component "USDA NASS Subnational", reporting unit `USA-MONTANA`, label `MONTANA`),
reports 111 rows for 1873-1888 (16 year(s)) — wheat, oats, barley and potatoes (area, production and yield) — before the
state existed. Until issue 658 those years were routed to the state row with
`disposition: back_cast`, which asserts that the values are a reconstruction onto a boundary
that did not yet exist. That was the wrong claim: the boundary DID exist, as Montana Territory, and
it is the same boundary the state was admitted with. The years are therefore observed data
for a territory that had no row, and they now route here as observed (blank disposition).
Created by Claude for issue 658.

## Territorial extent

The northern Great Plains and Rockies between the 49th parallel and Wyoming/Idaho, from the Bitterroot Range east to the Dakota line at 104°03'W: the modern state of Montana.

- **Polygon.** `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.27_1`, the modern state boundary, which is
  locally present and already attached to the successor state row. The polygon's measured
  area is 380,901 km² (projected, from `data/final/polygon_feature_index.csv`); that is a
  measurement of the attached geometry, not independent evidence about the territory.
- **Why the modern polygon is usable.** The territory's legislated boundary from 1864
  is the boundary the state was admitted with, so the geometry describes the same ground.
  The territory's legislated boundaries are those of the state, and the polygon is recorded as `assigned`. Large parts of the territory were Indian reservation land throughout the span; that is land tenure inside the boundary, not a boundary difference, and it does not change the polygon.
- **Year-stamped source.** `usaboundaries-newberry` (the Newberry Library's Atlas of
  Historical County Boundaries, via USAboundaries) is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and
  would give dated territorial outlines, but it is not fetched in this checkout, so it was not
  used.

## Decisions

### d-territory-row-not-back-cast

**The pre-statehood years route here as observed data, not to the state as back_cast**

Issue 657 separated two cases that the ledger had merged: a modern boundary projected onto
years when it did not exist (`back_cast`), and an earlier administration that did exist and
had no row. Montana Territory is the second case. Its boundary was the state's, so the series
counts the same ground before and after admission; what changed in 1889 was status,
not territory. The state row keeps its admission start, and this row takes the territorial
years.

### d-span-is-the-territory-not-the-data

**The span is the territory's own, not the data's**

The data covers 1873-1888; the row spans 1864-1889, the years this boundary
existed as Montana Territory. The alias in `pipelines/polity-autoimprove/state/applied_aliases.csv`
covers only the data years, so no year routes to a polity that did not exist.

### d-code

**`USA-MTT-1864-1889` follows the `<ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>` pattern**

The state rows use `USA-<abbr>-<start>-<end>`; the territory takes the same abbreviation
plus `T`, so the pair sorts together and the code cannot collide with the state row.

## Open questions

### oq-territorial-series-provenance

**Were the 1873-1888 figures published for the territory at the time, or rebuilt later?**

Quick Stats serves them under the state name as `SURVEY` data. The frame is the same either way; the provenance of the individual estimates is not established here.

### oq-boundary-survey-adjustments

**Surveyed-line adjustments between 1864 and 1889 are unchecked**

No legislated boundary change is known, but the eastern and southern lines were surveyed during the span. Any adjustment would be a sliver; it has not been checked against a year-stamped source such as `usaboundaries-newberry`, which is registered but not fetched here.

### oq-arizona-convention

**Arizona is modelled the other way**

[usa-az-1863-2025](usa-az-1863-2025.md) starts at the 1863 organisation of Arizona Territory
and carries the territory and the state in one row, so its 1882-1911 data is already observed
data on a polity. The eight territory rows created for issue 658 split territory from state
instead. Both are internally consistent; whether Arizona should be split to match is left
open, because it would change an existing row's span rather than add one.
