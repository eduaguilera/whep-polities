# Estado de México

## Summary

Estado de México is a first-order federal state of the United Mexican States, one of the original states named in the 1824 constitution that created the federation. This entry is typed `subnational` — not national — because it is a constituent unit inside the sovereign Mexican state, not a sovereign entity itself; national sovereignty over this territory is carried throughout by the MEX-1800-1848 and MEX-1848-2025 rows, and this row exists purely to carry the reporting territory that Mexican statistical sources tabulate separately at the state level. The state has not been static: at its 1824 founding it was several times its current size, including territory later split off as the states of Guerrero (1849) and Hidalgo and Morelos (both 1869), and Mexico City/Distrito Federal was carved out of it at the federation's founding. Today it is a densely populated state surrounding Mexico City on three sides, home to the country's largest state population and a major share of national manufacturing and agricultural output. The 1824 start_year follows this database's default rule for original 1824-admission Mexican states (Estado de México is not one of the three listed departures — Baja California, Baja California Sur, Quintana Roo — that were later territories), even though the state's own boundary was not stable until 1869. end_year 2025 reflects that it remains a current, active federal entity.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures 24,989 valued rows across five indicators for Mexico's Estado de México, previously routed to the national totals row MEX-1848-2025 (and possibly the pre-1848 MEX-1800-1848), because no subnational Mexican entity existed in this database to receive state-level data. Routing state-level statistics to the national row was wrong because Estado de México is roughly 1/32nd the area of Mexico as a whole and a distinct administrative and statistical unit — folding its figures into the country total either double-counts against other explicit state entries or misrepresents a regional series as a national one. What confirms the entity was distinct and not a residual/national-total case: the 1824 Federal Constitution names Estado de México among Mexico's original constituent states, and INEGI has continuously reported it as federal entity code 15 with its own statistical series (population, production, agriculture) independent of national aggregates, exactly the kind of dedicated sub-national reporting that qualifies a unit for its own polity row under this database's governing policy.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The routing decision identified `gadm-4.1-adm1` as the correct registered source for Mexican ADM1 states, but the cached `gadm41_adm1.gpkg` in this repository only covers 81 countries and Mexico is not one of them, so `polygon_source` is set to the registered slug `gadm-4.1-adm1` with `polygon_status: unassigned` (registered_source_unfetched route) rather than `assigned`. No `polygon_area_km2` is recorded because no feature is attached.

**Why this entry exists.** The routing decision reports 24,989 valued rows across five indicators previously matched to the national `MEX-1848-2025` row, which is roughly 32x the state's own area and represents the whole of Mexico, not one federal state within it. A state is not its country: routing these rows to the national total would silently aggregate Estado de México's output into all-Mexico figures and lose the sub-national signal the source data actually carries. No boundary/name-matching source was found to auto-confirm the unit, but its existence and statistical treatment as a distinct reporting territory is not in question — INEGI and Mexican federal census/production statistics have reported Estado de México as a discrete first-order division since the 19th century.

**Territory description.** The modern state of Estado de México wraps around three sides of Mexico City (Ciudad de México) in the country's central highlands, bordering the states of Querétaro, Hidalgo, Tlaxcala, Puebla, Morelos, Guerrero, and Michoacán. Its current area is approximately **22,357 km²** (INEGI figure for the present-day boundary), making it a mid-sized Mexican state by area but the most populous by population. This modern-boundary figure describes the state only from 1869 onward; for 1824-1869, before Hidalgo and Morelos were separated, the historical territory was substantially larger — plausibly 2-3x the modern figure — which is exactly the mismatch flagged in the open questions below and not yet resolved by any polygon in this database.

## Predecessors and successors

Estado de México has no predecessor or successor row in this table: it does not split from or merge into another entity across 1824-2025 at the level this database tracks. Its territory did shrink twice through internal splits (loss of Guerrero in 1849, loss of Hidalgo and Morelos in 1869), and Mexico City/Distrito Federal was separated from it at the very start of the 1824-2025 span, but none of those are tracked as separate WHEP polities feeding into or out of this one, so predecessor/successor stay empty rather than pointing at rows that do not exist.

## Sourced claims

- INEGI (Mexico's national statistics institute) lists Estado de México as state code 15, with capital Toluca de Lerdo, per the current federal division still in force since the 1869 split of Hidalgo and Morelos.
- The 1824 Federal Constitution of the United Mexican States (Constitución de 1824) named Estado de México among the original constituent states of the federation, with a territory then covering most of the modern states of Guerrero, Hidalgo, and Morelos in addition to the present-day Estado de México.
- Guerrero was constituted as a separate state by decree of October 27, 1849, and Hidalgo and Morelos were both separated from Estado de México in 1869, fixing the state's boundary close to its present form.

## Decisions

### d-code-format-mex-mex

**Used ISO 3166-2 style MEX-MEX-1824-2025 rather than a bespoke code**

The dominant subnational pattern is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end>. For Estado de México there is no existing WHEP convention (0 prior MEX subnational rows), so I picked the subunit token to match the state's own ISO 3166-2 code (MX-MEX), giving MEX-MEX-1824-2025. This looks unusual because iso3 and subunit are both 'MEX', but it is not a bespoke/historical-territory code: the state is not a defunct polity with its own proper name distinct from the modern administrative unit, so the bespoke pattern (used for ALK, HYD, RYU) does not apply. Consistency with the ISO 3166-2 subdivision code was preferred over inventing an abbreviation, since INEGI's own numeric state code (15) is not alphabetic and would be less legible than MEX.

## Open questions

### oq-polygon-fetch-mexico-gadm

**GADM 4.1 ADM1 needs Mexico rows fetched before a feature_id can be assigned**

The routing decision found gadm-4.1-adm1 is the correct registered source for Mexican states, but the locally cached gadm41_adm1.gpkg only covers 81 countries and Mexico is not among them. polygon_source is therefore set to the registered slug with polygon_status: unassigned rather than assigned, per the registered_source_unfetched convention. Whoever next runs the GADM ingest for Mexico should extract the Estado de México ADM1 feature (INEGI state code 15) and set polygon_feature_id here; until then this entry has no geometry and no area figure.

### oq-start-year-1824-confirmation

**1824 admission date is assumed from the country default rule, not independently confirmed**

The routing reasoning applied Mexico's default admission-year rule (1824 for original federal states) to Estado de México without checking Mexican federal history specifically. Estado de México was indeed one of the 19 original states named in the 1824 federal constitution, but its territory in 1824 was far larger than today — it originally included what are now the states of Guerrero (split off 1849), Hidalgo (split off 1869), and Morelos (split off 1869), plus Mexico City / Distrito Federal was carved out of it in 1824 itself. This entry's territory description below refers to the MODERN boundary (post-1869), which means for at least 1824-1869 the polygon-to-be-assigned will substantially over-claim or misrepresent the actual historical extent. Whether the pre-1869 decades should instead be a separate polity or carry an explicit territorial caveat is unresolved.

### oq-data-rows-not-verified

**The 24,989 valued rows cited in the routing decision were not independently re-examined here**

The routing_reasoning states 24,989 valued rows across five indicators motivate this entry, but this page-authoring pass did not re-query the underlying data tables to confirm the indicator list, year coverage, or source names. If those rows turn out to be concentrated in only part of the 1824-2025 span, or to actually belong to a differently-bounded reporting unit (e.g. only the post-1869 state, or including Mexico City before 1824's split), the start_year and territorial extent claims here would need revision.
