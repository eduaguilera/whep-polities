# Guainía (department of Colombia)

## Summary

Guainía is a department of Colombia occupying a remote stretch of the country's southeastern Amazon-Orinoco border region, bordering Venezuela to the east and Brazil to the south. This page covers it as a first-level administrative department from 1991, when the 1991 Constitution converted Colombia's remaining territorios nacionales (national territories administered directly by Bogotá without their own departmental government) into full departments, through 2025, the open/current end used for still-active departments in this database. Before 1991 Guainía had no department-level government of its own — it was a comisaría and then territorio nacional, and its statistics for that era appear as part of Colombia's national totals or an intermediate territorial administration, not as a distinct department. That is why `type` is `subnational` and the polity does not exist as a row before 1991: there is no separate sub-national administration to attach a row to in the earlier period, only the territory counted inside national or territorio-nacional totals. The department's capital is Inírida, on the Guainía/Inírida river system in the sparsely populated far east of the country. Guainía's 1991 creation is one of the explicit departures the country convention records from the default rule used for departments that already existed before this database's start: Amazonas, Arauca, Casanare, Guainía, Guaviare, Putumayo, San Andrés, Vaupés and Vichada were all promoted from territorio nacional to department at the same 1991 constitutional moment, rather than having existed as departments across the database's full span. This page is the department-level follow-on for that promotion, exactly as COL-AMA-1991-2025 is for Amazonas.

**Why this entry exists.** This entry captures the department-level portion of Colombian subnational statistics (source: juan-subnational) tabulated for Guainía from 1991 onward. Before this decision, Guainía-labelled data — including years after 1991 — would have matched only the national row COL-1922-2025, which is roughly 16x larger than the department (1,141,748 km2 for Colombia's mainland+islands vs. ~72,238 km2 for Guainía); routing post-1991 department-level statistics there would silently attribute a small eastern-border territory's figures to the whole country. That was wrong because Colombia's 1991 Constitution gave Guainía its own departmental government and reporting identity from that year, distinct from the pre-1991 territorio nacional/comisaría era when it had no such separate administration and no GADM admin-1-level candidate existed to match against (0 matches in the checked GADM admin-1 set). The historical source confirming this entity's distinctness is the 1991 Constitution itself, converting territorios nacionales into departments, plus the country convention's explicit enumerated-departure list, which already treats Amazonas, Arauca, Casanare and Guainía identically on this point — this entry applies the same verdict already recorded for the closest sibling unit, COL-AMAZONAS, to Guainía. Pre-1991 data (1915-1990) for this unit is back_cast to the national polities covering those years, per the routing decision, consistent with the dominant interpolated_scaled/scaled/fallback allocation methods observed in that period's rows, rather than direct observation of a department that did not yet exist.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Not yet assigned. No polygon available in the GeoPackage for this period. The intended source is `gadm-4.1-adm1`, which is registered in `scripts/sources.yaml` and marked present_locally, but the actual local GADM 4.1 admin-1 extract only covers 81 countries and Colombia is not among them — zero fetched geometry exists for any Colombian department right now, so this is a data-fetch gap rather than a boundary-selection problem. This is the `registered_source_unfetched` case, matching the routing decision: the source is real and registered, just not fetched locally, so `polygon_feature_id` is left null rather than a guessed GID_1.

**Territory description:** Guainía department occupies a large, sparsely populated wedge of Colombia's far east, in the transition zone between the Orinoco and Amazon basins, bordering Venezuela (Amazonas state) to the east across the Guainía/Negro river system and Brazil to the southeast, with Vichada to the north and Guaviare/Vaupés to the west and south. Its capital and only significant town is Inírida, at the confluence of the Inírida, Guaviare and Atabapo rivers — findable on a modern map near Colombia's border tripoint region with Venezuela. The department's official area is commonly cited as approximately 72,238 km2 (Colombian national statistics, DANE), making it one of Colombia's larger but least populated departments, covered mostly by tropical forest, savanna and the Estrella Fluvial de Inírida wetlands. No figure here is measured from an attached polygon — there is none yet — these are stated administrative-boundary figures pending the GADM fetch.

Because the 1991 boundary is the modern one (Guainía has not been re-partitioned since its creation as a department, though its territorio nacional predecessor had different, larger extents that also covered parts of what is now Guaviare and Vichada in earlier 20th-century administrative arrangements), once GADM 4.1 Colombia data is fetched, the department's present-day GADM geometry can be assigned directly rather than treated as a retrojected proxy, since this polity's entire 1991-2025 span postdates the boundary GADM would represent.

Guainía has no predecessor polity row of its own: before 1991 it was a comisaría/territorio nacional administered directly by the national government of Colombia, not a self-governing unit with its own reporting identity, so its pre-1991 existence is folded into the national chain (COL-1922-2025 and predecessors) rather than represented by a separate department-level page. This page's predecessor field is left empty rather than pointing at COL-1922-2025, because that national row's successor field does not list this page (successor: []) and editing it from here would create an asymmetric, gate-failing edge — see oq-col1922-successor-edge. No successor is declared either: end_year 2025 is the open/current end used for still-current Colombian departments, and Guainía has not been superseded or re-partitioned since its 1991 elevation.

## Sourced claims

- Guainía was elevated from comisaría/territorio nacional to full department (departamento) status under Colombia's 1991 Constitution (Constitución Política de Colombia, 1991), which converted the country's remaining national territories into departments with elected governors and assemblies.
- GADM 4.1's admin-1 layer digitises Colombia's 32 departments including Guainía at present-day boundaries; as of this ingest that layer is registered in scripts/sources.yaml but the locally fetched subset (covering 81 of GADM's countries) does not include Colombia, so no feature can yet be assigned to this row.

## Decisions

### d-code-pattern-col-guainia

**Code follows the dominant ISO3-SUBUNIT-start-end pattern**

Chose COL-GUA-1991-2025 over a bespoke code. Colombia has no country-specific subnational precedent beyond the sibling departments already created in this run (COL-AMA-1991-2025 and others), all of which follow <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> — 57 of 66 subnational rows in the table use this pattern. Guainía is an ordinary first-level department promoted from territorio nacional status in 1991, with no distinct historical name of its own the way Alaska Territory or Hyderabad State have, so the bespoke-code exception does not apply. GUA is used as the subunit token; it is not a standard ISO/DANE two-letter code but is readable and does not collide with the other COL rows already present (AMA for Amazonas, etc.).
