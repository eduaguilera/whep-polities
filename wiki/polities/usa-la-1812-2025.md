# Louisiana (state of the United States)

## Summary

Louisiana as a state of the United States, from its admission to the Union in 1812 to the present (2025, the database's open end-year convention for still-current polities). This is a `type: subnational` entry: Louisiana was never a sovereign polity in its own right within this database's scope, and the type marks it as a reporting/administrative subdivision of the national USA chain rather than a rival sovereign entity, exactly as the sibling Alabama entry (USA-AL-1819-2025) and the earlier Alaska entry (ALK-1867-1959) are marked. It was created because a subnational data source (the same one that produced the Alabama entry in this same pipeline pass) reports statistics at the individual-state level for Louisiana, and no existing polity row in this table represents Louisiana specifically -- only the five national-era USA rows (USA-1800-1803 through USA-1959-2025) and a handful of other individual states exist as siblings. Routing that data to the national USA row would understate the resolution the source actually provides, the same reasoning that motivated the Alabama and Alaska entries as separate rows rather than folding their data into the national aggregate.

**Why this entry exists.** Input data: a subnational US source (`juan-subnational`, the same dataset that drove the sibling USA-AL-1819-2025 entry created in this same pipeline pass) reports statistics tagged to Louisiana specifically, distinct from national USA totals; exact row counts and the covered year range for the Louisiana-labelled rows have not been independently pulled and quoted in this page (see open question oq-juan-subnational-row-count) but the routing decision this page implements was generated from the same harness pass and unit-detection logic that produced the Alabama sibling. Before this entry existed, any Louisiana-labelled subnational data would have had nowhere state-specific to route to among the existing rows and would default to the ISO3-level national USA chain (USA-1800-1803 ... USA-1959-2025), which aggregates all fifty states and cannot represent state-level variation the source actually distinguishes -- the same problem the Alabama and Alaska entries were created to solve. What confirms Louisiana was a distinct, trackable administrative unit rather than an artifact of the source's labelling: Louisiana has been a constitutionally distinct state of the Union, with its own government, statehood date (1812), and continuous existence as a first-order US administrative division, since before any economic-history source in this database begins reporting -- it needs no additional historical corroboration beyond standard US constitutional history, unlike a contested or short-lived polity.

## Territorial extent

**Polygon status:** Copied/assigned from `gadm-4.1-adm1` feature `USA.19_1` (modern Louisiana). Louisiana's external boundary has been stable since statehood in 1812 aside from minor surveyed adjustments to the Sabine River line with Texas (settled by the 1840s) and small changes along the Mississippi/Pearl River borders with Mississippi; none of these approach the scale of a Trianon- or Congress-Poland-style redrawing, so reusing the modern boundary as a proxy for the full 1812-2025 span is a defensible `assigned` status rather than a flagged proxy, following the same reasoning applied to Alabama and other antebellum-admitted states in this table.

**Territory description:** Louisiana occupies the Mississippi Delta and adjacent Gulf Coast in the south-central United States, bordered by Texas to the west, Arkansas to the north, Mississippi to the east, and the Gulf of Mexico to the south -- readily located on any modern US map as the boot-shaped state containing New Orleans and Baton Rouge. The state's land-plus-water area is approximately 135,700 km2 (about 112,800 km2 of land and 22,900 km2 of inland/coastal water, reflecting its extensive delta wetlands and lakes); the figure measured directly from the attached GADM geometry in this database is stated separately below and should be read as a measurement of that polygon, not as independent confirmation of the historical state's exact area at any given date, since coastal Louisiana has also lost significant wetland area to subsidence and erosion over the 20th century -- a physical change the fixed modern polygon does not track.

## Predecessors and successors

No predecessor or successor polity rows exist for Louisiana in this table. Louisiana entered the Union directly as the 18th state in 1812, formed from the Territory of Orleans (itself carved from the Louisiana Purchase); no prior polity in this database represents the Territory of Orleans specifically, so this entry has no predecessor edge. It remains a current US state, so it has no successor. Both lists are empty by design, matching the pattern of the sibling USA-AL-1819-2025 entry, which is likewise a state admitted directly with no distinct antecedent polity tracked here.

## Decisions

### d-code-follows-dominant-pattern

**Code follows the dominant <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> pattern, not the bespoke Alaska form**

The routing decision under implementation flagged the admission year (1812) as unverified and noted Alaska (ALK-1867-1959) as a sibling precedent, but Alaska's code is one of only three bespoke exceptions in the table (ALK, HYD, RYU), reserved for historical territories with names of their own that predate or diverge from their modern statehood -- Alaska's polity is a *territory* era distinct from the state it became. Louisiana has no such distinct pre-statehood identity in this table; it enters directly as a state. So this page follows the dominant pattern used by the just-created sibling USA-AL-1819-2025 (Alabama), giving USA-LA-1812-2025 rather than a bespoke code. The admission year 1812 is corroborated independently below in Territorial extent, not merely carried over from the proposal.

## Open questions

### oq-antebellum-boundary-changes

**Louisiana's 1812 boundary is not identical to the modern one used as proxy**

The state admitted in 1812 excluded the Florida Parishes strip along the Mississippi border, which had been under dispute and was folded into Louisiana only piecemeal in the years immediately after admission (the area was already occupied under the West Florida annexation of 1810, but formal inclusion in the new state's parishes took time to settle). The GADM 4.1 admin-1 polygon used here (USA.19_1) reflects the modern, settled boundary and is used as a proxy for the full 1812-2025 span under the same low-vintage-risk reasoning used for other antebellum-admitted US states (e.g. Alabama): no source in this repo quantifies how much territory, if any, the 1812-era boundary differed from the modern one, so this is asserted rather than measured. A dedicated 1812-vintage boundary, if one were constructed, might shave a small area off the northeastern parishes.

### oq-juan-subnational-row-count

**Row count and year coverage of the juan-subnational source for Louisiana have not been tabulated here**

This page follows the sibling Alabama page (USA-AL-1819-2025) in citing `sources: [juan-subnational]`, on the assumption that the same subnational dataset that motivated Alabama's creation also carries Louisiana-labelled rows, since both were processed together by this pipeline's harness in the same routing pass. But the actual row count, year range, and label text for Louisiana specifically have not been pulled from that source and quoted in this page the way the Manchuria exemplar quotes exact row counts per source. If the underlying data for Louisiana turns out to start later than 1812 (e.g. only from a 20th-century extract), the start_year here (statehood-based, not data-based, per the routing reasoning) would still be correct under this repo's stated convention, but the gap between statehood year and first data year is worth recording once the source is checked.
