# Beylik of Tunis

## Summary

The Beylik of Tunis (1800-1881) tracks the autonomous Ottoman tributary state in modern Tunisia from the database start year to the establishment of the French protectorate. Tunisia was an Ottoman province from the sixteenth century; from the seventeenth century it became semi-autonomous under the Husainid dynasty (established 1705). Biger describes it as "a province of the Ottoman Empire in the nineteenth century, until French protection was imposed by force by a treaty signed on 12 May 1881" — the Treaty of Bardo, which ends this row. The row ends when TUN-1881-2025 begins. CShapes does not cover the pre-1886 period; the polygon is back-projected from the 1886 CShapes entry (area ~15.19 sq deg, measured 155,359 km² in ESRI:54034).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** CShapes 2.0, COW 616, vintage 1886 (back-projected); the
  pre-1886 period is outside CShapes' 1886–2019 scope.
  [cshapes-2.0 §scope](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#scope)
- **Polygon status.** PROXY. The vintage (1886) sits five years *after* this row's
  span ends, because CShapes' coverage begins in 1886 and offers no step inside
  1800–1880 at all. It is the earliest available vintage of the same feature, and
  CShapes records only two steps for COW 616 — 1886–1955 and 1956–2019 — at an
  identical 155,359 km², so there is no in-span alternative and no later
  divergence to back out.
- **Approximation direction and magnitude.** The 1886 boundary is the French
  protectorate's, and the Bardo treaty of 1881 transferred authority rather than
  territory: France took over the Beylik, it did not enlarge it. Measured in
  ESRI:54034 the source feature is 155,359 km² and the published (simplified)
  geometry 155,471 km², against a modern Tunisia of 163,610 km² — so the
  direction is a ~5% *understatement* of present-day extent, from CShapes' 1886
  treatment of the Algerian and Libyan desert frontiers, and an exact match to
  the successor row, which is the intent.
- **Area.** CShapes records **two** entries for COW 616 — 1886–1955 and 1956–2019 —
  at the same area (~15.19 sq deg, 155,358 km² in the shapefile's own `area` field
  and 155,359 km² measured in ESRI:54034); the 1956 break is the independence date,
  not a territorial change. An earlier version of this page said "three entries",
  which the shapefile does not support. The same area is back-projected for
  1800–1880. No territorial changes are known for this period.
- **COW.** TUN, 616.
- **Boundaries.** Tunisia is bounded by Algeria to the west, Libya to the east, and the Mediterranean to the north and east. No major boundary changes are recorded during this period.

## Decisions

- [log decision-swe-mng-tun-restructure](../log.md#decision-swe-mng-tun-restructure) — Chain restructure confirming this row as the predecessor to TUN-1881-2025.
- **2026-08-13 (issue 159): rebound from `paine-2024/Tunis` to `cshapes-2.0/616@1886`.**
  Issue 159 measured a **3.55×** area step at 1881 — 43,752 km² to 155,471 km² — the
  largest source-changing step in the database, and read it as a convention
  difference: Paine modelling effective control, CShapes claimed territory, with the
  Sahara as the difference. **Re-measuring rejected that reading.** Of the 111,741 km²
  the successor holds and this row did not, only 61,369 km² lies south of 33.44°N.
  **42,728 km² is north-WESTERN Tunisia** — Béja, Jendouba, Le Kef, Kasserine and
  Gafsa all fall *outside* the Paine polygon while Kairouan, Siliana and Sfax fall
  inside it (point-in-polygon, ESRI:54034 for areas). The Medjerda valley is the
  Beylik's grain belt and the seat of its tax base; no account of Husainid effective
  control excludes it while including Kairouan. So the polygon was not a stricter
  convention, it was the wrong shape: Paine's `Tunis` feature carries
  `Source = "44 North Africa - map 1848.jpg"`, one traced atlas page, with
  `SQKM = 0`.
  Three further facts decided it: (1) the ingest note for
  [paine-et-al-2024](../sources/paine-et-al-2024.md) states the dataset's coverage as
  *sub-Saharan African precolonial polities*, and of the **41** rows in this database
  bound to `paine-2024`, this was the **only** one outside sub-Saharan Africa;
  (2) this page's own prose and Sourced-claims section had described the polygon as
  back-projected CShapes 1886 all along, and its Contradictions section flagged the
  `paine-2024` frontmatter as unverifiable; (3) the step it produced is not an event —
  the Treaty of Bardo transferred authority over the Beylik, it did not quadruple it.
  After the rebind the 1881 step measures **1.00×**, and the family's two rows carry
  the same source, so a per-km² series crossing 1881 no longer shows a discontinuity
  that did not happen.
  **Not done:** `polygon_area_km2` is still null on both TUN rows, so
  `validate_polygons` check A still cannot examine either (issue 59), and the Beylik's
  *actual* pre-1881 southern frontier — CShapes' 1886 line is the protectorate's — is
  left as an open question rather than guessed at.

## Open questions

### oq-pre-1881-southern-frontier

_(replaces `oq-paine-2024-source`, closed 2026-08-13: the source **is** ingested as
[paine-et-al-2024](../sources/paine-et-al-2024.md), and this row no longer uses it.)_

The polygon is CShapes' 1886 protectorate boundary back-projected across 1800–1880,
so it asserts the protectorate's desert frontiers for the Beylik. Biger gives no
pre-1881 boundary detail for Tunisia, and Husainid authority in the far south
(Nefzaoua, the Dahar and the Ouara plateau) was tributary rather than
administered — the reason the previous `paine-2024` binding excluded it. A source
that draws the Beylik's own southern limit would replace the back-projection; until
one exists the choice is between a documented over-statement of the south and a
polygon that also loses the north-west, and the over-statement is the smaller error.
Any such polygon must cover Béja, Jendouba, Le Kef, Kasserine and Gafsa.

### oq-husainid-boundaries

The Husainid dynasty (from 1705) and earlier Ottoman governance did not significantly alter Tunisia's external boundaries. However, the exact extent of Tunisian administrative authority over border regions (particularly near Algeria and Libya) in the 1800-1881 period is not documented in currently ingested sources. Biger does not give detailed pre-1881 boundary information for Tunisia.
