# La Paz (department of Bolivia)

## Summary

La Paz is a department (admin-1 unit) of northwestern Bolivia, spanning the Andean altiplano around Lake Titicaca and the city of La Paz down through the Yungas valleys into Amazonian lowlands in its north. Its capital, the city of La Paz, has served as the seat of Bolivia's executive and legislative branches since the early 20th century, even though Sucre (in Chuquisaca) remains the country's constitutional capital and seat of the judiciary. La Paz is one of the departments organized at the republic's founding in 1825/1826, not one of the three later-created departures documented elsewhere in this table (Tarija 1831, Beni 1842, Pando 1938), so its span here starts in 1825 rather than at a documented later separation date. This entry is `type: subnational` because La Paz has never been sovereign in its own right: it is carried as a first-level administrative reporting unit inside Bolivia, following the convention already applied to Beni (BOL-BEN-1842-2025), Chuquisaca (BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025), and Cochabamba (BOL-COCH-1825-2025) — a department-level admin-1 unit that appears as a distinct reporting unit in agricultural, trade, or census statistics gets its own polity row rather than being folded into the national total.

**Why this entry exists.** This row is created directly from a routing decision (unit_id `BOL-LAPAZ`) rather than from a specific data extract examined here: the routing stage identified La Paz, a Bolivian department, as a first-level administrative unit that this repository's country convention for Bolivia requires to have its own polity row, following the precedent set by BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and continued by BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025 and BOL-COCH-1825-2025. The routing_reasoning confirms no existing candidate polity represents La Paz specifically: the only polities sharing ISO3 BOL are the four national-era rows (BOL-1825-1903, BOL-1903-1909, BOL-1909-1938, BOL-1938-2025), all whole-country totals rather than the department, and the routing decision explicitly rejected the data extract's proposed 1900 start date in favor of the department-founding convention's 1825/1826 start, per its span_basis. What confirms La Paz is a distinct, long-standing administrative entity rather than an artefact of routing is Bolivia's own department history: it is one of the departments organized at the republic's founding, is not among the three documented later departures (Tarija 1831, Beni 1842, Pando 1938), and has been administered separately from Chuquisaca, Cochabamba, and the other departments ever since, hosting the country's executive and legislative seat of government since the early 20th century — a distinction from Sucre's judicial-capital role that itself confirms La Paz's administrative distinctness. As with the three prior Bolivian department entries, no specific dataset or row count has yet been examined for this entry; it was created purely from the routing-stage convention, and what data currently routes here should be checked once the row exists (see open questions).

**Territory description:** La Paz department occupies northwestern Bolivia, bordering Peru to the northwest and Chile briefly to the west, and the Bolivian departments of Oruro, Cochabamba, and Beni. It spans three distinct zones: the high Andean altiplano around Lake Titicaca (shared with Peru) and the city of La Paz/El Alto conurbation, the steep Yungas valleys descending the eastern Andean slope, and Amazonian lowland rainforest in its northern reaches bordering Pando and Beni. Its capital and largest city is La Paz, seat of Bolivia's executive and legislative branches (though not the constitutional capital, which is Sucre). ## Decisions

### d-code-format-lapaz-three-letter

**Used BOL-LP-1825-2025 rather than a longer mnemonic or the ISO 3166-2 letter**

Bolivia's ISO 3166-2 subdivision code for La Paz is BO-L, a bare letter. The dominant table pattern for this country's subnational rows, set by BOL-BEN-1842-2025 and continued in BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025 and BOL-COCH-1825-2025, is <ISO3>-<SUBUNIT>-<start>-<end> with a short readable mnemonic. LP is used here (two letters, matching La Paz's common two-word abbreviation as used in Bolivian department-code tables and airport/postal shorthand) rather than a three-letter form like LAP or LPZ, since La Paz does not collide with any other ISO3 or existing BOL subunit code in this table, and a two-letter form is unambiguous once qualified by the BOL- prefix. This departs slightly from the three-to-four-letter length of BEN/CHUQ/COCH; if a future reviewer prefers strict length consistency, LPZ (matching the city's IATA/postal abbreviation) is the fallback.

### d-container-tiled-four-eras-lapaz

**Tiled the container across all four BOL national eras (1825-1903, 1903-1909, 1909-1938, 1938-2025), not just BOL-1825-1903 as the routing decision proposed**

The routing decision's routing_concerns explicitly flagged that container assignment should come from the harness's containment chain rather than the single BOL-1825-1903 code it asserted, and left container_code null in the proposed object. Since this entry's end_year is 2025 (La Paz is a current department, still populated and administered), a single 1825-1903 edge would leave 1903-2025 uncontained, which the containment gate rejects. Reading the polity table's four BOL national-era rows (site/polities.csv lines 87-90) and tiling La Paz's full 1825-2025 span across all four mirrors exactly the solution already adopted for BOL-BEN-1842-2025, BOL-CHUQ-1825-2025, and BOL-COCH-1825-2025, each containered by the same four edges for the same structural reason: the routing stage only ever proposes the first-overlapping national era, never the full chain a long-lived admin-1 unit actually needs.
