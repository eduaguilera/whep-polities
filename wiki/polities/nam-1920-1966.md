# Namibia (1920-1966)

## Summary

Namibia (1920-1966) covers South West Africa under the League of Nations (later UN) mandate administered by the Union of South Africa, from 17 December 1920 — when CShapes records the mandate starting — to 26 October 1966, when the UN General Assembly Resolution 2145 revoked South Africa's mandate. CShapes records this as status=mandate, owner=ZAF (COW 560). Despite the UN revocation in 1966, South Africa refused to relinquish control; the WHEP split here follows the CShapes status change, which marks the formal international legal transition even if de facto administration continued.

## Territorial extent

Identical territory throughout. South-western Africa bounded by Portuguese Angola (north), Bechuanaland/Botswana (east), South Africa (south), and the Atlantic Ocean (west), with the Caprivi Strip. CShapes 2.0 area ~72.31 (unchanged from occupation period). Polygon source: CShapes 2.0 (dependencies=TRUE). [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)

## Contradictions

The WHEP split at 1966 follows the CShapes status change (mandate → colony) driven by the UN revocation. However, since the territory and administration were unchanged, the WHEP polity-definition rule (split only on territorial change) might favour merging NAM-1920-1966 and NAM-1966-1990 into a single row. The current chain follows CShapes status distinctions rather than strict territorial change. This is a potential chain-architecture question.

## Open questions

### oq-nam-1920-1966-merge-candidate

Under the WHEP polity-definition rule, splits should reflect territorial change, not legal/administrative status changes. The CShapes area is identical for NAM-1920-1966 and NAM-1966-1990. A case can be made that these two rows should be merged into a single NAM-1920-1990 row. The counter-argument is that the mandate vs. post-mandate distinction has analytical significance for trade and economic history. A human should decide.
