# USSR (1945-1991)

## Summary

The CSV labels this row "USSR (1945-1991)"
[database](../../data/final/polities_database.csv). **This label is
correct** — the USSR existed from 30 December 1922 until its formal
dissolution on 26 December 1991. No anachronism. This is the final
row in the F228 chain, covering **47 years** of the Soviet superpower
era.

The USSR was the largest country in the world at ~22.4 million km².
Post-WWII territorial gains included Karelia, Konigsberg/Kaliningrad,
and the Kuril Islands (inherited from the previous row). Cold War.
Space race. Warsaw Pact. Brezhnev stagnation. Gorbachev reforms
(glasnost, perestroika). Fall of the Berlin Wall 1989. Baltic
independence declarations 1990. Failed August 1991 coup. USSR
formally dissolved 26 December 1991 into 15 successor republics.

This is the **largest dissolution event in the wiki by number of
successor states** (15, vs Austria-Hungary's 7).

Predecessor per CSV: [f228-1940-1945](f228-1940-1945.md).
Successor per CSV: 15 republics — RUS-1991-2014, BLR-1991-2025,
EST-1991-2025, LTU-1991-2025, LVA-1991-2025, MDA-1991-2025,
UKR-1991-2014, ARM-1991-2025, AZE-1991-2025, GEO-1991-2025,
KAZ-1991-2025, KGZ-1991-2025, TJK-1991-2025, TKM-1991-2025,
UZB-1991-2025. **None has a wiki page yet — massive coverage gap.**

This completes the F228 chain: 1800 -> 1856 -> 1905 -> 1914 -> 1917
-> 1918 -> 1920 -> 1921 -> 1940 -> 1945 -> 1991 (11 rows total,
covering 191 years of Russian/Soviet history).

## Territorial extent

- **1945: post-WWII territorial peak.** Soviet borders at their
  maximum extent. Gains from the previous row: Karelia (from
  Finland), Konigsberg/Kaliningrad (from Germany), Kuril Islands
  and South Sakhalin (from Japan), Carpathian Ruthenia (from
  Czechoslovakia).
- **1945-1991: essentially stable borders.** Soviet external borders
  were stable throughout the Cold War period. The only significant
  internal change was the **transfer of Crimea from the RSFSR to
  the Ukrainian SSR** (1954) — an internal administrative move with
  no effect on the USSR's external boundaries.
- **Area: ~22.4 million km²** — the largest country in the world by
  area throughout this period.

## Sourced claims

- **Biger §russia.** "Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union
  the RSFSR separated from the other 14 republics... 25,000,000
  Russians found themselves living outside Russia"
  [biger-1995 §russia](../sources/biger-1995.md#russia). Confirms
  15 successor states and the scale of the diaspora problem.
- **1945-1991: Cold War era.** Soviet superpower in bipolar world
  order. Warsaw Pact (1955). Space race (Sputnik 1957, Gagarin
  1961). Cuban Missile Crisis (1962). Detente. Afghanistan
  invasion (1979)
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md).
- **1985-1991: Gorbachev reforms.** Glasnost (openness) and
  perestroika (restructuring) launched by Mikhail Gorbachev.
  Unintended acceleration of centrifugal forces
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md).
- **1989: Fall of the Berlin Wall.** Collapse of Soviet-backed
  regimes across Eastern Europe
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md).
- **1990: Baltic independence declarations.** Lithuania (11 March
  1990), Estonia, and Latvia declared independence, beginning the
  dissolution process
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md).
- **1991-08-19: Failed August coup.** Hardline attempt to depose
  Gorbachev collapsed after three days, accelerating the breakup
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md).
- **1991-12-26: USSR formally dissolved.** The Supreme Soviet voted
  to dissolve the Union. 15 successor states emerged
  [wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11](../sources/wikipedia-russian-empire-2026-04-11.md).

### oq-cold-war-territorial-stability

Soviet borders were essentially stable 1945-1991. The only changes
were internal — Crimea transfer from RSFSR to Ukrainian SSR (1954),
etc. This 47-year stability contrasts with the frequent boundary
changes in the preceding F228 rows. The CSV correctly treats this
as a single row.
