# Germany (to 1919)

## Summary

The current CSV tracks this row as "Germany" from **1800** to
**1919** — a **120-year single row** [database] that contains
multiple events the same docs/ files describe as major transitions
but does not split on any of them. The row covers:

- **1800–1866**: Kingdom of Prussia + the German Confederation era
- **1864–1867**: Second Schleswig War → Austro-Prussian War →
  dissolution of the German Confederation → formation of the North
  German Confederation
- **1870–1871**: Franco-Prussian War
- **1871-01-18**: Proclamation of the German Empire at Versailles,
  formally unifying the North German Confederation with Bavaria,
  Württemberg, Baden, Hesse-Darmstadt, and Alsace-Lorraine
- **1871–1918**: German Empire era — Bismarckian Reich, Berlin
  Conference colonial acquisitions, WWI entry, military collapse
- **1918–1919**: Armistice, abdication, Weimar Republic transition,
  Treaty of Versailles

docs/03_ENTRIES_RATIONALE.md:115 literally describes this row as
"German states → Empire (1871) → loss of Alsace-Lorraine (1919)"
but the CSV does not split at 1871. Under the critical-stance rule
[log decision-csv-is-evidence-not-authority], this row is a
candidate for splitting under a strict reading of
[log decision-whep-polity-definition]. See
[log proposal-deu-ger-chain-audit] Finding 4.

Several other audit findings bear on this chain — see the
proposal entry for the full set. The most impactful:

- **Finding 1** — GER-1800-2025 is documented as an aggregate
  trade row (docs/00, docs/01, docs/03, docs/06, docs/11) but the
  CSV has it as `polity_type = national`. Direct docs-vs-CSV
  contradiction.
- **Finding 3** — no Prussia (PRU) row despite Prussia being the
  core pre-1871 German state.

Predecessor per CSV: NA (pre-database floor). Successor per CSV:
[deu-1919-1920] (the brief 1919–1920 interregnum between
Versailles and full Weimar constitution).

## Territorial extent

- **Polygon source.** `CShapes 2.0 + CShapes-Europe` per the CSV
  [database]. CShapes starts 1886 [cshapes-2.0 §scope], so
  1800–1885 is either CShapes-Europe or a back-projection from
  1886. The 1886+ period is CShapes proper.
- **Massive territorial changes within the row window.** In 1871
  years alone the polity's effective borders changed dramatically:
  - 1864: Second Schleswig War — Prussia + Austria take
    Schleswig, Holstein, Lauenburg from Denmark
    [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1864]
  - 1866: Austro-Prussian War — Prussia annexes Hanover,
    Hesse-Kassel, Nassau, Frankfurt (~95,000 km² added directly
    to Prussia; see docs/03 pre-1871 list of states that
    disappear as WHEP rows at 1866 or 1871)
    [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1866]
  - 1867: North German Confederation formed, integrating 22
    northern German states under Prussian leadership
    [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1867]
  - 1871-01-18: German Empire proclaimed, adding Bavaria,
    Württemberg, Baden, Hesse-Darmstadt to the Confederation
    and annexing Alsace-Lorraine (~14,500 km²) from France
    [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1871-01-18],
    [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1871-alsace-lorraine]
  - 1884–1885: Berlin Conference authorizes the colonial empire
    (German Southwest Africa, German East Africa, Cameroon,
    Togoland, German New Guinea — tracked separately as WHEP
    colonial rows) [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1884-1885]
  - 1919-06-28: Treaty of Versailles — Germany loses Alsace-Lorraine
    (back to France), Eupen-Malmedy (to Belgium), Northern Schleswig
    (to Denmark), West Prussia + Posen (to Poland / "Polish
    Corridor"), Upper Silesia (partially, to Poland), Memel
    Territory (to Lithuania eventually), Saar Basin (to League
    of Nations administration 15 years), and the entire colonial
    empire (to Allies as League mandates). Total metropolitan
    loss: ~65,000 km²
    [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1919-06-28]
- **A single polygon cannot represent this row faithfully.** The
  1800 Prussia polygon, the 1871 German Empire polygon (including
  Alsace-Lorraine + all southern German states), and the 1919
  Weimar polygon (reduced post-Versailles) are three very different
  shapes. The CShapes 2.0 post-1886 polygon presumably represents
  the German Empire at some point between 1886 and 1918, but this
  has not been verified for the CShapes+CShapes-Europe hybrid
  polygon specified by the CSV.
- **COW code 260** [database]. **Critical-stance observation:**
  COW 260 is "German Federal Republic" (West Germany / reunified
  Germany post-1949), not the Prussia / German Empire. COW uses
  code **255** for "Germany (Prussia)", which is the code on
  GER-1800-2025. So this row (1800–1919) is using the *modern*
  FRG's COW code for a polity that historically ran as the
  Prussia-led German Empire under COW 255. This is an internal
  inconsistency between DEU's label ("Germany (to 1919)") and its
  cow_code (260, FRG). Not flagged as a separate audit finding
  because it's a consequence of the DEU/GER split issue already
  covered in the proposal.

## Predecessors and successors

- **Predecessor:** NA (pre-database floor). Pre-1800 Prussia
  existed as a distinct kingdom from 1701; pre-1806 it was part
  of the Holy Roman Empire. The database floor of 1800 puts
  the early history out of scope.
- **Successor:** [deu-1919-1920]. This is a 1-year row covering
  the 1919–1920 period between the Treaty of Versailles (28 June
  1919) and the full Weimar constitution (11 August 1919) — or
  some other 1-year window; the CSV's end points are not
  documented. The split from this row to deu-1919-1920 at 1919
  is a real territorial event: the Treaty of Versailles reduced
  Germany by ~65,000 km² and ended the colonial empire.

## Sourced claims

- **1834: Zollverein customs union.** The Prussian-led German
  Customs Union [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1834]. The
  CSV's adjacent row `GER-1800-2025 "Germany/Zollverein"`
  references this in its name but is documented elsewhere as a
  trade-aggregate row rather than a political polity; see
  [log proposal-deu-ger-chain-audit] Finding 1.
- **1864: Second Schleswig War.** "The Second Schleswig War
  against Denmark in 1864"
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1864]. Prussia and Austria
  defeated Denmark and took Schleswig (Prussia) + Holstein
  (Austria) under the 1865 Gastein Convention. The WHEP row
  `DNK-1800-1864 Denmark (with Schleswig-Holstein)` ends at
  1864 and is followed by `DNK-1864-1920 Denmark (to 1920)`
  [database], which is consistent with this event on the Danish
  side. The WHEP row `SHL-1800-1866` covers Schleswig-Holstein
  1800–1866.
- **1866: Austro-Prussian War and German Confederation
  dissolution.** "The Austro-Prussian War in 1866"
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1866]. Prussia annexed
  Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau, Frankfurt, and Schleswig-Holstein
  directly. The WHEP rows HAN-1800-1866, HES-1800-1871 (end
  1871, not 1866 — note the discrepancy),
  SHL-1800-1866 [database] reflect parts of this but the
  pattern is not fully consistent. Cross-referenced with
  [biger-1995 §austria] which says 1866 (not 1867) was "the
  real territorial-political turning point" and that "in the
  Treaty of Versailles (1919), the boundaries of 1866 were
  restored" for Austria [biger-1995 §austria].
- **1867: North German Confederation.** "North German
  Confederation, comprising the 22 states north of the Main"
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1867]. Prussian-led
  political union replacing the dissolved German Confederation.
- **1870–1871: Franco-Prussian War.** "The Franco-Prussian War
  of 1870 overwhelmed the remaining opposition to unified
  Germany" [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1870-1871]. Ran
  19 July 1870 – 10 May 1871 (Treaty of Frankfurt).
- **1871-01-18: German Empire proclaimed.** "William was
  proclaimed Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of
  Versailles" [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1871-01-18].
  The Empire combined the North German Confederation + the four
  southern German states (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden,
  Hesse-Darmstadt) + the soon-to-be-annexed Alsace-Lorraine.
  This is **the largest single-event territorial-economic
  consolidation in 19th-century Europe** and the canonical
  candidate mid-row split point for this WHEP row. See
  [log proposal-deu-ger-chain-audit] Finding 4.
- **1871-04-16: Imperial Constitution.** Formalized the political
  structure [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1871-04-16].
- **1871: Annexation of Alsace-Lorraine.** ~14,500 km²
  transferred from France to the new Empire via the Treaty of
  Frankfurt (10 May 1871)
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1871-alsace-lorraine].
  The French side of this transfer will be recorded on
  [fra-1800-1919] once that polity page is created.
- **1884–1885: Berlin Conference.** "Berlin Conference"
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1884-1885]. Authorized
  the European partition of Africa and kicked off Germany's
  colonial empire — German Southwest Africa, German East Africa,
  Cameroon, Togoland, German New Guinea. WHEP tracks these as
  separate colonial rows (GSW, GEA, etc.), so the colonial
  acquisitions are **not** part of this row's metropolitan
  territorial extent.
- **1914-07-28: WWI entry.** "World War I began on 28 July 1914"
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1914-07-28]. Germany
  declared war on Russia on 1 August 1914 following mobilization.
- **1918-11-09: Abdication and republic proclaimed.** "Abdication
  of Wilhelm II and republic proclaimed: 9 November 1918"
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1918-11-09]. The German
  Empire formally ceased with Wilhelm II's abdication. The
  Weimar Republic was proclaimed in Berlin the same day.
- **1918-11-11: Armistice of Compiègne.**
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1918-11-11]. Signed at
  05:00, took effect at 11:00.
- **1919-06-28: Treaty of Versailles.**
  [wikipedia-german-empire-2026-04-11 §1919-06-28]. Germany lost
  Alsace-Lorraine (to France), Eupen-Malmedy (to Belgium),
  Northern Schleswig (to Denmark), West Prussia and Posen (to
  Poland, creating the "Polish Corridor"), Upper Silesia
  (partially, to Poland), Memel Territory (to Lithuania), Saar
  Basin (under League of Nations administration for 15 years),
  and the entire colonial empire (to Allies as League mandates).
  Total metropolitan loss: ~65,000 km². The WHEP CSV uses
  **1919** as the end date of this row, aligning with the
  treaty.

## Open questions

### oq-biger-germany

Biger 1995's GERMANY main entry (pages ~224–240, estimated)
was not read in this ingest because of context budget. A
future iteration should add a `§germany` section to
`wiki/sources/biger-1995.md` covering GERMANY main entry plus
GERMANY–FRANCE (for Alsace-Lorraine), GERMANY–POLAND (for the
1919 Polish Corridor), and GERMANY–DENMARK (for Schleswig).
Biger will likely give exact dates for the 1866 Prussian
annexations and the 1919 territorial losses.

### oq-1871-split-candidate

Under a strict reading of the WHEP polity-definition rule
[log decision-whep-polity-definition], the 1871 German
unification is a candidate mid-row split point:
- Real territorial event (Alsace-Lorraine annexation,
  ~14,500 km²)
- Real economic integration (the four southern German states
  joined the Empire's customs union and political union)
- Specific date (18 January 1871 proclamation at Versailles)

docs/03_ENTRIES_RATIONALE.md:115 acknowledges the 1871
unification as an event within this row. The CSV does not
split. This is covered in [log proposal-deu-ger-chain-audit]
Finding 4 and is Tier X (repo-rule decision).

### oq-1866-austro-prussian-split-candidate

The 1866 Austro-Prussian War is an earlier candidate split
point. Prussia annexed Hanover, Hesse-Kassel, Nassau,
Frankfurt, and Schleswig-Holstein directly in 1866 (per
standard historical fact; not yet pinned to a specific Biger
or Wikipedia quote). The WHEP rows for these states all end
at 1866 or 1871 but the DEU row doesn't split at 1866. If
1871 is considered too political a date, 1866 is the strictly
territorial date (Prussia's first round of direct annexations).

### oq-pru-row-existence

No Prussia row exists in the CSV despite Prussia being the
core pre-1871 German state. See [log proposal-deu-ger-chain-audit]
Finding 3. Until that proposal is resolved, any claim made on
this row about "Prussia" is implicitly referring to a polity
that is not directly represented in the CSV.

### oq-cow-code-255-vs-260

This row has `cow_code = 260` (German Federal Republic, post-1949)
but the historical polity it describes (Prussia / German Empire
1800–1919) would correspond to COW 255 under COW's own codes.
The CSV's adjacent row `GER-1800-2025` uses the correct COW 255.
This is an internal inconsistency that the audit proposal's
Finding 1 (reconcile GER as aggregate) would partially resolve.
Flagged here because it's not directly addressed in the proposal.

### oq-1919-end-date

The CSV ends this row at 1919 without day precision. The
options are:
- 1918-11-09 (abdication + Weimar proclamation)
- 1918-11-11 (Armistice of Compiègne)
- 1919-06-28 (Treaty of Versailles signed)
- 1920-01-10 (Treaty of Versailles came into force)

Biger's convention for Austria is to use 1919 (Saint-Germain)
rather than 1918 (armistice); the WHEP CSV uses 1919 for
Germany. Consistent. But the specific day is not pinned.
