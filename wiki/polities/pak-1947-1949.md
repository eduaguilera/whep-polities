# Pakistan (1947-1949)

## Summary

Pakistan (1947-1949) covers Pakistan at independence from Britain (15 August 1947) until a CShapes polygon change on 5 January 1949, which expanded Pakistan's area from approximately 861,625 km² to approximately 942,826 km². This territorial increase almost certainly reflects the Karachi Agreement of 27 July 1949, which formalized the ceasefire line in Kashmir following the First Kashmir War (1947–1948), attributing Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan to Pakistani-administered territory. Pakistan at independence comprised two geographically separate wings: West Pakistan (the core territory including Punjab, Sindh, Balochistan, and the Northwest Frontier Province) and East Pakistan (the Bengal region). COW codes Pakistan as independent from 15 August 1947 (ccode=770).

## Territorial extent

Polygon source is CShapes 2.0. CShapes records Pakistan with COW code 770 as independent from 1947-08-15, with an area of approximately 861,625 km² in the first step (1947-08-15 to 1949-01-04). The second step (from 1949-01-05) shows approximately 942,826 km², a substantial increase reflecting Pakistani-administered Kashmir territory. [cshapes-2.0 §coding-changes](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-changes)

The 1947 CShapes polygon is flagged with `b_def` (boundary definition dummy variable), indicating that some borders — particularly the Kashmir ceasefire line — were not yet fully defined at independence. [cshapes-2.0 §coding-borders](../sources/cshapes-2.0.md#coding-borders)

## Sourced claims

## Open questions

### oq-british-india-predecessor

Pakistan was created by the partition of British India. The WHEP CSV has no predecessor row listed for PAK-1947-1949 (predecessor=NA in the CSV). A British India row should logically precede Pakistan, but whether WHEP tracks British India as a single entity or through multiple rows (Bengal, Punjab, etc.) is unclear. A future ingest should identify the correct predecessor chain and link it.

### oq-1949-event-identification

The CShapes 1949-01-05 date boundary almost certainly reflects the First Kashmir War ceasefire, but the exact event — the UN Security Council ceasefire resolution (UNSCR 39, 20 January 1948; UNSCR 47, 21 April 1948; or the Karachi Agreement 27 July 1949) — should be confirmed. The 5 January 1949 date does not match any of these standard dates. A future ingest should check for a diplomatic or field-command ceasefire event on or around 5 January 1949.
