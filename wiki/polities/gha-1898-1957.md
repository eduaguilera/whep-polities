# Ghana (1898-1957)

## Summary

The British Gold Coast colony in its expanded configuration, from 1898 (after the incorporation of Ashanti territory and northern territories) until 1956 (when British Togoland joined following a plebiscite, leading to the 1957 boundary that became independent Ghana). "The Ghanaians were the first people in sub-Saharan Africa to become independent from colonial rule when they gained independence from the British in 1957" [biger-1995 ghana](../sources/biger-1995.md#ghana). "Gold Coast became an independent state on 6 March 1957, adopting the name Ghana. Ivory Coast gained independence on 7 August 1960" [biger-1995 ghana](../sources/biger-1995.md#ghana). The 1898 start date marks the incorporation of the Northern Territories and Ashanti after the Fourth Anglo-Ashanti War (1896). CShapes records the boundary change.

## Territorial extent

Polygon from CShapes 2.0. Ghana: area 92,098 sq mi [238,533 sq km]. Bounded by Ivory Coast (415 mi; 668 km), Burkina Faso (341 mi; 549 km), and Togo (545 mi; 877 km) [biger-1995 ghana](../sources/biger-1995.md#ghana). The British established the Gold Coast colony on 13 January 1886; northern territories were made part of the colony in 1897 and the kingdom of Ashanti was formally annexed in 1901 (p.243-244).

The CShapes polygon (~238,533 km²) covers the Gold Coast Colony proper and does NOT include British Togoland (~33,773 km²). British Togoland was a UN Trust Territory administered by Britain as an integral part of the Gold Coast from 1920 onwards (unified administration, common budget, common customs union). FAO data labelled "Gold Coast and British Togoland" or variant spellings (see Data routing below) is routed to this polity; this results in a ~14% understatement of the geographic territory covered by the data label. This is an accepted approximation given the unified administrative and economic treatment of the two territories. British Togoland was formally incorporated into Ghana following the 1956 UN plebiscite, after which GHA-1957-2025 covers the combined territory.

## The IIA series is mostly ex-German Togoland, not the Gold Coast (added 2026-08-18)

This page documents the FAO-1952 "Gold Coast and British Togoland" routing for 1949-1951 and says
nothing about `ghana|iia|1909-1945` (102 rows), which is the larger IIA assertion routed here. Measured
against the raw IIA extract, that label is not predominantly Gold Coast data:

```
layer B `iia/ghana`, 82 dated production values
  german togoland        52   63%
  british gold coast     22   27%
  british togoland        0    0%
  french togoland         0    0%
```

**The source distinguishes all four territories and the database has a polity for each.** The raw
extract carries `british gold coast` (693 rows, 1909-1945), `german togoland` (160 rows, 1910-1945),
`british togoland` (105 rows, 1922-1938) and `french togoland` (487 rows, 1922-1938). WHEP has
GHA-1898-1957, GTO-1884-1920, BTL-1920-1957, FTO-1920-1960 and GCT-1919-1956 ("Gold Coast and British
Togoland"). Nothing was missing; the harmonisation pooled them anyway.

**Two specific consequences.**

First, `german togoland` runs to 1945 in the source even though the colony was partitioned into British
and French mandates in 1920 — IIA kept the old heading. Split by era, this label is 44% german togoland
before 1920 and **69% after**, so the post-partition years are mostly ex-German territory sitting on the
Gold Coast row.

Second, the two labels that DO describe the post-1920 mandates properly — `british togoland` and
`french togoland` — contribute **nothing** to this label, and nothing to any other. French Togoland
became independent Togo and was never under Gold Coast administration under any arrangement.

**The same raw label also feeds a second modern country.** layer B `iia/togo` (10 values) is **100%**
`german togoland`. So one source label is split across `ghana` and `togo` with no rule visible in
either, which is why a fix here has to be a re-derivation from the raw labels rather than a reroute of
this assertion.

**GCT-1919-1956 is a candidate target but not obviously the right one.** It is Gold Coast plus BRITISH
Togoland; the label's dominant contributor is undivided German Togoland, which includes the French
share. Choosing GCT would be right only if the post-1920 `german togoland` heading in fact covers the
British mandate alone — which the source's own separate `british togoland` label argues against.

`ghana|iia|1909-1945` is quarantined on this measurement (`state/quarantine.csv`, issue 315). The
FAO-1952 routing documented below is unaffected: that label is explicitly "Gold Coast and British
Togoland" and GCT is the right home for it.

## Decisions

### dec-gold-coast-british-togoland-fao-routing

FAO 1952 yearbook labels "Gold Coast and British Togoland" (and four abbreviation/case variants) for years 1949–1951 are routed to GHA-1898-1957 rather than creating a separate polity. Rationale: (1) Britain administered British Togoland as an integral part of the Gold Coast from 1920 — unified budget, customs, and administration; (2) the FAO 1952 yearbook interchanges "Gold Coast", "Gold Coast and Br Togoland", and similar labels for what is effectively the same reporting unit; (3) the territory difference (~33,773 km² / ~14%) is a documented approximation, not a fundamental mismatch. No separate WHEP polity for "Gold Coast incl. British Togoland" is warranted. The polygon understates the combined territory by ~14%; this is flagged as an accepted approximation.

### dec-gha-1956-hole-closed

**`end_year` 1956 -> 1957 on 2026-08-17 (issue 252), code renamed `GHA-1898-1956` -> `GHA-1898-1957`.**
`end_year` is EXCLUSIVE, so the old columns covered 1898-1955 and left **1956 in no
Ghana row**: `GHA-1957-2025` begins in 1957. Two independent things say the year was
meant to be inside this row rather than outside it:

| evidence | reading |
|---|---|
| the alias `Gold Coast` [fao1952] carries `year_end: 1956` (36 observed rows) | written against the CODE, inclusive; a consistent pair is `end_year == year_end + 1` |
| CShapes 2.0 breaks gwcode 452 on **1956-03-06**, so 1956 is the last calendar year the Gold-Coast-proper step (212,416.75 km²) opens in | the boundary belongs at the 1956/1957 turn, not at 1955/1956 |

Direction chosen deliberately. The alternative — starting `GHA-1957-2025` in 1956 —
would have published the post-plebiscite 239,046.81 km² polygon (Gold Coast **plus**
British Togoland) for 1956, in which year [btl-1920-1957](btl-1920-1957.md) already
holds Togoland, so the same ~33,771 km² would be counted twice. Extending this row
keeps 1956 on the Gold Coast proper and leaves British Togoland where it is.

## Open questions

### oq-gha-1956-vs-1957

PARTLY ANSWERED 2026-08-17 (issue 252). The question asked whether "a brief 1956-1957
transition row is needed", and the immediate reason it looked necessary — that 1956 was
covered by nothing — is gone: this row now covers it. What remains open is the narrower
territorial point, that CShapes gives 1956-03-06 to 1957-03-05 its own step at
239,046.81 km² (Gold Coast with British Togoland integrated after the May 1956
plebiscite), which no WHEP row reproduces. Modelling it would require deciding what
happens to `BTL-1920-1957` for that year; until then 1956 is served by the
Gold-Coast-proper polygon, which understates the integrated territory by ~14% — the
same accepted approximation this page already documents for the combined FAO labels.
