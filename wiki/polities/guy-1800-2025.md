# Guyana

## Summary

Guyana is a country on the northern coast of South America. Biger 1995 covers Guyana extensively in the Guyana-Venezuela boundary entry (pp.260-262), documenting the long history of European colonization and the major Essequibo territorial dispute with Venezuela [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana). The first European settlers arrived from the Netherlands in 1581, and the territory changed hands between the Dutch and British multiple times before the British purchased the colonies from Holland and united them into British Guiana in 1831. Guyana became independent on 26 May 1966. WHEP tracks Guyana as a continuous row from 1800 because the territory remained essentially constant.

## Territorial extent

The Guyana-Venezuela boundary runs for 462 miles (743 km.) from the Atlantic coast to the tripoint with Brazil on Mount Roraima [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana). The largest territory disputed in Latin America is the Essequibo region between Venezuela and Guyana, covering some 50,000 sq. miles (130,000 sq. km.) (p.262). ## Sourced claims

- The first European settlers arrived in northern South America from the Netherlands in 1581 (p.261). [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana)
- Between 1781 and 1814 the colonies of Berbice, Demerara, and Essequibo were ruled alternately by the Dutch and British (p.261). [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana)
- The British purchased the colonies from Holland and in 1831 the three colonies were united into the colony of British Guiana (p.261). [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana)
- Guyana became independent on 26 May 1966 (p.261). [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana)
- The Essequibo dispute with Venezuela covers some 50,000 sq. miles (130,000 sq. km.), more than five times the area of Belize and two-thirds of Guyana (p.262). [biger-1995 &sect;guyana](../sources/biger-1995.md#guyana)
