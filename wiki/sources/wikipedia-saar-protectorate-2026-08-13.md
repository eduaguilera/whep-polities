# Saar Protectorate — Wikipedia (accessed 2026-08-13)

## Why it was ingested

[saa-1947-1957](../polities/saa-1947-1957.md) carried five claims citing
`wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-06-24.md`, a file that was never ingested
(whep-polities issue 5). The existing `wikipedia-late-colonial-2026-04-16.md`

## What it adds

### establishment

- "On 10 July 1945, US forces left the Saar, and French troops established
  their occupational administration. On 16 February 1946, France
  disentangled the Saar from the Allied zones of occupation and established
  the separate Saar Protectorate, which was de facto no longer under the
  joint Allied jurisdiction by the Allied Control Council for Germany."
- "With effect from 20 July 1946, 109 municipalities of the Prussian Rhine
  Province within the French zone were added to the Saar Protectorate. By
  18 December 1946 customs controls were established between the Saar and
  allied occupied Germany."
- "On 15 December 1947 the Saar was constituted by its constitution as the
  Saarland (took effect two days later), with an elected government under
  the control of the French high commissioner Gilbert Grandval."
- "On 16 July 1947 the Saar mark replaced the Reichsmark as legal tender in
  the Saar Protectorate, followed by the integration of the Saar into the
  French currency area on 15 November the same year."
- "On 23 March 1948 the customs union with France was confirmed, taking
  effect on 1 April."
- Boundary churn after the constitution: "By further territorial
  redeployments between the Saar Protectorate, constituted in early 1947,
  and neighbouring Rhineland-Palatinate ..., 61 municipalities returned to
  Germany, while 13 other municipalities were ceded to the Saar Protectorate
  between 8 June 1947 and 1949, followed by one further Palatine
  municipality incorporated into the Saar in the latter year."

Note what is **absent**: no "Saar Statute" of 17 January 1947. In this
article the *Saarstatut* is the 1954 plan, below.

### saar-statute-1954

- "In the Paris Agreements of 23 October 1954, France offered to establish
  an independent 'Saarland', under the auspices of the Western European
  Union (WEU), but a referendum held on 23 October 1955 rejected this plan
  by 67.7% to 32.3% (out of a 96.5% turnout: 423,434 against, 201,975 for)
  despite the public support of West German Chancellor Konrad Adenauer for
  the plan. The rejection of the plan by the Sarrois was interpreted as
  support for the Saar to join the Federal Republic of Germany."
- The Minister-President list dates the fall of the plan the same way:
  Johannes Hoffmann "resigned after the Saar statute failed" (second term
  ending 1955).

### accession-1957

- "On 27 October 1956 the Saar Treaty established that Saarland should be
  allowed to become a state of West Germany, as provided by article 23 of
  its Grundgesetz (constitution), and so Saarland did on 1 January 1957."
- "Geographically, the post–World War II protectorate corresponded to the
  current German state of Saarland (established after its incorporation
  into West Germany as a state on 1 January 1957)."
- "On joining West Germany in 1957, it became the smallest 'federal state'
  (Bundesland), the Saarland, not counting the 'city states'."

### economic-reintegration-1959

- "The treaty also stated that economic union with West Germany was to be
  completed by 1960, with the exact date of the replacement of the Saar and
  French franc by the Deutsche Mark being kept a secret called 'Day X'
  (Tag X). Although the Saar became a state of West Germany (as Saarland)
  on 1 January 1957, the franc remained legal tender in Saarland until
  6 July 1959. Thus on that date the Kleine Wiedervereinigung (little
  reunification) was completed, after more than 13 years of separation."

### separate-representation

- "The National Olympic Committee (NOC) of the Saarland was founded in the
  spring of 1950 in the Saar Protectorate, which existed from 1947 to 1956,
  a region of western Germany that was occupied in 1945 by France. As a
  separate team, Saar took part in its sole Olympic Games at the 1952
  Summer Olympics before being allowed to rejoin the German team in 1956."
  ([Saar at the 1952 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saar_at_the_1952_Summer_Olympics))
- Separate currency: the Saar mark (1947), then the Saar franc, with "only
  French franc banknotes" circulating from 1954.

## Known limitations

- **No ISO or IOC code.** Neither article gives a country code for the
  Saar. The Olympic article establishes that Saar competed as a separate
  team in 1952; it does not name the three-letter code, and ISO 3166 did
  not exist until 1974, i.e. after the protectorate ended. The polity
  page's former "assigned ISO 3166 alpha-3 SAA during its existence" claim
  is therefore not just uncited but anachronistic, and has been rewritten.
- **No area figure**; the 2,570/2,571 km² pair on the polity page comes
  from FAO 1952 and GADM 4.1, not from here.
- The Olympic article dates the protectorate "1947 to 1956" while the main
  article dates the accession to 1 January 1957 — a rounding of the
  27 October 1956 treaty, not a competing claim.
- Wikipedia is a secondary source; the 1947 constitution and the 1956 Saar
  Treaty are published instruments and should be preferred if a WHEP
  boundary turns on the exact day.
