# History of Luxembourg (Wikipedia snapshot, 2026-04-11)

## Why it was ingested

To back the LUX-1839-2025 polity page with narrative historical claims —
CShapes gives borders but says nothing about independence dates,
personal unions, or customs unions, which is exactly what a human
making polity decisions needs.

## What it adds

The following dated claims were extracted from the article as accessed
on 2026-04-11. Each is quoted verbatim; the `§` anchor is the year the
claim concerns. If Wikipedia is later edited, this file remains the
authoritative record of what was read.

- **§1815** — Congress of Vienna: "gave formal autonomy to Luxembourg",
  establishing grand duchy status in personal union with the
  Netherlands.
- **§1839** — Treaty of London partitioned the duchy: "the predominantly
  Oil-speaking geographically larger western part of the duchy was
  ceded to Belgium." Luxembourg emerged as a formally independent grand
  duchy with roughly half its prior territory, now predominantly
  German-speaking.
- **§1842** — Luxembourg "integrated it into the German Zollverein",
  joining the German Customs Union to offset the economic effects of
  the 1839 partition.
- **§1867** — Second Treaty of London "guaranteed the perpetual
  independence and neutrality of the state"; the Prussian garrison
  withdrew and the fortress was demolished.
- **§1890** — On the death of William III of the Netherlands, the
  "grand duchy passed to the House of Nassau-Weilburg", ending the
  personal union with the Netherlands (Salic succession).
- **§1921** — After World War I, Luxembourg "entered into an economic
  and monetary union with Belgium" (the Belgium-Luxembourg Economic
  Union, BLEU).

## Known limitations

- Wikipedia is a **tertiary** source. Every claim above should ideally
  be corroborated by an academic reference (Polity V, COW state-system
  membership dates, a national history) before a polity page moves
  from `draft` to `reviewed`.
- The article is community-edited and will drift. This file reflects
  the 2026-04-11 snapshot; a later access-date source file supersedes
  it rather than overwriting it.
- The article is a narrative; it does not assert legal definitions
  (e.g. whether the personal union was a "state" in the Westphalian
  sense). Do not over-read.
- One quoted phrase contains a Wikipedia typo/variant: "Oil-speaking"
  is almost certainly "Oïl-speaking" (Langues d'oïl, French/Walloon
  dialect group). Preserved verbatim here per the immutability rule.
